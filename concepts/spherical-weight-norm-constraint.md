## Definition

**The spherical norm constraint** is an intervention in which, during training, the L2 norm of an object (the model's weights, or the activations of the residual stream) is held fixed, so that the parameters or the representations are forced onto a sphere (hypersphere) of a chosen radius; the radial (magnitude) degree of freedom is thereby removed, and information can be encoded only by the direction of the vector. In its most explicit form the notion is put at the centre by Yildirim as a "fully bounded spherical topology", which enforces L2 normalisation throughout the residual stream and entirely removes the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md) — that is, delayed generalisation ([grokking](grokking.md)) — on [modular addition](modular-arithmetic.md) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

## Elaboration

The mechanism of the two forms of constraint is different, but the idea is common: to deprive the network of the ability to encode information in a norm. In the **weight** form (Omnigrok) the constraint is implemented as a projection — after each ordinary optimisation step the weights are rescaled back to their original norm, which keeps them on a sphere in weight space \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. In the **representational** form (Yildirim) a projection operator applies strict L2 normalisation to the residual stream (the accumulating vector channel through which the transformer's layers exchange information) before each sublayer and after each residual addition, while the unembedding matrix (the linear layer turning the hidden vector into logits) is normalised too, and the logits are scaled by a fixed temperature \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

The connection with grokking is this: without a constraint, a network under cross-entropy loss tends to inflate the norm of the logits in order to lower the loss — this is so-called [naive loss minimisation](nlm.md), leading to [softmax collapse](softmax-collapse.md); and it is the high-norm "memorising" solutions that create the long plateau before the abrupt [phase transition](phase-transition.md) to generalisation. A spherical constraint structurally forbids such solutions: it collapses the gap in norms between the memorising and the generalising solution and therefore removes or sharply shortens the delay \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\].

The effect is not universal, however. Yildirim shows that on commutative tasks (modular addition and multiplication) the constraint aligns the representations with the continuous circular geometry of the Fourier solution (the "Clock" algorithm as against the fragmented memorising "Pizza", see [Clock vs Pizza](clock-vs-pizza.md)) and removes the memorisation phase entirely, but on the [non-commutative](group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md) composition of the group S5 the same connection fails: success there rests on discrete coset structures rather than on continuous [Fourier features](fourier-features-circuits.md) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Some works contest the purity of the effect: GrokTransfer notes that constraining the weight norm to a sphere "introduces instability in the training process and still involves a phase transition" \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\], while an empirical study beyond neural networks finds no spherical geometry of the "[Goldilocks zone](goldilocks-zone.md)" (the hypothesised spherical shell in weight space where generalisation is better than outside it) and calls the requirement of such a shell "too stringent" \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. The reading is supported by Omnigrok's original finding — "training with constrained weight norm can almost eliminate grokking" \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] — and by Truong's [norm-separation](norm-separation-delay-law.md) theory, which derives that the length of the delay is proportional to the logarithm of the ratio of the memorising and the generalising solutions' norms, and that a spherical topology acts directly on that mechanism \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. Constraining the weight norm (parameter space)

The original, weight-space reading (Omnigrok): the controlled object is the model's parameters themselves, and the order parameter is the L2 norm of the weights. The constraint is implemented as a projection in optimisation space: after each unconstrained step the weights are scaled back to the target norm, so that the trajectory runs on a sphere of fixed radius. The distinguishing machinery is where the sphere lives: in weight space, its radius corresponding to the "Goldilocks radius" (the shell on which generalisation is better). This form manipulates the LU / Goldilocks-zone picture directly \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], and it is exactly its empirical rigidity and stability that are contested \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]\[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\].

### B. Constraining the norm of representations (the residual stream)

Yildirim's representational reading: the controlled object is not the parameter vector but the activations of the residual stream (plus the unembedding matrix). The sphere lives in representation space: the output of each sublayer is renormalised to unit norm, and the logits are confined to a cosine geometry (cosine similarity × temperature). The distinguishing machinery is that the constraint removes magnitude as a representational axis, so that information becomes purely angular, and this is an architectural intervention (before training) rather than a recomputation of the norm during optimisation. Formally only the activation is constrained, not the whole parameter vector, yet the gap in norms that sets the delay still collapses, and the memorisation phase disappears even at zero [weight decay](weight-decay.md) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]\[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\].

### Contested

- **Miller et al.** \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]: the requirement of a spherical "Goldilocks zone" is too stringent, and in the case of grokking they studied (Gaussian regression) no such spherical geometry is observed empirically — instead there is a more complicated geometry of weight space.
- **Xu et al. (GrokTransfer)** \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]: constraining the weight norm to a sphere of the appropriate radius does accelerate generalisation, but it introduces instability into training and does not remove the phase transition itself — so it is not a clean solution to the delay problem.

### In support

- **Liu et al. (Omnigrok)** \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]: training with a constrained weight norm can almost entirely eliminate grokking — the original empirical confirmation of the effect within the LU / Goldilocks-zone mechanism.
- **Truong et al.** \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]: the norm-separation delay law is complementary to spherical topology — the latter mechanically collapses the norm ratio ‖θ_mem‖/‖θ_post‖, which by their theory sets the length of the delay, and this explains why a bounded model generalises without a memorisation phase.
