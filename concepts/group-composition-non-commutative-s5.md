## Definition

**Group composition** is a class of algorithmic [grokking](grokking.md) tasks in which the network learns to predict the result of a group's binary operation (the composition a∘b of two of its elements) from an input pair. A group here is a set with an associative binary operation, an identity and inverses. The key divide runs along commutativity: [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md) is commutative (a+b = b+a), whereas the chief "hard" subclass is the **non-commutative (non-abelian) groups**, where the order of the operands matters (a∘b ≠ b∘a). The canonical representative is the symmetric group S5: all 120 permutations of five elements under composition. The setting was introduced by Power et al. as one of the algorithmic datasets on which grokking is observed \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

## Elaboration

S5 appears already in the founding work: among the algorithmic datasets Power et al. single out the task of learning the product in the abstract group S5 \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The substantive difference from modular arithmetic is the mechanism of the solution. For cyclic (abelian) groups the network learns [Fourier features](fourier-features-circuits.md) (sinusoidal representations of frequencies), whereas for the non-commutative S5 [mechanistic interpretability](mechanistic-interpretability.md) finds the solution resting on discrete coset structures (subsets of the form gH, generated by multiplying a subgroup H by an element g) rather than on continuous Fourier components. McCracken et al. state the universality hypothesis for all group-theoretic datasets, connecting coset circuits (subnetworks implementing the operation through cosets) in permutation groups with [cosets](group-representations-cosets.md) and frequencies in modular addition \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. Kvinge et al. systematically study which group-theoretic structures narrow networks learn when trained to predict group operations, and stress that the symmetric group Sn is non-commutative \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗.

The chief disputed nuance is whether the mechanisms and theories established on commutative modular arithmetic carry over to non-commutative composition. In favour of transfer: Truong et al. show that the signature of spectral entropy collapse appears uniformly both in modular arithmetic and in composition of permutations in S5 \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], while Tian extends the scaling law of feature [emergence](emergence.md) to non-abelian groups as well \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. Against: Yildirim uses the non-commutative S5 as a negative control and finds that the [spherical constraint](spherical-weight-norm-constraint.md), which removes grokking on cyclic arithmetic, fails entirely on S5: bypassing the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md) depends on the alignment of architectural priors with the task's intrinsic symmetry and is not a universal effect \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. S5 thus plays the part of a critical test: it separates the accounts tied to the Fourier geometry of the abelian case from those claiming a general [phase transition](phase-transition.md).

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. A group operation as a generalisation of modular arithmetic

A definition through an arbitrary binary group operation: the task is to predict a∘b from the pair (a, b), and modular arithmetic turns out to be the special (commutative) case of the cyclic group Zp. The distinguishing machinery of this reading is a single group-theoretic language in which the datasets differ only by the structure of the group (cyclic, a product of cyclic ones, permutation), and it is this that makes it possible to raise the question of a universal abstract algorithm across different groups \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]\[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

### B. Non-commutativity as the source of a different mechanism

A definition that singles S5 out as a regime of its own: since composition of permutations is non-commutative (a∘b ≠ b∘a), the representation is obliged to encode the order of the operands, and the solution rests not on Fourier frequencies but on discrete coset structures. The key source of difference here is not the size of the group but the absence of commutativity: it is that which changes the [order parameter](order-parameter.md) of the solution (cosets instead of frequencies) and makes S5 a diagnostic negative control for theories derived in the abelian case \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗\[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

### Contested

Yildirim: what removes grokking on cyclic arithmetic (a spherical constraint on the norm of the representations) does not work at all on the non-commutative S5 — the models generalise on no seed while reaching perfect training accuracy. Whence the conclusion: removing the delay in generalisation is not a universal optimisation effect but a consequence of the architectural prior being aligned with the symmetry of the task \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

### In support

Tian and Truong et al.: mechanisms found on abelian modular arithmetic reproduce on non-abelian groups. In Tian the scaling law of feature emergence is validated on non-abelian groups with maximal irreducible dimension 2 as well \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; in Truong et al. the signature of spectral entropy collapse agrees between Z/pZ and composition of permutations in S5 \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], which supports a single mechanism across the type of group.
