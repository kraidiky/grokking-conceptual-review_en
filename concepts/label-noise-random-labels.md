## Definition

Label noise is the deliberate replacement of true training labels by random ones: either for part of the examples or for all of them at once. Into the [grokking](grokking.md) corpus the setting was brought by Power et al.: $k$ "outliers" — equations whose answers have been randomly permuted — are introduced into the training sample of the [canonical testbed](canonical-grokking-testbed.md), and the effect of their number on the onset of generalisation is measured \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

![The effect of randomly labelled outliers on data efficiency: the more noise, the more data is needed to generalise (fig. 6 of Power et al.)](assets/label-noise-outliers.png)

## Elaboration

In the corpus the notion appears in three roles, differing in the share of corrupted labels and in the aim. **Partial noise (outliers)** tests the robustness of generalisation to exceptions: a small number of outliers (up to 1000) does not noticeably affect generalisation performance, while a large one hinders it significantly \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]; the authors themselves read the robustness to small noise as evidence that the network's capacity far exceeds what is needed to memorise the training labels, so that the very fact of generalisation requires a non-trivial explanation (appendix A.4 of the paper). **Full noise (random labels)** is a methodological device going back to Zhang et al. (2021; a work outside the corpus): when all the labels are random there is nothing generalisable in the data, and training is forced down to pure memorisation. This yields memorisation reference networks — for comparing memorising and generalising circuits (a circuit being a subnetwork implementing a sub-algorithm; see [circuit efficiency](circuit-efficiency.md)) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], for measuring memorisation capacity directly on the [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md) task \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], and as a negative control for complexity measures: without generalisation, the estimate of the network's [Kolmogorov complexity](kolmogorov-complexity.md) does not fall \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]. The third role is **breaking correlations**: settings with noisy labels are among the setups that break the link between generalisation and [neural collapse](neural-collapse.md), and therefore serve as an instrument for separating necessary conditions of generalisation from phenomena that merely accompany it \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. Outliers: partial noise with the task's structure preserved

Power et al.'s setting. The control parameter is the number $k$ of corrupted equations; the true structure of the operation is preserved in the remaining data, so a generalising solution has to learn the rule and separately memorise $k$ exceptions. What is observed is a shift of the generalisation boundary: growing $k$ narrows the range of [training-data fractions](data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md) at which training converges to generalising models, but up to $k$ of the order of a thousand the effect is weak \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The distinguishing mechanics is a competition of "a simple rule plus exceptions" against pure memorisation, not the absence of a rule as such.

### B. Random labels: full noise as an isolator of memorisation

The device: all the training labels are made random (following Zhang et al., 2021), after which generalisation is impossible in principle and training builds only a memorising solution. This turns the setting into a measuring instrument. Varma et al. thus obtain "$C_{\text{mem}}$-only" networks, the reference for their comparison of the efficiency of memorising and generalising circuits \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. Huang et al. measure memorisation capacity as the model's accuracy on the full set of pairs with random labels, assigning the commutative pairs $(a+b)$ and $(b+a)$ the same random label so that the network cannot exploit the symmetry of the task \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. Zhang et al. use random labels as a negative control for the complexity measure BDM (Block Decomposition Method — a block approximation of Kolmogorov complexity): without generalisation, the network's complexity does not collapse \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\].

### C. Noisy labels as a breaker of generalisation correlates

A role in which label noise is not the object of study but a condition of the experiment. Existing work on neural collapse shows that a network can generalise without it, and that setups such as noisy labels can break the link between generalisation and NC \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]; hence a correlate of generalisation that does not survive label noise is an accompanying phenomenon and not a necessary condition. It is on this distinction that Han et al. build their main conclusion about flatness and NC.
