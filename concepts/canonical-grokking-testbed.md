## Definition

**The canonical grokking testbed** is the experimental setting of Power et al. (2022), inherited by most works of the corpus as the standard ground for studying [grokking](grokking.md). Unlike the card "[modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md)", which describes a mathematical class of tasks, the testbed is the whole frame of presentation: *"The datasets we consider are binary operation tables of the form $a\circ b=c$ where $a,b,c$ are discrete symbols with no internal structure, and $\circ$ is a binary operation"* \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\] — a binary operation table presented as equations of five uninformative tokens \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\], with a random fraction of the table in the training set as the main control parameter \[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\] and a small decoder-only transformer as the default model \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\]. The quintessence of the testbed is the canonical example: division mod 97 with half the data in training \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]. "Following Power et al." in later works means a reproduction of precisely this frame, not merely the choice of an arithmetic task.

## Elaboration

The make-up of the testbed is fixed in a single work. **The data** are an operation table turned into a set of equations: every element, the operation sign and the equals sign are separate tokens \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]; a list of 12 operations (nine modular ones at $p=97$ and three on the group $S_{5}$, including [non-commutative](group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md) ones) is fixed in appendix A.1.1. **The symbols are uninformative** — the property that constitutes the whole class. The authors state it in passing, in a single phrase: *"the network is not made aware of any internal structure of the elements, and has to learn about their properties only from their interactions with other elements"* \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\], with an example — *"For example the network doesn’t see numbers in decimal notation, or permutations in line notation"* \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\] — and from that wording it is easy to miss how strong a restriction has been imposed. Unfolded: each of the 97 residues is presented as a separate token, and the tokens are in no way related to one another — the network is given neither magnitude, nor order, nor adjacency: the symbol for 5 is no closer to the symbol for 6 than to the symbol for 91, and nothing hints that 96 and 0 are adjacent modulo p; there is neither a positional notation from which to derive the arithmetic digit by digit, nor any feature shared by the numbers of one residue class. Exactly the same holds for $S_{5}$: a permutation is presented as a label rather than as a sequence, so neither its length, nor its parity, nor its cycle decomposition is communicated to the network. Hence the order relation between the residues is not input data but a learned structure: the circular topology of modular addition, the adjacency by adding 8, the partition of $S_{5}$ into cosets of a subgroup — the network recovers all of it solely from the statistics of the co-occurrence of symbols in the equations. This is worth keeping in mind when carrying conclusions over to natural data, where tokens do, on the contrary, carry order, proximity and shared features. **The main lever** is the [fraction of the table](data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md) in training: *"For each training run, we chose a fraction of all available equations at random and declared them to be the training set, with the rest of equations being the validation set"* \[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\]. **The model and the optimisation** are a two-layer decoder-only transformer of width 128 with 4 heads (~4·10⁵ parameters) \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\]; the main series uses AdamW with a weight decay of 1, while the canonical figure was obtained in a different regime (Adam without weight decay at a stretched budget) — see the section "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" in the Power card on the conflation of the two regimes in inheritance.

The heirs reproduce the frame with pointwise variations, preserving the core — a table with uninformative symbols and a split fraction. Nanda et al. shifted the modulus and the recipe: *"In our mainline experiment, we take $P=113$ and use a one-layer ReLU transformer"* \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] at 30 % of the data; Gromov replaced the model and the loss — *"fully-connected two-layer networks that exhibit grokking on various modular arithmetic tasks under vanilla gradient descent with the MSE loss function in the absence of any regularization"* \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; Grokfast returned to *"the same modular multiplication task devised to report the grokking phenomenon"* \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]; later works take the *"four primary modular arithmetic operations ($+,-,\times,\div$) over the finite field $\mathcal{Z}_{p}=\{0,\dots,p-1\}$ with $p=97$"* \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\] or begin at 35 % of the data, *"to first observe grokking, as demonstrated in prior work"* \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]; Furuta et al. extended the set of operations to polynomials within the same frame \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\].

What the testbed does **not** fix — and what the heirs' conclusions diverge over — is the loss function and the optimizer (MSE with vanilla gradient descent against cross-entropy with AdamW — on this choice depends whether regularisation is needed for grokking at all); the primality of the modulus (it is needed only for division — Gromov works with an arbitrary integer $p$); and the exact set of hyperparameters — the heirs sometimes glue two of Power's regimes into one setup (an example is analysed in the item mc-2201-02177 of the Furuta card).

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The narrow sense: the canonical example

The testbed is identified with the canonical example of figure 1 of Power: *"the binary operation of division mod 97 with 50% of the data in the training set"* \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\], where each residue is presented as a separate symbol. In this usage "the testbed" is a concrete reference task on which new methods and measures are demonstrated.

### B. The broad sense: the frame for presenting operation tables

The testbed is the whole class of settings "operation table + uninformative symbols + split fraction", including non-arithmetic operations ($S_{5}$), polynomial extensions and variations of the model (an MLP in Gromov, a one-layer transformer in Nanda). In this usage, membership of the testbed is determined by the encoding and the data lever rather than by the concrete operation.

### Stepping outside

Works that leave the testbed deliberately: Hwang et al. take the investigation *"beyond the standard modular arithmetic testbed to a diverse suite of algebraic, structural, and relational domains"* \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\], arguing that generalisation is determined by the intrinsic symmetry of the task rather than by properties of the reference ground; the [real-world data](vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md) line (Liu et al., Omnigrok) shows that the phenomenon is reproducible outside the testbed as well — but in an induced setting.
