## Definition

**An optimizer** is the algorithm that updates the weights of a network at every step from the gradients of the loss; it is "critical to the success of neural network training, dictating how quickly and effectively a model converges to a solution" \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗. In the [grokking](grokking.md) corpus the choice of optimizer is not a neutral detail of the setup: already in the founding work Power et al. showed that "the details of optimisation" (the type of optimizer, [weight decay](weight-decay.md), the learning rate) substantially affect whether delayed generalisation happens at all \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The adaptive optimizers Adam and AdamW are used almost everywhere (more rarely plain SGD and new methods such as Muon), and it was precisely with an anomaly of adaptive optimizers at late stages of training that Thilak et al. first connected the mechanism of grokking \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

## Elaboration

Optimizers fall into two families. Plain SGD (stochastic gradient descent) moves all the parameters at a common rate proportional to the gradient. The adaptive optimizers Adam / AdamW assign every parameter a rate of its own, normalising the step by running estimates of the first and second moments of the gradient; AdamW additionally decouples the weight decay (the regularisation pulling the weights towards zero) from the gradient of the loss. Since grokking in the classical setting requires precisely weight decay, the optimizer and its coupling to the regularisation end up at the centre of the explanations: Thilak et al. showed empirically that without explicit regularisation grokking almost exclusively arises at the onset of a [slingshot](slingshot.md) — a cyclic anomaly of adaptive optimizers in which training periodically jumps between a stable and an unstable regime (in the spirit of the [Edge of Stability](edge-of-stability.md)) \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. This line is supported by works that read the dynamics of AdamW itself as the driver of the transition: AdamW is described as a "variance-gated" stochastic system in which the accumulation of gradient variance opens access to the generalising solution \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], and the adaptive momentum as a condition for a noticeable grokking even where plain gradient descent gives none \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. The reading of the slingshot as the internal dynamics of the optimizer is contested, however: it has been shown that the norm spikes are caused not by the optimizer but by the finite precision of the arithmetic in computing the cross-entropy \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. A separate thread of the discussion is the choice of SGD against AdamW itself: some works argue that the optimizer must "decouple memorisation from contraction", and that SGD fails entirely where AdamW reliably groks \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; the cause is linked to ill-conditioning of the gradients (a large spread of gradient scales across directions), which preconditioning optimizers (Muon, Gauss-Newton, and specially constructed methods) correct, accelerating grokking \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]\[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]\[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\]. The opposite position is taken by the reading of grokking as a [noise-driven](gradient-noise.md) escape from a metastable state, where the crossing of an energy barrier is provided precisely by SGD noise rather than by adaptive mechanics \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The optimizer as a parameter update rule

The general (non-grokking) definition: an optimizer is the procedure that sets how a gradient turns into a step in the parameters, and it is what determines the speed and the quality of convergence \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗. The key distinction inside the notion is between non-adaptive SGD (a single step scale) and adaptive Adam / AdamW (a per-coordinate scale from the moments of the gradient). Power et al. already read the choice and the tuning of the optimizer as a control parameter of data efficiency and of the very fact of late generalisation \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

### B. The adaptive optimizer and the slingshot as a condition of grokking

A definition through an anomaly of the adaptive optimizer: in the absence of explicit regularisation, grokking is tied to the slingshot — the cyclic phase jumps of an adaptive optimizer at late stages, tracked by the weight norm of the last layer \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. The distinguishing "machinery" here is that the control parameter is neither the dataset nor the weight decay but the instability of the adaptive optimizer's step itself; in the supporting formulation the role of the controlling quantity is played by the accumulated gradient variance, which "gates" the entry into the generalising basin \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]\[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\].

### C. The choice of SGD against AdamW and the conditioning of the gradients

A definition through the properties of the optimizer rather than through the regularisation: grokking sets in only if the optimizer is able to separate memorisation from the contraction of the norm, which is why AdamW groks while SGD at the same hyperparameters does not \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. The source of difference is named outright — the conditioning of the gradient matrix: ill-conditioned gradients stretch out the delayed generalisation, while preconditioning (higher-order methods, Muon, an equalisation of the singular rates) accelerates it \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]\[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]\[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\].

### Contested

What is contested is that the observed effects are the internal dynamics of an adaptive optimizer. One work shows that the slingshot is caused by the finite precision of the cross-entropy arithmetic rather than by the optimizer as such \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. Another moves the cause of grokking onto SGD noise: the model leaves a metastable state when the noise throws it across an energy barrier — that is, an adaptive optimizer is not required for the effect \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\].

### In support

Joining the thesis that the type and the dynamics of the optimizer govern grokking: AdamW as a variance-gated system \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], the adaptive momentum as an amplifier of grokking \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\], the SGD/AdamW difference through the decoupling of memorisation and contraction \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], and the explanations through the conditioning and preconditioning of the gradients \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]\[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]\[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\].
