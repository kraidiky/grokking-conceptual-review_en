## Definition

The neural tangent kernel (NTK) is the kernel to which the training of a neural network reduces in the "lazy" (linearised) regime: if the network is approximated by a model linear in its parameters around the initial point, then gradient descent is equivalent to a kernel method with the kernel `K(x, x′) = ∇wf(x)·∇wf(x′)` computed at initialisation. In the theory of [grokking](grokking.md) the NTK was introduced as the object that fixes the initial (memorising) phase: under squared error, the solution the network finds in this regime is exactly kernel regression with the initial NTK \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

![Grokking on modular arithmetic in a setting where the initial lazy phase is described by kernel regression with the NTK (fig. 2 of Kumar et al.)](assets/ntk-grokking-curves.png)

## Elaboration

The NTK came into grokking research as an instrument separating "memorisation" from "generalisation". The key hypothesis (Kumar et al.) is that grokking is a passage from the **lazy regime** (the network changes its output while barely changing its internal features, that is, `∇wf` stays approximately constant) to the **rich / feature-learning regime** (the network rearranges its features to fit the task) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. In the lazy regime the network behaves as a kernel predictor with a fixed initial NTK, so the early phase reproduces the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md): the network fits the training sample but generalises poorly, and the late jump in generalisation is the departure from the linearised approximation. This view links grokking to a wider circle of phenomena: to the sharpness of the late jump ([phase transition](phase-transition.md)), to the reading of grokking as an instance of the sudden appearance of abilities ([emergence](emergence.md)), and to Davies et al.'s observation on the kinship of grokking and [double descent](double-descent.md) as phenomena about the speed at which patterns of different complexity are acquired \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Neither a [slingshot](slingshot.md) nor an adaptive [optimiser](optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md) is required for grokking: the mechanism is stated through the NTK and needs neither a decay of the weight norm nor anomalies of Adam \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

How well the initial NTK is "aligned" with the task is measured by the **task-model kernel alignment**, taken in the general case as the centred kernel alignment CKA — a scalar measure of the agreement between the kernel's leading eigenvectors and the target labels. This quantity plays the part of an **[order parameter](order-parameter.md)** (in the spirit of statistical physics — a scalar whose change marks a change of regime): under low alignment a linear kernel model is bound to be poor on the task, and generalisation is deferred until the network learns features \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Later works sharpen and support this picture. Mohamadi et al. track the empirical kernel **eNTK** (a finite-width estimate of the NTK from the gradients of the output on the data) and show that it barely changes before overfitting and changes sharply afterwards, confirming that the main [feature learning](feature-emergence-feature-learning.md) happens already outside the kernel regime \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. Jiang et al. add to this picture [spectral bias](frequency-principle-spectral-bias.md) (the tendency of a network to learn low-frequency components first) as the cause holding the network in the lazy NTK regime, and show that preconditioned (Gauss–Newton) descent explores the NTK modes evenly and shortens the grokking delay \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The NTK as the kernel of the linearised model

The first, "mechanical" definition: the NTK is the Gram matrix of the gradients of the network's output with respect to the parameters, taken at initialisation; in the lazy regime the network is equivalent to a model linear in the parameters, and under MSE its training minimum coincides with kernel regression on that kernel \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The distinguishing machinery here is the **fixedness of the kernel in time**: as long as `∇wf` does not change, the kernel equals the initial NTK, and feature learning is defined precisely as the network leaving that linearised subspace. That is why the "memorising" solution of grokking is identified with the early lazy dynamics.

### B. NTK alignment as an order parameter of grokking

The second definition focuses not on the kernel itself but on its **agreement with the task**. In Kumar et al. there are two control parameters: the [rate](learning-rate.md) of feature learning (a laziness parameter α scaling the network's output) and the initial alignment of the NTK with the labels (in general form, the CKA) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The difference from reading A: the NTK is of interest here not as a fixed kernel but as the source of an **order parameter** — grokking arises when the leading eigenvectors of the initial NTK and the labels are misaligned, yet there is enough data for generalisation to become possible after all \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The testable consequence that separates this frame from the others: worsening the initial alignment strengthens and lengthens grokking rather than removing it.

### In support

Mohamadi et al. support the "kernel → rich regime" frame but shift the source of the difficulty. The key feature of their reading: the failure of the kernel regime on modular addition is not the accidental low alignment of a particular network but a **property of the task itself through permutation equivariance** (the invariance of the learning algorithm to permutations of the symbols): no permutation-equivariant kernel method generalises until it has seen a constant fraction of all possible data, although the same kernel can reach zero training error \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. Empirically they confirm the transition by measuring a sharp rise in the change of the eNTK immediately after overfitting \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\].

Jiang et al. support the same hypothesis from the optimisation side. Their distinguishing mechanism is **spectral bias**: the slow convergence of modes with small NTK eigenvalues holds the network in the lazy regime, whereas preconditioned descent (Levenberg–Marquardt, Gauss–Newton), by equalising the convergence rates of all the modes, exhausts the NTK subspace evenly and nearly removes the grokking delay; this they read as direct evidence that grokking is a transitional behaviour between the lazy NTK regime and the rich one \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\].

Cullen et al. approach from a third side — that of a complexity measure of the solution. The distinguishing machinery here is the local learning coefficient (LLC, a quantity of the Bayesian theory of singular learning): for the NTK linearisation it is expressed through the rank of the Fisher matrix, that is, exactly half the number of directions in which the model actually changes \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. This ties the lazy regime to something countable: as long as the network remains a kernel, its complexity is set by the rank of the kernel, and the passage to the rich regime shows up as the LLC diverging from that kernel estimate.
