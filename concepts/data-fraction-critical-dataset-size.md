## Definition

**The critical dataset size** (Dcrit) is the volume of the training sample separating two regimes of behaviour: if there is more data than Dcrit, the network eventually generalises ([groks](grokking.md)), and if less, it stays at [memorisation](memorization-phase.md). The same threshold is conveniently expressed through the **data fraction** — the share of all admissible examples of the task given over to training (in code often the parameter `TRAIN_FRAC`). Empirically the dependence of grokking on the size of the dataset was first noted by Power et al. (2022): smaller datasets require more optimisation steps before generalisation \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. It was formalised as the "critical training set size" in the [effective theory](effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md) of representations of Liu et al. (2022) \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\] and named the critical size Dcrit in the theory of [circuit efficiency](circuit-efficiency.md) of Varma et al. (2023) \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\].

## Elaboration

The canonical mechanism is **circuit efficiency** (a circuit is a subnetwork implementing some function): by Varma et al. the memorising circuit (which stores individual examples) becomes the **less efficient** the larger the training set, whereas the generalising circuit (which implements the general algorithm of the task) keeps its efficiency independently of the size of the data. Hence there exists a crossover point — the critical size Dcrit, at which both circuits are equally efficient \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. This threshold sets a whole family of behaviours. If a grokked network is trained further on a dataset noticeably **smaller** than Dcrit, memorisation becomes advantageous again and the network regresses to a poor test accuracy — this is [ungrokking](ungrokking.md); and if the size is taken **right around** Dcrit, a [phase transition](phase-transition.md) does occur, but leads only to an intermediate accuracy — [semi-grokking](semi-grokking.md) \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. Huang et al. independently reproduce Dcrit within the frame of circuit competition, defining it as a narrow range inside which the efficiencies are comparable and beyond which grokking is likely \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; the same work links the critical size to a unified view of grokking, [double descent](double-descent.md) and [emergent abilities](emergence.md).

The effective theory of Liu et al. gives an independent reading of the same threshold: the critical size is the **smallest** volume of data sufficient to determine the "right" structured representation of the task uniquely \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]; Gromov derives the critical amount of data from a dynamical model as a condition for "relatively fast" grokking \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\], while Kumar et al. formulate it as a **window**: there must be enough data for generalisation to become possible at all, but not so much that the training and the test loss coincide from the very beginning (the passage from the "lazy" regime without feature learning into the "rich" one — [lazy-to-rich](lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)) \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. In practice, below the threshold grokking is simply **unobservable**: Singh et al. study data-scarce regimes where the number of training examples falls below the critical one, and show that distillation can induce grokking even there \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. The dependence is confirmed in statistical-physics settings as well: in the Ising model the grokking delay grows as the dataset size is reduced \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\], and a sparse sub-sampling of the data reproduces the canonical grokking curve \[[3.7](#ref-3-7)\].

A number of works, however, **contest** the primacy of the size as such. Wang et al., on implicit-reasoning tasks, find that the speed of grokking is determined by the **ratio** of inferred to atomic facts in the training data and hardly depends on the absolute volume; hence a replacement of the "critical size" by a **critical distribution** of data is proposed \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. He et al., in the same line, make the ratio (inferred/atomic) the main control parameter of the speed of grokking \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]. The current picture thus holds two competing control parameters — **how much** data (Dcrit, the fraction) and **which data, and in what proportion**.

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The smallest volume that determines the representation (the effective theory, Liu)

A definition through the expressibility of the structure: the critical size is the minimum number of training examples that suffices to fix the target structured representation of the task uniquely (up to linear transformations) \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. The source of difference: the threshold here is a property of the **task and its representation** (the informational sufficiency of the sample) rather than of the training dynamics.

### B. The circuit-efficiency crossover Dcrit (Varma)

A definition through two competing circuits: Dcrit is the dataset size at which the memorising and the generalising circuits are equally efficient; above it generalisation wins, below it memorisation does \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. The source of difference: the control parameter is the size of the data, but the threshold is set by the **relative efficiency of the subnetworks** (with [weight decay](weight-decay.md) — an L2 penalty on the weight norms — mandatory) rather than by informational sufficiency. Huang et al. join this reading, placing Dcrit in the frame of circuit competition \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\].

### C. The data fraction as a controllable parameter of the experiment

An operational definition: instead of the absolute volume one fixes the **fraction** `TRAIN_FRAC` of all admissible examples given over to training and regulates through it the onset and the delay of grokking \[[3.8](#ref-3-8)\]. The source of difference: this is not a theoretical threshold but a direct experimental lever; the critical size shows up as the value of the fraction below which generalisation does not set in.

The most direct measurement of this lever is in Nanda et al. \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]: on the canonical testbed ($P=113$) grokking exists only at 30–50 % of the data — above that generalisation is immediate, below it there is none even after 40 thousand epochs. The window is set not by the fraction alone, however: at $P=53$ it opens only after the [weight decay](weight-decay.md) is raised to $\lambda=5$, and at $P=401$ there is none at all — at none of six values of $\lambda$ does the model do anything but generalise at once or overfit \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]. This is the only early null result on a large modulus in the literature.

### Contested

What decides grokking — the **size** or the **distribution** of the data. Wang et al. show that the speed of generalisation depends on the ratio of inferred to atomic facts rather than on the absolute size, and propose to speak of a **critical distribution** instead of a critical size \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. He et al. join in, making that ratio (inferred/atomic) the primary determinant of the speed of grokking \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]. The source of difference: the control parameter shifts from the cardinality of the sample to its **composition / the proportion of classes of examples**. The root of this branch is older than its papers: back in 2023 Gromov said in two sentences that the critical fraction depends on the way the examples are selected and that a guided choice would lower it — a foresight without an experiment \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\].

### In support

Joining the view that the threshold is set precisely by the size/fraction of the data: circuit efficiency at the critical size (Huang, \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]); the unobservability of grokking below the critical threshold (Singh, \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]); the dependence on the size as one of "many aspects" through the LU mechanism — the mismatch of the training and the test loss landscapes (Omnigrok, \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]); the critical volume as a condition of fast grokking (Gromov, \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]); the size as a window between the "lazy" and the "rich" regimes (Kumar, \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]); the growth of the delay as the dataset shrinks in the Ising model (Hutchison, \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\]); the reproduction of the grokking curve by sparse sub-sampling (Ersoy, \[[3.7](#ref-3-7)\]); the data fraction `TRAIN_FRAC` as a direct control lever of grokking (Salah, \[[3.8](#ref-3-8)\]); the ratio of inferred to atomic facts instead of the absolute volume, which in a real-world corpus turned out to be an order of magnitude below the threshold (Abramov, \[[3.9](#ref-3-9)\]).
