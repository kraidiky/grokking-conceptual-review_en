## Definition

**The role of gradient noise** is the question of whether grokking needs the stochastic noise of optimisation (the minibatch noise of SGD, injected Gaussian noise, the "temperature" lr/B) — and one of the lines of the corpus where the theories and the founding experiments are pulled against each other. The primary source is Power et al.: they both put forward the noise hypothesis — the effects *"may be due to the noise from SGD driving the optimization to flatter/simpler solutions that generalize better"* \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\] — and themselves set its earliest limit: *"some generalization happens even with full batch optimizers and models without weight or activation noise at high percentages of training data"* \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. The hypothesis and its limitation live in one and the same PDF.

## Elaboration

**Noise as an intervention in Power.** In the sweep over optimisation details ([appendix A.1.2](../papers/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-a1-2)) noise figures as an explicit knob: Adam with Gaussian noise in the update direction and Adam with Gaussian noise in the weights both enter the comparison and improve data efficiency — but lose to weight decay. That is, already in Power noise **helps**, but is not a condition.

**The full-batch chain: the founding observations are noiseless.** An editor's summary of the works' own setups (each row is a direct quotation or a setup fact from the primary source): the six most cited empirical works on grokking train with a full batch or nearly so, that is, there is no minibatch gradient noise in their runs.

| Work | Batch setting |
|---|---|
| Power et al. | batch min(512, half the dataset); the full-batch ablation generalises \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\] |
| Nanda et al. | *"we use full batch gradient descent using the AdamW optimizer"* \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\] |
| Liu et al. 2205.10343 | *"large batch size (with small learning rate) leads to comprehension, implying that smaller batch size seems harmful"* \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\] |
| Varma et al. | *"with full batch training (that is, using the entire training dataset for each update) using the AdamW optimiser"* \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\] |
| Notsawo et al. 2023 | a minibatch the size of the whole training sample ([§2.2](../papers/2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens/original/2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens.md#p2-8)) |
| Prieto et al. | both models are trained full-batch ([§2.2](../papers/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p3-4)) |

More than that: grokking reproduces under fully deterministic optimisation — [Gromov](../papers/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) on vanilla gradient descent with no regularisation at all \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\], [Kumar et al.](../papers/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) on full-batch gradient descent; and even the [slingshot effect](slingshot.md) — the main "instability" line — reproduces under full-batch, deterministic Adam (Thilak et al., appendix A). And in Liu et al. a smaller batch in the toy setting is outright **harmful** \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]. The consequence: any theory in which the delay is set by a noise temperature (lr/B and its relatives) is obliged to account for grokking at zero minibatch noise.

**Noise theories.** Three lines of the corpus put noise at the foundation of the mechanism. [Ersoy & Wiesner](../papers/2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking/2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking.card.md): grokking is an escape from a metastable state, *"a model in a low-accuracy metastable state escapes only when SGD noise drives it across an energy barrier, with escape times following Arrhenius scaling"* \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], with an effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}}\propto\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}/B$ — the setting is honestly noisy (SGD, deliberate trapping), and the question to it is transfer to the noiseless runs of the chain. [Rubin et al.](../papers/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.card.md): networks *"trained with MSE loss to equilibrium using Langevin dynamics"* \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\] — temperature here enters the setting of an equilibrium Bayesian theory itself, not an account of SGD trajectories. [2603.15492](../papers/2603.15492.grokking-as-a-variance-limited-phase-transition-spectral-gating-and-the-epsilon-stability-threshold/2603.15492.grokking-as-a-variance-limited-phase-transition-spectral-gating-and-the-epsilon-stability-threshold.card.md) builds a "variance-limited" theory of the transition around the variance of an adaptive [optimiser](optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)'s updates. A neighbouring dispute is [when regularisation is necessary](regularization-necessity.md), in which noise figures as one of the knobs that can replace regularisation. Standing apart is the numerical noise of finite precision: the line of [slingshots](slingshot.md) and [Softmax Collapse](softmax-collapse.md), where the "noise" is arithmetic rather than stochastic.

## Contested

The role of noise as a necessary condition is bounded by the primary sources themselves: Power's full-batch ablation \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\], Nanda's throughout full-batch setup \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\], the harmfulness of a smaller batch in Liu et al. \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\], the full-batch Varma \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\], Notsawo and Prieto, the deterministic gradient descent of Gromov \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\] and of Kumar. None of these works argues with the noise theories explicitly — the limitation arises out of their setups, and before this card it was not recorded in any wiki artefact.

**\[3.3\]** 2505.11411 — Zhang, Shang, Yang, Zhang, "Is Grokking a Computational Glass Relaxation?". ###### ref-3-4
**\[3.4\]** 2412.18624 — Kozyrev, "How to explain grokking". Nuance: noise is not an incidental circumstance but the whole mechanism — without the Gaussian term there is no Gibbs distribution, no entropic term and no second phase. The temperature $\theta$ is introduced as a scalar constant and is never once related to the learning rate or the batch size; SGD noise is anisotropic and point-dependent, whereas the stationarity of $e^{-\beta f}$ requires isotropy — and there is no qualification about this. The transfer from SGLD to real optimisers is declared the author's belief and is not tested. It stands in direct opposition to Barak et al. (2207.08799), where Langevin random search as an account of the plateau is rejected by a theorem in deterministic gradient flow. [`"We believe the same effect will work for other forms of stochastic gradient descent (such as mini-batch procedure)."`](../papers/2412.18624.how-to-explain-grokking/2412.18624.how-to-explain-grokking.card.md#p3-13).\
Also (the setting itself): [`"Continuous limit of SGLD is described by the stochastic differential equation (SDE) for Brownian motion in a potential"`](../papers/2412.18624.how-to-explain-grokking/2412.18624.how-to-explain-grokking.card.md#p2-3).
###### ref-3-5
**\[3.5\]** 2608.01833 — Chan, Zhang, Shang, Zhang, Yang 2026, "Tunneling the Loss Landscape: Bypassing Memorization with Monte Carlo Parameter Swapping". A comparison of perturbations on a deliberately deterministic basis (full batch, Adam or AdamW, no dropout — so that the only source of randomness is the noise introduced): a baseline Adam with a small probe, AdamW with a probe, Adam with large Gaussian noise $\sigma=10^{-2}$ and Adam with swapping of values within a layer; all three perturbed settings reach generalisation earlier than the baseline, whence the general conclusion that the acceleration is invariably tied to random exploration of parameter space. Nuance: in the same figure, plain large Gaussian noise generalises earlier than the authors' own swap, and this is not noted in the text; baseline (i) does not generalise at all within 10000 epochs, so "earlier than the baseline" conceals the fact that the baseline never generalises; and weight decay is counted among the "perturbations" although it is not random. [`"These results suggest that perturbations, regardless of form, can substantially alter the timing of delayed generalization."`](../papers/2608.01833.tunneling-the-loss-landscape-bypassing-memorization-with-monte-carlo-parameter-swapping/original/2608.01833.tunneling-the-loss-landscape-bypassing-memorization-with-monte-carlo-parameter-swapping.md#p5-5).

### Contested

**\[4.1\]** 2402.15555 — Humayun, Balestriero, Baraniuk 2024, "Deep Networks Always Grok and Here is Why". The observation: raising the batch size from 64 to 512 accelerates adversarial grokking. Nuance: the explanation through a reduction of SGD noise stands in a figure caption and is nowhere tested — the work contains neither an estimate of the gradient variance nor full-batch experiments. [`"Increasing the batch-size expedites grokking. This indicates that reduced SGD noise allows region migration to occur earlier in training."`](../papers/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/original/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.md#fig-17).

**\[4.2\]** 2310.16441 — Levi et al. 2023. Nuance: full-batch GD in all the experiments and the gradient-flow limit in the analysis; the account through random walks among optimal solutions is mentioned in the survey and not used. There is no comparison with SGD, so the conclusion "gradient noise is not needed at all" does not follow from the work. [`"We train with full batch gradient descent, in all instances."`](../papers/2310.16441.grokking-in-linear-estimators-a-solvable-model-that-groks-without-understanding/original/2310.16441.grokking-in-linear-estimators-a-solvable-model-that-groks-without-understanding.md#p14-1).

**\[4.3\]** 2505.15624 — AlQuabeh, Bojković, Nwadike, Inui, "Mechanistic Insights into Grokking from the Embedding Layer". Fits the sparsity of embedding updates into the dispute about gradient noise: the heavy-tailedness of the noise, by which SGD's lag is explained, is related to the randomness of updates for rare tokens, and the argument that noise alone is not enough (SGD loses to Adam under full-batch training as well) is brought in as a counter. Nuance: there are no measurements of noise of their own, and the quantity $p_{i}$ on which the whole analysis rests is nowhere measured — three splitting strategies stand in for it. [`"This observation aligns with our findings regarding the randomness in embedding updates for low-$p$ tokens."`](../papers/2505.15624.mechanistic-insights-into-grokking-from-the-embedding-layer/original/2505.15624.mechanistic-insights-into-grokking-from-the-embedding-layer.md#p14-3).\
Also (token frequencies in the batches): [`"Imbalanced sampling leads to uneven token occurrences in mini-batches, causing some tokens to be absent in multiple updates while others appear frequently."`](../papers/2505.15624.mechanistic-insights-into-grokking-from-the-embedding-layer/original/2505.15624.mechanistic-insights-into-grokking-from-the-embedding-layer.md#fig-8).

**\[4.4\]** 2406.06158 — Kunin et al., "Get rich quick: exact solutions reveal how unbalanced initializations promote rapid feature learning". Nuance: the authors themselves name the omission of SGD as a limitation, and it is decisive — the conservation of $\delta$ is the foundation of the whole theory, and under stochastic descent it stops being exact; meanwhile the experimental part (Gabors, the random hierarchy model, grokking) is trained stochastically, so the gap between the theory and the experiments runs exactly here. [`"Another limitation is our omission of discretization and stochastic effects of SGD, which disrupt the conservation laws central to our study and introduce additional simplicity biases [74, 75, 76, 77]."`](../papers/2406.06158.get-rich-quick-exact-solutions-reveal-how-unbalanced-initializations-promote-rapid-feature-learning/2406.06158.get-rich-quick-exact-solutions-reveal-how-unbalanced-initializations-promote-rapid-feature-learning.card.md#p10-3).

###### ref-2-5
**\[2.5\]** 2207.08799 — Barak et al., "Hidden Progress in Deep Learning: SGD Learns Parities Near the Computational Limit". Also: [`"the network takes much longer to reach slightly-better-than-trivial accuracy than it takes to go from slightly better than trivial to perfect accuracy"`](../papers/2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit/2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit.card.md#p9-4).
