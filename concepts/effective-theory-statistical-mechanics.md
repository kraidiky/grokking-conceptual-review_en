## Definition

**Effective theory / statistical mechanics** is a family of physically motivated approaches that describe [grokking](grokking.md) and kindred phenomena of learning not through particular weight values but through a few macroscopic (coarse-grained, detail-averaged) quantities: an order parameter, a control parameter and phases — on the model of the physics of phase transitions. The notion of an "effective theory" was introduced by Liu et al. (2022): instead of gradient descent on the task's true loss they model the dynamics of learning by a simpler "effective loss" depending only on the representations (embeddings), and show that it predicts the critical training-set size and the trajectories of the representations \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

![The phase diagram of the effective theory: the competition of representation formation and memorisation for the addition group (fig. 6 of Liu et al.)](assets/effective-theory-phase-diagram.png)

## Elaboration

In physics an "effective theory" is a device of model reduction: a complex phenomenon is described by a simple and intuitive picture, with the inessential degrees of freedom discarded. Liu et al. carry the device over to learning: their **effective loss** (a simplified objective depending only on the embeddings, not on the decoder) reproduces the dynamics of the representations and yields a phase diagram — a map of training regimes in the coordinates of hyperparameters — with four phases ("comprehension", grokking, [memorisation](memorization-phase.md) and "confusion") \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The second, statistical-mechanical derivation reads grokking as a genuine [phase transition](phase-transition.md): an **[order parameter](order-parameter.md)** (a macroscopic quantity characterising the state of the system) changes by a jump when the **control parameter** crosses a critical threshold. Rubin et al. formalise this through mean-field theory (an approximation in which each element feels the averaged field of the rest) and Landau theory, assigning grokking to a first-order transition (with a jump of the order parameter) \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; Ersoy et al. describe it as a noise-driven escape from a metastable phase (a long-lived but not globally stable state) under [L2 regularisation](weight-decay.md) \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\]; Hong et al. carry the Landau language of an order parameter over to the training of small language models \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]. Merrill et al. state what an effective theory of such a phenomenon should be (it should uncover the causal mechanisms of the transitions, predict on which tasks they will occur, and separate the statistical from the computational aspect), and recall that grokking-like transitions have long been studied in the statistical-physics community \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. The disputed nuance is how far this is a "real" singular transition rather than a steep but smooth crossover (a gradual passage without non-analyticity) or a pure analogy: Bi et al. turn the phase-transition language into a falsifiable claim by introducing an extensive size variable (the group order p) and an order parameter (the head–tail spectral contrast), applying finite-size scaling from condensed-matter physics; their comparison of susceptibilities comes out decidedly against a smooth crossover \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. Nakaishi et al. extend the effective-model and statistical-physics view beyond grokking, using LLMs as controllable effective models of natural language near criticality \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗.

A limitation for the temperature readings: the six most cited empirical works train with a full batch, without minibatch noise — the chain is assembled in the card on the [role of gradient noise](gradient-noise.md).

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The effective theory of representations (a dynamical reduction)

Liu et al.'s definition: the effective theory of grokking is a reduced dynamics of **representations**, set by an effective loss that depends only on the embeddings and ignores the decoder. The key machinery here is not an order parameter but the replacement of true gradient descent by the gradient flow of a simplified loss; from it are derived the critical training-set size (the least [data fraction](data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md) that uniquely fixes the [structured representation](structured-representation-learning.md)) and the trajectories of the representations \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

### B. The statistical mechanics of phase transitions

A definition through the physics of phase transitions: grokking is a genuine phase transition possessing an **order parameter** and a **control parameter**, rather than merely a sharp stretch of a learning curve. The distinguishing machinery is a mean-field / Landau description and a classification by the order of the transition (first order — with a jump of the order parameter) \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]\[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]\[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\]. The source of the difference from reading A is the level of description: here the system is characterised by equilibrium macroscopic quantities, whereas in Liu et al. the emphasis is on the non-equilibrium dynamics of the representations.

### In support

The works joining the statistical-mechanical reading differ in the machinery they introduce. Merrill et al. set the minimal programme of an effective theory (causal mechanisms, prediction of tasks, separation of the statistical and the computational aspect) and appeal to the tradition of statistical physics \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. Rubin et al. give an equilibrium (Bayesian) mean-field description and assign grokking to a first-order transition, extending the picture to arbitrary deep networks \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. Nakaishi et al. carry the effective-model approach over to LLMs, finding a phase transition in a parameter analogous to temperature and criticality at the phase boundary \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗. Hong et al. apply a Landau order parameter to the training of small language models \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]. Bi et al. translate the analogy into a falsifiable finite-size test (an extensive size variable plus a spectral order parameter, finite-size scaling) \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. Ersoy et al. read grokking as a noise-driven escape from a metastable phase and a first-order transition in L2 \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\].
