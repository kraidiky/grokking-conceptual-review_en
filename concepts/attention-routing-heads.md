## Definition

"Attention routing" is the mechanism by which attention heads (the parallel submodules of a self-attention layer, each with its own attention matrix) redistribute information between the positions of the input sequence depending on the data, through learned query–key interactions (the dot products of query and key vectors, which set the attention weights). In work on [grokking](grokking.md) the notion is examined from two angles: which heads exactly carry the generalising computation (the heuristic core) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\], and whether data-dependent routing is a necessary "degree of freedom" of the architecture \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

![The attention patterns of four heads from the "equals" token to the first operand: a periodic routing from which the learned algorithm can be read (fig. 9 of Nanda et al.)](assets/attention-patterns-heads.png)

## Elaboration

Bhaskar et al. \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\], using structured pruning (a learned zeroing of subsets of weights that leaves a subnetwork approximating the full model's behaviour), isolate inside a single trained language model a family of subnetworks — "subsets of attention heads and MLP layers" — and find that all of them, whether they generalise or not, share a common set of attention heads, which they call the "heuristic core". These heads emerge early in training and compute "shallow", non-generalising features; the model begins to generalise by growing additional heads on top of the core. Routing is thereby read as the question of which circuit (a subnetwork implementing a sub-algorithm) of attention heads is actually responsible for generalisation — a story akin to the passage from the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md) to generalisation in grokking.

The limiting case that attention is reduced to under simplification is called in the corpus **uniform attention** or a **bag of tokens** (CBOW).

The second angle is set by Yıldırım \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]: he calls data-dependent attention routing one of the two independent "degrees of freedom" of a standard transformer (alongside the unconstrained norm of the residual stream) and, to test its role, introduces the Uniform Attention Ablation — replacing the learned query–key weights by a fixed uniform distribution, which reduces attention to a "bag of tokens" (a CBOW aggregator averaging the input tokens with no regard to their relative position). Leaning on the theoretical result that [modular addition](modular-arithmetic.md) is realisable by uniform aggregation, he shows that data-dependent routing is "not strictly necessary" for the commutative task, and that removing it can bypass grokking altogether (a model with normalisation generalises straight away). This reads grokking as a [phase transition](phase-transition.md) that architecture can bypass, but the effect turns out to be task-specific: on the [non-commutative](group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md) composition of permutations in S5 removing the routing gives no acceleration. A related story is the difference between the [Clock and Pizza](clock-vs-pizza.md) algorithms, where it is exactly the type of attention (uniform against learned) that distinguishes two solutions of the same task.

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. Attention heads as computational circuits (the heuristic core)

Here routing is the distribution of the computation across attention heads: what separates the readings is not the "strength" of attention but the composition of the subnetwork and the order in which heads appear. A common "heuristic core" of heads emerges early and computes non-generalising heuristics, while generalisation is added later by separate heads on top of the core; the key source of difference between a generalising and a non-generalising model is which heads are connected, not any change in the core heads themselves \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

### B. Routing as an architectural degree of freedom

Here routing is a separate, controllable and removable degree of freedom: the adaptivity of the query–key weights. The control parameter is the Uniform Attention Ablation itself, which forcibly sets uniform weights (for instance `[1/3, 1/3, 1/3]` over three tokens); comparing learned and uniform routing all else being equal is precisely the test of its contribution to the generalisation delay \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

### Contested

Yıldırım \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\] contests the necessity of data-dependent routing: for commutative modular addition a uniform "bag of tokens" is enough, so learned query–key routing is not a load-bearing degree of freedom, and ablating it does not break generalisation but accelerates it. The testable demarcation from reading A: if routing were necessary for generalisation, a uniform ablation would collapse the accuracy — but the model with LayerNorm generalises under uniform attention, whereas on the non-commutative S5 there is no acceleration, and that is what separates the commutative tasks from the rest.

### In support

Li et al. \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] support the claim that the computation really is localised in attention: their analytic characterisation of the features of one-hidden-layer networks (each neuron tuned to its own Fourier spectrum) carries over to the transformer as well — "similar computational mechanisms" are observed in the attention matrices of a one-layer transformer. The distinguishing source: not an ablation but a direct analysis of the attention matrices as the carrier of the same [Fourier circuit](fourier-features-circuits.md) (the subnetwork implementing modular addition through frequencies) as the MLP neurons.
