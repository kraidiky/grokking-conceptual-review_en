## Definition

**Mechanistic interpretability** (MI) is an approach to analysing neural networks consisting in the *reverse engineering* of a network's learned behaviour: decomposing it into individual internal components and recovering, from the weights and activations, the human-readable algorithm the network actually executes \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. In the context of [grokking](grokking.md) the method became foundational after Nanda et al. (2023) fully reconstructed the algorithm of [modular addition](modular-arithmetic.md) in a small transformer, showing that the "sudden" jump in generalisation is in fact assembled from a continuous internal process \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

![The reconstructed algorithm of a one-layer transformer for modular addition: projecting the numbers onto a circle and adding rotations (fig. 1 of Nanda et al.)](assets/mechint-reversed-algorithm.png)

## Elaboration

The basic unit of analysis in MI is a **circuit**: a subnetwork implementing a specific sub-algorithm, isolated by weights, activations and ablations (switching components off) \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. As applied to [grokking](grokking.md), Nanda et al. reconstructed the circuit that, through a discrete Fourier transform and trigonometric identities, reduces addition to a rotation on a circle, and on that basis introduced continuous *progress measures* (metrics read off the internal structure that change monotonically where the external accuracy stands on a plateau), splitting training into the phases "memorisation — circuit formation — cleanup" \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. MI thereby supplied a mechanistic argument against reading grokking as a discontinuity: behind the jump lies the gradual amplification of a structured circuit (see [phase transition](phase-transition.md)) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The second major channel is mechanistic *explanation* rather than description alone: Varma et al. formalised grokking as a competition of two families of circuits — a memorising one (see the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md)) and a generalising one — where weight decay prefers the more "efficient" (lower-norm) generalising circuit \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. MI's toolkit has since widened from a factor-by-factor analysis of [Fourier circuits](fourier-features-circuits.md) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] to decoding intermediate activations with a *logit lens* (projecting the hidden states into logit space with the unembedding matrix) \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\] and a feature-level view through *monosemantic neurons* (neurons in one-to-one correspondence with a human-interpretable feature). The method itself is meanwhile contested: it relies on manual labour to isolate circuits, scales poorly and is subjective \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\], is largely post hoc (it analyses weights already frozen after grokking) \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\] and, as critics note, remains descriptive rather than predictive, its circuit analyses being tied to a particular task and generalising poorly between architectures \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. Reverse engineering into a human-readable algorithm (the circuit level)

A definition through the result: MI is the recovery of the exact algorithm a network executes, down to a specific circuit (for modular addition, the "Clock" circuit on a discrete Fourier transform) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The distinguishing machinery is verifiability: the circuit is confirmed by ablations in Fourier space and carries over to more complex modular arithmetic (subtraction, multiplication, polynomials) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. The key nuance is that the circuit need not be unique: for one and the same task networks learn different algorithms ([the clock against the pizza](clock-vs-pizza.md)), so a "human-readable algorithm" is a family rather than a single canon.

### B. Mechanistic explanation through competition of circuits

A definition through a causal mechanism rather than a map alone: MI answers not "what is inside" but "why grokking happens". The distinguishing parameter is [circuit efficiency](circuit-efficiency.md) (the norm cost per unit of logit): with several circuits at perfect training accuracy, [weight decay](weight-decay.md) strengthens the more efficient generalising circuit and weakens the memorising one, which is what triggers the late rise of test accuracy \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. Here MI meets optimisation theory: the observed structure is explained as a consequence of the [implicit bias](margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md) of gradient descent.

### C. Feature-level interpretability: monosemantic neurons and the logit lens

A definition through a unit of analysis other than the circuit: not an algorithmic subnetwork but an individual *feature*. Monosemantic neurons give a one-to-one correspondence with a human-interpretable feature (as against polysemantic ones, which respond to many features); the logit lens, instead of analysing weights, decodes the intermediate activations of all layers into prediction space, tracking the "maturation" of generalising circuits over the course of grokking \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]. The difference from A and B is the point of entry (a feature or an activation rather than an isolated subnetwork) and the reliance on a decoding instrument rather than on manual reconstruction of an algorithm.

### Contested

The criticism is addressed not to the existence of circuits but to the epistemic standing of the method. Three independent nuances: (1) MI requires manual labour to isolate circuits, scales poorly to large models and is subject to subjective bias \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]; (2) it is largely post hoc — the mechanisms are inferred from weights already frozen after grokking rather than tracked causally over training \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]; (3) circuit analyses are descriptive rather than predictive and are tied to a particular task, so they transfer poorly between architectures \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\]; and separately it is noted that no link between MI and the layer-wise dynamics of feature ranks has yet been established \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\].

### In support

Works that accept MI as a working method and attach nuances of their own: a transfer of the reverse engineering of Fourier circuits to complex modular arithmetic \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; a mechanistic analysis of the internals revealing different generalising circuits for composition and comparison \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; an analytically derivable mechanistic account of the moment of grokking itself \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]; the decoding of the maturation of generalising circuits with a logit lens \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]; and a reading of the structure of the internal representations as consistent with the MI paradigm \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗.
