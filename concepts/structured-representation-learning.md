## Definition

**Structured representation learning** is an explanation of [grokking](grokking.md) according to which delayed generalisation arises not from simple [memorisation](memorization-phase.md) of the training examples but from the gradual formation of *structured internal representations* — the vectors of the input embeddings (learnable numerical codes of the symbols) and the feature maps of the hidden layers, which acquire a geometry matching the structure of the task (for example, a circular arrangement of the embeddings for [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md)). Generalisation sets in when the network learns such a representation, not at the moment it fits the training sample \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The phenomenon and its representational reading were introduced by Liu et al. (2022) on algorithmic datasets \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\] and generalised to [real-world data](vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md) in Omnigrok \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

## Elaboration

In the original "[effective theory](effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)" of Liu et al., generalisation *originates from* structured representations, and representation learning happens only in the "[Goldilocks zone](goldilocks-zone.md)" — a narrow range between memorisation and "confusion", where resource constraints force the network to look for an efficient solution \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Omnigrok links this to the mismatch of the train/test loss landscapes in the weight norm (the LU mechanism) and attributes the "dramatic" nature of grokking precisely to representation learning \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. Mechanistically, "structuredness" means a task-aligned geometry: circular embeddings and trigonometric circuits for modular addition (the algorithmic choice between the variants is analysed in the card [clock vs pizza](clock-vs-pizza.md)) \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\]\[[1.8](#ref-1-8)\], low-dimensional manifolds and "[neural collapse](neural-collapse.md)" (the contraction of the within-class variance of the representations to a simple symmetric configuration) \[[1.7](#ref-1-7)\], low-rank symmetry-aligned representations \[[1.9](#ref-1-9)\]. A related reading describes grokking as [feature learning](feature-emergence-feature-learning.md) beyond the "lazy"/[NTK](neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md) regime (the Neural Tangent Kernel approximation, in which the network is equivalent to a fixed kernel and the features do not evolve), that is, as a first-order [phase transition](phase-transition.md) in feature space \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. At the scale of large language models the same principle shows up as the evolution of expert "paths" from instance-specific to structured and transferable \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\] and as the induction of structured compressed representations of facts and rules, necessary for implicit reasoning \[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\].

Some works *contest* particular nuances of this explanation. It has been shown that grokking is possible with no observable structure in PCA projections of the embeddings at all \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\], that is, the visible circular structure is not necessary. Others link the acceleration of generalisation neither to a reduction of the weight norm nor to a "compression" of the representation, but to the discovery of a good subnetwork (in the spirit of the [lottery ticket](sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md) hypothesis) \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]; yet another line argues that the "generalising circuit" is not a sudden acquisition of new reasoning but only the integration of already memorised facts into an established inference path \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\]. On the other hand, a great many works *join* the representational account: some use the rank of the features as an indicator of generalisation and the phenomenon of [double descent](double-descent.md) in the ranks \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], others build practical acceleration methods on it through the transfer of informative embeddings \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\], still others empirically extract an abstract algebraic structure from the representations \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗ or show that grokked models form more modular representations \[[3.7](#ref-3-7)\]. Separately worth noting is the tie to transferability: distillation from a grokked model forms useful representations in the "Goldilocks zone" and at the same time mitigates [catastrophic forgetting](catastrophic-forgetting.md) \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. The adjacent "compression" explanation (grokking as a growth of the simplicity of the solution, measured by the [linear mapping number](nlm.md)) and the reading through the [slingshot](slingshot.md) remain separate mechanisms that merely touch on the representational one (see the section "Passing mentions").

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The effective theory of representations and the "Goldilocks zone"

The base formulation of Liu et al.: generalisation *originates from* structured representations, whose dynamics and dependence on the size of the training set are predictable by the effective theory; representation learning happens only in the "Goldilocks zone" between memorisation and confusion, while on transformers the grokking phase is shifted towards memorisation, which is what produces the delay \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The key distinguishing feature here is the tie between the appearance of a representation and the *[critical dataset size](data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)*: generalisation is possible only with enough examples to determine the structure uniquely. Omnigrok keeps the same definition but derives it from the geometry of the [loss landscape](loss-landscape-basins.md) in the weight norm (the LU mechanism) and lifts the tie to algorithmic tasks \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

### B. Feature learning as a first-order phase transition (the adaptive kernel)

A definition through the theory of features: grokking is a departure from the "lazy"/GP regime, in which the network *generates* useful internal representations of the teacher, sharply different from the pre-training ones \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. The distinguishing machinery is the adaptive kernel: the covariances of the neurons' pre-nonlinearities are read as an order parameter of feature learning, and grokking is mapped onto the mixed phase *after a first-order phase transition*, that is, onto an abrupt rather than a smooth rearrangement of the representations.

### C. The geometry of representations: neural collapse and low-dimensional structures

A definition through geometry: generalisation sets in when the representations contract into a low-dimensional, structured configuration. In the version through neural collapse the controlling quantity is the contraction of the *population within-class variance* of the representations; it is precisely the gap in time scales between fitting the training set and the progress of the collapse that explains the delay \[[1.7](#ref-1-7)\]. In the symmetry version the source of difference is the *intrinsic symmetry of the task*: a three-stage dynamics (memorisation → acquisition of the symmetry → geometric organisation) sets the concrete form of the low-rank task-aligned geometry, which norm explanations alone cannot predict \[[1.9](#ref-1-9)\].

### D. Structured algorithmic representations (circular embeddings)

A definition through a concrete algorithmic artefact: for modular arithmetic "structure" is the circular arrangement of the embeddings, on which the network implements a symmetric (trigonometric) algorithm that generalises to unseen pairs \[[1.8](#ref-1-8)\]. The distinguishing thesis of the universal version is that disparate neural solutions reduce to a single abstract algorithm (the approximate Chinese Remainder Theorem), where the neurons fire only on "approximate [cosets](group-representations-cosets.md)" and the number of features needed grows as O(log n) \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\].

### E. Structured representations and transferability at scale (LLMs)

A definition that lifts the tie to small synthetic tasks: in the pre-training of large language models, structuredness is measured by *transferability between examples*. The distinguishing machinery is the "paths" of experts in an MoE (the choice of experts across layers): they evolve from random, instance-specific ones to structured ones transferable between samples even when the loss has already converged, and that is the passage from memorisation to generalisation \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]. In the implicit-reasoning version, the structure is compressed representations of facts and rules, whose absence blocks systematic generalisation over knowledge \[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\].

### Contested

The circular/observable structure of the embeddings is not necessary: similar generalisation behaviour is attained in an ordinary MLP without embeddings, and grokking is observed without any structure appearing in PCA space \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]. The driver is not the norm or compression but a structural subnetwork: the mitigation of the delay is linked not to a reduction of the weight norm or a growth of sparsity but to the discovery of good subnetworks \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. The substantive status of the new representation is contested by a functional analysis: the "generalising circuit" gives no new paradigmatic reasoning but only integrates already memorised atomic facts into an established inference path \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\].

### In support

The rank of the features as an indicator: grokking is accompanied by a collapse of the feature ranks, and the internal rank is a more reliable predictor of generalisation than the weight norm \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. The data determine the representation: with enough data to fix the structured representation uniquely, perfect generalisation is attained \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. The mechanism through representations: grokking starts when the model learns the hidden structure of the data, and an informative embedding removes the delay \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\], which makes it possible to build a "structured representation of the mechanism" of knowledge processing \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. Distillation forms useful representations in the "Goldilocks zone" and mitigates forgetting \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. The gradual assembly of the structure: the internal circuits smoothly form compositional structures before the jump in accuracy \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\]; grokked models form more modular representations \[[3.7](#ref-3-7)\]; an abstract algebraic structure is extracted from the representations \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗; the geometry of the representations is read through a low-dimensional "execution manifold" \[[3.9](#ref-3-9)\]; RFM kernels learn block-circulant feature transformations as a manifestation of gradual feature learning \[[3.10](#ref-3-10)\].
