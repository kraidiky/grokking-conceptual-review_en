## Definition

**The lazy→rich transition** is a reading of [grokking](grokking.md) in which delayed generalisation arises because the network first trains in the "lazy" regime (lazy / kernel / [NTK](neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md) regime — the weights barely move from their initialisation and the network behaves as a linear model around the starting point, effectively solving a kernel regression with its initial neural tangent kernel), and then passes into the "rich" regime ([feature learning](feature-emergence-feature-learning.md) proper, where the internal representations rearrange to fit the task) \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The mechanism, and the very phrase "grokking as the transition from lazy to rich", were introduced by Kumar et al. (2024) on a polynomial regression task with a two-layer network, where grokking reproduces even without regularisation \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

![A scheme of the parameter dynamics under grokking: a fast lazy arrival at the interpolating manifold, then a slow drift towards the generalising solution (fig. 1 of Kumar et al.)](assets/lazy-to-rich-dynamics.png)

## Elaboration

In the lazy regime the network first fits the training sample with a kernel regression solution using its initial features — this corresponds to the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md) (the training error is already low, the test set does not yet generalise); generalisation comes only with late feature learning, when the network leaves the kernel regime \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The key control parameters in Kumar et al. are the [rate](learning-rate.md) of feature learning (which can be tuned exactly by a multiplier scaling the network's output) and the alignment of the initial features (the leading eigenvectors of the NTK) with the target function; the stronger the misalignment at a sufficient but not excessive sample size, the later the transition occurs \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. This reading is closely tied to the description of grokking as a [phase transition](phase-transition.md): the sharp jump of the test curve answers to the moment of leaving the lazy regime. A number of works join the account and sharpen it: Mohamadi et al. prove for [modular addition](modular-arithmetic.md) that generalisation is hopelessly impossible in the kernel regime and that the network leaves it only after overfitting \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; Tian decomposes the "rich" phase into two stages (independent and interactive feature learning) \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; Jiang et al. show that preconditioned gradient descent (Gauss–Newton style methods that smooth out [spectral bias](frequency-principle-spectral-bias.md), the tendency of a network to learn low frequencies first) hastens entry into the rich regime \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]; Xu et al. give a rigorous proof of grokking through lazy→rich in ridge regression \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]. At the same time the reading is contested: Prieto et al. point out that for cross-entropy loss and deeper networks the lazy theories do not predict the observed behaviour, and that the root of the delay is the numerical [softmax collapse](softmax-collapse.md) and the [naive loss minimisation](nlm.md) (NLM) associated with it, rather than the change of regime as such \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. Kernel regime → rich regime as a provable mechanism (Mohamadi)

A formulation through the provable departure from the kernel regime: as long as the "kernel regime" holds approximately, no permutation-equivariant model generalises on modular addition without a constant fraction of all the data; generalisation is possible only after leaving the kernel regime, and grokking is a consequence of the passage from kernel-like behaviour to the limiting behaviour of gradient descent \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. The difference from Kumar et al.'s original formulation: here the dichotomy "early kernel regime / late feature learning" acquires theorems (a sample lower bound in the kernel regime and the existence of generalising networks with bounded ℓ∞ norm outside it) rather than only empirics.

### B. Tian's three-stage decomposition (Li2)

A definition through three stages of the dynamics (the Li2 framework): (I) lazy learning — the top layer overfits on random hidden representations and the network "memorises", as it were; (II) independent feature learning, when the backpropagated gradient rearranges each hidden node separately; (III) interactive feature learning \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. The key difference from the binary lazy↔rich picture: the "rich" phase is split in two, and the initial memorisation is read as a necessary stage (lazy learning as ridge regression on random features) that must precede feature learning.

### Contested

Prieto et al. show that the known lazy theories are tied to the inductive bias of the MSE loss and account for grokking without regularisation only for shallow networks; for cross-entropy and deep models the lazy→rich transition does not predict what is observed, and the decisive factor turns out to be a numerical effect (softmax collapse from floating-point errors) \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. Hence their question to the frame itself: why do grokking tasks induce lazy training at all, and why is [weight decay](weight-decay.md) often required to enter the feature-learning regime.

### In support

Jiang et al. join the "rich learning regime grokking hypothesis": the source of the delay is spectral bias and a protracted stay in the lazy (NTK) regime, and the control lever is a gradient preconditioner that shortens that stay and hastens entry into the rich regime \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. Xu et al. attribute grokking to the same lazy→rich passage and carry it through to a rigorous end-to-end proof in overparameterised linear (ridge) regression, amplifying or removing the effect at will by tuning hyperparameters \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\].
