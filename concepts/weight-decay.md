## Definition

**Weight decay** (L2 regularisation) is a regularisation device that adds to the loss a penalty proportional to the square of the L2 norm of the weights (equivalent to a component-wise "decay" of the weights — multiplying them by a coefficient slightly less than one at every step of the [optimizer](optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)). In the context of [grokking](grokking.md) — the delayed generalisation in which a network first memorises the training sample almost perfectly and generalises orders of magnitude later — weight decay has historically been the key control factor: it was the very device singled out as the one that most effectively accelerates the passage to generalisation on small algorithmic tasks \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

The direction and the law of weight decay's influence on the grokking time is the subject of a separate dispute in the corpus, with formally contradictory evidence (the law $t\propto\gamma^{-1}$ of Omnigrok 2022 against the numbers of Nanda's appendix D.1 and Notsawo's map); it is set out in the card [the direction of weight decay's influence](weight-decay-direction.md).

## Elaboration

The empirical observation of Power et al. (weight decay noticeably improves generalisation on the tasks studied, \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]) received two influential mechanistic explanations. Omnigrok links the effect to the geometry of the [loss landscape](loss-landscape-basins.md): if the (smoothed) training and test losses are plotted as functions of the weight norm, they resemble the letters "L" and "U" respectively, so generalisation lives in the region of small norm, and weight decay is precisely the force that pulls the weights out of the overfitting region (large norm) into the generalisation region \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. The second explanation is "[circuit efficiency](circuit-efficiency.md)" (circuits here are subnetworks implementing a computation): given several solutions with a perfect training accuracy, weight decay prefers the circuit that needs a smaller parameter norm per unit of logit, that is, the generalising circuit displaces the memorising one (see the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md)) \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. Hence the typical reading of grokking as an abrupt [phase transition](phase-transition.md) whose pace is set precisely by the regularisation: a quantitative "[delay law](norm-separation-delay-law.md)" predicts an inverse dependence of the length of the grokking on the weight decay \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], the post-memorisation dynamics is formalised as a minimisation of the weight norm on the [zero-loss manifold](weight-norm-minimization.md) (the set of parameters with a zero training error) driven by weight decay \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], and the jump itself is described as a first-order phase transition in the strength of the L2 \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. At the same time a body of work has accumulated contesting the necessity of weight decay: grokking is reproduced with no regularisation at all \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]\[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\], including by increasing the depth of the network \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\], and the L2 norm itself turns out to be an unreliable indicator of generalisation \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\]; moreover, in an architecture with a bounded geometry regularisation is not needed \[[2.5](#ref-2-5)\], and under the wrong optimizer weight decay is present but gives no grokking \[[2.6](#ref-2-6)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. A penalty on the L2 norm of the weights (an explicit regulariser)

The base reading: weight decay is an addition to the loss that penalises the square of the L2 norm of the parameters and therefore continuously reduces that norm. In grokking this reading becomes substantive through the geometry of Omnigrok: since the test loss as a function of the weight norm has the shape of a "U" with its minimum at a small norm, weight decay physically moves the model along the norm axis from the overfitting region into the generalisation region, which is what explains the dependence of grokking on the size of the data and on the strength of the weight decay \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. The key parameter here is the weight norm, and weight decay is the controlling force pushing it down.

### B. A selector of the efficient circuit

A definition through the competition of solutions: if several circuits attain a strong training accuracy, weight decay selects the one that is "more efficient" — the one that needs a smaller parameter norm to produce a given logit \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. The difference from reading A is that the source of difference is not the global norm as such but the relative parametric cost of the generalising and the memorising circuits: weight decay here does not merely "shrink" the weights but tips the scales in favour of the generalising subnetwork, and grokking is the moment of that switch.

### C. A parameter of the pace of the phase transition

A definition through the control of the dynamics: weight decay is a control parameter that sets not the fact but the length of the grokking. The "norm-separation law" establishes that the delay between memorisation and generalisation is inversely proportional to the strength of the weight decay (empirically with a very close fit) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. In this frame weight decay is defined by its role in the kinetics of the transition rather than in the selection of a solution: it sets the rate at which the norm contracts between competing interpolating representations.

### Contested

A number of works contest the status of weight decay as a necessary condition or a genuine cause of grokking. A direct refutation: generalisation does not depend on smaller weight norms or on weight decay, and weight decay mostly just mitigates rounding errors \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\]; grokking arises both without weight decay \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\] and without regularisation in a way the old norm theories do not explain (the passage from the "lazy" to the "rich" training regime) \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. It has been shown that over-parameterisation through depth makes it possible to grok and to [ungrok](ungrokking.md) with no explicit regularisation at all \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\], and that a bounded spherical geometry of the network stabilises the solution without relying on weight decay to control the scale \[[2.5](#ref-2-5)\]. Finally, weight decay is not sufficient by itself: under isotropic noise (SGLD) or ordinary SGD there is no grokking even when weight decay is present \[[2.6](#ref-2-6)\].

### In support

Another line of work, by contrast, reinforces the norm-centric reading. The post-grokking dynamics reduces formally to a minimisation of the weight norm on the zero-loss manifold, and precisely as "representation learning driven by weight decay" \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. A quantitative theory turns the qualitative role of weight decay into a testable "delay law" with an inverse scaling in the weight decay \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. And a statistical-physics reading describes grokking as a hysteresis in first-order phase transitions in the strength of the L2 \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\].
