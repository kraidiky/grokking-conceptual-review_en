## Definition

"Vision and real-world data" is a collective notion for the cases in which [grokking](grokking.md) (the phenomenon of delayed generalisation) is observed not on synthetic algorithmic tasks ([modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md) and the like) but on "natural" datasets — above all in image classification (MNIST, CIFAR, ImageNet), and also on text and molecules. The first demonstration of grokking beyond algorithmic data was given by Liu et al. already in their work on the effective theory of representation learning — on MNIST, with a reduced sample and an increased initialisation scale: *"we now demonstrate, for the first time, that grokking (significantly delayed generalization) is a more general phenomenon in machine learning"* \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. The extension was made broad and systematic by the same authors' next work, Omnigrok: analysing the [loss landscapes](loss-landscape-basins.md), they showed that at a suitable scale of the initial weight norm the network groks, and that one can *"induce grokking on tasks involving images, language and molecules"* \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Grokking thereby stops being an artefact of a narrow class of synthetic tasks and is read as a phenomenon reproducible on real, including visual, data.

## Elaboration

Grokking was initially observed exclusively on algorithmic datasets (modular arithmetic), so Omnigrok's key question was whether it arises on data of another nature. The answer is yes, but with a qualification — the demonstration required a special "induction": scaling the initial weight norm (large-init-norm), since without it the effect on images is weaker and often does not appear \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Mechanistically Omnigrok explains the delay by an "LU mechanism" — a mismatch of the training and test loss landscapes as functions of the weight norm (the curves resemble an "L" and a "U"); grokking on real data is meanwhile less dramatic than on algorithmic data. Even earlier, Thilak et al., in their study of the [slingshot](slingshot.md) mechanism, trained a fully connected ReLU network on a small subset of CIFAR-10 and observed grokking-like dynamics on visual data \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. A whole series of works then confirmed grokking on real and visual data, each with its own mechanism: the passage from the "lazy" to the "rich" training regime (that is, from a regime in which the features barely change to one of active [feature learning](feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)) on MNIST \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; frequency dynamics (the network learns the less salient frequency components first) on synthetic and real data \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; a demonstration on the ResNet-18 architecture \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]; a tie to [neural collapse](neural-collapse.md) (the contraction of the within-class variance of the representations) on MNIST \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]; scaling up to CIFAR/SVHN/ImageNet \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\] and even a transfer to the classification of 2D Ising-model configurations treated as images \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. At the same time some works contest the "naturalness" and the robustness of visual grokking: on Han et al.'s account, on real visual settings (ResNet on CIFAR-10, ViT on ImageNet) grokking typically does not arise by itself and has to be induced artificially by regularising the solution away from the "flatness" of the landscape \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]; and Prakash & Martin show that after successful generalisation on a subset of MNIST comes [anti-grokking](anti-grokking.md) — a late collapse of generalisation back to chance level \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\], which links visual grokking with [catastrophic forgetting](catastrophic-forgetting.md).

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. Delayed generalisation beyond algorithmic tasks (Omnigrok, the LU mechanism)

Liu et al.'s basic reading: grokking is not a property of algorithmic data but a general effect of delayed generalisation, determined by the geometry of the loss landscapes. The distinguishing machinery here is the mismatch of the training and test landscapes as functions of the weight norm: to obtain grokking on images the authors deliberately control the initial weight norm, and by the same device in reverse they remove it on algorithmic data \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. That is, the source of difference between "visual" and "algorithmic" grokking is the sharpness of the signal, explained by representation learning, rather than the possibility of the phenomenon.

### B. A mechanism-independent phenomenology on real data

An alternative frame: "visual grokking" is not one theory but a reproducible empirical phenomenon that different mechanisms converge on. Its distinguishing mark is the same temporal signature (the training accuracy saturates early, the test accuracy rises much later), but now on MNIST/CIFAR/ImageNet rather than on synthetic data. The key source of difference between the works is which control factor is declared the cause: the [rate](learning-rate.md) of feature learning (lazy → rich) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], frequency alignment \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], the geometry of the representations and neural collapse \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\], or the structural [sparsity of a subnetwork](sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md) \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. This multiplicity of explanations is itself an argument that we are dealing with a general phenomenon of learning.

### Contested

The nuance "visual grokking arises spontaneously and yields robust generalisation" is contested from two sides. Han et al.: on real visual settings (ResNet/CIFAR-10, ViT/ImageNet) grokking "does not typically arise" and appears only as a side effect of deliberate regularisation against the flatness of the landscape — that is, it is induced rather than natural \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. Prakash & Martin: even where visual grokking has been achieved (a subset of MNIST), prolonged training brings a late collapse of generalisation ([anti-grokking](anti-grokking.md)) — the robustness of the solution is called into question \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\].

### In support

The nuance "grokking is a general phenomenon reproducible on real and visual data" is supported by a series of works, each presenting its own mechanism and its own visual setting: MNIST through the [lazy](lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md) → rich passage \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; MNIST and other real data through frequency dynamics \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; ResNet-18 through the relation between embedding uniformity and the data distribution \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]; MNIST through neural collapse and the information bottleneck \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]; the classification of 2D Ising-model images through the thinning of subnetworks \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]; and scaling up to ImageNet in an unlearning task \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\].

A three-class fashion MNIST coarsened to $6\times 6$ (dress/sneaker/bag, 10% of the sample, a 36-qubit MPS classifier) with a deliberately confounding class; the negative control is PRISMA hyperspectral imagery (43 features after Boruta selection: vineyard/olive/arable), where the same model overfits. Nuance: the bag class is systematically anti-predicted on the test set — an accuracy below 10% against a chance level of 33% — and the comparison of tasks varies the subject, the noise, the number of features and the number of examples all at once, so the conclusion that "the structure of the data governs the interpretable dynamics" is not separable from the other differences. [`"Bag class performs poorly as a confounding case, with training accuracy around $25\%$, with oscillations, and test performance reaching a value even smaller than $10\%$."`](../papers/2507.23346.transfer-entropy-and-o-information-to-detect-grokking-in-tensor-network-multi-class-classification-problems/2507.23346.transfer-entropy-and-o-information-to-detect-grokking-in-tensor-network-multi-class-classification-problems.card.md#p7-10).

### Inheriting

###### ref-3-9
**\[3.9\]** 2408.11804 — Yunis et al., "Approaching Deep Learning through the Spectral Dynamics of Weights". The corpus's widest set of carriers for a single spectral measure: CIFAR-10 (VGG-16), MNIST generation (a diffusion UNet), LibriSpeech (a bidirectional LSTM), Wikitext-103 (a twelve-layer transformer), with the hyperparameters deliberately taken from the literature. An essential caveat: none of the four tasks exhibits grokking, and the fall of the effective rank there is a background tendency of training. [`"This ensures that any correlations observed are likely a reflection of common practices, not introduced bias on our part."`](../papers/2408.11804.approaching-deep-learning-through-the-spectral-dynamics-of-weights/original/2408.11804.approaching-deep-learning-through-the-spectral-dynamics-of-weights.md#p6-6).\
