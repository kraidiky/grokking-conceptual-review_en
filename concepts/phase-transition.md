## Definition

**A phase transition** in the context of [grokking](grokking.md) is the reading of delayed generalisation as an abrupt, jump-like change of regime: the test accuracy holds for a long time on a low plateau and then suddenly rises, which in shape resembles a phase transition in physics \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]\[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\].

![A schematic phase diagram of the phases of training: grokking as a transition on the pattern of first-order phase transitions (fig. 1 of Rubin et al.)](assets/phase-transition-diagram.png)

## Elaboration

The analogy is worked out at different levels — from a purely observable shape of the curve to literal statistical physics with an [order parameter](order-parameter.md) (a quantity that changes by a jump at the transition point). At the empirical level this is simply an abrupt jump of generalisation after overfitting \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]\[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\]. In the statistical-physics reading grokking is formalised as a genuine phase transition — of first order (with a jump and metastability) \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]\[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] or as a critical point with diverging quantities \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]. Separate lines link the transition to a change of training regimes (kernel→rich: from the "lazy" one with almost unchanging features to the "rich" one with [feature learning](feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)) \[[1.8](#ref-1-8)\], to a cyclic instability of the [optimizer](optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md) (the [slingshot](slingshot.md)) \[[1.9](#ref-1-9)\] and to a structural rearrangement of the network itself \[[1.10](#ref-1-10)\]\[[1.11](#ref-1-11)\]. The counter-position contests the discontinuity: behind the "jump" there may stand a gradual process \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\], the "phase-transition" rhetoric is required to be made falsifiable \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\], and the transition itself is read as a removable geometric mismatch rather than an inevitable artefact of optimisation \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\]. The phase transition is usually associated with the displacement of the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md) by a generalising solution.

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The phenomenological analogy: a jump on the curve

The weakest sense: "phase transition" means only the observable **shape of the curve** — an abrupt jump of the test accuracy after a long plateau, with no claims about a control parameter or an order parameter. The key difference from the stronger readings: no physical machinery is postulated, and only the step-like character of the passage from overfitting to generalisation is recorded \[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\].

### B. A formal statistical-physics transition: first order against a critical point

Here the analogy is carried through to literal statistical physics, and the key watershed is the **type of transition**:
- **First order with metastability** \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]: an explicit order parameter Φ is introduced (the overlap of a neuron's weights with the "teacher" direction) along with a control parameter u (an effective interaction set by the sample size, the [noise](gradient-noise.md) and the width of the network); at u_c a second minimum of the action, aligned with the teacher, appears and coexists with the previous one over a finite interval — a metastable "droplet" phase and a jump of Φ, as in a liquid–vapour transition. The source of difference: a discontinuity and a coexistence of phases (hysteresis).
- **A critical point / critical slowing down** \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]: the control parameter is λ = d/N with a critical λ_c = 1/2 (the edge of linear separability); there is no order parameter in Landau's sense, but the weight norm and the "grokking time" **diverge** as λ→1/2. The source of difference: not a jump but a divergence; the delay itself is the critical slowing down, and the mechanism requires no regularisation.

The same language of criticality (order–disorder, temperature) is applied to text generated by LLMs as well \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗ — but that work is about the criticality of language rather than about grokking directly.

### C. A transition defined by an internal order parameter

The transition is tied not to the accuracy curve but to an **internal scalar** which crosses a threshold or collapses: synergistic information as a [progress measure](progress-measures.md) identifies grokking as an [emergent](emergence.md) transition \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]; the collapse of the spectral entropy of the covariance of the representations gives the transition an "event" at the crossing of a threshold \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\]; and the appearance of a **sparse subnetwork** out of a dense one is itself a phase transition \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The source of difference: the transition is localised in a measurable internal quantity rather than in the observable accuracy.

### D. A transition between training regimes (kernel/lazy → rich)

A "phase" is defined by the **dynamics of feature learning**: the [NTK](neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)/lazy regime (the features are frozen and only memorisation goes on) against the rich regime (the features evolve and generalisation sets in); the jump in accuracy is a consequence of the "unfreezing" of the feature map rather than its root cause \[[1.8](#ref-1-8)\]. An essential caveat: that same work partly contests lazy initialization as the sole cause, moving the lever onto the informativeness of the embedding.

### E. A cyclic transition governed by the optimizer (the slingshot)

Not a one-way transition but **repeating cycles** of "stability↔instability" in the terminal phase of training, caused by the instability of the adaptive optimizer (Adam/AdamW): the weight norm of the last layer oscillates, the adaptation of the features happens only at the moment each cycle breaks, and grokking "rides" on those cycles \[[1.9](#ref-1-9)\]. The source of difference: the multiplicity and periodicity of the transition, plus an optimizer-side driver, rather than a single change of solution.

### F. A thermodynamic / structural transition of the network itself

The transition is referred to the **structure of the network as a physical system**: under [weight decay](weight-decay.md) as a deterministic annealing the network passes from a "liquid" high-entropy connected state into a "solid" sparse one; the order parameter is the sparsity / entropy, and the analogue of the heat capacity is the dip ΔP (the gap between the two top softmax probabilities) \[[1.11](#ref-1-11)\]. In the robustness version the role of the order parameter is played by the robustness radius ε(W*), which grows as the weight norm falls, and the abrupt jump is reproduced as a cumulative Gaussian of the fraction of test points that have fallen inside that radius \[[1.10](#ref-1-10)\]. The source of difference: the transition is defined in an internal structural/robustness quantity.

### Contested

- **Continuous internal progress** \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]: the jump is visible only in the read-out metric; continuous progress measures (the restricted/excluded loss, the Gini coefficient over the Fourier components) show the circuit forming gradually long before the test accuracy rises, while the jump itself happens at the "cleanup" stage, when weight decay removes the memorising components. The source of difference: the "suddenness" is a lag of the metric rather than a discontinuity of the mechanism.
- **A falsifiable finite-size test** \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]: demands a genuine finite-size scaling — a crossing of the Binder cumulant over the group order p and a power law of the susceptibility against a saturating null (ΔAIC = 16.8 in favour of a transition rather than a smooth crossover); the order of the transition, meanwhile, remains open. The source of difference: a falsifiable FSS protocol instead of an analogy.
- **A removable geometric mismatch** \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\]: the delay is not inevitable — a [spherical constraint](spherical-weight-norm-constraint.md) removes it entirely on the abelian Zp but fails on the non-abelian S5; whether a transition is present depends on the match of the architectural prior with the symmetry of the task. The source of difference: the transition can be "switched off", so it is not intrinsic to the optimisation.

### In support

The works that support a "genuine phase transition" disagree about what exactly sets it:
- **A metastable escape, first order** \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]: the control parameter is the strength of the L2; grokking = a thermally activated (Arrhenius) escape across a loss barrier between coexisting metastable phases, with an effective temperature T_eff ∝ lr/batch, which makes the delay exponentially sensitive to hyperparameters. The difference: hysteresis and an activated escape. The earliest two-sided measurement of such a hysteresis on the canonical task is appendix A.2 of Varma et al.: leaving the memorising state requires $D\sim2000$, while generalisation under ungrokking holds down to $\sim 800$–1000 \[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]; the connection is the editor's — the authors themselves do not use the term "hysteresis".
- **Variance-limited / spectral gating** \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]: the generalising basin is sharper (of higher curvature) than the optimizer's stability ceiling; grokking sets in only when the accumulated gradient variance lifts the ceiling 2/η and opens access to the sharp geometry (the [edge of stability](edge-of-stability.md)). The difference: the trigger is kinetic — a crossing of the stability threshold; and the basin is sharp rather than flat.
- **Norm-driven** \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]: the transition is governed by the gap in norms between the memorising and the generalising solutions (the [norm-separation](norm-separation-delay-law.md) delay law).
- **A genuine transition rather than an artefact of the metric** \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]: the reality is demonstrated by synchronous discontinuities in a set of **continuous** order parameters (dispersion statistics of the vocabulary and others), answering the criticism that "emergence is a mirage". The difference: co-localised discontinuities in several continuous probes at once.
