## Definition

**The learning rate** (η) is the scalar hyperparameter of gradient [optimisers](optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md) that sets the scale of the weight update at each iteration. In the context of [grokking](grokking.md) the learning rate is one of the control parameters (alongside [weight decay](weight-decay.md) — the penalty on the weight norm — the batch size and the training-data fraction) on which it depends whether delayed generalisation arrives and how quickly \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Conceptually the learning rate determines how far the optimiser moves along the negative gradient; adaptive methods (Adam) assign each parameter its own effective rate \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

![A phase diagram over the decoder learning rate and the batch size: generalisation lives in a bounded region of hyperparameters (fig. 13 of Liu et al.)](assets/learning-rate-phase-diagram.png)

## Elaboration

Sensitivity to the learning rate was measured already in the founding paper: in Power et al. generalisation happened only when the rate was tuned within a window one order of magnitude wide \[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]. The learning rate enters grokking in two ways. First, as an axis of a phase diagram: Liu et al. build diagrams of how learning performance depends on the representation learning rate and the decoder learning rate, since it is these parameters that regulate the competition between encoder and decoder \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Second, as the scale of the step: plain SGD takes a step in one direction with a single learning rate and therefore lingers long in the "lazy" regime — the regime in which the representations barely change and the network behaves as a linear model around its initialisation — before passing into the "rich" regime of [feature learning](feature-emergence-feature-learning.md); Adam, by assigning each parameter its own effective rate, shortens the lazy phase \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. Quantitatively, a number of works tie the size of the grokking delay to the learning rate: the Norm-Separation Delay Law gives an inverse scaling of the delay with the learning rate (within the reading of grokking as a [phase transition](phase-transition.md)) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; the time to grokking is made a function of the learning rate and the regularisation strength in a general setting \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; and the critical control parameter is declared to be not the batch size as such but the ratio of the learning rate to the batch size — the higher it is, the wider (flatter) the minima to which training converges \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. The dependence is, however, non-monotone: at too large a learning rate Adam finds not generalising but [memorising](memorization-phase.md) solutions \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\] — that is, raising η does not accelerate grokking without limit, and past a certain threshold it destroys generalisation.

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. An axis of the phase diagram: the representation and the decoder learning rate

Liu et al. split the learning rate into a representation learning rate and a decoder learning rate and build a phase diagram of the regimes (grokking / memorisation / no learning) in the coordinates of these two rates and the decoder weight decay. The key feature of this reading: the learning rate here is not a single scalar but two independent control parameters that regulate the competition of encoder and decoder — and it is their ratio, rather than the absolute value, that fixes the outcome \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

### B. The scale of the step and the lazy→rich transition; Adam's adaptive rate

Jiang et al. read the learning rate as a multiplier of the step along the negative gradient: with a single scalar (SGD) the progress is limited by the slowest [NTK](neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md) mode (the neural tangent kernel — the linear approximation of the network around its initialisation), which keeps the network in the lazy regime for a long time; Adam assigns each parameter its own effective rate, shortening the lazy phase before the rich regime of feature learning. The difference from reading A: what matters is not the range of values but the structure of the rate — a single one against a per-component one \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

### Contested

Tian shows empirically that a large learning rate (0.005) carries Adam to non-generalising (memorising) solutions, whereas small ones (0.001–0.002) give generalising Fourier solutions. Hence a consequence contesting the naive thesis "just raise η": the relation of the learning rate to grokking is non-monotone — past a certain threshold, raising η does not accelerate generalisation but breaks it \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

### In support

Three works join the thesis that the learning rate quantitatively fixes the delay and the outcome of grokking. Truong et al. give an inverse scaling of the delay with η (R2 = 0.92) within the norm-separation delay law, where the effective rate of norm contraction is proportional to the learning rate \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]. Notsawo et al. characterise the relation of the grokking time to the regularisation strength and the learning rate in a general setting \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. Notsawo et al. (another work) declare the critical control parameter to be the ratio of the learning rate to the batch size, with the minimum becoming wider as it grows \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\].
