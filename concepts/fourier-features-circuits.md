## Definition

**Fourier features** are periodic (sine/cosine) representations of the inputs on a small set of "key frequencies" into which a network encodes numbers when learning [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md) (`c = (a + b) mod p`, the residue of a sum modulo a prime). A **Fourier circuit** is a subnetwork implementing a sub-algorithm which, relying on those features, performs addition through trigonometric identities (the addition of angles on a circle). The mechanism was first reverse-engineered in full by Nanda et al.: a transformer that has [grokked](grokking.md) uses discrete Fourier transforms and trigonometric identities to reduce the addition of numbers to a rotation about a circle \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Independently and analytically, the same periodic structure of the features was derived by Gromov, who showed that grokking modular arithmetic corresponds to learning particular feature maps whose structure is determined by the task itself \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

## Elaboration

The Fourier circuit is the canonical illustration that behind the jump in generalisation there stands not "magic" but a concrete algorithm legible in the weights. By the analysis of Nanda et al., the embedding matrix maps the inputs `a`, `b` to sines and cosines on a sparse set of key frequencies \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\] (**key frequencies** — the few frequencies `w_k` on which almost all the norm of the representation is concentrated, while the remaining ~95% of the frequencies the network suppresses), after which the attention and MLP blocks compute the sine and cosine of the sum by trigonometric identities. The same analysis sets out a three-phase picture of training — memorisation, circuit formation, cleanup — in which the long [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md) gives way to the strengthening of an already structural Fourier mechanism; in that sense the "abruptness" of grokking as an [emergent phenomenon](emergence.md) (a sudden qualitative jump as training is scaled) turns out to be apparent.

Around this mechanism a broad body of support and one principled dispute have taken shape. On the supporting side, theory explains *why* the network arrives precisely at Fourier: Li et al. prove that the max-margin solution (**max-margin** — the classifier that maximises the margin between the correct and the incorrect logits) for a `k`-input modular sum is implemented through Fourier features, while Tian shows that for an abelian group the emergent representation must be a Fourier basis. On the contesting side, McCracken et al. **contest the fundamentality** of a particular Fourier pattern at the level of neurons: the different observable frequency pictures (in particular the [Clock and Pizza](clock-vs-pizza.md) circuits) are not different algorithms but one abstract algorithm (the approximate Chinese remainder theorem) to which they reduce by a change of variable (a "renormalisation" of the frequencies). In other words, what exactly a Fourier feature is — a real computational entity or a convenient basis of description — remains a matter of discussion, whereas the fact of the periodicity of the representations after grokking is practically uncontested. This Fourier mechanism should not be confused with the [phase transition](phase-transition.md) of training: a Fourier circuit is the *solution* the network arrives at, whereas a phase transition is the form of the *dynamics* of that arrival.

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The mechanistic Fourier circuit (reverse engineering) — Nanda et al.

A definition through a computational mechanism recovered from the weights. The distinguishing machinery here is a concrete sequence of operations: the embedding encodes the inputs as sines/cosines on the key frequencies, and the attention and MLP blocks turn the addition of numbers into the addition of angles by trigonometric identities and "read" the answer off the logits. The key distinguishing feature of this reading is its reliance on *empirical* verification (the network operationalises trigonometric identities to compute the sine and cosine of `w_k(a + b)` \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]), confirmed by ablations in Fourier space; this is a description of a concrete trained network, not a guarantee for all networks.

### B. The analytical solution: periodic feature maps — Gromov

A definition through a closed analytical form of the weights. The distinguishing machinery is not a post-hoc recovery but a *construction*: Gromov writes out an exact solution for a two-layer MLP whose weights are periodic in the indices of the inputs, with the set of frequencies set by the modulus. The distinguishing source of difference from reading A is the periodicity of the weights with respect to the indices of the inputs as the first ingredient of the exact solution \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]: periodicity here is a proved property of an analytical minimum, and its coincidence with what ordinary gradient descent finds is a separately verifiable claim rather than a postulate. The Fourier structure thereby acquires the status not only of an observation but of a derivation.

### Contested. A single abstract algorithm: the neuron-level Fourier pattern is not fundamental

The contesting reading (McCracken et al.): the variability of the frequency pictures at the level of neurons is an artefact of the level of description rather than evidence of different computations. The controlled parameter here is the frequency of a neuron: after it is normalised (a "renormalisation", the change of variable `h(x) = cos(2πf x/n)`), the neurons of different transformers behave identically, and all the schemes are read as an approximate Chinese remainder theorem (a decomposition of `mod n` into independent computations over coprime divisors). The testable consequence that distinguishes this position: the "pizza" and "clock" circuits show a qualitative equivalence after the remapping \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\], that is, the concrete set of frequencies of a neuron is interchangeable and carries no independent algorithmic meaning.

### In support. The theoretical necessity of a Fourier basis

The supporting reading (Li et al.; Tian): Fourier features are not an accident of a particular run but a consequence of the objective being optimised. The distinguishing machinery is a proof through [margin maximisation](margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md): in Li et al. every neuron focuses on one distinct Fourier frequency \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], while Tian reduces this to representation theory: for an abelian group all the irreducible representations are one-dimensional, so the resulting representation turns out to be a Fourier basis \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. The testable consequence that distinguishes this position from the purely empirical A/B: the Fourier structure ought to reproduce under different training procedures and to extend beyond addition — which Furuta et al. confirm for more complex modular operations \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\].

**\[4.13\]** 2606.13753 — Truong et al. 2026, "The Weight Norm Sets the Grokking Timescale: A Causal Delay Law". Measures the Fourier concentration of the embedding by the Gini coefficient of the power spectrum over tokens and shows that it grows in the same window in which weight decay relaxes the norm — that is, on the descent leading to grokking. From this a division of labour between the two explanations is proposed: the circuits govern what is learned, and the relaxation of the norm governs when it generalises. The nuance that drops out first in retelling: the link is only temporal, there is no intervention on the circuits, and the authors admit this three times — even as the heading "A bridge between two explanations" and the sixth item of the list of contributions put it on a par with the measured results.
