## Definition

**Weight-norm minimization** is a reading of [grokking](grokking.md) in which delayed generalisation is explained by a movement of the parameters towards a solution of smaller weight norm (usually the L2 norm of all trainable parameters) along the **zero-loss manifold** (the set of weight configurations on which the training error is already essentially zero). Once the training set has been fully memorised, further training under [weight decay](weight-decay.md) (L2 regularisation) keeps shifting the network along that manifold towards low-norm, more "efficient" and generalising solutions; the moment the network reaches such a solution is what is observed as grokking \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. The idea goes back to the thought that among the many solutions with zero training error weight decay prefers the ones that require a smaller parameter norm \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\], and is formalised as a norm-driven transition along the interpolation manifold \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\].

![Generalising solutions are concentrated near a sphere in weight space: generalisation as a movement towards a smaller norm (fig. 1 of Liu et al.)](assets/weight-norm-sphere.png)

## Elaboration

The zero-loss manifold is a surface in weight space along which the training loss equals zero (the network has already fitted the training data perfectly) while the weights themselves can still change freely. The key argument for norm minimization was given by Varma et al.: if several "circuits" (subnetworks implementing one or another solution of the task) give an equally good training accuracy, weight decay favours the more "efficient" ones — those that reach the required logit at a smaller parameter norm; the generalising circuit is usually more efficient than the memorising one, so as the norm falls it displaces the memorising circuit \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Musat carried this through to a formal statement: in the limit of an infinitesimal learning rate and weight-decay coefficient, gradient descent after memorisation is equivalent to a constrained optimisation — the minimisation of the weight norm on the zero-loss manifold \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. Truong et al. made the picture quantitative: high-norm (memorising) and low-norm (structured) solutions coexist on one and the same interpolation manifold, and weight decay contracts the norm exponentially from the former towards the latter, so that the grokking delay is the time it takes to traverse the geometric gap between them (a "norm-separation [delay law](norm-separation-delay-law.md)") \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. In this frame grokking appears as a [phase transition](phase-transition.md) governed by the norm, rather than merely as late generalisation after the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md).

The corpus describes the same picture in the language of **constrained optimisation** as well: minimising the norm subject to the constraint "zero training error".

This reading is actively contested. Many works show that grokking also sets in under a **growing** weight norm: Kumar et al., in the regime of the transition from lazy to rich training, record a growth of the norm which "cannot be explained by any existing theories" \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]; Prakash et al. observe grokking with no weight decay at all (with a growing norm), concluding that "the weight norm alone is not enough" \[[2.6](#ref-2-6)\]; Singh et al. state outright that "generalisation does not depend on smaller weight norms or weight decay" \[[2.8](#ref-2-8)\]. Others redefine the status of the norm as an indicator: Minegishi et al. attribute the mitigation of the delay not to a fall of the norm but to the discovery of a good subnetwork \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]; Tan et al. show that a fall of the norm is a sufficient but not a necessary condition \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\]; Fan et al. find that "the dynamics of the weight norm are not indicative" of generalisation \[[2.5](#ref-2-5)\]; Notsawo et al. argue that "the ℓ2 norm is an unreliable proxy for generalisation" \[[2.7](#ref-2-7)\]; Liu et al. propose the complexity measure LMN in place of L2 \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\], while Truong et al., in another work, point out that norm accounts do not supply a measurable quantity that causally precedes the transition \[[2.9](#ref-2-9)\]. Alongside the criticism there is support as well: Huang et al. describe the transition as the displacement of the memorising circuit by the generalising one, which is "more efficient in terms of parameter norms" \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\], and Xu et al., in a ridge-regression setting, prove that gradient descent converges to a small-weight-norm solution that generalises well \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. The phenomenon borders on other themes of the grokking circle — [circuit efficiency](circuit-efficiency.md), [double descent](double-descent.md) (in the reading through competition of solutions) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] — and the anomalous growth of the norm at the end of training is linked to [slingshot](slingshot.md) loss spikes and to the late collapse of generalisation ([anti-grokking](anti-grokking.md)).

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. Norm minimization as constrained optimisation

A definition through constrained optimisation (Musat): once the training loss has hit zero, training is in effect solving the problem of minimising the weight norm subject to the constraint of "staying on the zero-loss manifold". The distinguishing machinery here is the limiting regime (an infinitesimal learning rate and weight-decay coefficient) in which this statement is proved rigorously, and from which a closed-form expression for the post-memorisation dynamics of the first layer of a two-layer network is derived \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]. Grokking here is not a separate event but the end point of a deterministic drift along the manifold.

### B. Circuit efficiency: a smaller norm per unit of logit

A definition through "efficiency" (Varma; joined by Huang): the control parameter is the ratio of the logit produced to the parameter norm spent. The generalising circuit produces the same logit at a smaller norm, so under weight decay it wins the competition against the memorising one; the moment of switching is grokking \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The difference from version A: here the norm is not the global objective of the optimisation but a parameter of the relative "exchange rate" between two competing subnetworks, and the speed of the transition is set by how much more efficient the generalising circuit is than the memorising one \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\].

### C. A norm-driven phase transition and the delay law

A definition through the order of magnitude of the delay (Truong): the controlling quantity is the geometric gap in norm between the high-norm memorising and the low-norm structured solutions coexisting on the interpolation manifold. Weight decay contracts the norm exponentially, so the grokking delay scales as the time it takes to traverse that gap — formalised as a "norm-separation delay law" \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]. Unlike A and B, what is predicted here is not only the direction of the drift but a quantitative dependence of the plateau's length on the strength of the regularisation.

### Contested

The common argument of those who contest: the weight norm is neither the cause nor a reliable indicator of grokking. Some works demonstrate grokking under a **growing** norm and without weight decay \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]\[[2.6](#ref-2-6)\]\[[2.8](#ref-2-8)\]; some — that the fall of the norm merely correlates with the transition or precedes it without being necessary \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]\[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\]\[[2.5](#ref-2-5)\]; some propose to replace the norm by another measure — LMN (a non-linear complexity measure) \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\], an ℓ2-independent indicator \[[2.7](#ref-2-7)\] or the normalised spectral entropy of the representations \[[2.9](#ref-2-9)\].

### In support

The works that join reinforce norm minimization from two sides: mechanistically — through the competition of circuits, where the generalising one is more efficient "in terms of parameter norms" \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] — and theoretically, through a provable result in ridge regression, where gradient descent provably converges to a low-norm generalising solution \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\].
