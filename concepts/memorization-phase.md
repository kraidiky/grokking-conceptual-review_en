## Definition

**The memorization phase (the plateau)** is the initial stage of training under [grokking](grokking.md), at which the model attains an almost perfect accuracy on the training set (memorises / overfits) while the test accuracy remains low \[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\]. This protracted plateau precedes the abrupt transition to generalisation \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]\[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\].

![The memorisation plateau: the training loss falls, the test loss rises from the hundredth step — the network memorises first, generalisation comes orders of magnitude later (fig. 4 of Power et al.)](assets/memorization-plateau.png)

## Elaboration

During the memorization phase the network "memorises" the training data without yet having built a generalising mechanism \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]\[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]; the length of this plateau can be very large and is itself an object of study \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\]. Mechanistically, memorisation is read as a separate "memorising" solution/circuit (a subnetwork storing the examples) within the frame of [circuit efficiency](circuit-efficiency.md); it dominates until the generalising one appears \[[1.11](#ref-1-11)\]\[[1.12](#ref-1-12)\], and the whole process is decomposed into several consecutive phases (memorisation → circuit formation → cleanup, the removal of the superfluous memorising components) \[[1.9](#ref-1-9)\]\[[1.10](#ref-1-10)\]. A number of works read the memorization phase as a **stable high-norm state**, after which the optimisation minimises the norm on the [zero-loss manifold](weight-norm-minimization.md) (the set of parameters with a zero training error) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]\[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. The intrusiveness/inevitability of the plateau is contested, however: architectural constraints are able to **eliminate the memorization phase entirely** \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]; "memorisation" itself in the lazy regime (lazy/[NTK](neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md): the features barely change) is imperfect \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]; and overfitting can occur with **no** subsequent grokking at all — because of numerical errors \[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\]. The «memorisation → generalisation» transition is observed even where there is no training set at all: under online training on a formal language, where a fresh batch is sampled at every step, what is memorised is not examples but the seen «entity–property» pairs, and the end of such a phase is described by the authors as a memorising solution giving way to a generalising one \[[5.2](#ref-5-2)\]↗.

The corpus also calls the same period the **post-memorization dynamics**, when what is at issue is what happens inside the network after the fit.

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The classical sense: the overfitting / interpolation phase

The phase at which the network fits (memorises) the training sample almost perfectly at a low generalisation \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]\[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]\[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]\[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\].

### B. The plateau before the abrupt transition

A definition through the position in time: a protracted plateau preceding the sudden transition "memorisation → generalisation" \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\]\[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]\[[1.7](#ref-1-7)\]\[[1.8](#ref-1-8)\].

### C. Memorisation as a separate solution / circuit

A definition through the competition of solutions: memorisation is a separate "memorising" solution (a high-LLC state) that dominates until the switch to the generalising circuit \[[1.11](#ref-1-11)\]\[[1.12](#ref-1-12)\]. The founding work, however, itself gives direct counterevidence against an unconditional displacement: when corrupted equations are introduced, the memorised exceptions coexist with the generalising solution in the same weights (appendix A.4) \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\].

### D. The first phase in a multi-phase decomposition

A definition as the first named stage in a sequence of training phases (memorisation, circuit formation, cleanup / symmetry acquisition) \[[1.9](#ref-1-9)\]\[[1.10](#ref-1-10)\].
