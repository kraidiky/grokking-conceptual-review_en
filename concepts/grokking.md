## Definition

**Grokking** is the phenomenon of delayed generalisation in which a network first fits the training sample almost perfectly (a train accuracy close to 100 %) at a low generalising ability, and then, after substantially longer training — often orders of magnitude more steps — the test accuracy rises abruptly to a high value \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The term and the phenomenon itself were introduced by Power et al. (2022) on small algorithmic datasets \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\].

![The canonical grokking curve: the training accuracy reaches almost 100 % within a thousand steps, the test accuracy stays at chance level up to a hundred thousand steps and only by a million comes to the ceiling (division modulo 97, 50 % of the data in training; fig. 1 of Power et al.)](assets/grokking-delayed-generalization.png)

## Elaboration

Grokking was originally observed on algorithmic tasks (above all [modular arithmetic](modular-arithmetic.md)) under training with [weight decay](weight-decay.md), where the gap between the [memorisation phase](memorization-phase.md) and the onset of generalisation can be orders of magnitude in the number of optimisation steps \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The transition itself is as a rule abrupt and is increasingly described as a [phase transition](phase-transition.md) — a jump of the test accuracy after a long plateau \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]\[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\], up to formalisations as a norm-driven one (driven by the fall of the weight norm) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] or as a first-order phase transition \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]. A number of works, however, contest the discontinuity of the transition: mechanistic progress measures (continuous [progress measures](progress-measures.md) read off the internal structure of the network) show that behind the "suddenness" there stands a gradual process \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]. Mechanistically, grokking is linked to a memorising solution being replaced by a generalising one — the displacement of an inefficient memorising circuit (a subnetwork storing examples) by an efficient generalising circuit/subnetwork implementing the algorithm of the task ([circuit efficiency](circuit-efficiency.md)) \[[1.7](#ref-1-7)\]\[[1.8](#ref-1-8)\]\[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]; some works, meanwhile, attribute the effect not to the fall of the weight norm \[[2.6](#ref-2-6)\] but to the discovery of a good subnetwork \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. Grokking is reproduced without explicit regularisation as well \[[2.4](#ref-2-4)\]\[[2.5](#ref-2-5)\]\[[2.3](#ref-2-3)\], and even outside [SGD](optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md) and neural networks \[[2.9](#ref-2-9)\]. Separate works contest the very content of the transition — reading it as a removable geometric mismatch \[[2.8](#ref-2-8)\] and questioning whether grokking yields genuine generalising reasoning \[[2.7](#ref-2-7)\]. It was later shown that it is not confined to algorithmic tasks: it is reproduced on [real-world data](vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md) — first on MNIST as early as the effective-theory paper \[[4.1](#ref-4-1)\], systematically in Omnigrok \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\] — and even in the pre-training of large language models \[[1.10](#ref-1-10)\]. The current understanding thus reads grokking as a general phenomenon of learning rather than an artefact of a narrow class of synthetic tasks.

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. The classical empirical sense: delayed generalisation after overfitting

The base formulation of Power et al.: generalisation sets in much later than the overfitting of the training data \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Omnigrok generalises the same definition, lifting the tie to algorithmic tasks \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\].

### B. A sudden / abrupt phase transition

The emphasis on the jump: grokking is defined as an **abrupt transition** from memorisation to generalisation after long training \[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]\[[1.6](#ref-1-6)\]\[[1.11](#ref-1-11)\]\[[1.12](#ref-1-12)\]. The phenomenon is formalised as a phase transition — up to a "variance-limited" one \[[1.5](#ref-1-5)\] and an "emergent phase transition" \[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\], a norm-driven transition \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]\[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\], a first-order transition \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\] or a genuine reorganisation rather than an artefact of the metric \[[3.4](#ref-3-4)\]. The counter-position: behind the jump there stands a gradual process rather than a discontinuity \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\].

### C. A competition of the memorising and the generalising solution

A definition through two competing solutions: the network first finds a memorising circuit, and grokking is the moment of switching to a more efficient generalising circuit/subnetwork \[[1.7](#ref-1-7)\]\[[1.8](#ref-1-8)\]\[[3.5](#ref-3-5)\]. Here grokking is not so much "late generalisation" as **a change of the dominant solution**; some works attribute the cause to the discovery of a good subnetwork rather than to a fall of the norm \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

### D. Compression / a growth of the simplicity of the solution

A definition through compression: the generalising solution is "simpler"/more efficient than the memorising one and therefore appears later; grokking = the process of compressing the complexity of the network down to a generalising minimum \[[1.9](#ref-1-9)\].

### E. The extended reading: beyond algorithmic tasks

A definition lifting the tie to synthetic data: grokking is a general effect of delayed generalisation, observed on real-world data \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\], in the practical pre-training of LLMs \[[1.10](#ref-1-10)\] and even outside neural networks and SGD \[[2.9](#ref-2-9)\]. The strongest support for this reading is the transfer to a genuine factual corpus: grokking is reproduced on multi-hop questions over 2WikiMultiHopQA, that is, it is not a peculiarity of deliberately built toy datasets \[[3.6](#ref-3-6)\]. The boundary of this reading is drawn by an external work on emergence: in online training, where there is no training set and an example is almost surely not repeated, the «memorisation → generalisation» transition is observed all the same, while the explanations of grokking that lean on a phase of complete memorisation of the training data are declared inapplicable to such a setting \[[5.5](#ref-5-5)\]↗.

### F. Which curve to define it on: loss against accuracy

An axis along which the corpus splits three ways: which curve and which threshold make a run "grokked". Kumar et al. distinguish "grokking in loss" from "grokking in accuracy" and prove that the first is a strictly stronger condition: grokking in loss implies grokking in accuracy but not conversely \[[2.5](#ref-2-5)\] (their §16.3 separately analyses cases of grokking in accuracy only, which are not worth studying). Nanda et al. narrow the term from the loss side: without an initial divergence of the training and test loss curves there is no grokking \[[2.2](#ref-2-2)\]. The third pole is Notsawo et al., who dissolve the axis for the sake of simplicity: any situation with generalisation is called grokking \[[2.10](#ref-2-10)\]. The practical consequence: the verdict "grokked / did not grok" for one and the same run can change when the metric or the threshold is changed.

### Earlier observations (before the term)

The only published claim in the corpus that the phenomenon itself is older than the term: Mohamadi et al. write that delayed generalisation was "perhaps earlier observed" by Li, Wang & Arora when training diagonal linear networks with label-noise SGD through sharpness minimisation, before it was known as grokking \[[4.13](#ref-4-13)\]. A check against the primary source (task-018) shows that the hedge "perhaps" carries real weight: the paper \[[5.1](#ref-5-1)\]↗ contains neither the word "grokking" nor any experiments with training curves — the "observation" there is theoretical. What is proved is a two-phase dynamics: a generalising sparse solution is reached after interpolation, on the slow scale $\Theta(\eta^{-2})$, by a drift along the zero-loss manifold with an implicit minimisation of sharpness. The scheme "interpolation -> drift along the manifold -> a simple solution" structurally coincides with the corpus line "interpolation, then norm minimisation" (2505.20172, which cites that very work), so the claim is apt as a hedged claim to a prehistory rather than as a fact of grokking having been observed before Power et al.

Also (the mechanism of the arrival): [`"This work did not address, however, the mechanism-by-which and reason-for-which such a solution is reached via gradient descent, for a general word"`](../papers/2509.06931.learning-words-in-groups-fusion-algebras-tensor-ranks-and-grokking/2509.06931.learning-words-in-groups-fusion-algebras-tensor-ranks-and-grokking.card.md#p20-2).

###### ref-4-15
**\[4.15\]** 2509.10562 — Lopatin, Kozyrev & Pechen 2025, "Predator–Prey Model: Driven Hunt for Accelerated Grokking". Grokking is accepted as a given and treated: a second agent (a "predator") drives the "prey" along the ravine of the landscape, turning a diffusion along the bottom into a transport; the acceleration numbers are single runs with no comparison against Grokfast or NeuralGrok. [`"Since the gradient for the predator is not called, for both ModuloOperation and for MNIST we get an acceleration of grokking of about 100 times in the number of iterations"`](../papers/2509.10562.predator-prey-model-driven-hunt-for-accelerated-grokking/2509.10562.predator-prey-model-driven-hunt-for-accelerated-grokking.card.md#p8-3)..

###### ref-4-16
**\[4.16\]** 2512.00686 — Lakkapragada 2025, "Using physics-inspired Singular Learning Theory to understand grokking & other phase transitions in modern neural networks". The grokking time is proposed to be predicted by an Arrhenius hypothesis through the change of the SLT free energy; the actual fit explains about six per cent of the spread over 168 selected runs, while the accompanying histogram yields a rare statistic: a complexity jump $\Delta\lambda\approx-22$ — the grokked solution is simpler than the memorising one. ###### ref-4-17
**\[4.17\]** 2602.12039 — Beck, Bar-Sinai & Levi 2026, "The Implicit Bias of Logit Regularization". A rare mechanism in which the control parameter of grokking is the strength of the regularisation: a logit penalty shifts the interpolation threshold from a half to unity, and in the band between the thresholds the model first overfits along the unregularised bias and then converges to a clustering of the logits; the time scale of the delay is not derived, and the single binary experiment is set at a tenfold weakened signal. ###### ref-4-18
**\[4.18\]** 2602.18649 — Xu 2026, "Global Low-Rank, Local Full-Rank: The Holographic Encoding of Learned Algorithms". Grokking as a testbed for a "principle of holographic encoding": the training trajectory is low-dimensional and the weights are locally full-rank; the link to grokking itself remains a claim — there is not a single training curve in the paper, and there is no control by a non-grokking model separating the holographic property from a property of any trained network. ###### ref-4-19
**\[4.19\]** 2604.07380 — Xu 2026, "The Lifecycle of the Spectral Edge: From Gradient Learning to Weight-Decay Compression". The co-direction of the gradient and the weight-decay components of the update along the spectral edge is declared a "microscopic signature of a phase transition"; there is no definition of grokking and no training curves in the paper, and the check "no alignment without grokking" is identical to the check "no weight decay", so the causal link remains a claim. ###### ref-4-20
**\[4.20\]** 2605.12394 — Prakash & Martin 2026, "Detecting overfitting in Neural Networks during long-horizon grokking using Random Matrix Theory". Grokking is taken as a laboratory of overfitting over a long horizon, and the main lesson is stated outright: the generalising rule found at grokking can be lost under continued optimisation — grokking ceases to be the end of training; the grokking itself in the MLP is induced by multiplying the initial weights by eight, and the main run is a single seed. [`"The main lesson is that grokking is not always the end of training. A model can first learn a rule that generalizes, and then lose that generalization under continued optimization"`](../papers/2605.12394.detecting-overfitting-in-neural-networks-during-long-horizon-grokking-using-random-matrix-theory/original/2605.12394.detecting-overfitting-in-neural-networks-during-long-horizon-grokking-using-random-matrix-theory.md#p8-3)..

###### ref-4-21
**\[4.21\]** 2606.04405 — Li 2026, "Low-Rank Decay for Grokking in Scale-Invariant Transformers: A Spectral-Geometric View". Grokking as the target of a spectral regulariser: a step along the polar factor of the nuclear norm brings the model to a test accuracy above 0.95 within three thousand steps — a horizon at which canonical grokking has not yet set in, and the device has almost no delay between memorisation and generalisation; the causal link between the rank collapse and the generalisation is admitted outright to be unestablished. ###### ref-4-22
**\[4.22\]** 2606.05863 — Tan, Gai & Zhang 2026, "Deciphering Two Training Clocks in Grokking via Deep Linear Network Theory with Conditional ReLU Reduction". Grokking is rebuilt around a comparison of two stopping times — a classifier clock (logarithmic under an assumption of growing margins) and a representation clock (polynomial under an assumption of a sharp KL tail); both laws are conditional, their assumptions are directly incompatible for a single trajectory, and in the paper's own experiment the estimators of the clocks are never computed. [`"The training loss can become small on the classifier clock, while the structural quantity needed for robust generalization continues to evolve on the representation clock"`](../papers/2606.05863.deciphering-two-training-clocks-in-grokking-via-deep-linear-network-theory-with-conditional-relu-reduction/2606.05863.deciphering-two-training-clocks-in-grokking-via-deep-linear-network-theory-with-conditional-relu-reduction.card.md#p16-9)..
