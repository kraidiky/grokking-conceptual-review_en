## Definition

**The slingshot effect (mechanism)** is an optimisation anomaly of adaptive [optimisers](optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md) (Adam/AdamW) at the very late stages of training (the terminal phase, after training accuracy has reached nearly 100%): training cycles between stable and unstable regimes; a full cycle is a fast growth of the last layer's weight norm, a spike of the training loss at the break, and a plateau of the norm \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. It was introduced by Thilak et al. (2022) in the study of [grokking](grokking.md).

![A full slingshot cycle: cycles of "norm growth — training-loss spike — norm plateau", with an inset of a single cycle (fig. 1 of Thilak et al.)](assets/slingshot-full-cycle.png)

## Elaboration

The observable sign of the slingshot is the **cyclic behaviour of the last layer's weight norm**: each cycle is a fast growth of the norm, then a sharp destabilisation (a training-loss spike with a brief "failure" on the training examples), then a plateau \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The phenomenon is tied to adaptive optimisers and observed in the regime **without explicit regularisation** — that condition stands in the formulation of the observation itself \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. It is also described as cyclic [phase transitions](phase-transition.md) between stable and unstable training regimes \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]\[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\]. The relation to the strength of regularisation is measured directly in Thilak et al., and it is twofold. In a [weight decay](weight-decay.md) sweep over $0{-}1.0$ (AdamW; division, addition, multiplication) both the slingshot and grokking disappear already at the smallest non-zero value $0.1$ — what disappears is the delay, not generalisation: validation reaches 100% within a few thousand epochs against tens of thousands at $\mathrm{wd}=0$ (figs. 39–40); the interval $0{-}0.1$ was not probed, so the destruction threshold is known only from above. What vanishes is exactly the norm cycle by which the slingshot is recognised — the spike instability does not decrease, and by the authors' own captions to figs. 39–41 it even strengthens as weight decay grows \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The frequency of the cycles, meanwhile, is governed by Adam's numerical parameter $\epsilon$: the smaller $\epsilon$, the more cycles \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The interpretations diverge: some see in the slingshot a functionally necessary mechanism — an injection of variance (a useful addition of [gradient noise](gradient-noise.md)) \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\] or an implicit regulariser (a hidden constraint on the complexity of the solution) \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]; others recast it as a numerical artefact of low precision \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\].

## Alternative definitions and nuances

### A. An optimisation anomaly of adaptive optimisers

Thilak et al.'s original reading: the slingshot is an **optimisation anomaly** of an adaptive optimiser in the terminal phase; it is recognised by the **cyclic oscillation of the last layer's weight norm** and by the alternation of stable and unstable regimes \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]\[[1.3](#ref-1-3)\]; formally, a cyclic phase transition in the training dynamics \[[1.2](#ref-1-2)\]\[[1.4](#ref-1-4)\]. The anomaly is not useless in their account: the mechanism exposes an implicit bias of Adam that often promotes generalisation, by arresting the growth of the norm and the feature learning that ensues \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. Its necessity is conditional: without explicit regularisation grokking almost exclusively coincides with the onset of the slingshot and is absent without it, but weight decay yields comparable generalisation much faster and with no slingshot at all, and the authors themselves conclude that the slingshot may not be the only way to good generalisation \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]. The source of the difference: a **useful implicit bias of the optimiser** (it does not appear without Adam/AdamW), an almost obligatory companion of grokking in the regularisation-free regime — but not a universal cause of generalisation: it can be replaced by explicit regularisation.

### B. A functional mechanism: variance injection / implicit regularisation

A recasting of the slingshot as **functionally necessary** rather than incidental: in the spectral-gating picture it is an **injection of gradient variance**, without which the optimiser cannot enter the sharp generalising basin — the slingshot is "formalised not as an anomaly but as a variance-injection mechanism" \[[3.1](#ref-3-1)\]; in the progress-measures line it is read as an **implicit regulariser** \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. The source of the difference: the slingshot here is the **cause** of generalisation (a useful pumping of variance or regularisation), not a harmless artefact.

### C. A numerical glitch of low precision

One more reading: the slingshot spikes are a consequence of **the limits of floating-point arithmetic** at high model confidence (the authors call it Numerical Feature Inflation) rather than of optimisation dynamics as such; hence the question in the title, "Grokking or Glitching?" \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\]. The source of the difference: the slingshot is a **numerical artefact of low precision**, whose reproducibility depends on precision, rather than a universal property of the optimiser. The seed of this reading is already in Thilak et al. themselves: both the frequency and the presence of the cycles are governed there by Adam's numerical stability parameter $\epsilon$ (see the Elaboration, \[[1.1](#ref-1-1)\]).

The genealogy of the reading is older than the year of work \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\] suggests. The first direct connection was made by Nanda et al. 2023 in a single sentence of appendix D.2: slingshots could be induced on a one-layer model by lowering the precision of the loss computation, with a sketch of the mechanism — the gradients round to zero and the spread of their scales grows \[[3.2](#ref-3-2)\]. Prieto et al. analysed the arithmetic of the failure itself ([Softmax Collapse](softmax-collapse.md)) and conjectured in appendix H that the slingshot is optimisation saving itself from full SC \[[3.3](#ref-3-3)\]; the work \[[2.1](#ref-2-1)\] carried the reading through to a complete mechanism and names Nanda et al. outright as the first to connect the slingshot with numerical instability.
