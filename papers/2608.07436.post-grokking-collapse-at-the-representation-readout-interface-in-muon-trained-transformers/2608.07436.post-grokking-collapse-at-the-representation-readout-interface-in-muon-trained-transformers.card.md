# Janati, El Maghraoui, Kanavalau, Belfatmi 2026 — Post-Grokking Collapse at the Representation–Readout Interface in Muon-Trained Transformers

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Ali Janati** (Data Science Institute, Columbia University), **Kaoutar El Maghraoui** (Department of Computer Science, Columbia University), **Andrei Kanavalau** (Stanford University), **Anass Belfatmi** (CentraleSupélec).

arXiv [2608.07436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07436), August 2026 (v1), 34 pages, 6 figures, 20 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The subject is what happens to a grokked circuit after the grokking under a split routing of optimisers (Muon on the hidden matrices, AdamW on the embeddings and the output head): all nine Muon settings in the sweep reach a stable $95$% and all then lose their generalisation, in AdamW the same happens in four of the seven grokking settings, and the difference between the optimisers lies in a severity two orders of magnitude apart rather than in the presence of the failure. The mechanism of the failure is tied to the representation–readout interface, which the loss recognises only jointly (through the product $W_{U}h$); freezing the embeddings and the head after the circuit has formed removes the collapse cleanly over five runs and $451{,}400$ post-grokking steps. A separate result is the blindness of the standard progress measures: over the very interval in which the accuracy falls from $100$% to $19.04$%, the set of leading frequencies within the task family does not change (a Jaccard index of $1.0000$) and the distribution of the power keeps a cosine of $0.9899$.

## Concepts covered

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — a prediction made before the measurement and confirmed: the set of leading frequencies within a family is invariant to any invertible change of basis, the distribution of power over them is invariant to an orthogonal one, and measures of the function itself are invariant to neither — and over the interval between two adjacent checkpoints, in which the training accuracy falls from $100$% to $21.12$% and the held-out one to $19.04$%, the support does not change at all (a Jaccard index of $1.0000$), the power shifts by one per cent (a cosine of $0.9899$), and the mean margin of the isolated circuit goes from $+18.14$ to $-46.85$ ([`p26-3`](#p26-3)). The analysis: the spectral check reports an intact circuit at exactly the step at which the model has ceased to compute the task — and this is the work's most transferable result; the measures are applied not to something arbitrary but to precisely the modes that carry the algorithm, so the check is set up in its strictest form.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — "the circuit exists but is masked" as a state in its own right: a model containing a circuit that gives exactly $100$% in isolation answers wrongly, because the task part of the margin ($+5.72$) is overridden by an almost equal opposing contribution from the remainder ($-6.43$); rescaling the task part alone, with no further training and without touching the readout, lifts the model from $45.85$% to $99.91$%, and the state itself is met with twice — before the grokking, when the family accounts for one per cent of the representation, and after the collapse, when it has fallen from $84$% to $20$% ([`p25-2`](#p25-2)). The analysis: hence a reading of grokking as a masking that resolves upwards — the cleanup era of Nanda et al. is expressed as a growth of the same power fraction from $1.05$% to $70.62$% with the family already sufficient from step $3{,}000$; but the claim rests on a single AdamW trajectory, and "the two transitions are one measurement run in opposite directions" is an interpretation rather than a separate experiment.

**[Optimisers](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the split routing cuts through what the loss recognises only jointly: the Newton–Schulz iteration normalises its input before the orthogonalisation and returns singular values around unity, whatever generated them, so that in the hidden group the elasticity of the applied step with respect to the gradient norm is $-0.026$ against $+1.51$ and $+1.47$ for the embeddings and the readout, and over the $691$ steps of the quiet window the two sides diverge by a factor of $8.0$ per parameter; the same update rule also sets the spread of the representation — $326.09$ effective conjugate pairs against AdamW's $4.95$ ([`p11-4`](#p11-4)). The analysis: the attribution of the failure to Muon alone is demolished by the work itself — four of the seven grokking AdamW settings lose their generalisation too, and the severity there grows monotonically with the learning rate; the ablation of the normalisation and the orthogonalisation is done in one go and without matching the learning rate (it had to be raised from $0.03$ to $0.3\text{–}1.0$), and the freezing itself removes the instability without bringing the spread anywhere near AdamW's — so the spectral spread and the instability are different things.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — an exact partition of the $p^{2}$ modes into six pairwise disjoint families makes the ablation of a family and the sufficiency of its complement a single measurement (they agree to $1.71$% within the same bounds, which serves as a check of the filters), and over the forty-three solved points spanning five seeds and three optimisation regimes the family $(k,k)$ in isolation gives exactly $100.00$% everywhere while subtraction, $a$-only, $b$-only and the constant mode give exactly $0.88$%, the chance level; and the basis within the family is exact: moving the pairs to other diagonal frequencies at a preserved power gives $2.64$%, and a random phase per pair gives $0.19$%, below chance ([`p19-3`](#p19-3)). The analysis: the prediction is checked by swapping the task — on $(a-b)\bmod 113$ from the same initial state the roles exchange exactly, so the family is chosen by the task rather than by the analysis; two branches from a bit-identical state arrive at different bases of one algorithm (a cosine of the readout matrices of $-0.028$), and the circuit does not transfer without its own readout.

**[Anti-grokking](../../concepts/anti-grokking.md)** — the work cites Prakash & Martin and distances itself from them outright: in anti-grokking the training accuracy stays perfect while the test one falls, whereas here both fall over the same interval ($100\to 21.12$% and $100\to 19.04$%), and a mechanism acting on the generalisation alone would have left the training sample solved ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). The analysis: something else, and more important, does coincide — both works assert that the late collapse escapes the existing progress measures, but they treat this differently: there a spectral statistic over the weights is proposed, here it is shown that the spectrum of the representation is precisely blind; and the nearest neighbour by subject, 2602.02859, is not cited at all.

**[Grokking time](../../concepts/grokking-time.md)** — the work distinguishes a gain in steps from a gain in time: a Muon step is $1.75\text{–}1.80$ times dearer than an AdamW step, which turns an advantage of $2.22$ in steps into $1.28$ in wall time ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)). This contests the customary measuring of speed-ups in epochs.

**[Over-parametrisation and depth](../../concepts/overparameterization-depth.md)** — depth enters the sweep here on a par with the modulus, the training fraction and the width, as an axis for checking the robustness of the result rather than as a factor under study ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)).

**[Seed variance and reproducibility](../../concepts/seed-variance-reproducibility.md)** — the design of the experiment removes the seed as a source of difference: the two branches grow from one state and coincide bit for bit before the intervention, with a maximum absolute difference of zero ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)). A paired comparison here is stronger than a comparison of means across runs.

**[Selective weight decay](../../concepts/selective-weight-decay.md)** — the unembedding is singled out as a separate group so as to give the output head its own learning rate and decay and to freeze it independently ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)). There is a setting in the sweep with a zero decay on the readout head, and the collapse occurs just the same — a penalty on it is not the cause.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`, 45 items, 13 of them marked as predicted before the experiment and confirmed).

**The strong point — interventions rather than correlations.** Branching from a bit-identical state makes the causal statements checkable: freezing either of the two groups suppresses the failure, within the auxiliary group it is precisely the readout matrix whose motion is required (freezing the embeddings delays the collapse by $50\text{–}200$ steps, freezing the readout removes it altogether), and the hidden matrices together with the readout reproduce the failure. The distinction between "the circuit exists but is masked" and "there is no circuit" is checked by a rescaling with no further training. The exact partition of the modes provides a self-check of the filters. The second operation supplies a family provably incapable of computing the first task — and the spread thereby acquires a floor to be measured from, ceasing to be an arbitrary scale. The ready recipe for stability (fix the embeddings and the head after the circuit has formed) is useful independently of whether the explanation through a change of basis is right.

**The speed-up is measured in steps, not in seconds.** A Muon step costs $1.75\text{–}1.80$ times more than an AdamW step, but the conversion into wall time is given only for depths $2$ and $4$. At depth $1$, where the advantage in steps is $1.52$, the same conversion makes Muon about $15$% **slower** than AdamW in time; this is nowhere said in the work. The advantage at depth $4$ ($>5.70$) is a lower bound right-censored by the budget, and in seconds it gives $>3.37$.

**What remains an interpretation.** What is measured is a functional incompatibility (each branch reads its own representation at $100$% and the other's at chance), not a fitted change of basis: fitting $R$ and checking through a corrected readout is assigned by the authors outright to further work. The ablation of the normalisation and the orthogonalisation is confounded with the raising of the learning rate. The factor of $8.0$ is a mean over magnitudes, while the instantaneous ratio within the same window falls from $16.2$ to $3.3$. The runs are on Apple MPS in float32, where reproduction is not bit-exact: two attempts at one setting at depth $4$ give a stable generalisation at $52{,}600$ and $101{,}300$ steps; the matched branches, the substitution of a foreign readout and the layer-wise analysis remain single-seed — as is admitted outright.

**How the work will be cited** ("Muon makes grokking unstable"; "freezing the embeddings and the head cures the instability") — more precisely: a post-grokking collapse occurs under AdamW too, under Muon it is universal across the sweep, and the difference in severity is two orders of magnitude; and the cure is complete at depths $1$ and $2$ while at depth $4$ it is partial — $175$ of the $1{,}738$ measurements still fall below $95$%, though the dips below $90$% are reduced twentyfold.
