# Sun et al. 2025 — RL Grokking Recipe: How Does RL Unlock and Transfer New Algorithms in LLMs?

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Yiyou Sun**, **Yuhan Cao**, **Pohao Huang**, **Haoyue Bai**, **Hannaneh Hajishirzi**, **Nouha Dziri**, **Dawn Song** (UC Berkeley; University of Wisconsin, Madison; University of Washington; Ai2).

arXiv [2509.21016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21016), September 2025 (v2 October 2025), 32 pages, 11 figures, 2 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 22, in the corpus — 0. The first carry-over in the corpus of the vocabulary of grokking into the RL fine-tuning of large language models: on the synthetic DELTA suite (Manufactoria puzzles with a DSL of their own, the BouncingSim simulation, competitive programming, SQL, LEAN) it is shown that on families where the base model has pass@128 = 0, a staged GRPO training — a warm-up with a dense reward by the fraction of tests passed, then a binary "all tests passed" reward — gives a long plateau of nearly zero reward and then a sharp jump to a nearly perfect accuracy, which the authors call grokking. The role of the delayed generalisation here is played by a delayed exit from the zero-reward region: there is no memorisation phase, the fraction of full passes is measured on the training data, and no explanation of supervised grokking is applicable to this mechanics (weight decay is switched off together with the KL penalty) — only the shape of the curve coincides.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the word is carried into an RL setting in a way that does not meet the authors' own definition: they define grokking after Power et al. ("the model memorises a small training set and only much later suddenly generalises"), but in the experiments there is no memorisation phase — the fraction of full passes on the *training* data stays near zero throughout the plateau (~450 GRPO steps) and then jumps; the transition happens inside the fitting to the training signal itself, and a delayed rise of the test curve at a saturated training one is shown in no figure ([`p6-9`](#p6-9)). The analysis: a delay of generalisation is nevertheless present, but it is measured by a "before/after" comparison (pass@128 = 0 in the reference model against 100 % full passes after the staged RL) rather than by a curve over time; and of the supervised grokking of the corpus only the shape of the curve remains — there is neither weight decay nor a KL penalty here (`--beta 0.0`), and the transition is driven by the exploration escaping the zero-reward region.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the same three-phase curve (a plateau → a sharp jump → convergence) is reproduced in four settings: Qwen3-4B on Manufactoria-HAS (a jump around step 450) and on a mixture of six BouncingSim families (a jump around step 200 up to 0.7 full passes — under a direct binary reward, with no warm-up), Qwen3-4B on REGEX and Nemotron-14B on HAS ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). The analysis: the transition happens with no regularisation at all — the driving cause is named as the sparsity of the reward (GRPO has no gradient while no rollout is successful), so this is a transition in the mechanics of exploration rather than in a competition between memorisation and generalisation; each curve is a single run with no seeds and no error bars, and the mechanism of the transition is not analysed (no weights, no circuits — only reward curves).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the role of the hidden progress on the plateau is played by the fraction of tests passed: during the warm-up it rises while the binary reward is still zero, and "the model begins to accumulate positive gradients" at a fraction of full passes below 1 % ([`p6-8`](#p6-8)); the limit of the indicator is shown honestly too — on the harder PREPEND family the fraction of tests passed grows modestly, saturates, and the full passes stay at zero throughout the training. The analysis: unlike the supervised progress measures of the corpus (the recovery of circuits, FVE, compression), the progress measure here is simply a second, denser reward for the same task; no internal diagnostics of the model stands behind it.

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — a contribution to the "sharpening or discovery" dispute (Yue et al. and Wu et al. against ProRL): on the Manufactoria-HAS family, where the reference model Qwen3-4B-Instruct gives 0 % full passes at pass@128, the staged RL training brings the model to 100 %, whence the conclusion that RL is capable of discovering strategies the base model did not execute ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). The analysis: the argument rests on an empirical pass@128 = 0 — zero out of 128 samples does not mean a zero support of the distribution, and the Yue/Wu dispute is precisely about the support; the authors themselves admit that in easy regimes RL merely sharpens what is already there, and which regime obtains depends on the reward, the data mixture and the difficulty of the task; and the corpus's neighbour by design (2504.20571, a delayed jump under RLVR on a single example) is not cited, while the conclusions of the two works are opposite.

**[Anti-grokking](../../concepts/anti-grokking.md)** — an observation of a post-convergence collapse independent of the Prakash–Martin line: on Manufactoria-REGEX, continuing the RL training after the curve has levelled off destroys the quality ("RL collapse"), whence the conclusion that a stabilisation or an early stop is needed once the solution has consolidated ([`p31-2`](#p31-2)). The analysis: the phenomenon is shown by a single curve of a single run and is not analysed in any way — no weight norms, no spectral indicators; and no parallel with supervised anti-grokking (the loss of generalisation when training continues long after the grokking) is drawn in the paper, the phrase "grokking collapse" from that line going unmentioned.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — an honest design and honest limits.** DELTA gives controlled families with templated generators and a graded reward; the recipe "a dense reward as a warm-up, a binary one as a fixation" carries the techniques of the grokking-acceleration line over into RL; the limit of the recipe is shown on PREPEND (which does not open up), and the selectivity of the curriculum on the REGEX/COMPR pair (only a structurally related family transfers); and the RL collapse after convergence is a rare honest observation. The before/after example (appendix A.1.1) is the only evidence about the substance of the learned strategy: before the RL an automaton with no restart after a broken partial match, after the RL one with a correct restart.

**"Grokking" here is defined and not fulfilled.** There is no memorisation phase; what is measured is the training curve, not the test one; and a delayed rise of the test set at a saturated training set appears in no figure. Internal inconsistencies: the references to the panels of figure 5 are astray (the warm-up by 5(a), the dynamics by 5(b), while in the appendix the same curve is called "figure 6 (c)" with no panel (c) present); one phase is said to be both "<0.01 %" and "<1 %"; the reference model is called now Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, now Qwen3-4B-Instruct; the sixth BouncingSim family is called ROT_BALL in table 2 against MULTI_OBJ in the text; and HAS is described now as a subsequence, now as a substring.

**The statistics: one curve per claim.** The whole argument about learnability rests on one family (HAS, which by the paper's own ladder is the EASY rung) and one 4B model; appendix C.1 adds three curves — each a single run; "averaged over 4 runs" applies only to the evaluation. The comparison of strategies for acceleration comes with no statement of the control's budget and no rule for choosing the switching point. And the mechanism of the transition is not analysed at all.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that RL causes grokking and takes the model beyond its base") — more precisely: on one family of puzzles and one 4B model it is shown that under a staged reward the fraction of full passes on the training data goes from zero to one in about 450 GRPO steps, while pass@128 changes from 0 to 100 %; the work contains no memorisation phase, no test curve over time and no analysis of the mechanism.

###### p6-3

**Our contribution: a clean testbed and clear evidence for RL enable grokking in LLMs.** Existing evidence in favor of RL’s generalization often comes from large, heterogeneous training corpora. This makes it difficult to isolate why and how RL might discover novel strategies. To address this, DELTA offers a controlled environment: synthetic problem families that are both out-of-distribution (requiring novel strategies) and internally consistent (free of data confounds). We focus on the Manufactoria-HAS family (742 training / 100 test instances), where the reference model *Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507* achieves **0% full pass rate at pass@128**. As shown in Figure 4, our staged RL training strategies enables the model to fully solve this family, achieving 100% full pass rate. Next, we detail how this is made possible.

###### p6-8

**Warm-up phase.** Even though it can not serve as a full surrogate loss, we find that the per-test pass rate can serve as an important warm-up stage that pushes the model out of the all-zero region. As shown in Figure 5(a), this signal allows the model to move beyond the all-zero region: although the full-pass rate remains $<1\%$, the model begins to accumulate positive gradients.

###### p6-9

**Exploration and grokking.** From this warm-up checkpoint, we switch to RL with the binary full-pass reward. Figure 5(b) illustrates the dynamics: For $\sim$450 steps, the model remains in an *exploration phase*, with full-pass rate still $<1$%. After a sudden **grokking moment**, the model discovers the key strategy to solve the family. Training then enters a *convergence phase*, where RL sharpens and consistently reinforces the successful reasoning path. At convergence, the RL-trained model achieves nearly a 100% absolute improvement in pass@k compared to the reference model (Figure 4). We also observe this phenomena with other model families, sizes, and problem domains in Appendix C.1

**[p. 7]**

###### p9-1

**Training dynamics (Fig. 9a).** We again observe a sharp *grokking* phase transition: after a long plateau of near-zero reward, performance on the training mixture jumps around the step 200 to 0.7 full pass rate, indicating the emergence of stable simulation code that handles elastic collisions.

###### p31-2

Panel (a) shows *Qwen3-4B-Instruct* trained on Manufactoria–REGEX. After a long plateau, performance surges and subsequently enters a convergence regime. Continued training eventually triggers an *RL collapse*, highlighting the need for stabilization or early stopping once solutions consolidate. Panel (b) uses the same model on *BouncingSim–All*, a real-world ball-bouncing simulation coding **[p. 32]** suite for real-world coding tasks. The same exploration to phase-transition to a convergence pattern appears. Panel (c) swaps the model family and scale to **Nemotron-14B** on *Manufactoria–HAS*, again reproducing the grokking phenomenon.

Together, these results indicate that grokking is not an artifact of a particular backbone or a single synthetic family. It emerges with different parameter counts, across independent model lineages, and on tasks ranging from symbolic program synthesis to physics-driven simulation code. This supports the view that RL can *discover* new procedural strategies rather than merely sharpening pre-trained ones.
