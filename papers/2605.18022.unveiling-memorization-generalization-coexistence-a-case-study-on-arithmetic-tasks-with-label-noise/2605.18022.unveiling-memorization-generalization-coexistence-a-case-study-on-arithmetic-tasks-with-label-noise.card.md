# Liu, Lu 2026 — Unveiling Memorization–Generalization Coexistence: A Case Study on Arithmetic Tasks with Label Noise

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Linyu Liu** (Taylor Lab, Huawei Technologies), **Pinyan Lu** (Taylor Lab, Huawei; Shanghai University of Finance and Economics).

arXiv [2605.18022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18022), May 2026 (v1), 27 pages, 32 figures (41 files), 3 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The subject is the coexistence of the memorisation of noisy labels with generalisation in one overfitted network: a two-layer model on modular arithmetic ($P=113$, width 17–4097, label noise up to 80%) forms a generalising structure inside itself, but its expression in the output is suppressed by the need to fit the noise. The key instrument is frequency filtering (FF): keeping the dominant frequency of every neuron restores an "almost perfect" test accuracy even at 80% noise, while the remainder carries the memorisation. Plus a double descent in the size (bigger is better in the overfitted region), a counterintuitive order of learning (the noisy labels are memorised before the clean ones under a full batch) and a negative result on a neuron-level partition: the generalising structure is smeared across the network, and a selection of neurons falls far short of FF.

## Concepts covered

**[Label noise and random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — the central claim: even with 80% of the labels corrupted the overfitted model learns the rule *inside* — the subnetwork of the dominant frequency restores an almost perfect test accuracy, whereas the raw output is badly spoiled; and there is a counterintuitive order of learning — the noisy labels are memorised before the clean ones in the very first full-batch epochs, and the gap is the larger the smaller the fraction of noise ([`p6-7`](#p6-7)). Analysis: the number for the test accuracy after FF never appears in the text — only "near-perfect" and a plot; the order "noise before clean" is explained by the gradient (an example of a small noisy subset receives a larger push) and is honestly bounded — under mini-batches it weakens or disappears; and the MNIST table is weak: the noisy accuracy of epoch 1 (10.87%) is indistinguishable from chance with 10 classes, and the conclusion rests on an anomalously low clean accuracy (2.86%).

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — coexistence as a separation in the weights rather than in time: generalisation and memorisation are encoded in the same neurons but at different frequencies — the G part (the dominant frequency) yields the test accuracy, the R part (the remainder) fits the noise and yields almost no test accuracy; the transfer of FF to a transformer: keeping the top 20 frequencies of the unembedding head drops the accuracy on the noisy labels from 100% to 36.8% and raises the test accuracy from 76.8% to 87.8% ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). Analysis: the coexistence is documented only at the final point of training — when the internal rule arises and how it relates to the moment of grokking is shown in no figure, although the introduction invites a temporal picture; the "suppression" of the expression of the rule is claimed but not measured (the decomposition is not additive in the function, and the contributions of the parts to the output are not separated); the configuration of the transformer is not described, and the "top 20" is chosen after the fact.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the generalisation part of the ReLU model is close to Morwani's analytical solution for a quadratic activation: cosines of one frequency per neuron with the phase relation $\varphi^{(a)}+\varphi^{(b)}\approx\varphi^{(c)}$ (for subtraction, with a minus), and after FF every frequency is covered by at least one neuron from a width of 513 — numerically consistent with the analytical bound $M\geq 4(P-1)=448$; replacing an already trained ReLU by a quadratic activation or by a "reverse ReLU" preserves or improves the test accuracy while lowering the accuracy on the noise ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)). Analysis: three independent cross-checks (FF across the board — clean data, a quadratic activation, subtraction; a replacement of the activation; a Phase MSE that grows with the noise) make for an unusually thorough verification; but there is a single seed 42, there are no repetitions and no spreads anywhere, and some of the conclusions are read off differences of curves by eye.

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — reproduced along four axes (activations, a training fraction of 40/50/60%, the optimizers AdamW/Adam/Muon, the tasks): in the overfitted region bigger is better, and strengthening the regularisation shifts the interpolation threshold towards larger sizes; the quadratic and the GeLU activations have ceilings of test accuracy across the whole range of widths, explained by a symmetry of the absorption of the noise; and with a tied first layer ($\boldsymbol{U}=\boldsymbol{V}$) asymmetric noise is not interpolable and the "coexistence phase" does not arise ([`p3-9`](#p3-9)). Analysis: "bigger is better" coexists with the ceilings only if read as "does not decrease"; robustness across "noise-to-training ratios" is claimed, but table 1 has no column for the noise fraction; and the explanations of the ceilings and of the low cross-entropy of the large models are given by argument.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**What is valuable — FF as a cheap instrument and a thorough cross-check.** Frequency filtering separates memorisation from generalisation directly in the weights and is tested across the board: it improves the test accuracy on clean data, breaks through the ceiling of the quadratic activation, works on subtraction and carries over to a transformer; the phase relations and the coverage of the frequencies agree numerically with the analytical solution (a threshold of 513 against $4(P-1)=448$); the negative result on a neuron-level partition is substantive — the structure is distributed rather than localised, and even FF on top of a selected subnetwork does not catch up with FF on the full model; and the proposed task-independent measure Str works on multiplication too, where the IPR is useless.

**No numbers, no seeds, no time.** The main quantitative claim ("a near-perfect test accuracy at 80% noise") has no number in the text; there is a single seed 42; the coexistence is shown only at the final point, so there is no temporal link with the grokking; the selection of $\mathcal{M}^{R}$ in algorithm 1 requires knowing which labels are corrupted (unavailable in practice), and the threshold $\tau$ is not given; and there are traces of an unedited text (a broken item 4 in the contributions, "3. 3." in Result 3.1).

**How the work will be cited** ("the network learns the rule even at 80% label noise") — more precisely: on addition modulo 113, in a two-layer network, by reading the weights with a frequency filter at the final point of training and with no numerical value of the restored accuracy given in the text.
