## Concepts covered

**[Structured representation](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the observation from which the whole work starts: the training dynamics is determined by the embedding of the data. On modular addition at $p=113$ four embeddings are checked — one-hot, binary, Fourier and GPT — and it turns out that the one-hot and the GPT ones give a distinct grokking while the binary and the Fourier ones give a **continuous** progress of the generalisation, with the Fourier embedding reaching memorisation and perfect generalisation simultaneously ([`p4-1`](#p4-1), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)). What separates them is neither generality nor dimension but the presence of information about the task itself. Hence the method **GrokTransfer**: train a small weak model until it groks to a non-trivial (though far from optimal) quality, extract its embedding layer and use it to initialise the embedding of a large target model ([`p1-8`](#p1-8), [`p5-2`](#p5-2)). The technique is deliberately simple and is taken not from an observation of the dynamics but from the explanation: if the embedding decides whether the generalisation will be delayed, then a meaningful embedding need only be **obtained cheaply**. The embedding enters the target model as a low-rank product $A\cdot B$, with both matrices trainable ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)).

**[Grokfast / gradient filtering](../../concepts/gradient-low-pass-filtering.md)** — the corpus's only direct comparison of two acceleration techniques on one setting: on modular addition with a two-layer transformer the time gap of GrokTransfer equals $46$, whereas for GrokFast it is $1119$ ([`p10-2`](#p10-2), [`fig-8`](#fig-8)). The techniques are built differently: GrokFast amplifies the slow component of the gradient, whereas GrokTransfer does not touch the training at all and changes only the initial embedding.

**[The lazy-to-rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the work **argues** with the explanation of grokking through a gap between the kernel regime and the feature-learning one. The argument is twofold: grokking persists even after a careful tuning of the initialisation scale, and the change of the empirical NTK develops almost identically under all four embeddings — those that grok and those that do not ([`p4-2`](#p4-2), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)). Hence the conclusion that the kernel regime may not be the sole cause of grokking.

**[Initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the same argument in the other direction: reducing the initialisation scale speeds grokking up but does not remove it ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). This is a direct objection to the Omnigrok line, in which a large initialisation scale is declared the cause.

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** and **[modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the empirical part is conducted on three tasks: modular addition, modular multiplication and $(40,3)$-parity ([`p9-3`](#p8-6)). The parity task required a three-layer weak model: a two-layer network without biases does not generalise on it at all ([`p9-5`](#p9-1)).

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — the theoretical part is rigorous and narrow. On a high-dimensional XOR-cluster task ($p=80000$, $n=400$, noise $\varepsilon=0.05$) it is proved that under five assumptions (A1)–(A5), after **one** step of gradient descent the target model classifies all the training points correctly and generalises with an error of at most $\exp(-\Omega(\log^{2}n))$ ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)). The key assumption (A2) is not postulated but measured: the ratio of the norms of the complementary and the generalising subnetworks behaves as $\varepsilon/\sqrt{n}$ ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)).

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — why the technique works at all is visible on XOR: a weak network of **three** neurons cannot learn all four features $[\pm 1,\pm 1]$ and sticks at $75$%, yet the three directions it does learn are the feature directions ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). Projecting onto them gives an embedding with a high signal-to-noise ratio — and that suffices. The authors stress that the result is attained **not** in the kernel regime but in the feature-learning one ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)).

**[The sparse subnetwork / lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — a comparison with Minegishi et al.: a lottery ticket also nearly removes the gap, but it can only be obtained by training the same model at the same initialisation **all the way to generalisation** ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). Here, by contrast, the weak model may be much smaller than the target and even of a different architecture.

**[Causal interventions](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the ablation is made over the strength of the weak model: the embeddings are taken at epochs $100,\ldots,2000$, and the quality of the target model turns out to be positively related to the quality of the weak one ([`p9-7`](#p9-3), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)). Hence the authors' working hypothesis: the target model generalises well only after **the weak one has grokked**. The extreme case is checked separately: a weak model with a single neuron gives an embedding under which the task is in principle unsolvable ([`p27-6`](#p27-6), [`fig-10`](#fig-10)).

**[The slingshot](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — the oscillations of the accuracy in the second row of figure 6 are attributed to the slingshot mechanism and to an instability at a large learning rate; the authors do not change their choice of settings on account of them, since both are held to be useful for generalisation ([`p9-4`](#p8-7)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the aim of the work is stated unusually for the corpus: grokking here is not a phenomenon to be explained but **a nuisance to be removed**, since it harms the predictability and the efficiency of the training ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)). The measure of success is accordingly the *time gap* between the first epoch with $95$% on the training set and the first epoch with $95$% on the test set ([`p9-7`](#p9-3)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no explanation of *why* it is the embedding that decides: it is shown that it decides, and a way of exploiting that is exhibited. Nor is there a theory beyond XOR — the authors name this as their first limitation ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the task; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the Fourier solution, from which the Fourier embedding is taken; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the explanation through the initialisation scale, contested here; [Kumar et al. 2024](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — the lazy-to-rich transition, contested here; [Grokfast](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md) — the acceleration technique compared against directly; [Merrill et al. 2023](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md) — the source of the $(q,k)$-parity setting; [Furuta et al. 2024](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md) — pre-grokked models, the nearest predecessor in conception.

**[Accelerating grokking](../../concepts/accelerated-grokking.md)** — the acceleration here is achieved not by changing the dynamics but by replacing the starting point: a meaningful embedding carried over from a weaker model gives a continuous progress from the outset ([`p1-7`](#p1-7)). The motive is named outright — the delay harms the predictability and the efficiency ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)); and it is noted separately that holding the norm on a sphere also speeds things up, at the price of a new hyperparameter ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)).
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is engineering in spirit: one observation, one technique, one theorem and conscientious empirics.

**The observation about the embeddings is the most valuable part.** Four embeddings on one task, everything else equal, give a qualitatively different dynamics ([`fig-2`](#fig-2)). The experiment is cheap, reproducible and discriminating: it shows that "grokking" is a property neither of the task nor of the architecture but of the **representation of the input**. For the corpus this is a strong argument independent of any explanations through norms and subnetworks.

**The objection to the lazy-to-rich line is made carefully but does not close the question.** It is shown that grokking survives a tuning of the initialisation scale and that the change of the empirical NTK is almost identical under grokking and non-grokking embeddings ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). This refutes the claim "the kernel regime is the sole cause" but not the claim "a lazy-to-rich transition occurs": the authors measure one indicator (the Frobenius norm of the difference of pseudo-NTKs) on one task.

**The theorem is strong in form and narrow in scope.** One step of gradient descent — and both the fitting and the generalisation ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)). But this is XOR clusters with two feature directions, with $a$ and $U$ fixed, and five assumptions. That the embedding transfer helps on modular arithmetic too is shown only empirically; the authors put this first among their limitations ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)).

**Assumption (A2) is an exemplary move.** Instead of postulating the value of the ratio of the norms, the authors measure it and show a dependence $\varepsilon/\sqrt{n}$ independent of the dimension $p$ ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)). A theoretical assumption thereby acquires an empirical foundation — a rarity in the corpus.

**"The target model generalises only after the weak one has grokked" is a hypothesis, not a result.** The ablation gives a relation between the quality of the weak model and that of the target ([`p9-7`](#p9-3)), but the turning point is not isolated, no threshold is named, and the relation itself is shown by one plot on one task. The authors do call it a hypothesis.

**The comparison with GrokFast is set up honestly but narrowly.** A time gap of $46$ against $1119$ ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)) is one task, one architecture and one metric. The fivefold gain in time ([`p10-1`](#p10-1), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)) was counted in wall-clock hours on particular hardware; a FLOPs-based estimate is given separately and yields the same order.

**The hyperparameter selection needs to be read attentively.** The settings are chosen by a grid search, and the best is declared to be the one that reaches $90\text{–}99$% on the validation set **fastest** ([`p9-4`](#p8-7)). The work's measure of success is speed; the selection criterion is speed too. The comparison remains meaningful, since it is applied identically to both methods, but the conclusions have less margin than they appear to.

**Negative checks are given, and this does the work credit.** A weak model with a single neuron does not help at all — and why is explained ([`p27-6`](#p27-6)). A randomly initialised low-rank embedding $A\cdot B$ is checked separately, to show that what matters is the transfer rather than the low-rank structure itself ([`fig-15`](#fig-15)).

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the corpus's first work for which grokking is not an object of explanation but a defect. Its contribution is twofold: the observation that the embedding of the input by itself decides whether the generalisation will be delayed, and a cheap technique exploiting that. Along the way it demotes two stock explanations — through the initialisation scale and through the kernel regime — to insufficient ones.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2210-01117
**Omnigrok (2210.01117) — the caveat about inducement.** `"the grokking phenomenon has since been observed in other settings such as learning group operations (Chughtai et al., 2023), sparse parity (Barak et al., 2022), and image classification (Liu et al., 2023)"`. "Observed" presents grokking on images as something met with, whereas in the cited work it is ["induced"](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p1-3) — ["in a slightly non-standard setting where it is relatively weak"](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p1-9). Elsewhere the paper is itself more precise: it names a "relatively large" default initialisation scale as the cause of the delay, with a reference to the same works; the warning concerns the wording of the survey list, not the paper's own position.

###### mc-2309-02390
**Varma et al. (2309.02390) — the sparse/dense subnetwork thesis ascribed to them.** `"Merrill et al. (2023) and Varma et al. (2023) contributed the occurrence of grokking to the competition of sparse (generalizing) and dense (complementary) subnetworks"`. The structural thesis about sparsity is [Merrill et al.](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md), where it is confirmed for sparse parity; in Varma the circuits are ["internal mechanisms… which we loosely call circuits"](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md#p1-6), with no localisation and no claims about sparsity. The group citation ascribes a measured structure to a work that spoke only of efficiency.

###### mc-2301-05217
**Nanda et al. (2301.05217) — "inverse trigonometric functions" and float64.** (1) `"Nanda et al. (2023) reverse-engineered a grokked transformer model for modular addition and reported that the learned algorithm is a composition of trigonometric and inverse trigonometric functions"`: [the target's algorithm is trigonometric identities and a rotation around a circle](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md#sec-3-1); there are no inverse trigonometric functions in the work. The same formula is later repeated almost verbatim in [2511.04760](../2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold/2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold.card.md) — the miscitation propagated by copying. (2) `"Nanda et al. (2023) also suggested to set the precision to be float64 to mitigate the Slingshot phenomenon"`: the word "float64" does not appear in the paper; the recommendation lives in the authors' code and blog, whereas [the paper contains the reverse experiment: lowering the precision induces slingshots](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md#sec-d-2). The target's setup (p=113, the architecture) is meanwhile reproduced correctly.

###### mc-2407-12332
**Mohamadi et al. (2407.12332) — "reducing the initialisation accelerates grokking".** `"They observed that reducing the initialization scale can accelerate grokking and hypothesized that grokking arises from a time gap between the NTK regime and the feature-learning regime"`: in Mohamadi [reducing the initialisation scale mitigates the train-test gap to the point of removing it, but at the price of slower training](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/original/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.md#fig-2) — not "accelerates". The group hypothesis about a regime gap the paper goes on to contest honestly itself: in their runs grokking persists even under a calibrated initialisation ("the kernel regime may not be the sole cause of grokking"). Elsewhere it cites the target accurately and is the only work in the corpus to distinguish correctly the same-named eNTK work by the same authors.
