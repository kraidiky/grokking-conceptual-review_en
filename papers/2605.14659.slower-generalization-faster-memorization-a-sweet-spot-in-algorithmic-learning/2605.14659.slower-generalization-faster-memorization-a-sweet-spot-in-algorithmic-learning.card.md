# So, Lee, No 2026 — Slower Generalization, Faster Memorization: A Sweet Spot in Algorithmic Learning

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Shin So**, **Kyelim Lee**, **Albert No** (Yonsei University; correspondence — albertno@yonsei.ac.kr).

arXiv [2605.14659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14659), May 2026 (v1), 17 pages, 11 figures (16 files). Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The question is posed to the right of the critical-dataset-size threshold: not "when generalisation becomes possible" but "at what dataset size it is reached in the fewest optimiser steps". On the generation of a Needleman–Wunsch matrix (a short input — two strings of length 5, a long linked output — a $6\times 6$ matrix, credit only for an exact match) small transformers reach a strict validation threshold fastest at an *intermediate* dataset size: to the right of the "sweet spot" generalisation stays attainable but takes more steps. In the band where weak validation appears, a growth of $N$ speeds up the fitting of the *training* set — a partly learned rule makes the fit easier. Multiplication and addition (a short output) give no interior optimum; and a random-suffix probe (four bits appended to every target) separates rule-mediated fitting from per-example memorisation.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the dataset size is made a free variable and the time is measured to an accuracy threshold: a sweep over $N$ shows in one timing diagram delayed generalisation (small $N$), an almost synchronous convergence of the training and the validation, and a post-threshold slowdown (large $N$); it is presented as a refinement of the classical picture rather than a dispute with it ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). Analysis: there is an internal tension — "it refines rather than contradicts" is claimed, and at once the main evidence is said to be that the model does *not* finish memorising before generalisation begins, which is precisely a denial of the sequential picture; the "memorisation" of the title conflicts with §4.3, where the acceleration of the fit is explained by its not being per-example; and the gap $I_{V}-I_{T}$ is nowhere plotted as a quantity — the cheapest thing that could have been taken.

**[Data fraction / the critical size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the main conclusion: the critical size $N_{c}(\tau)$ (when validation becomes attainable at all) and the speed-optimal size $N^{\star}(\tau)=\arg\min_{N}I_{V}(\tau;N)$ are different quantities, and in the observed sweep $N_{c}(\tau)<N^{\star}(\tau)<N_{\max}$: the fastest generaliser is not the largest dataset ([`p4-10`](#p4-10)). Analysis: there is not a single number — neither $N^{\star}$, nor $N_{c}$, nor a ratio of times appears in the text, and the main conclusion exists only as a reading off the figures; the non-monotonicity is nowhere tested statistically (the main-text figures carry no error bands); a normalisation to epochs restores a monotone picture, and the decomposition "steps = epochs $\times N/160$" — the one check that would tell a substantive phenomenon from the arithmetic of an unchanged batch — is not carried out; and the post-threshold expectation is attributed to the critical-size works as a blanket list with no place named, so the opponent is partly a straw man.

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — a random-suffix probe as a cheap separator: four random bits not derivable from the input are appended to every target; the "length" view predicts that a short suffix is easier to fit — the opposite is observed: at large $N$ the matrix of 36 linked cells is fitted *before* the four bits, while at small $N$ both grow at the same rate at a zero validation accuracy; the gap measure $G=A_{T}-A_{R}$ becomes positive exactly when the validation begins to rise, and on the early stretch $A_{T}\approx A_{R}+A_{V}$ ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). Analysis: the chance level for $A_{R}$ (1/16) is nowhere named, although $A_{R}$ serves as the subtrahend; the probe changes the task (the target = the matrix plus the bits), so its runs are not the same as those of the main sweep, and the transfer of the mechanism is an assumption; and the decomposition is presented only for the early stage, which the authors themselves state.

**[The canonical testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — a bench new to the corpus: the generation of a dynamic-programming table, in which a short input induces a long mutually linked output (the universe is finite, $4^{10}$ pairs; the row-wise serialisation is consistent with the recurrence); the authors predicted before the check that an interior optimum is to be expected where there is both a reusable rule and a large, compound output, and confirmed it on a control: NW yes, addition and multiplication no ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)). Analysis: the control across tasks is not aligned by coverage of the universe (NW at most ~9.5%, multiplication ~a half, addition a vanishing fraction), and the tasks differ at once in the complexity of the rule, the length of the output and the coverage — "it is about the length of the output" does not follow unambiguously; neither code nor data is released, and the compute accounting does not add up internally; and the literature on DP-table generation and on neural algorithmic reasoning is not cited at all.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**What is valuable — the separation of two sizes and the suffix probe.** The question "what happens to the right of the critical dataset size" has hardly been posed by the corpus; the apparatus $I_{T}(\tau;N)$, $I_{V}(\tau;N)$ is a natural generalisation of the grokking gap to the dataset-size axis; two predictions were made before the experiment and confirmed (the domain of the mechanism — fig. 5; the "length" prediction of the suffix probe refuted in the predicted direction); and the censoring and the step-wise (not epoch-wise) nature of the conclusion are stated honestly, including the admission that a normalisation to epochs restores a monotone picture.

**A conclusion without numbers and without statistics.** Not a single number in the text: neither $N^{\star}$, nor $N_{c}$, nor a ratio of times — everything is read off the figures; the non-monotonicity is not tested statistically at five seeds, and the error bands are only in the appendix; the whole extent of the phenomenon is about a decade (times between ~3k and ~30k steps at a budget of 50k); the decomposition of the optimum into "epochs $\times$ coverage" is not done; and reproduction is hampered — the code and the data are not released, and the GPU-hour accounting does not add up.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that more data slows generalisation / grokking") — more precisely: on one synthetic task of generating a dynamic-programming table, at an unchanged batch of 160 and a budget of 50,000 steps, the time to the threshold *in steps* is non-monotone in $N$, whereas the same time *in epochs* falls monotonically.
