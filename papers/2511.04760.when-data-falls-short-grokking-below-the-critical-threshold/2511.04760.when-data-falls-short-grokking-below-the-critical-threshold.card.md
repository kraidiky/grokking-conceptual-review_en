## Concepts covered

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the work's main notion and its main target. The threshold is taken from the corpus (Varma et al., Zhu et al.) and confirmed by the authors' own experiments: at 25 % of the data and below there is no grokking at all, even at twice the number of iterations ([`p3-3`](#p3-3), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). The claim, however, is that the threshold is **not absolute**: distilling knowledge from a model that has already grokked yields generalisation at 20 % of the data ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — considered not as a phenomenon requiring explanation but as an **asset** that can be transferred. The question is posed outright: can a grokked model be used to train another model on a different distribution ([`p1-5`](#p1-5))? The answer: yes, and the student both groks and spends fewer steps doing so.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work undertakes to **refute** it as an explanation. Grokking is shown under ordinary Adam without weight decay and at a steadily **growing** weight norm ([`p1-6`](#p1-6), [`p10-1`](#p10-1)). Three refuted claims are named by name: the cleanup era of Nanda et al., the smaller norm spheres of Liu et al. and the circuit efficiency of Varma et al. ([`p10-2`](#p10-2), [`p10-3`](#p10-3)).

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the same objection from another side: the generalising solutions here have a norm **larger** than the initial one, which directly contradicts the picture "the generalising solution lies on a sphere of smaller norm" ([`p10-2`](#p10-2), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)).

**[Numerical stability](../../concepts/numerical-stability-fix.md)** — the explanation of Prieto et al. is adopted: weight decay chiefly mitigates floating-point errors ([`p1-6`](#p1-6)). Their softmax replacement is taken over too: a cross-entropy with StableMax, which by itself cuts the time to grokking to about 6000 iterations against an order of $10^{4}$ ([`p3-3`](#p3-3), [`p3-5`](#p3-5)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the setting: a decoder-only single-layer transformer, the tasks $(a\pm b)\%P$, the input $[a,b,@,P]$ ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)). The distribution shift is introduced by a **change of modulus** at the same sign of the operation: $p_{1}$ is $P=113$ and $p_{2}$ is $P=107$ ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). An essential detail of the method: the distillation is performed over the probability outputs at the **operation** token rather than at the $P$ token, so that operation-level representations are learned rather than modulus-bound ones ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)).

**[Catastrophic forgetting](../../concepts/catastrophic-forgetting.md)** — the third experiment: a model grokked on $p_{1}$ continues its pre-training on $p_{2}$. Without KD the earlier task is forgotten at once and completely; with KD the accuracy on it stays almost flawless while the new one generalises quickly ([`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`p5-5`](#p5-5)).

**[Transferring embeddings from a weaker model](../../concepts/freezing-subnetwork.md)** — the nearest rival is named in the survey: the transfer of embeddings from a weaker model to a stronger one (Xu et al., 2025) ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). The difference of the authors' own method is claimed thus: it removes the phase transition with no additional data and no excessive pre-training ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)).

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the concluding reading: since neither weight decay nor the weight norm is needed, the decisive role is played by the **transfer of representations** through KD ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). This is a claim about a cause, and it is not checked in the work.

**[Semi-grokking](../../concepts/semi-grokking.md)** and **[ungrokking](../../concepts/ungrokking.md)** — named in the survey as what happens below the threshold in Varma et al. ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)); the present work shows that under KD a full generalisation, not a semi-grokking, sets in below the threshold.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)**, **[the Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)**, **[the slingshot](../../concepts/slingshot.md)**, **[phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — listed in the survey as the existing explanations ([`p2-2`](#p2-2), [`p2-3`](#p2-3)), but the proposed picture is compared with none of them.

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no analysis of **what exactly** is transferred in the distillation: the claim about a transfer of representations is made in the conclusion and is supported by no measurement ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). There is no number of runs and no spread bands in any figure. Nor is there a check beyond modular addition and subtraction: all the experiments are on two operations and two prime moduli ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)).

**[Distribution shift](../../concepts/distribution-shift.md)** — the shift becomes a condition of the problem rather than an obstacle: a grokked teacher trains a student on a different distribution ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)). The setting is chosen in the scarce-data regime, below the critical threshold, where grokking is unobservable on its own ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)).
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is short and wholly experimental: three settings, not a single theorem, ten pages.

**The main result is simple and checkable: the threshold moves.** Without KD, at 20 % of the data nothing happens in 30 000 iterations; with KD the generalisation sets in quickly ([`p5-1`](#p5-1), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). This is not an explanation of grokking but a demonstration that the "critical dataset size" is a property of the **pair** "task — source of the training signal" rather than of the task alone.

**The objection about weight decay carries weight and is supported from two sides.** Grokking is obtained under Adam without weight decay, and the weight norm rises rather than falls ([`p10-1`](#p10-1), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)). Three refuted pictures are named by name, and each is refuted substantively: if the norm rises, then neither "cleanup", nor "a smaller sphere", nor "efficiency through a smaller norm" can be necessary.

**The refutation is nevertheless narrower than it sounds.** What is shown is that a decay of the norm is not **necessary** when training **through distillation**; no experiment checks whether it is needed when training from scratch above the threshold, where those theories were formulated. It also matters that the student receives its signal from a teacher that itself grokked at 35 % of the data — it may be that the necessary "cleanup" has already happened in the teacher.

**The strongest experiment is the third, with the combined distribution.** The large model $f_{M}$ does not generalise under joint training on $(p_{1},p_{2})$ if the data of at least one distribution are scarce, but it does generalise when trained **by distillation alone** from two small grokked models ([`p5-2`](#p5-2), [`p5-3`](#p5-3)). This is hard to explain by anything other than a transfer of the structure of the solution: the true labels are the same and the outcome is different.

**The claim about the cause is made in the conclusion and is not checked.** "A more decisive role is played by mechanisms such as the transfer of representations through KD" ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)) — plausible, but the work contains not a single measurement of representations: no spectra, no Fourier analysis of the embeddings, no comparison of the internal geometry of the teacher and the student. Yet it is precisely such a measurement that would distinguish a transfer of representations from a plain smoothing of the labels.

**Three benefits of KD are enumerated from other works rather than measured here.** Label smoothing as a regularisation, the introduction of relations between classes, and a correction of the gradients by the difficulty of an example ([`p4-1`](#p4-1)) — all of this is taken from the distillation literature. Which of the three operates in grokking is not established; the simple check (distillation from a **non**-grokked teacher) the work does not carry out.

**The distribution shift here is very mild.** $p_{1}$ and $p_{2}$ differ only in the prime modulus at one and the same operation ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)). This is a legitimate setting, but the result cannot be carried over to "distribution shift" in the usual sense: the teacher and the student solve one and the same algebraic problem in different fields.

**The continual pre-training is shown convincingly, but the measurements are narrow.** Without KD the earlier task is forgotten almost instantly, with KD it is retained ([`p5-5`](#p5-5)). What is measured, however, is only the accuracy on two tasks; not a single forgetting measure from the continual-learning literature is given.

**The reproducibility setup is middling.** The hyperparameters are named ($\gamma=1e{-}3$, $\lambda=1$, a batch of 2048, 15–30 thousand epochs, a V100) ([`p3-10`](#p3-10)), but the number of runs is not stated, there are no spread bands, and there is no link to code in the paper at all.

**An observation worth remembering separately.** With data below the critical size, in the continual pre-training setting a sudden transition from $\sim 92\%$ to $100\%$ accuracy is noticed around 28 thousand steps ([`fig-6`](#fig-6)) — that is, the grokking does not disappear entirely but shifts and shrinks. The work notes this in passing and does not analyse it.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the wiki's only work in which a grokked model serves as a **source** of grokking for another, and the only one in which the critical dataset size is circumvented rather than explained. The objection about the weight norm is valuable too: it rests not on an argument but on a direct observation of a growing norm at a successful generalisation. The price: a very mild distribution shift, an absence of measurements of representations under a claim precisely about their transfer, an absence of the number of runs, and a complete absence of theory.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2309-02390
**Varma et al. (2309.02390) — a numerical threshold and "semi-grokking below the threshold".** (1) `"For algorithmic addition and subtraction tasks, prior studies define the critical data size to be around 25% of the dataset (Varma et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 2024)"`: Varma did not define it as a fraction — their semi-grokking range of 1500–2050 examples out of 12769 gives about 12–16 per cent, the threshold depends on the seed, and the authors themselves call the concept ["an oversimplified notion"](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md#sec-a-2); the number 25 was carried over onto Varma from another work. (2) `"Training below this threshold yields semi-grokking, and fine-tuning grokked models on such small data can cause "ungrokking.""`: in Varma semi-grokking lives [in the vicinity of the critical size](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md#sec-5-3), whereas below the threshold there is simply no generalisation; the second half of the phrase — about ungrokking — is accurate. Elsewhere the paper cites the target flawlessly: the definition of the critical size as the minimum of data for generalisation.
