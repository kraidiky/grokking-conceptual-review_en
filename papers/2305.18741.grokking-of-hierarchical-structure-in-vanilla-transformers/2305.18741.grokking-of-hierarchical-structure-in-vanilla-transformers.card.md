## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work carries the name and frame of Power et al. 2022 over from algorithmic tasks to linguistic ones and gives the corpus a new term: *structural grokking* ([p1-3](#p1-3), "by analogy to existing findings on simple classification tasks"). It is presented as a hypothesis checked by experiment: [p2-4](#p2-4) says outright "We hypothesize a similar *structural grokking*", and [fig. 2](#fig-2) shows that on all three datasets the accuracy on the generalisation set goes on rising for many thousands of steps after the in-domain validation accuracy has saturated, in places approaching unity ([p2-7](#p2-7)). It matters what the work does not contain: grokking in the original sense — a delay on held-out data of the **same** distribution — is not shown here; the in-domain validation accuracy of every run reaches nearly 100 % early and stays there, and what is delayed is the generalisation to a deliberately constructed out-of-distribution set ([p2-3](#p2-3)). Nor is there any mechanism of the phenomenon or any intervention accelerating or abolishing it: the one lever found is the depth of the model, and it is not explained. Nor is it checked whether the deep models simply grok later than the 300 thousand steps allotted ([p2-6](#p2-6)).

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work re-marks what is to count as "memorisation": in the conclusion the training is described as a gradual shift "from memorization (high in-domain accuracy, poor out-of-domain accuracy) to generalization" ([p4-7](#p4-7)). That is, what is called memorisation here is the acquisition of the non-hierarchical (linear) rule, which explains the training set exactly as well as the hierarchical one does — rather than the memorisation of individual examples. The corpus's usual plateau — a low validation accuracy at a nearly perfect training accuracy — is absent from the plots altogether; in its place [fig. 2](#fig-2) shows a gap between the in-domain and the generalisation accuracy. The training loss, as appendix D shows, reaches a shelf at around 10 thousand steps — long before any transition ([p7-11](#p7-11), [fig. 5](#fig-5)) — and no "protracted plateau of the training loss" is observed in the work. The authors apply the word "plateaued" to performance on the training distribution ([p1-3](#p1-3)) rather than to an internal state of the network; the phase the corpus calls memorisation they do not isolate, and they draw no boundaries between phases.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the work gives a linguistic rendering of the formula of Liu et al. 2022 and does so explicitly: "Liu et al. 2022 note that, on algorithmic tasks, grokking “coincides with the emergence of structure in embeddings”. Similarly, for language tasks, we find that structural grokking coincides with the emergence of tree structured internal computations" ([p4-5](#p4-5)). Structure here is not the geometry of the embeddings but the tree-likeness of the computation itself: the tree-projection measure $t_{\text{score}}$ of Murty et al. 2023 says how closely a transformer's internal computation is approximated by a tree-structured encoder ([p4-3](#p4-3), [p7-7](#p7-7)). A negative observation valuable to the corpus stands beside it: the **fidelity** of the extracted trees to the reference syntax ($t_{\text{parseval}}$) promises nothing about generalisation — by it all the models converge to the syntactic annotation, those that generalise and those that do not ([p4-6](#p4-6), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). The words "coincides in time" are the limit of what is shown here: causality was not checked, and there is no intervention in the work (strengthening the tree-likeness and obtaining grokking where there was none).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work poses exactly the question this card is concerned with — "can we identify when it has happened (or predict when it will occur)?" ([p3-3](#p3-3)) — and sweeps three internal quantities, computing them every 3 thousand update steps ([p4-4](#p4-4)). It never uses the term "progress measures", though, and Nanda et al. 2023 are not cited in it. The answer obtained is discriminative rather than temporal: the tree-likeness singles out the **suitable depth**, not the moment of the transition ([p4-5](#p4-5), [fig. 3b](#fig-3)); not one of the three quantities is presented as a precursor of the jump in time. The authors cannot be credited with a progress measure — but neither can one fail to notice that two quantities the corpus considered candidates for such measures are rejected here by direct experiment.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work gives the corpus a distinct counterexample to norm-oriented explanations on linguistic material: the weight norms grow in every setting of both datasets, but do not distinguish the successful depths from the unsuccessful ones, and the shallowest models give the **largest** weight norm while never generalising hierarchically ([p4-5](#p4-5), [fig. 3b](#fig-3)). The norm is measured divided by the number of layers, so that different depths may be compared ([p4-4](#p4-4)). What the work does not contain: motion along a zero-loss manifold, a layer-by-layer analysis of the norm, a dependence of the transition time on the norm — the norm here is only a measured feature, rejected as a discriminator, rather than a mechanism.

**[The Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — the notion is named in the survey of related quantities: "Liu et al. 2022 identify a “goldilocks zone” in weight norm space where grokking occurs" ([p4-1](#p4-1)). The mention goes no further: the zone is not sought in the work, no boundaries in weight-norm space are drawn, and the whole subsequent analysis consists in the weight norm growing monotonically and being unfit to distinguish successful architectures ([p4-5](#p4-5)). The notion is used here rather than checked.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the work takes from Merrill et al. 2021 the chain "a growth of the weight norm leads to a saturation of attention" and measures its end: attention sparsity is defined as the negative mean entropy of all the attention distributions $\lbrace\mathbf{a}^{p}_{k}\rbrace$ ([p4-2](#p4-2)). The chain is reproduced — the sparsity grows in every model — but its predictive power for hierarchical generalisation is refuted: the shallowest models give the sparsest solutions and do not generalise ([p4-5](#p4-5)). Individual heads are not analysed in the work: neither which head routes what nor any head ablations are here — a single scalar mean over all the attention distributions of a four-head model is measured ([p7-4](#p7-4)).

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work leaves the proving ground entirely and deliberately. There is no binary operation table; there is no fraction of training data as a control quantity; the control quantity is the **depth** of the model ([p3-2](#p3-2)). The setting is inherited not from Power et al. but from McCoy et al. 2020: the training portion is ambiguous — it is equally explicable by a hierarchical and a non-hierarchical rule — while the check is run on a separate out-of-distribution set that only a learner of the hierarchical rule passes ([p2-3](#p2-3), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). From the proving ground of Power et al. only the name of the phenomenon and the idea that training must be continued past saturation are borrowed.

**[Emergence / emergent abilities](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — the word occurs in the paper only as a turn of phrase when related work is retold: "emergent linguistic structure" in Merrill et al. 2022 ([p4-2](#p4-2)) and "emergent hierarchical structure in transformers" in the statement of the question ([p3-3](#p3-3)). The work studies no jumps in model scale, seeks no thresholds, and does not apply the word "emergent" to its own findings: its dependence on depth is not a threshold but an inverted U ([p3-2](#p3-2)).

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — AdamW ($\beta_{1}$: 0.9, $\beta_{2}$: 0.999, $\epsilon$: 1e-7) is set as part of the setting and was not swept ([p7-6](#p7-6)). There is no comparison of optimisers, the role of adaptivity is not discussed, and no result is tied to the choice of AdamW. A gap of substance for the corpus: the weight-decay value in AdamW is nowhere named — even though the weight norm is analysed as one of the three internal features.

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — three values were swept, {1e-4, 5e-5, 1e-5}, and 1e-4 is named the best for all the experiments; in addition there is a linear warm-up from 0 to the final value over 10 thousand update steps ([p7-6](#p7-6)). The work does not measure any dependence of the phenomenon itself on the learning rate — the sweep serves to choose a setting rather than as a subject.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — mentioned once only, in a retelling of Power et al. 2022: "find weight decay to improve grokking speed" ([p4-1](#p4-1)). The work nowhere gives its own weight-decay value, including in appendix B with its full list of optimisation hyperparameters ([p7-6](#p7-6)); there are no experiments with and without regularisation. This work cannot be cited as evidence about the role of weight decay in grokking.

---

**[Systematic generalisation](../../concepts/systematic-generalization.md)** — the construction of the datasets is itself the definition of the notion: the training set is explained equally well by the hierarchical and the non-hierarchical rule, but on structurally novel inputs only the hierarchical one generalises ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)). The dependence on depth turns out to be an inverted U ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)), and earlier negative results are explained by the protocol — an early stopping on in-domain performance ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)).

**[Seed variance and reproducibility](../../concepts/seed-variance-reproducibility.md)** — ten seeds per depth are taken as the minimal discipline for comparing architectures ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)); without that the inverted U is indistinguishable from the spread.
## Points we could not reconcile

**The abstract says "on multiple datasets", the introduction "on two".** One and the same claim about the inverted U-shaped dependence on depth stands in the [abstract](#p1-2) as "On multiple datasets, structural grokking exhibits inverted U-shaped scaling in model depth" and in the [introduction](#p1-4) as "On two datasets, we show that structural grokking exhibits inverted U-shaped scaling behavior as a function of model depth". The second is correct: the sweep over depths is run only on Question-Formation and Tense-Inflection ([p3-2](#p3-2), [fig. 3a](#fig-3)); Dyck takes no part in it, nor in the analysis of the internal features ([p4-4](#p4-4)). Citing the abstract doubles the count of datasets: the work has three datasets, and the inverted U is measured on two.

**The label captions on the panels of figure 3b disagree with the curves themselves and with the text.** Above the "weight norm" and "attention sparsity" panels stand the labels "10 layer > 6 layer > 2 layer" (Question-Formation) and "6 layer > 4 layer > 2 layer" (Tense-Inflection), whereas by the figure's common legend (2 layers pink, 4 orange, 6 green, 10 purple) the upper curve on both panels is pink, that is, the two-layer model. The order of the curves is thus the reverse of the label — and agrees not with the label but with the [text](#p4-5): "shallow models learn the sparsest solutions as well as solutions with largest weight norms". On the "tree structuredness" panels the same labels ("6 layer > 10 layer > 2 layer", "4 layer > 6 layer > 2 layer") do match the order of the curves. The labels of the first two panels cannot be read as an ordering of magnitude; when citing figure 3b the text, not the label, is reliable.

**The numbers in the text about early stopping and the gaps in figure 2 do not fully agree.** The [text](#p3-1) gives "average generalization goes up from <40%, <50% to <90%, <80% on Question-Formation and Tense-Inflection, respectively", and the [caption and annotation of figure 2](#fig-2) give the gaps $\Delta$ between the in-domain and the generalisation accuracy: on Question-Formation ≈65 % at early stopping and ≈8 % at the end of training. At an in-domain accuracy of about 100 % the second number gives about 92 %, not "<90 %". The discrepancy is small and lies within the authors' "≈", but when citing exact values one should rely on the figure.

## What is shown, what is measured and what is conjectured

The distinction is noted here because the work's three claims rest on different supports, whereas in citations they merge into one.

**Shown by experiment.** A delayed generalisation on the out-of-distribution set on all three datasets ([fig. 2](#fig-2)); an inverted U in depth on two datasets — the fraction of the ten seeds whose generalisation accuracy eventually passes 80 % ([p3-2](#p3-2), [fig. 3a](#fig-3)); a growth of the weight norm and of the attention sparsity in every setting and their inability to tell successful depths from unsuccessful ones ([p4-5](#p4-5)); a convergence of the extracted trees to the reference syntax in every model, regardless of whether it generalises or not ([p4-6](#p4-6), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). A pair of obvious objections is removed separately: the training losses on Question-Formation reach a shelf at around 10 thousand steps, long before the transition, and all the depths eventually arrive at a comparable steady training loss, so the inverted U is not a consequence of a poor optimisation at the extreme depths ([p7-11](#p7-11), [fig. 5](#fig-5)).

**Measured, but after the fact.** The claim that "the suitable depth can be recognised by the tree-likeness" is presented in the [abstract](#p1-2) predictively — "optimal depth for grokking can be identified using the tree-structuredness metric" — and is obtained by comparison: the suitable depth was already known from the sweep, and the tree-likeness merely turned out to be greatest at it ([p4-5](#p4-5)). The experiment rests on three depths out of five — the shallowest, the suitable one and the deepest of those at which even one seed groks ([p4-4](#p4-4)); the comparability of $t_{\text{score}}$ across different depths is not justified, whereas for the weight norm a correction for the number of layers is deliberately introduced. There is no intervention in the work — strengthening the tree-likeness and obtaining grokking where there was none. The measure, moreover, is their own, from an earlier work by the same authors ([p4-3](#p4-3)), and no independent check of it is performed here.

**Conjectured, and caveated by the authors themselves.** That the findings may have wider implications — "we believe these results may have broader implications" ([p4-7](#p4-7)); that the phenomenon is general — "while we believe this to be a general phenomenon, we leave investigating similar behavior on other datasets for future work" ([p5-3](#p5-3)). The limits are listed in the same place: English only, three datasets only, the influence of the training set size not studied, and all the datasets built from context-free grammars, so that constructing similar checks on real language data is named a task for the future.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus: what exactly is distorted and which of the authors' paragraphs one should return to. The citing works' particular formulations are given here verbatim; separate `mc-*` entries have not yet been created in the cards of the citing works.

**"Grokking is shown on real, natural-language data" (erroneous).** The work is cited as a carry-over of grokking to real data and natural language: Yildirim (2509.17738) writes `"recent work shows that grokking also arises in deeper architectures and real-world datasets (Murty et al., 2023; Humayun et al., 2024)"`; and 2606.26050 has `"Murty et al. 2023 observe it for hierarchical structure in transformers trained on natural language"`. Neither follows from the work. All three datasets are synthetic and built from context-free grammars, two of them taken from McCoy et al. 2020 and the third the language of correctly nested brackets ([p2-5](#p2-5), [p7-2](#p7-2)); the authors themselves write in the [limitations](#p5-3) that "all datasets here are based on context-free grammars" and that constructing similar checks "on real language data is a good avenue for future work". The formulation "in deeper architectures" is besides the opposite of the chief result: deep models here generalise **worse**, and the 12 layers of Mueller et al. are named as one of the causes of the earlier failures ([p3-2](#p3-2), [fig. 3a](#fig-3)). By contrast, exact renderings of the same thesis: in 2606.00230, `"Subsequent work extends grokking to richer language-like synthetic tasks involving hierarchical structure (Murty et al., 2023)"`; in 2310.11282, `"More recent work has suggested that transformers can grok hierarchical linguistic structure after extremely prolonged training (Murty et al. 2023)"` — the second preserving the authors' "suggested" besides.

**"The tree-likeness predicts when a model will grok" (widening).** 2506.05718 says: `"They introduce a “tree-structuredness” (Murty et al., 2022) metric that significantly outperforms weight norm in predicting whether and when a model will grok"`. The first half is correct, the second is not: **when** the work nowhere predicts. The tree-likeness is measured over the course of the training, but what it singles out is not the moment of the transition but the suitable **depth** ([p4-5](#p4-5), [fig. 3b](#fig-3)); about the time of the transition the work says nothing, and the measure is compared with an answer already known from the sweep ([p4-4](#p4-4)). The first part of the same sentence — that the weight norm and the attention sparsity are unreliable — the same work conveys correctly.

**"The work is about compositional generalisation" (erroneous).** In 2602.16967 the work is placed in a list as `"compositional generalization [Murty et al. 2023]"`. The words "compositional generalization" do not appear in the paper; the subject is named "hierarchical generalization" from the first line of the [abstract](#p1-2) to the end, and compositional generalisation the authors reference separately and in the other direction — as an adjacent area in which prolonged training also helps (Csordás et al. 2021, [p4-7](#p4-7)).

Milder echoes of the same drift, not warranting separate analysis: `"NLP (Murty et al., 2023)"` in a list of grokking's domains (2402.16726); `"structural grokking has been shown to occur in language models, where models discover hierarchical sentence structures after extensive training"` (2510.04930) — the models here are trained from scratch on synthetic data rather than being "language models" in the everyday sense; and the assignment of the work to those who `"leveraged the phenomenon to improve model performance"` (2601.09049), whereas it proposes no technique for improving performance at all. An exemplary account of the distinction between classical and structural grokking is given by 2412.04619: `"In classic grokking, the model transitions from memorization to generalization, allowing it to achieve non-trivial performance on unseen data from the same distribution as the train set. In structural grokking, a model transitions from the simple linear rule to the hierarchical rule, allowing non-trivial performance on OOD data."`
