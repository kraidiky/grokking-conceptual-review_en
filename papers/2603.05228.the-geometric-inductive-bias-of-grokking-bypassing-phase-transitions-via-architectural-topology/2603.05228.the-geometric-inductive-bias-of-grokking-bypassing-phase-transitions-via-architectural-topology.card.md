## Concepts covered

**[The spherical norm constraint](../../concepts/spherical-weight-norm-constraint.md)** — the work carries this line to its extreme: a projection operator $\Pi_{S}(x)=x/\max(\Vert x\Vert_{2},\epsilon)$ is applied before every sublayer and immediately after every residual addition, so that the norm of the residual stream is fixed at unity throughout the network ([`p5-6`](#p5-6), [`p5-7`](#p5-7), [`p6-1`](#p6-1)). This alone is not enough: without a normalisation of the unembedding matrix the training inflates the logits, and so the logits are computed as a scaled cosine similarity with a fixed temperature $\tau=10.0$ ([`p6-5`](#p6-5), [`p6-7`](#p6-7)). An essential detail distinguishing the work from nGPT: the constraint is **not compensated for** in any way — no learnable per-dimension multipliers, no separate learning rates ([`p23-9`](#p23-9)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the claim is stronger than the usual "acceleration": under the constrained topology a separable memorisation phase **does not arise at all**, and the training and test accuracies rise together from the initialisation itself ([`p1-2`](#p1-2), [`p2-3`](#p2-3)). The numbers: the baseline models with LayerNorm and RMSNorm grok on average at epochs 54 160 and 51 240, and the constrained sphere at 2400–2480, and at a larger learning rate at 711–820 against the baselines' 7300–7800 ([`tab-1`](#tab-1), [`p9-3`](#p9-3)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the picture of the transition here is not merely shifted in time but disappears: in figure 1 the constrained models converge smoothly and at once, whereas the baselines pass through a protracted plateau with oscillations of the test accuracy ([`p9-4`](#p9-4), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)).

**[The slingshot](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — those oscillations are called by their proper name: alternating gradient spikes and fleeting collapses of the generalisation on the plateau, consistent with the "slingshot effect" of Thilak et al. ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)). It is noted separately that the gradients were deliberately not clipped so as not to obscure this behaviour.

**[Softmax collapse](../../concepts/softmax-collapse.md)** and **[the naive minimisation of the loss](../../concepts/nlm.md)** — the justification of the second half of intervention A. An unconstrained unembedding under a cross-entropy loss drives the magnitude of the logits up, which leads to a naive minimisation of the loss and a softmax collapse ([`p6-5`](#p6-5)); every spherical model required a normalisation of the unembedding for stable training. The converse check is telling too: constraining the unembedding alone under LayerNorm does **not** remove the delay, since the radial freedom remains inside the residual stream ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)).

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — intervention B is independent of the first. The flexibility of the routing through learned query–key interactions ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)) is declared a second superfluous degree of freedom alongside the magnitude of the residual ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)); the pre-softmax attention scores are zeroed, whereupon the attention distribution becomes uniform, $[1/3,1/3,1/3]$, and the attention layer reduces to a continuous-bag-of-words adder ([`p7-3`](#p7-3), [`p7-4`](#p7-4)). The foundation is theoretical: Huang and Li (2025) proved that modular addition is exactly realisable by a uniform sum of the tokens ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)); the same work is named in the survey as having proved an expressiveness gap between sinusoidal activations and ReLU ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). The outcome: a baseline model with LayerNorm and uniform attention reaches 100 % on all 10 seeds, bypassing the plateau entirely ([`p11-4`](#p11-4), [`tab-3`](#tab-3)), whence the conclusion that for a commutative operation a complex routing is strictly unnecessary ([`p12-2`](#p12-2)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the setting is the corpus's customary one: $p=113$, all $p\times p$ pairs, a 30 % training and 70 % test split, a single-layer transformer with $d_{\text{model}}=128$, four heads and an MLP of 512 ([`p8-1`](#p8-1), [`p9-2`](#p9-2)). The acceleration is checked on modular **multiplication** too: 2200 epochs against the baselines' 28 000–31 000, that is, about thirteen times ([`p11-1`](#p11-1), [`tab-2`](#tab-2)).

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the acceleration does not bypass the Fourier solution but arrives at it faster. A causal simplification retaining five principal frequencies preserves over 99 % of the accuracy (99.98 % under LayerNorm, 99.96 % in the fully constrained model) ([`p12-4`](#p12-4)). The fraction of variance explained (FVE), computed against ideal two-dimensional basis functions $\cos(\omega_{k}(a+b))$ and $\sin(\omega_{k}(a+b))$ ([`p13-1`](#p13-1)), reaches 62.55 % and 48.43 % at $\lambda=0.0$ ([`p13-4`](#p13-4)) against no more than 29 % at $\lambda=1.0$ ([`p13-3`](#p13-3)).

**[Causal simplification and intervention](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the methodological contribution the authors put first: not to analyse a trained network after the fact but to change the architecture **before** the training and look at the outcome ([`p2-1`](#p2-1), [`p14-4`](#p14-4)). A negative outcome is meanwhile of equal standing with a positive one: "a probe that always succeeded would be uninstructive; the failure is half the contribution" ([`p23-2`](#p23-2)).

**[Composition in a non-commutative group](../../concepts/group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md)** and **[cosets](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** — the negative control is the work's chief argument. On $S_{5}$ the baseline models grok on average by epoch 40 000, whereas both constrained spherical settings do not generalise on **any** seed within 100 000 epochs, remaining on a memorisation plateau at 100 % training accuracy ([`p13-7`](#p13-7), [`p13-8`](#p13-8), [`tab-4`](#tab-4)). The explanation leans on earlier analyses: successful solutions of $S_{5}$ are built on irreducible representations of higher dimension and on coset circuits rather than on a circular manifold ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)), and the conclusion itself is formulated as a dependence of the bypassing of the memorisation on the agreement of the architectural biases with the symmetry of the task ([`p2-5`](#p2-5)). The appendix adds that an $L_{1}$ normalisation (the geometry of a cross-polytope) does not work on $S_{5}$ either ([`p16-2`](#p16-2)).

**[The clock against the pizza](../../concepts/clock-vs-pizza.md)** — the superfluous degrees of freedom are related directly to a fragmented solution: an unconstrained magnitude permits the space to be divided by norm and disconnected decision regions to be built, characteristic of the "pizza" algorithm, whereas the sphere leaves only angular relations and thereby biases the training towards the "clock" ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`p6-3`](#p6-3)).

**[The norm-separation delay law](../../concepts/norm-separation-delay-law.md)** — the work joins directly with the concurrent work of Truong et al. (2026): the delay decomposes into an optimisation-escape phase, in which weight decay contracts the norms exponentially, and a phase of statistical confirmation ([`p15-1`](#p15-1)), with the norm separation $\Vert\theta_{\mathrm{mem}}\Vert^{2}>\Vert\theta_{\mathrm{post}}\Vert^{2}$ being a necessary condition of any positive delay, and the spherical topology removing the very possibility of building a memorising interpolant of large norm ([`p4-3`](#p4-3), [`p15-2`](#p15-2)). Through the same frame the friction with weight decay is explained too: a network with $\Pi_{S}$ is scale-invariant, so a contraction of the parameters does not increase the loss but does blow up the effective learning rate ([`p15-3`](#p15-3)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — here for the first time in the corpus it turns out to be **harmful**: under the constrained topology and a large learning rate, $\lambda=1.0$ gives a larger seed-to-seed spread (853 against 199) and a twofold slower convergence (1560 against 820 epochs) ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)), and the coherence of the Fourier circuit deteriorates under it ([`p13-3`](#p13-3)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the claimed outcome: a closed loop "interpret the circuits — design the architecture", which works in the reverse direction too. When the circuit is unknown, testing architectural constraints itself reports on the internal structure of the task, and the failure on $S_{5}$ is given as an example of such a report ([`p16-2`](#p16-2)).

**[The manifold and the compression of the representation](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the line of a "slow compression of the representation manifold" is named in the survey among the explanations of the delay ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)), and the work's own conclusion is formulated in the same terms: grokking is a representational rearrangement rather than an inevitable phase transition of the optimisation ([`p18-3`](#p18-3)).

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — an important distinction for the corpus: superfluous degrees of freedom do not merely permit memorisation but make it the **preferred** road; by cutting them away the authors obtain a training with no separable memorisation phase at all ([`p2-2`](#p2-2), [`p18-3`](#p18-3)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no check beyond artificial algorithmic tasks, and this is admitted to be a deliberate choice ([`p18-1`](#p18-1)). Nor is there an explanation of why the baseline models with uniform attention on $S_{5}$ reach 99 % but not 100 % ([`p22-3`](#p22-3)). And there is no analysis of what happens to the spherical topology on tasks with a mixed symmetry — hybrid architectures are named as future work ([`p18-1`](#p18-1)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon and the $S_{5}$ task; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the Fourier circuit to which the architecture is fitted; [Zhong et al. 2023](../../concepts/clock-vs-pizza.md) — the "clock" and the "pizza" (there is as yet no card for the paper in the wiki); [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md) — softmax collapse and the naive minimisation of the loss, on which the constraint of the output rests; [Lee et al. 2024](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md) — acceleration at the level of the optimiser, compared against; [Abramov et al. 2025](../2504.20752.grokking-in-the-wild-data-augmentation-for-real-world-multi-hop-reasoning-with-transformers/2504.20752.grokking-in-the-wild-data-augmentation-for-real-world-multi-hop-reasoning-with-transformers.card.md) — acceleration at the level of the data; [Musat 2026](../2511.01938.the-geometry-of-grokking-norm-minimization-on-the-zero-loss-manifold/2511.01938.the-geometry-of-grokking-norm-minimization-on-the-zero-loss-manifold.card.md) — norm minimisation on the zero-loss manifold, named in the survey.

**[Architectural inductive bias](../../concepts/architectural-inductive-bias.md)** — the work makes it a controllable quantity rather than a background: the topology is changed before the training, and the outcome turns out to be conditional — the delay can be bypassed, but only when the architectural biases agree with the symmetry of the task ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The negative control on $S_5$ ([`p2-5`](#p2-5)) and the reverse line of reasoning ([`p16-2`](#p16-2)) — judging the structure of a task from the properties of a constraint — matter here as much as the positive result.

**[The intrinsic symmetry of the task](../../concepts/intrinsic-task-symmetry.md)** — the symmetry of the task is paired with the architecture: a commutative operation is served by a "bag of tokens" ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)), whereas on a non-commutative one the same intervention fails. Symmetry here is neither measured nor encouraged — it serves as an indicator by which one predicts whether the constraint will work.

**[Grokking time](../../concepts/grokking-time.md)** — shortening the delay is set as a task in its own right, and the work enumerates the earlier approaches to it — data augmentation, the design of the optimiser — so as to set an architectural one against them ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The limiting case here is not "faster" but "there is no delay at all": both curves rise together from the initialisation itself.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is experimental and single-authored: two architectural interventions, one negative control and a spectral check.

**The design of the work is exemplary, and it rests on the negative control.** An acceleration by itself would prove little: any constraint on capacity may help the optimisation. The argument arises from the **difference** — the same constraint that removes the delay on $\mathbb{Z}_ {p}$ destroys the training on $S_{5}$ completely ([`p13-8`](#p13-8)). The author formulates this in advance as a checkable prediction ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)) rather than fitting an explanation after the fact.

**The two interventions are independent, and this matters.** Constraining the magnitude (A) and removing the routing (B) remove the delay **separately**: intervention B works on an ordinary LayerNorm model with no sphere whatever ([`p11-4`](#p11-4)). The delay on modular addition, then, has not one cause but at least two sufficient ones.

**The spectral check answers the main objection.** Without it an "acceleration" might mean that the model had found a different, roundabout solution. Simplification to five frequencies preserves over 99 % of the accuracy ([`p12-4`](#p12-4)), and on modular multiplication over 99.75 % on all the seeds ([`p11-2`](#p11-2)), that is, the accelerated models use the same Fourier circuit. This is the right check, and it is made before anyone demands it.

**The claim that "there is no memorisation phase at all" is stronger than the measurements allow.** The absence of a separable phase is established from the coincidence of the training and test accuracy curves ([`p2-3`](#p2-3), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)), while the smallest measurement step is 200 epochs (the epochs in the tables are multiples of 200). At a convergence within 711 epochs this is rather coarse: a gap of a hundred epochs is not discernible on such a grid. "The phase is shorter than the resolution of the measurement" would be more precise, and in one place the author admits it with the turn "in this experimental setting" ([`p18-3`](#p18-3)).

**The friction with weight decay is explained convincingly, but the explanation is borrowed.** The mechanism — scale invariance plus a blow-up of the effective learning rate ([`p15-3`](#p15-3)) — is known from the literature on normalised networks, and here it is applied to the concurrent work of Truong et al. There is no measurement of the author's own of the effective learning rate or of the angular updates in the paper; the argument remains qualitative.

**The connection with the norm-separation delay law is attractive and unchecked.** It is asserted that the spherical topology collapses $\log(\Vert\theta_{\mathrm{mem}}\Vert^{2}/\Vert\theta_{\mathrm{post}}\Vert^{2})$ ([`p15-2`](#p15-2)), but that ratio itself is nowhere measured. The author honestly caveats that $\Pi_{S}$ constrains the activations rather than the parameter vector ([`p15-2`](#p15-2)) — that is, the argument carries a conclusion from one object over to another with no experimental support.

**The negative control is stronger than the thesis requires, and this is worth saying aloud.** On $S_{5}$ the constrained sphere is not merely slower — it does not generalise at all, and with uniform attention gives 2.4 % over 120 classes, that is, the chance level ([`p22-2`](#p22-2), [`p22-3`](#p22-3)). What follows from this is not "agreement accelerates" but "disagreement breaks", which matters more in practice than the claim made.

**The $S_{5}$ numbers for the baseline models are given over the successful seeds only.** The means of 39 900 and 38 250 epochs are computed without the two failed seeds out of ten in each setting ([`tab-4`](#tab-4)), as the caption says outright. The comparison "the baselines grok, the spherical ones do not" remains correct, but the means are understated.

**The $S_{5}$ experiments with uniform attention are run on five seeds rather than ten** ([`p22-2`](#p22-2)), and this is the one place where the sample size is reduced with no explanation.

**A single-authored work with an acknowledged use of language models.** The acknowledgements say outright that large language models were used for polishing the language, for formatting and for routine programming, and that the author takes responsibility for the content ([`p18-4`](#p18-4)). This is more honest than is customary and deserves mention in the card precisely because such admissions are rare in the corpus.

**Its place in the corpus.** The work moves the question of accelerating grokking from the level of the data (Abramov et al.) and the optimiser (Lee et al., an acceleration of up to fifty times by a low-pass filtering of the gradient, [`p4-1`](#p4-1)) to the level of the architecture, and does so with a checkable prediction: the acceleration arises only when the geometry agrees with the symmetry of the task. The most valuable thing is not the acceleration but the **negative result**: a hard spherical constraint destroys a non-commutative task completely, whence it follows that there can be no single geometric bias for heterogeneous domains. The price: artificial tasks, a single author, a measurement grid of 200 epochs, and a connection with the norm-separation law that has remained an argument.
