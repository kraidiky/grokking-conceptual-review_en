# Manning-Coe, Gliozzi, Stapleton, Hirst, De Tomasi, Bradlyn & Berman 2025 — Grokking vs. Learning: Same features, different encodings

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Dmitry Manning-Coe**, **Jacopo Gliozzi**, **Giuseppe De Tomasi**, **Barry Bradlyn** (Institute for Condensed Matter Theory, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign), **Alexander G. Stapleton**, **Edward Hirst**, **David Berman** (Centre for Theoretical Physics, Queen Mary University of London).

arXiv [2502.01739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01739), February 2025, 36 pages, 34 figures, 1 table, 16 displayed formulas. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 2, in the corpus — 1. The first work in the corpus to compare a model that arrived at generalisation through grokking with a model trained along the ordinary "smooth" road — earlier work compared the pre- and post-grokking states of one run. The answer, on two tasks with features interpretable in advance (the phases of the two-dimensional Ising model; addition modulo 113): the features are the same, the encodings are different, and in a "compressive regime" found here the smooth training compresses five times better than grokking. The development of the model on the plateau turns out to depend on the task: for the Ising model there are no progress measures at all.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — a question is posed that the earlier works did not pose: does a grokked model differ from a smoothly trained one ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)). A separate operational definition is introduced — the grokking time $t_{\text{grok}}=t_{test}-t_{train}$, a run "grokking" if $t_{\text{grok}}>t_{\text{train}}$ ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)); under it grokking and smooth training turn out to be two ends of a continuous tuning of the weight multiplier rather than different phenomena.

**[Initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the load-bearing support of the work: grokking is induced by the Omnigrok recipe, by multiplying the initial weights by $w_{0}$ at unchanged data, architecture and regularisation — and it is this that makes the comparison possible ([`p3-8`](#p3-8)). The passage into grokking is around $w_{0}=3$ for the Ising model and $w_{0}=0.4$ for modular addition.

**[Fourier features](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the measure of the coincidence of the features: both modular-addition models localise in the Fourier basis, each neuron taking the sine and the cosine of one frequency; the mean IPR after Gromov is 0.43 under grokking and 0.53 under smooth training, both about a half ([`p4-3`](#p4-3), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). For the Ising model, in both regimes every neuron of the last layer correlates almost perfectly with the energy of the snapshot ([`p4-1`](#p4-1), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)) — the setting is deliberately tuned to the energy through the domain walls.

**[The sparse subnetwork and the lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the main finding about the encoding: in the smooth-training region of modular addition a "compressive regime" arises — a linear trade between the training loss of the final model and its compressibility under magnitude pruning ([`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)); at a larger weight decay 95 % of the weights are cut out at the price of 5 % of the accuracy — a compression of 25 times relative to the original model and of 5 times relative to what is attainable under grokking ([`p5-6`](#p5-6)). Under grokking itself the compressibility does not depend on $w_{0}$; for the Ising model there is no compressive regime at all ([`p5-7`](#p5-7)); and the regime is fragile — it disappears when the batch is raised from 64 to 200.

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — a direct objection to the line "generalisation as a passage to a more economical encoding": if that were so, grokking ought to compress better — the opposite is shown, smooth training encodes the same features more efficiently ([`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`p8-5`](#p8-5)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the universality of progress measures is refuted on the Ising task: in modular addition the Fourier localisation grows throughout the plateau (in agreement with Nanda et al. and Gromov), whereas for the Ising model all three measures are constant on the plateau — there is no hidden formation of the model ([`p6-4`](#p6-4), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)). Hence a direct question to the corpus: can a measure predicting the jump be built if the features do not develop before the transition ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). FIM measures requiring no manual tuning are also introduced: a spike of $|s|_{FIM}$ marks the period of grokking, and its roots give an independent way of locating grokking without the test accuracy ([`p35-1`](#p35-1)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — information geometry ties two observations together: for the Ising model the trajectory before the transition is almost entirely determined by the weight decay (the cosine proximity to the direction towards the origin is close to unity), and it is precisely there that the features do not develop on the plateau; for modular addition neither holds ([`p8-1`](#p8-1), [`fig-8`](#fig-8)). The compression of 25 times is achieved at a weight decay an order of magnitude larger, $3\times 10^{-4}$ ([`p5-6`](#p5-6)).

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong side — the design of the comparison and the negative results.** The final models of the two roads are compared at unchanged data, architecture and regularisation; the "features" (what has been learned) and the "encoding" (how compressible it is) are separated into distinct axes. Two negative results rare in the literature: the local learning coefficient (LLC) is unrelated to the compressibility, and Fisher pruning works worse than magnitude pruning — even though the Gini coefficient of the Fisher diagonals is higher; the authors call this an unresolved tension. The fragility of the main finding is shown honestly too: the compressive regime disappears at a batch of 200.

**The definition of grokking is the authors' own, and the threshold is arbitrary.** $t_{\text{grok}}>t_{\text{train}}$ coincides with none of the definitions accepted in the corpus; grokking is induced not by a small data fraction (here it is 70 %) but by the weight scale, and there is no direct comparison with the original regime of Power et al.

**"The same features" holds on average but not seed by seed.** Appendix C reports: under grokking three seeds deviate markedly and correlate with the raw magnetisation — grokking turns out to be the noisier road; the caption of fig. 13 meanwhile contradicts both the paper and itself. The coincidence is checked by correlating the pre-activations with a hand-made list of quantities — this is a coincidence of the leading feature, not of the computational circuit. The strongest compression undermines the thesis by itself: in the compressive regime the Fourier localisation falls apart almost completely, that is, the heavily compressed models store features that are no longer the same.

**Bookkeeping mismatches.** The count of the seeds is inconsistent between §3 and the captions of figs. 9–10; the Ising settings differ between sections (a transition at $w_{0}=3$ against "a multiplier of 10 as the representative of grokking"; a batch of 300 against 200); the notations $\tilde{r}$ and $\tilde{IPR}$ are swapped in §6.2; the abstract's claim of a "direct path in the information space" is obtained for the Ising model, whereas for modular addition the proximity is considerably lower. The whole FIM geometry rests on the assumption of diagonality, and the off-diagonal contributions are not estimated.

**The conclusion "grokking ought to be eliminated" is outweighed by the authors' own caveat.** It is drawn from two toy tasks in which the elimination is the tuning of one number; a paragraph below it is admitted that there may exist tasks amenable to grokking alone.

**How the work will be cited** ("grokking and ordinary training give the same models") — more precisely: on two toy tasks the leading feature coincides, but the encoding differs so much that smooth training compresses five times better, and the strongest compression breaks the feature itself.
