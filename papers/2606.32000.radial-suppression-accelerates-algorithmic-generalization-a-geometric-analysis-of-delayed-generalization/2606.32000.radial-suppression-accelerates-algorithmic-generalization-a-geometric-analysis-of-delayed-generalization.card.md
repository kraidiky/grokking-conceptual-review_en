# Tiwari, Chauhan & Singh 2026 — Radial Suppression Accelerates Algorithmic Generalization: A Geometric Analysis of Delayed Generalization

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Srijan Tiwari**, **Aditya Chauhan**, **Manjot Singh** (Indian Institute of Technology Roorkee).

arXiv [2606.32000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.32000), June 2026, 16 pages, 5 figures; formatted to the HiLD 2025 template (the 3rd Workshop on High-dimensional Learning Dynamics). Citations by Semantic Scholar — 1, in the corpus — 1. The cause of the grokking delay is moved from the space of the weights to the space of the activations: the cross-entropy "radially inflates" the hidden representations, driving the logits into the saturation of the softmax (the mechanism of Prieto), while a soft one-parameter penalty $L_{\text{norm}}=\frac{1}{d}(\|h\|_{2}-\sqrt{d})^{2}$ — a Lagrangian relaxation of a Riemannian flow on the hypersphere — turns the training into angular updates and speeds grokking up by as much as $6.3\times$ on an MLP ($1.5\times$ on a transformer, $2.3\times$ on nanoGPT), raising the effective rank from 135 to 443 out of 512 and lowering the Hessian trace by a factor of 30. The evidence is admitted by the authors outright to be correlational; the radius of the activations itself — the central quantity of the mechanism — is shown nowhere in the paper, and the "baseline run" does not grok at all.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — one more grokking accelerator and a rare frame declaring the delay to be "fundamentally a geometric phenomenon" of the activation space: the cross-entropy drives the radial inflation, locking the network in the memorisation basin, and radial suppression is a direct lever for speeding the transition up ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)); the decisive advantage is the low-data regime: at $f_{\text{train}}{=}0.3$ the penalty groks where a strong weight decay does not grok at all ([`p14-1`](#p14-1)). The analysis: the "up to $6\times$" holds only for the MLP (the transformer gives $1.5\times$), the paper's "baseline run" does not grok at all (the speed-up is measured from a strong WD), and the grokking time under the penalty for one and the same setting is given as two incompatible numbers (2,460 and 6,167 epochs).

**[Softmax collapse](../../concepts/softmax-collapse.md)** — the saturation mechanism is taken as the source of the radial inflation: growing $\|h\|_{2}$ is advantageous for the cross-entropy because it drives the logits into the saturating region of the softmax ([`p1-6`](#p1-6)); the intervention of Prieto is carried over from the parameter space ($\perp$Grad) into the activation space and softened from a gradient projection into a penalty in the loss ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)). The analysis: the radius $\|h(t)\|_{2}$ itself is shown nowhere in the paper — the inflation is judged from the fraction of radial gradient energy $\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ and from indirect consequences ($\sigma_{\max}$, the rank, the Hessian trace).

**[The spherical norm constraint](../../concepts/spherical-weight-norm-constraint.md)** — a soft relative of Yıldırım's hard spherical topology, carried over into the activation space: the penalty is "a continuous Lagrangian relaxation" of a Riemannian gradient flow on $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}(\sqrt{d})$, exactly a Riemannian descent on the sphere in the limit $\lambda\to\infty$, and an optimisation within a "thickened sphere" at a finite $\lambda$ ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)); in the taxonomy of interventions the hard projection is assigned to "complete" radial suppression with a collapse of the rank, and the soft penalty to "direct" suppression with the rank preserved ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)). The analysis: the comparison with the hard projection is not backed by experiment — only by a row in the taxonomy; and the relation of the working $\lambda{=}0.05$ to the limiting case is not measured.

**[Fourier features](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the assembly of the circuit as a readout of the accelerator: a Fourier coherence of $R^{2}>0.9$ at epoch 2,460 against 34,200 in the baseline run, and at convergence a block-diagonal per-neuron selectivity with clusters of about ${\sim}10$ neurons per frequency ($512/48\approx 10.7$), the circuit motif of Nanda ([`p12-2`](#p12-2)); a strong weight decay, by contrast, forces a sharp crystallisation of 2–3 principal modes, leaving the high frequencies under-populated ([`p11-3`](#p11-3)). The analysis: the epoch of the coherence is doubled within the paper — 2,460 in table 1 against 4,100 in the caption of figure 4, which refers to that same table.

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — a rare combination: a flattening without a spectral collapse — the Hessian trace falls by a factor of 30 (the normalised sharpness by 45) while the effective rank rises from 135 to 443, which distinguishes the penalty from isotropic regularisers that lower the sharpness by collapsing the spectrum ([`p5-3`](#p5-3)); the appendix adds an argument through the edge of stability: bounding $\|h\|_{2}$ bounds the spectral norm of the weights and keeps $\lambda_{max}$ below $2/\eta$ ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)). The analysis: the threshold $2/\eta$ is never once measured — the conclusion rests on a local linear substitution with the tensor terms discarded.

**[Spectral analysis (SVD/ESD/FVE)](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — an "antagonistic gradient" against rank 1: the implicit bias of the cross-entropy towards low-rank factorisations (via Li et al.) inflates $\sigma_{1}$ exponentially faster than the tail, whereas the strictly radial gradient of the penalty strikes precisely along $\sigma_{1}$, distributing the energy over the tail singular values ([`p9-6`](#p9-6)); the numbers: $\sigma_{\max}$ falls from $>52{,}000$ to $36.5$, and the stable rank rises from 135 to 443 out of 512 ([`p5-3`](#p5-3)). The analysis: the convergence of the Hutchinson estimator is checked only for the penalised model; for the baseline trace of 42.5 there is no such check.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — predictions made before the experiment and a cheap measure of radiality.** Three predictions are written out before the check and confirmed: the suppression of $\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ down to ${\sim}0.15$ against a null level of 1, the speed-up against isotropic regularisers ($6.3\times$ over a strong WD, $6.1\times$ over MAN), and the flattening with a rising rank. The quantity $\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ itself — a cheap measure of the "radiality" of a step, normalised so that the isotropic case gives unity — and the taxonomy of geometric interventions (weight decay, spectral normalisation, LayerNorm, MAN, $\perp$Grad, a projection onto the sphere) are inheritable by the corpus. The low-data result (at $f_{\text{train}}{=}0.3$ the penalty groks where a strong WD does not) and the harmlessness check on Tiny Shakespeare are presented honestly, with a direct admission that outside the target domain there is no speed-up.

**The central quantity is not measured, and the evidence is correlational.** The radius $\|h(t)\|_{2}$ — the very "radial inflation" — is shown nowhere; it is judged from the fraction of gradient energy and from indirect consequences. The authors themselves admit (§B.1, §C.3) that the penalty moves four quantities at once and that the present experiments cannot separate them. Proposition 1 is not a provable statement but a definition plus an observation; and the anisotropy of proposition 2 leans on the non-isotropy of $\Sigma_{x}$, which under the one-hot encoding customary for modular addition degenerates into a scalar one — while the encoding of the input is not stated in the paper at all.

**Numerical inconsistencies.** The grokking time under the penalty for one setting is doubled: 2,460 epochs in tables 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 against 6,167 in table 4 — and it is on table 4 that the conclusion "a LayerNorm without affine parameters gives 80 % of our speed-up" rests. The epoch of the Fourier coherence is doubled (2,460 against the 4,100 in the caption of fig. 4). The headline "up to $6\times$ on MLPs and transformers" does not hold for the transformer ($1.5\times$), and "half as many steps" for nanoGPT is in fact $2.3\times$. The explanation of nanoGPT's modest gain ("an affine LayerNorm already suppresses radially") contradicts table 9, in which it is precisely with an affine LayerNorm that the penalty gives the largest benefit. The training budget is given three different ways (100k/"$>$100k"/50k); the word "Baseline" means now a weak, now a strong WD; and there is no code.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that the cause of delayed generalisation is a radial inflation of the activations") — more precisely: on modular addition and one task of three-digit addition it is shown that a soft penalty on the norm of the activations speeds grokking up while simultaneously shifting four entangled quantities; the radius itself is not measured, and causality is not claimed by the authors outright.

###### p1-6

Neural networks trained on algorithmic tasks exhibit a distinct form of delayed generalization where models achieve near-perfect training accuracy early in optimization, yet test accuracy remains low for orders of magnitude more training before undergoing a sudden phase transition (Power et al., 2022; Barak et al., 2023). Mechanistic studies of modular arithmetic reveal that generalization requires discovering structured low-dimensional circuits (Nanda et al., 2023; Zhong et al., 2023), while the memorizing solution is characterized by unstructured, high-norm representations. We demonstrate that a cause of this is *radial inflation* where standard cross-entropy incentivizes outward growth of hidden activations to push logits into the saturating regime of softmax (Prieto et al., 2025). This inflates the dominant singular value while collapsing effective rank (Wang et al., 2026).

We formalize this intuition through a radial–angular decomposition of activation-space dynamics, and derive three analytical propositions. To test these propositions, we study a activation norm penalty that softly constrains hidden representations to a $\sqrt{d}$- radius hypersphere.

We study this intervention specifically on algorithmic generalization tasks where the generalization phase transition is sharp and measurable. Note that we do not claim universality. Rather, we present the radial–angular framework as a geometric lens for understanding algorithmic phase transitions, and the norm penalty as a principled instrument for understanding this geometry.

###### p2-6

Prieto et al. (2025) identify softmax-driven logit inflation as a grokking bottleneck; their $\perp$Grad projects gradients away from magnitude-scaling directions in *parameter* space. Yıldırım (2026) enforce hard $L_{2}$ projection onto a bounded spherical topology. Our approach provides a *soft*, *loss-based* variant operating in *activation* space.

###### p5-3

Table 1 reports spectral and curvature diagnostics. The penalty achieves a $30\times$ reduction in raw Hessian trace and a $45\times$ reduction in normalized sharpness, confirming the curvature prediction. Spectral compression is equally striking: $\sigma_{\max}$ drops from $>{52{,}000}$ to $36.5$, while effective rank rises from $135$ to $443$ out of $512$ dimensions. This combination—flatter landscape *and* higher rank—distinguishes the norm penalty from isotropic regularizers, which reduce sharpness by collapsing the spectrum rather than by redistributing it.

###### p6-2

Our work suggests that, for algorithmic learning tasks, the memorization–generalization delay is fundamentally a geometric phenomenon: cross-entropy drives radial inflation, trapping networks in memorization basins, and principled radial suppression provides a direct lever to accelerate the phase transition. Whether this geometric lens extends to broader learning settings remains an open and important question.

###### p8-5

The Edge of Stability dictates that gradient descent becomes unstable when $\lambda_{max}>2/\eta$, where $\eta$ is the learning rate. Our norm penalty, $L_{norm}=\frac{1}{d}(\|h\|_{2}-\sqrt{d})^{2}$, introduces an opposing restoring force. By locking $\|h\|_{2}\approx\sqrt{d}$, we artificially bound the spectral norm of $W$. Because $\lambda_{max}$ is a function of this bounded weight norm, the local sharpness is forcibly held below the $2/\eta$ threshold, explaining the dramatic reduction in Hessian trace observed in our experiments.

###### p8-6

**Proposition 2.** *The activation norm penalty acts as a continuous Lagrangian relaxation of a Riemannian gradient flow on the hypersphere $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}(\sqrt{d})$, where the penalty multiplier $\lambda$ dictates the stiffness of the manifold retraction.*

**Derivation.** Consider the continuous-time gradient flow of the activations $\dot{h}=-\nabla_{h}L_{total}$, where the total objective is $L_{total}=L_{CE}(h)+\frac{\lambda}{d}(\|h\|_{2}-\sqrt{d})^{2}$. The gradient evaluates to:

$$
\nabla_{h}L_{total}=\nabla_{h}L_{CE}+\frac{2\lambda}{d}\left(\frac{\|h\|_{2}-\sqrt{d}}{\|h\|_{2}}\right)h \tag{7}
$$

Let $P_{r}=\frac{hh^{T}}{\|h\|_{2}^{2}}$ be the radial projection matrix. We decompose the continuous flow into radial ($\dot{h}_{rad}$) and tangential ($\dot{h}_{tan}$) components:

$$
\dot{h}_{rad} =-P_{r}\nabla_{h}L_{CE}-\frac{2\lambda}{d}(\|h\|_{2}-\sqrt{d})\frac{h}{\|h\|_{2}} \tag{8}
$$

$$
\dot{h}_{tan} =-(I-P_{r})\nabla_{h}L_{CE} \tag{9}
$$

In the asymptotic limit as $\lambda\to\infty$, the penalty strictly dominates the radial dynamics, correcting any deviation from the target radius infinitely fast. Thus, $\dot{h}_{rad}\to 0$ and $\|h\|_{2}\to\sqrt{d}$. **[p. 9]** In this limit, the optimization trajectory reduces exactly to:

$$
\dot{h}=-(I-P_{r})\nabla_{h}L_{CE} \tag{10}
$$

This equation is the exact formulation of Riemannian gradient descent restricted to the manifold $\mathbb{S}^{d-1}(\sqrt{d})$ Absil et al. (2008). Because we operate at a finite $\lambda$, our method avoids the brittleness of hard retraction mappings, instead optimizing within a “thickened sphere” while maintaining the favorable angular dynamics characteristic of Riemannian optimization.

###### p9-4

where $\sigma_{i}$ are the singular values. Gradient flow on separable data without explicit regularization invariably converges to max-margin solutions, functioning as an implicit bias toward low-rank factorizations Li et al. (2020). Specifically, $L_{CE}$ inflates the dominant singular value $\sigma_{1}$ exponentially faster than the tail, driving $s(A)\to 1$.

Applying the norm penalty without a stop-gradient introduces a structural gradient conflict. The primary task attempts to maximize $\sigma_{1}$ to increase logit margins. Simultaneously, the radial gradient of the norm penalty, $-\frac{2\lambda}{d}(\|h\|_{2}-\sqrt{d})\frac{h}{\|h\|_{2}}$, aggressively pulls back the representation vector.

###### p9-6

Because this opposing gradient is strictly radial, it exerts its maximal suppressive force exactly along the direction of $\sigma_{1}$. By actively bounding $\sigma_{1}$ via this continuous “antagonistic regularization,” the optimization energy is distributed across the trailing singular values ($\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{k}$). This formally explains the empirical preservation of stable rank, ensuring the latent space maintains sufficient effective dimensionality for complex $\mathcal{O}(d)$ Fourier features to emerge prior to the phase transition.

###### p11-3

Figure 4 reveals qualitatively different assembly dynamics across conditions. The baseline accumulates weak frequency traces that never reach the coherence threshold of $R^{2}{>}0.9$ reported in Table 1. Strong weight decay forces a sudden crystallization but concentrates capacity into a sparse subset of 2–3 dominant frequencies, consistent with the low effective rank (402) and high $\sigma_{\max}$ reported in Table 2. The norm penalty, by contrast, produces a distributed assembly: frequencies emerge sequentially beginning from low-$k$ modes, spreading representational energy across many orthogonal directions. This distributed encoding is mechanistically consistent with the penalty’s $30\times$ reduction in Hessian trace (Table 1): when information is spread evenly across many frequency channels rather than concentrated in a few, the loss landscape exhibits lower curvature along every direction.

###### p12-2

As shown in Figure 5, the penalty produces a clean block-diagonal structure: coherent clusters of approximately 10 neurons (consistent with $512/48\approx 10.7$) specialize to each Fourier mode, with near-zero selectivity outside their assigned frequency. This neuron-cluster-per-frequency organization is the circuit motif identified by Nanda et al. (2023) as the hallmark of a well-formed modular-arithmetic Fourier circuit. The unpenalized baseline, whose radial inflation suppresses $\tilde{\Phi}_{\mathrm{rad}}$ from the first epoch (Figure 1), shows diffuse correlations with no frequency preference—consistent with the low dominant Fourier magnitude ($|F|_{\max}{=}1.2$) in Table 1. Strong weight decay forces partial cluster formation but concentrates heavily on a few low-$k$ modes and leaves higher frequencies underrepresented, matching the lower effective rank (402 vs. 443) reported in Table 2. Together, Figures 4 and 5 connect the aggregate spectral diagnostics in §4 to a concrete circuit-level picture: radial suppression enables the progressive, distributed assembly of a modular Fourier circuit that would otherwise be blocked by norm-driven spectral collapse.
