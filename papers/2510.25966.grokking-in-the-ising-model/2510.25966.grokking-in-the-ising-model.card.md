# Hutchison & Yevick 2025 — Grokking in the Ising Model

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Karolina Hutchison**, **David Yevick** (the physics department of the University of Waterloo).

arXiv [2510.25966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25966), October 2025, version v2, 17 pages, 20 figures (31 panels), 2 tables, 2 displayed formulas. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 1, in the corpus — 2. The first work to study grokking in an Ising-model setting: a dense network classifies 8×8 spin lattices into four energy bands, and the delayed generalisation is interpreted as a transition from a connected network to a group of sparse subnetworks with a sparsity growing monotonically with depth. It is the third paper of the Waterloo line — alongside [2411.05353](../2411.05353.controlling-grokking-with-nonlinearity-and-data-symmetry/2411.05353.controlling-grokking-with-nonlinearity-and-data-symmetry.card.md) (the control of the non-linearity) and [2507.11645](../2507.11645.tracing-the-path-to-grokking-embeddings-dropout-and-network-activation/original/2507.11645.tracing-the-path-to-grokking-embeddings-dropout-and-network-activation.md) (harbinger metrics). ## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work extends the testbed of the phenomenon to a physical problem: the first demonstration of grokking in an Ising-model setting ([`p15-1`](#p15-1)). It matters that the regime here differs from that of modular arithmetic: the ceiling of the test accuracy is about 0.83 rather than unity ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)), and the grokking is not found but manufactured — the initial amplitude and the weight decay are tuned "in order to enable grokking" by the Omnigrok recipe ([`p4-1`](#p4-1)).

**[The sparse subnetwork](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the main measurement of the work: within the grokking window (10000–17000 epochs) the fraction of near-zero weights ($|w|<0.01$) in the inter-layer matrices grows, and grows monotonically with the depth of the layer ([`p9-1`](#p9-1), [`fig-10`](#fig-10)); the transition to a sparse subnetwork itself is attributed to Merrill et al. ("as first reported in [11]"), the authors' own addition being the monotonicity in depth. The control: under the default initialisation and a zero weight decay the sparsity does not change and there is no grokking ([`fig-11`](#fig-11)). There is no intervention: the sparsity is nowhere imposed by force, and the causality is claimed in words.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** and **[the initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — both knobs of Omnigrok are reproduced on Ising data: the delay grows with the initialisation amplitude $c$ and falls with the weight decay $\lambda$; at $\lambda = 0$ there is no generalisation at all ([`p6-1`](#p6-1), [`fig-3`](#fig-3), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). The authors call this outright a repetition of a known dependence on a new task.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — a heuristic frame rather than a derived one: grokking is presented as a "phase transition" from a connected "liquid" state of high entropy into a "solid" sparse network, the optimiser as a deterministic annealing with a "computational heat bath", and dropout as a "thermal motion" leading to a "supercooled liquid" ([`p15-2`](#p15-2)). The authors themselves caveat that the statistical quantities of a network "cannot be unambiguously quantified in contrast to classical statistical mechanics" ([`p16-1`](#p16-1)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — two instruments: the dip of the mean difference of the two largest softmax "probabilities" $\overline{\Delta P} = \overline{P_{max} - P_{second}}$ within the transition window, presented as an analogue of the peak of the specific heat ([`p14-2`](#p14-2), [`fig-19`](#fig-19)), and the number of PCA components accounting for 90 % of the variance of the gradients ([`p12-2`](#p12-2), [`fig-15`](#fig-15)) — the only result the authors themselves call new, and the one interpreted inconsistently (see below).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the claimed measurement contribution: "novel PCA-based network layer analysis techniques" ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)), applied to the gradients ([`fig-14`](#fig-14)) and to the activations of the hidden layers ([`fig-16`](#fig-16)–[`fig-18`](#fig-18)): at a 100 % training accuracy the representations are still structureless, and with the onset of the grokking the activations gather into clusters by class ([`p14-1`](#p14-1)).

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the concluding picture: the last, sparse layers "as in a CNN" successively extract the global features of the input classes, and that is what makes the generalisation possible ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)); this is an analogy, illustrated by the spline frame of Humayun et al. (formulas (1)–(2)), not a derivation from it.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the paper carefully distances itself from the early reading: a fall of the weight norms "does not necessarily induce grokking but may correlate with it" ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)).

**[Vision and real data](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the Ising model is presented as a "generator of images with maximum randomness subject to a given correlation among spins" ([`p1-8`](#p1-8)): grokking is carried over to the classification of configurations treated as images, with a controllable closeness between the training and the test sets ($n$-inverted sets, [`p3-1`](#p3-1)).

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — an inherited dependence: the delay grows as the dataset shrinks ([`p2-2`](#p2-2), a paraphrase of Power et al.); the authors' own axis of data closeness is the fraction of lattices that change class when $n$ spins are inverted (52 % at $n=5$ against 20 % at $n=2$, [`p3-1`](#p3-1)).

**[The compression of representations](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the spline frame ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)): during a transition mediated by weight decay the linear regions migrate towards the decision boundary and the complexity of the remaining regions falls — borrowed from Humayun et al. together with the conclusion about the smoothing of the boundaries ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)).

**[Grokking outside neural networks](../../concepts/grokking-in-non-neural-models.md)** — the task is taken from physics: the Ising model serves as a controllable generator of data with a known correlation structure ([`p1-8`](#p1-8)). The source of the delay is traced to a rearrangement of the network itself — a monotone decrease of the number of active neurons across the layers ([`p1-7`](#p1-7)) — rather than to properties of the data.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The novelty claim is narrow and honest**: the first setting of grokking within an Ising frame; the novelty is of the setting, not of the mechanism. The main experimental result is a reproduction of both Omnigrok dependences (the initialisation amplitude, the weight decay) on spin data, and the authors call it that themselves.

**The strongest place is the control** ([`fig-11`](#fig-11)): under the default settings the sparsity does not change with the epoch and there is no grokking — this ties the growth of the sparsity to the phenomenon rather than to the passage of time. The robustness is checked by substitutions (MSE instead of the cross-entropy, tanh instead of ReLU — stated, not shown).

**A key caveat about the regime**: the grokking here is manufactured by tuning two knobs "in order to enable grokking", and the ceiling of the generalisation is 0.83 rather than unity. This is a materially different regime from the canonical testbed, and the difference matters when comparing with works in which both accuracies run into 100 %.

**The mechanism is an interpretation, not a derivation.** The spline frame (1)–(2) and the argument about the migration of the linear regions are taken over from Humayun et al. in full; the analogy "the late layers as in a CNN" is not derived from the formulas but illustrated by them. There is not a single intervention on the sparsity — a coincidence of windows is shown, and the causality is claimed in words.

**Internal roughness.** The sparsity threshold $|w|<0.01$ is taken without justification and is not varied, while the main curve hangs on it ([`fig-10`](#fig-10)). Repeats are claimed only for the dropout (4 runs), fig. 15 (5 in a row) and fig. 20 (3 computations) — the rest are single runs. Of the loss landscape it is said on p. 12 that it "increases in complexity … resulting in … simplification of the landscape" — a complication and a simplification in one sentence as cause and effect. The dip of $\overline{\Delta P}$ is called "universal physical behavior" on the strength of the paper's own sample. The grokking window in the text is 10000–17000 epochs, while in fig. 19 the marks stand at about 8500 and 15000; the discrepancy is not addressed. The abstract speaks of "active weights", the introduction of "active neurons", as though they were the same thing.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that grokking is a transition to sparse subnetworks") — more precisely: on a single MLP on a single Ising task, with the knobs deliberately tuned for grokking, the fraction of near-zero weights grows with the depth exactly within the delay window; a "group of sparse subnetworks" is an interpretation of that picture, not checked by an intervention.
