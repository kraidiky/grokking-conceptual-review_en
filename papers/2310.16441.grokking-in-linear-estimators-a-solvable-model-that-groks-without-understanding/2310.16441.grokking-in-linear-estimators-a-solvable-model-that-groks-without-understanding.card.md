## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the corpus the cheapest mechanism it knows on which grokking is reproduced: a linear perceptron without biases, MSE loss, Gaussian inputs, a teacher — student task with a perfect teacher ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)). Grokking here is defined operationally and without remainder: it is the state of affairs in which the training loss falls below a threshold $\epsilon$ earlier than the generalization loss does ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)), and since the accuracy turns out to be an explicit function of the loss ([`p5-6`](#p5-6)), the sharpness of the rise in accuracy corresponds to no event in the dynamics — "nothing of any interest happens at this threshold, and the loss dynamics is unaware of its existence" ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)). Hence the title thesis: grokking "does not represent a transition from 'memorization' to 'understanding', nor any other qualitative gain in whatever generalization abilities the network may have" ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), repeated in the discussion — [`p11-2`](#p11-2)); in the abstract the same is given with a hedge — the sharp rise in generalization accuracy "may not signify" such a transition and "may simply be an artefact of the accuracy measure" ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). What the work does not have: neither algorithmic data, nor discrete symbols, nor representation learning, nor non-linearity — and hence no claim to explain the grokking of Power et al.; the authors present not an explanation of the original phenomenon but a demonstration that the curves alone are not enough to conclude anything about "understanding". They do not claim priority for the thesis: grokking is called an artefact of the accuracy measure "as previously reported in [11]", that is, in Omnigrok.

**[The memorization phase / plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work is remarkable in that it has no memorization phase at all. Both losses fall monotonically from the very start, and the gap between the accuracy curves comes not from the network being busy with something on a plateau but from the generalization loss being the norm of the same vector $D=S-T$ in a different metric and therefore falling "somewhat more slowly" ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). The plateau in the accuracy plots ([`fig-1`](#fig-1), centre) is the stretch where the loss is still above $\epsilon$; no substantive difference between "before" and "after" occurs in the weights. For the corpus this is a negative exemplar: an observed accuracy plateau does not prove the existence of a memorization phase.

**[The reference grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — nothing is taken from the testbed of Power et al. but the name of the phenomenon: no binary-operation table, no uninformative symbols, no training fraction as a lever, no transformer. Instead there are $N_{\mathrm{tr}}$ samples from $\mathcal{N}(0,{\boldsymbol{I}})$, a perfect teacher and full-batch gradient descent ([`p3-2`](#p3-2), [`p14-1`](#p14-1)). The one inherited detail is the conversion of regression into classification by a threshold $\epsilon$, and it is taken from Omnigrok rather than from Power et al. ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). Accordingly, none of the work's conclusions has been checked on algorithmic data, and transferability in the reverse direction — from a linear estimator to a transformer — is not claimed.

**[Data fraction / critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the work gives the corpus a continuous analogue of the critical dataset size: a single control parameter $\lambda=d_{\mathrm{in}}/N_{\mathrm{tr}}$, the ratio of the input dimension to the number of training samples, which "mostly determines" the grokking time ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). The separation by $\lambda$ is strict and derived rather than measured: for $\lambda<1$ the Marchenko — Pastur distribution has no zero eigenvalues, both losses go to zero and perfect generalization is always attained; for $\lambda>1$ a fraction $1-\frac{1}{\lambda}$ of zero eigenvalues leaves the generalization loss on a shelf, and perfect generalization is impossible altogether ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)). The grokking time itself is largest near $\lambda\simeq 1$ and vanishes as $\lambda\simeq 0$ ([`p6-3`](#p6-3), [`fig-1`](#fig-1) on the right). What the work does not have: the words "critical dataset size", a threshold in the number of examples as such (the threshold here is in a ratio, not in a volume) and any finite-sample analysis at all — the divergence at $\lambda\to 1$ is a statement about the limit $d_{\mathrm{in}},N_{\mathrm{tr}}\to\infty$, whereas all the experiments run at a single finite $d_{\mathrm{in}}=10^{3}$.

**[Weight decay / L2 regularization](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the corpus's only analytical treatment of how weight decay acts on the grokking time. The technique is simple: $\gamma$ shifts the eigenvalues of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tr}}$ by $\gamma/2$ and adds a term from the teacher ([`p8-1`](#p8-1), [`p8-2`](#p8-2)); the relation between accuracy and loss $\gamma$ does not affect at all, "since $\gamma$ affects only the gradient and not the accuracy" ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)). The conclusion is two-sided and stated as such: weight decay "can either suppress or enhance grokking — depending on the number of network parameters and the number of training samples" ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). In the underdetermined regime ($\lambda<1$) both losses reach non-zero shelves, the generalization one higher, whence the threshold in $\epsilon$: above it weight decay "has no effect whatsoever", below it the grokking time decreases ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)). In the overdetermined regime ($\lambda>1$) the significant time scale of the generalization loss is set by $1/\gamma$, which suppresses grokking — and this is attributed to an observation of Omnigrok rather than the authors' own ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)). What the work does not have: the dependence of the grokking time on $\gamma$ in analytical form — the asymptotic eq. 16 contains no variable $\gamma$ at all, and in the plot ([`fig-3`](#fig-3), on the right) the curves are almost horizontal and bend only at the very border of the "No Grokking" region.

**[When regularization is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work takes the side of "not necessary", and in the strongest form at that: the whole main analysis is carried out at $\gamma=0$ ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)), and grokking is there by construction. The argument is structural rather than empirical: the gradients are set by the training loss, the generalization loss falls at the same rate but with a constant lag ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)), and no norm penalty is required for that. What the work does not have: a comparison of regularized and unregularized runs on one task as an experiment — weight decay enters the analysis as one more term of the equation rather than as an intervention that switches the phenomenon on or off.

**[Effective theory / statistical mechanics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — the work carries a ready-made apparatus into the corpus: random matrix theory and the statistical physics of learning in linear estimators in high dimension, with a direct pedigree from Crisanti, Sompolinsky and Seung, Sompolinsky, Tishby through to Gaussian-covariate models ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). There is one coarse-grained quantity here — the spectrum of the empirical covariance; the macroscopic description is obtained by replacing the sum over the spectrum with an expectation over the Marchenko — Pastur distribution ([`p4-7`](#p4-7)). What the work does not have: an order parameter, a phase diagram in the sense of the physics of phases, and the language of the effective loss of Liu et al. at all — "phase diagram" here means a contour map of the grokking time over $\gamma,d_{\mathrm{out}},\lambda$, built by numerically solving $\mathcal{A}(t^{*})=0.95$ from the authors' own formulas ([`p9-2`](#p9-2), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)), that is, a map of the theory rather than a measurement.

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — a rare case for the corpus in which the delayed generalization is read off the spectrum of the **data** rather than of the weights: the whole behaviour is determined by the eigenvalues $\nu_{i}$ of the Gram matrix of the training set, obeying the Marchenko — Pastur distribution with edges $\lambda_{\pm}=(1\pm\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}$ ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)). The lower edge $\lambda_{-}$ sets both quantities in the main formula: the rate of the late decay is $4\eta_{0}\lambda_{-}$, and the constant ratio of the losses is $1/\lambda_{-}$ ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). Spectra of weight matrices, ESD, SVD and the fraction of explained variance are absent from the work: the network is linear, and its only matrix enters the analysis solely through the difference $D=S-T$.

**[Learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — $\eta_{0}$ enters all the results as a common time factor: $\Delta t_{\mathrm{grok}}\propto 1/\eta_{0}$ ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)), and it is itself rescaled by the network architecture — $\eta_{0}\to\eta_{0}/d_{\mathrm{out}}$ at an output dimension greater than one ([`p7-6`](#p7-6)) and $\eta_{0}\to\eta_{0}/(2d_{\mathrm{out}}^{2})$ in a two-layer network with a frozen kernel ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)). That is, all the "architectural" lengthenings of grokking in the work reduce to an effectively smaller learning rate. What the work does not have: experiments at different $\eta_{0}$ — all the runs are taken at $\eta=\eta_{0}=0.01$, and the analysis itself is carried out in the gradient-flow limit, where large steps are excluded by construction; the large-learning-rate regime is named as a direction for future work ([`p11-3`](#p11-3)).

**[Initialization scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — here this lever is removed rather than reproduced. Rescaling the student's weights $S\to\alpha S$ gives "a trivial rescaling of both loss functions" $\mathcal{L}\to\frac{1+\alpha^{2}}{2}\mathcal{L}$, which "is equivalent to choosing a different threshold parameter $\epsilon\to\frac{2\epsilon}{1+\alpha^{2}}$ and leaves the results unchanged" ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)). For the corpus this is a direct narrowing of the Omnigrok picture, in which the initialization scale is a lever in its own right: in the linear model it is no more than a redefinition of the threshold. A caveat: the list of contributions asserts the opposite — that initialization "raises the effective accuracy value required for saturation and therefore increases the grokking time" ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)); the discrepancy is examined below, in the section on inconsistencies.

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — the work gives a prediction and does not test it: under $y\to y+\delta$, $\delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\delta}^{2})$ the student's dynamics does not change, while the generalization loss acquires a constant addition proportional to $\sigma^{2}_{\delta}$, whence at small $\epsilon$ "grokking to perfect generalization cannot occur — only to some finite accuracy" ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)). There is not a single label-noise experiment in the paper; this is one sentence of a theoretical corollary.

**[The neural tangent kernel (NTK)](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the NTK is invoked as an argument about applicability rather than as a subject: at $d_{h}\gg 1$ the network "will begin to linearize, eventually converging to the neural tangent kernel regime", and then the results of the linear section should carry over "up to a redefinition of the kernel depending on the non-linearity" ([`p10-3`](#p10-3)). The work does not compute the kernel, does not check the width at which the transition occurs, and rests on a single 1000-$d_{h}$-5 network with $\tanh$ ([`fig-5`](#fig-5), bottom row).

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the work sits entirely in the lazy half of this opposition, and in the two-layer case the laziness is introduced by assumption: the trace of the Hessian (which is the kernel) is "fixed at its value at initialization", $h_{0}\simeq 1/2$ ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)), after which the coupled pair of equations eq. 18 reduces to the one-layer solution. The words lazy, rich and feature learning the authors do not use of their own dynamics, they do not measure the kernel and do not check against the contemporaneous work of Kumar et al. (2310.06110) — an explanation through a change of regime cannot be attributed to them. The significance for the corpus is the reverse: it is shown that grokking comes about without any transition to a rich regime.

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — a mention only. The survey names works that "confirmed the presence of epoch-wise descent structures" ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)), and the work of Davies et al. on reducing grokking and double descent to one another ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). At the same time $\lambda=1$ in their setting is exactly the interpolation threshold, and the grokking time diverges there — the connection with double descent suggests itself but is not drawn in a single word.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work uses the vocabulary of phases descriptively and cautiously: "the phase of perfect generalization" in its retelling of Power et al. ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)) and "the phase diagram of grokking" as the name of a contour map ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)). There is no order parameter, no critical point, no critical slowing down and no term "criticality" in the paper: the divergence of the grokking time as $\lambda\to 1$ is presented as a property of a formula ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). This matters, because the same group later reinterpreted this result in terms of a critical point — see the section on erroneous retellings.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — mentioned exactly in order to be distanced from: "some works conjecture that grokking is underlain by 'slingshots' [21] or 'oscillations' [17], but our explanation works without that dynamics" ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). There are no loss spikes of the authors' own in the setting by construction — full-batch gradient descent in the gradient-flow limit.

**[Learning structured representations](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — named as the closest adjacent approach and set aside as unnecessary: Liu et al. show perfect generalization "when there is enough data for the structured representation to be determined" ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)), whereas in a linear estimator there is nothing to learn — the teacher is realizable, and the whole dynamics reduces to the decay of the norm of $D=S-T$. The work does not contest the analysis of representations but shows that it is not required for grokking-like curves to appear.

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — the setting is fully deterministic: full-batch gradient descent in all the experiments ([`p14-1`](#p14-1)) and the gradient-flow limit in the analysis. The explanation through "random walks among optimal solutions" is mentioned in the list of proposed mechanisms ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)) and is not used in the authors' own analysis. There is no comparison with SGD in the work, so the conclusion "gradient noise is not needed for grokking at all" does not follow from it directly — all that follows is that in this setting it takes no part.

**[Optimizer](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the only optimizer used is full-batch GD ([`p14-1`](#p14-1)); Adam, AdamW and mini-batches do not appear in the work, and studying the effect of different optimizers on grokking is named as a direction for future work ([`p11-3`](#p11-3)).

**[Edge of stability](../../concepts/edge-of-stability.md)** — likewise a single sentence of future work: to go beyond the gradient-flow limit and study multilayer networks at a large learning rate, "connecting catapult / edge-of-stability dynamics with the analysis of grokking" ([`p11-3`](#p11-3)). There are no catapults and no sharpness measurements in the paper.

**[Grokking outside neural networks](../../concepts/grokking-in-non-neural-models.md)** — the work shows analytically and numerically that grokking arises in linear networks too, on a linear teacher — student task with Gaussian inputs ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). It formulates its own caveat itself: in a shallow transformer on algorithmic data the dynamics is *entirely different* ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)), so a coincidence of curves is not a coincidence of mechanisms.

**[Grokking time](../../concepts/grokking-time.md)** — the time here is not measured but derived: the dynamics is solved in the gradient-flow limit, and the time turns out to be a function of the initial conditions ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). This is stronger than empirical fits, but narrower in its domain of validity.

**[Phase diagram](../../concepts/phase-diagram.md)** — the map is built with an explicit counting condition: the white regions mean the absence of grokking, since the generalization accuracy does not reach $95\%$ ([`fig-4`](#fig-4)). The named threshold distinguishes such a diagram from qualitative maps where the boundary is drawn by eye.
## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

**Exact is what is solved at the level of the equations.** Eqs. 4–5 are exact: $D(t)=e^{-2\eta_{0}\Sigma_{\mathrm{tr}}t}D_{0}$ and the expressions for both losses through $D_{0}$ and $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tr}}$ — the authors write just that: "these expressions for the losses are exact" ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)). Exact too is the closed form of the training loss through the regularized confluent hypergeometric function (eq. 9); for the generalization loss no closed form could be found, which is admitted outright ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)). The strongest place in the work lies in the appendix and is not carried into the main text: the identity $d\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gen}}/dt=-4\eta_{0}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ (eq. 21, [`p14-3`](#p14-3)) — it makes the lag of the generalization loss a consequence of the structure of the task rather than an observation.

**Approximate is what is called "exact predictions".** Between the exact expressions and the main formula eq. 12 lie: the approximation that the projections of $D$ onto all eigenvectors are equal ([`p4-7`](#p4-7), with no error estimate), the replacement of the sum over the spectrum by an expectation over Marchenko — Pastur (eq. 8), a late-time expansion at $\eta_{0}t\gg\sqrt{\lambda}$ ([`p14-4`](#p14-4)), the replacement of the Lambert function by its logarithm and an expansion in $\epsilon\ll 1$ ([`p15-1`](#p15-1)). Plus the conditions of applicability, which are needed simultaneously: $d_{\mathrm{out}}=1$, $\gamma=0$ and strictly $\lambda<1$ ([`p14-2`](#p14-2)), the gradient-flow limit and the limit of large $d_{\mathrm{in}},N_{\mathrm{tr}}$. The abstract does not draw this distinction: "exact predictions" refers there both to eq. 5 and to eq. 12.

**Shown numerically but not derived.** The non-monotonicity of the grokking time in $d_{\mathrm{out}}$ with a maximum at $d_{\mathrm{out}}^{\mathrm{max}}\simeq 50$ is obtained by numerically solving eq. 13 at their set of parameters ([`fig-2`](#fig-2)); there is no closed expression for $d_{\mathrm{out}}^{\mathrm{max}}$. The same goes for the three phase diagrams ([`fig-4`](#fig-4)) — a numerical solution of $\mathcal{A}(t^{*})=0.95$ from the authors' own analytical expressions, that is, maps of the theory rather than measurements. The dependence of the grokking time on $\gamma$ is put into a plot ([`fig-3`](#fig-3), on the right), whereas the asymptotic eq. 16 contains no variable $\gamma$ at all; the curves in the plot are almost horizontal and bend only at the border of the region where there is no grokking — but this relation between formula and figure is not examined in the text.

**Experimental verification: strong in one respect and absent in another.** The agreement of the analytics with experiment in [`fig-1`](#fig-1) is attained with no fitting parameters and in both regimes $\lambda\lessgtr 1$ — in the losses, in the accuracies and in the dependence $\Delta t_{\mathrm{grok}}(\lambda)$ itself ([`p5-2`](#p5-2)); [`fig-6`](#fig-6) separately checks the late asymptotics against the exact solution. At the same time the whole experimental part is provided for by one paragraph of appendix A ([`p14-1`](#p14-1)): no number of repeats, no seeds, no variance, no link to code. Everything is measured at a single $d_{\mathrm{in}}=10^{3}$ and a single $\eta_{0}=0.01$; there is no check of the scaling in $d_{\mathrm{in}} $ — that is, no check of the very limit in which the theory is derived.

**Conjectured and untested.** Label noise is treated in one sentence with not a single experiment ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)). The two-layer case is reduced to the one-layer one by the frozen-kernel assumption ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)) rather than derived. Non-linearity is checked with a single 1000-$d_{h}$-5 architecture with $\tanh$ in one figure, and the authors hedge themselves: the results carry over "beyond the linear regimes, in some cases" ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)), and in the discussion they are called "preliminary evidence" ([`p11-1`](#p11-1)).

## Points we could not reconcile

**The asymptotics with weight decay is declared common to both regimes but derived for only one.** Of the overdetermined regime it is said: "still, the late behaviour is the same as in eq. 16, by the same arguments discussed above" ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)). However, eq. 16 is derived at $\lambda<1$ ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)) and contains $1-\lambda$ and $(1-\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}$ in the denominators: at $\lambda>1$ it gives negative losses and a negative grokking time. What exactly carries over into the overdetermined regime cannot be recovered from the text.

**The two-layer network is assigned at once to two mutually exclusive domains of validity.** In one paragraph it is said that the setting "can generally be assigned to the overdetermined regime, so long as $d_{h}\gg 1$, independently of $d_{\mathrm{out}}$ and at any $\lambda$", and immediately that "all the results derived earlier for $\lambda<1$ remain valid" ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)). For $\lambda<1$ that was the underdetermined regime in the one-layer model.

**Initialization is spoken of three times and differently.** In the list of contributions: initialization "raises the effective accuracy value required for saturation and therefore increases the grokking time" ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). In section 3.1.3: rescaling $S\to\alpha S$ "is equivalent to choosing a different threshold parameter" and "leaves the results unchanged" ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)). And from appendix B it follows that at leading order $\Delta t_{\mathrm{grok}}$ does not depend on $\epsilon$ at all — the dependence moves into the second term of eq. 26, which vanishes as $\epsilon\to 0$ ([`p15-2`](#p15-2)). The three statements are compatible only under different tacit assumptions about the order of the expansion, and none of them is stated.

**The domain of validity of the main formula is named in the appendix and not repeated in the main text.** Eq. 12 is derived at $\lambda<1$ ([`p14-2`](#p14-2)) and at $\lambda>1$ is undefined altogether: $1-\sqrt{\lambda}<0$, and the logarithm leaves the real domain. In the main text, in the discussion of "the largest difference of grokking times falls near $\lambda\simeq 1$", the restriction is not repeated ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)), and [`fig-1`](#fig-1) also shows runs at $\lambda=1.5$. Separately: "diverges quadratically" is an imprecise description — the divergence is $(1-\lambda)^{-2}$ multiplied by a logarithm.

**Minor inconsistencies of notation that do not affect the outcome.** The eigenvectors of $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tr}}$ are called uniformly distributed on the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{out}}}$ ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)), whereas $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tr}}$ is of size $d_{\mathrm{in}}\times d_{\mathrm{in}}$ and the sphere should be in $\mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{in}}}$. The quantity $D^{T}\Sigma D=\mathcal{L}$ is called the standard deviation of $z$ ([`p5-5`](#p5-5)), whereas it is the variance. In eq. 10 there is a superfluous factor of two before the probability: for $z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathcal{L})$ one already has $\Pr(|z|\leq\sqrt{\epsilon})=\mathrm{Erf}(\sqrt{\epsilon/2\mathcal{L}})$. In the bibliography, "Eric Bodin" instead of Antoine Bodin ([`p11-6`](#p11-6)).

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the corpus works that cite this one. Each entry says what exactly is distorted and leads to the authorial paragraphs carrying the lost caveat; the citing works' specific formulations are given here verbatim, because there are no "Erroneous citations" sections on their cards yet.

**"Grokking is explained by feature learning" (erroneous).** Doshi et al., in their list of explanations of grokking, place this work in the category of feature-learning mechanisms: [`"and other feature learning mechanisms [23, 43]"`](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/original/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.md#p1-4), where [23] is the work under analysis. This inverts its thesis: the whole point of the linear estimator is that there are no features and nothing to learn — the teacher is realizable by a linear transformation ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)), and the gap between the curves comes from the spectrum of the Gram matrix ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)), not from a reorganization of representations. The work belongs among those that "show that grokking is possible for (analytically solvable toy) models" — that is its actual category.

**"Levi et al. — binary logistic classification" (erroneous).** Notsawo et al. list: `"Beck et al. (2024) and Levi et al. (2024) on binary logistic classification"` (the paragraph of appendix A.1 of their card carries no anchor, so no link is set). There is no logistic loss in the paper: the loss is the standard MSE ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)), and the "classification" comes from a threshold $\epsilon$ placed on top of a regression ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). The logistic setting is a neighbouring work of the same group, [Beck et al. 2410.04489](../2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability/2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability.card.md), and the label has been carried over from it. A model of accurate distinction is Kim et al. (2509.20829), where "the high-dimensional limit of the linear model is analysed in the regression setting (Levi et al., 2024) and in binary classification (Beck et al., 2025)".

**"Grokking without understanding — a sharp drop in error driven by the $\ell_{2}$ norm and not reaching the optimum" (erroneous).** The phrase from the paper's title is borrowed by Notsawo et al. for their own, different phenomenon: [`"We call this phenomenon “grokking without understanding” like Levi et al. (2024) who illustrated it in the case of linear classification"`](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/original/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.md#p7-4). In Notsawo et al. this is a transition that "does not result in convergence to the best solution" and arises even where there is no solution at all; here, by contrast, at $\lambda<1$ the student converges to the teacher exactly ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)), and "without understanding" means only the absence of a transition from memorization to understanding, that is, a property of the accuracy measure ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)). In their appendix Notsawo et al. spell this distinction out themselves. The phrase then hardened into a definition: Yılmaz (2602.19533) writes `"Moreover, Levi et al. 2024 and Notsawo et al. 2025 document cases of *“grokking without understanding”*: a sharp decrease in the test error during training, driven by changes in the $\ell_{2}$-norm of the model parameters, but that does not result in convergence to an optimal solution."` Neither the $\ell_{2}$ norm as a driving quantity nor any non-convergence is present in the paper under analysis; this work has no card yet, so no link is set.

**"The work does not consider weight decay" (erroneous).** Xu et al., distancing themselves from a predecessor, write: [`"(3) they do not include weight decay as we do."`](../2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression/original/2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression.md#p2-1). Weight decay is announced here in the list of contributions ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)), it is given section 3.3 with the derivation of eqs. 14–16 ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)–[`p8-6`](#p8-6)), figure 3 ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)) and appendix D. The defensible version of the same reproach — that $\gamma$ drops out of the asymptotic expression for the grokking time in the underdetermined regime — is not the one written in the text. The other points of the same paragraph (non-rigour, Gaussian data only) are correct, and elsewhere the same work cites the paper accurately, placing it among those that analyse grokking without explicit regularization.

**"Grokking happens near a critical point, this is critical slowing down" (over-extending, introduced later by the same group).** The words "critical point", "criticality" and "critical slowing down" are absent from the paper: the divergence of the grokking time as $\lambda\to 1$ is presented as a property of a formula ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)), and the only "critical" in the text is the "critical dimension" in $d_{\mathrm{out}}$ ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). The reinterpretation was introduced a year later in [Beck et al. (2410.04489)](../2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability/2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability.card.md) — a work by the same authors, and there it is marked honestly: [`"(though not necessarily stated in these terms)"`](../2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability/2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability.card.md#p2-1). In the criticality section of that same work the caveat has already dropped out: `"which was explained in terms of a “critical slowing down” effect, arising from a vanishing eigenvalue of the data covariance near criticality"` (the "Criticality" paragraph of their card carries no anchor, so no link is set). There too the divergence is called logarithmic, $\propto\log{(1-\sqrt{\lambda})}$, whereas in eq. 12 the logarithm is multiplied by $1/(1-\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}$, and the authors themselves call the divergence quadratic ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). The same phrase is carried into 2602.12039 (no card). The mechanism is meanwhile described correctly by the citing works: the vanishing eigenvalue of the Gram matrix is precisely the lower edge of the Marchenko — Pastur spectrum ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)).

**"Grokking is caused by distribution shift" (erroneous).** In 2502.01774 (no card) it is said: `"The paper supports our hypothesis that grokking is caused by distribution shifts in the specific case of liner models."` There is no distribution shift whatsoever in the work under analysis: the training and the generalization points are drawn from one and the same isotropic Gaussian distribution, and the gap arises from the difference between the empirical covariance $\Sigma_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and its expectation $\Sigma_{\mathrm{gen}}={\boldsymbol{I}}$ — a difference between sample and exact quantities that the authors treat separately and call decisive for their derivation ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)).

**Exemplary retellings.** Lyu et al. (2311.18817) — the corpus's only brief retelling that retains the threshold construction of accuracy on which everything rests: [`"Levi et al. 2024 leveraged random matrix theory to analyze grokking in a standard linear regression setting, where the test accuracy can provably exhibit sharp transition if the accuracy is defined as the fraction of points whose regression loss is smaller than a small threshold."`](../2311.18817.dichotomy-of-early-and-late-phase-implicit-biases-can-provably-induce-grokking/original/2311.18817.dichotomy-of-early-and-late-phase-implicit-biases-can-provably-induce-grokking.md#p16-3). The title thesis is conveyed correctly by Wang et al. ([2503.23298](../2503.23298.learning-towards-emergence-inhibiting-monosemantic-neurons-on-pre-trained-models/2503.23298.learning-towards-emergence-inhibiting-monosemantic-neurons-on-pre-trained-models.card.md)) — the doubt as to whether grokking is owed to the accuracy measure itself, with an apt parallel to the dispute about emergence; outside the corpus cards, 2604.12434 (which reproduces the threshold construction) and 2602.08302 belong to the same category.

**Softer echoes.** The work is cited as "grokking occurs in linear models too" without conveying the argument: [2310.17247](../2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity/2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity.card.md) (which also strengthens a non-rigorous derivation to "proved"), [2402.16726](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md), [2510.04930](../2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking/2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking.card.md), [2511.04760](../2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold/2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold.card.md), [2603.01968](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md), [2606.17120](../2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking/2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking.card.md).
