## Concepts covered

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the work poses the corpus a question nobody had put directly before it: what happens if **only** the optimisation method is changed and nothing else is touched. Muon is set against AdamW at declaredly equal weight-decay strengths ([p5-3](#p5-3)), and the result claimed is a single pair of numbers: the mean grokking epoch falls from 153.09 to 102.89 ([fig-5](#fig-5)), that is, by about a factor of 1.49. It matters for the corpus how exactly Muon is described by the authors: "a newer optimiser designed to improve the training dynamics, incorporating spectral norm constraints and approximations of second-order information" ([p5-2](#p5-2)). What the work does not contain: not a single setting value of the comparison itself. The learning rate is named neither for AdamW nor for Muon; the weight-decay value declared equal is nowhere given; for Muon neither the momentum, nor the number of Newton–Schulz iterations, nor which matrices are given to it and which are left to AdamW is stated. Nor is there a number of runs — only "several independent runs each… using different random seeds" ([p5-3](#p5-3)). A comparison at untuned learning rates therefore confounds the update rule with the tuning, and "isolating the effect of their differing update mechanisms" by equalising weight decay alone does not succeed.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the notion neither a new observation nor a new explanation: it takes grokking as a ready-made phenomenon and measures one of its quantities, the time of onset. What is valuable here is the operational measure the authors set in a separate box: "Grokking is operationally defined as the first epoch at which the validation accuracy reaches or exceeds 95 %, occurring after the training accuracy has already settled near 100 %" ([p4-2](#p4-2)). The measure counts the **first crossing** of the threshold and does not require it to be held — which matters for the corpus, because later works on Muon found a collapse of the validation accuracy after the grokking, to which such a measure is blind. What the work does not contain: learning curves. Not a single "training/validation accuracy against epoch" pair is given in the paper, so the second half of its own definition — that the training accuracy did in fact reach 100 % earlier — is shown for not a single run.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting is taken from Power et al. unchanged and undiscussed: a binary operation table modulo 97, a random permutation of the full set and the allocation of a fraction to training ([p2-4](#p2-4)), a small transformer, and delayed generalisation as the object of measurement. Its own addition to the proving ground is two tasks Power et al. did not have: the greatest common divisor and the parity of a 10-bit number ([fig-2](#fig-2)). What the work does not contain: a justification of why the proving ground is suitable for comparing optimisation methods, and a breakdown of the result by task — the runs of all six tasks are merged into one sample.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the modulus is one and the same, 97, for all the arithmetic tasks; the full set is "all $97\times 97=9409$ possible input combinations" ([p2-4](#p2-4)); and there are five operations — addition, multiplication, division, exponentiation and the greatest common divisor ([fig-2](#fig-2)). The work merely uses this class of tasks and establishes nothing about it: no analysis of the learnt representation, no comparison of the operations with each other, and no statement of whether they differ in grokking time. A separate obscurity in the description: the line "Gcd — greatest common divisor, mod 97" does not explain what exactly is taken modulo — the greatest common divisor is not a modular operation.

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the training fraction is not studied here, but it enters the setting unnoticed as a quantity that **differs by task**: 50 %, 80 %, 80 %, 70 %, 50 %, 50 % for Gcd, Mod-add, Mod-div, Mod-exp, Mod-mul and Parity respectively ([fig-2](#fig-2)). The choice is nowhere justified. For a corpus in which the training fraction is itself a control quantity of grokking, this means that the six rows of the sample differ in more than the operation, and merging their runs into one sample admixes to the comparison of methods a spread over fractions as well. What the work does not contain: a sweep of the training fraction, a comparison of tasks at an equal fraction, or any mention that the fraction affects the grokking time.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — weight decay is present in the setting as a condition equalised between the methods: "Both optimisers were set to equal weight-decay strengths in order to isolate the effect of their differing update mechanisms" ([p5-3](#p5-3)). The value is meanwhile never named — not in the text, not in the captions, and not in the hyperparameter list, in which only $\beta_{1}=0.9$ and $\beta_{2}=0.98$ are given for AdamW ([p5-2](#p5-2)). What the work does not contain: no runs without weight decay, no sweep of its magnitude and no analysis of how it interacts with the optimisation method — although that interaction is precisely what is named as a direction for further work in the conclusion ([p5-7](#p5-7)).

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work accepts the "necessary" version as a settled question and refers to it in a single sentence: "Prior work has established that weight decay and other regularisation techniques are crucial prerequisites for observing the grokking phenomenon [7, 8]" ([p1-4](#p1-4)). There is no support beneath that sentence: of the two references one ([8]) does not exist at all, and the other ([7] = Liu et al. 2205.10343) shows the opposite — that a sufficient regularisation **removes** grokking (see ["Erroneous citations"](#mc-2205-10343)). The work offers no evidence of its own for the necessity of regularisation and does not investigate the question; in the corpus's dispute it is not a participant but an example of how one of the positions is passed on with its source already lost.

**[StableMax / perp-Grad](../../concepts/numerical-stability-fix.md)** — the numerical-stability line enters here as the second factor of the experimental design: alongside the ordinary softmax, stablemax and sparsemax are compared, "motivated by works indicating that numerical-stability difficulties in the ordinary softmax may bear on grokking [4]" ([p3-1](#p3-1)); stablemax is described as "a variant employing a piecewise transformation designed to improve numerical stability" ([p3-2](#p3-2)), and the formulas of all three are written out ([fig-3](#fig-3)). What the work does not contain — and this is the chief opportunity missed — is **a result for that factor**. A design of 2 methods × 3 softmax variants would have allowed a measurement of whether Muon's gain overlaps with the softmax fix, but only the main effect of the method is reported; not a word is said about what stablemax and sparsemax gave and how they combine with Muon. Of the second factor, ⊥Grad was not taken.

**[Softmax collapse](../../concepts/softmax-collapse.md)** — the notion appears exactly once, in row 2 of the table of conjectured mechanisms: Muon "Keeps the training stable and avoids "softmax collapse"" ([fig-1](#fig-1)). This is a hypothesis put forward **before** the experiment and in no way checked after it: neither the logits, nor signs of absorption in floating-point addition, nor a comparison of the ordinary softmax with stablemax under Muon is measured in the work. The authors cannot be credited with evidence that Muon prevents softmax collapse — all they have is a table row.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work uses the vocabulary of "escaping memorisation" while measuring nothing that vocabulary describes: Muon "appears to steer the model away from purely memorising solutions, thereby easing an earlier discovery of the true regularity" ([p5-7](#p5-7)), and row 1 of the table promises that orthogonalised updates "Help to escape memorisation and discover genuine regularities" ([fig-1](#fig-1)). The hedge "appears to" stands in the authors themselves and must be preserved when citing. What the work does not contain: the length of the plateau, the moment at which the training accuracy reaches 100 %, and any quantity separating memorising from generalising solutions — only the epoch of the first crossing of the validation threshold is measured.

**[Gradient orthogonality](../../concepts/orthogonal-gradient-perp-grad.md)** — the work uses the word "orthogonalised" in a different sense from the corpus card, and the two must not be confused. Row 1 of the table states that Muon "Employs orthogonalised gradient updates" ([fig-1](#fig-1)): in Muon it is the **update matrix** that is orthogonalised (by a Newton–Schulz iteration on the momentum buffer, whereupon its singular values approach one), whereas the ⊥Grad of Prieto et al. removes from the gradient the component **along the direction of the weights**. These are different operations with different aims. What the work does not contain: neither Muon's update formula, nor the number of Newton–Schulz iterations, nor an ablation of that operation — that is, there is nothing here with which to check that orthogonalisation is what matters.

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the spectral argument is declared in the work and never once measured. The abstract calls Muon a method "distinguished by its use of spectral norm constraints and second-order information" ([p1-2](#p1-2)), and row 2 of the table promises that it "Imposes spectral norm constraints" and thereby "Keeps the weights from running away" ([fig-1](#fig-1)). Neither the singular values of the weights, nor their spectra, nor layerwise norms are given in the paper; besides, the property named is described inaccurately — Muon normalises and orthogonalises the **update** rather than constraining the spectral norm of the weights. For the corpus this place matters because it is here that later works separated orthogonalisation from spectral scaling and measured each apart.

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — mentioned for the sake of demarcation rather than of content: the work's sixth task is "predicting the parity bit for 10-bit binary strings, with 1024 possible combinations" ([p2-4](#p2-4)), that is, the **full** parity of all ten bits rather than the $k$-sparse parity of the corpus, in which the relevant bits are far fewer than the length of the string. There is no hidden subset, no parameters $(n,k)$ and no computational hardness here; assigning this work to the sparse-parity proving ground would be an error.

## Points we could not reconcile

**Seven tasks in the abstract against six in the paper's own table.** The abstract declares experiments "on seven numerical tasks (predominantly modular arithmetic)" ([p1-2](#p1-2)), whereas the table of datasets lists exactly six rows — Gcd, Mod-add, Mod-div, Mod-exp, Mod-mul, Parity ([fig-2](#fig-2)) — and the enumeration in the text gives the same six: five modular operations and parity ([p2-4](#p2-4)). A seventh task is nowhere named and appears nowhere else. In citing the scale of the experiments one must take six.

**The abstract promises an assessment of the joint effect of two factors; results for the second are absent from the work.** It is declared that the setup "systematically varied the optimiser (Muon against AdamW) and the softmax activation function (ordinary softmax, stablemax and sparsemax) in order to assess their **joint** effect on the training dynamics" ([p1-2](#p1-2)). The results section reports only the main effect of the optimisation method ([p5-4](#p5-4), [p5-5](#p5-5)); no breakdown by softmax variant and no assessment of any interaction is given in the work. The experimental design that would have allowed this to be measured was left unused.

**The reported "$t$-statistic — $p$-value" pair is mutually impossible.** The values reported are $\approx 5.0175$ and $\approx 6.33\times 10^{-8}$ ([p5-5](#p5-5)). The two-sided normal tail at such a statistic equals 5.2×10⁻⁷ and the one-sided 2.6×10⁻⁷; the Student distribution at any finite number of degrees of freedom has heavier tails than the normal, so $p$ at such a $t$ cannot be smaller than 5.2×10⁻⁷ at any sample size. The reported value is understated by about a factor of eight and could not have come out of the declared two-sample $t$-test. Neither the number of degrees of freedom nor the sample size is stated in the work.

**The "tighter distribution" diverges from the paper's own figure.** The text says that Muon "exhibited a tighter distribution of grokking times, as depicted in figure 4" ([p5-6](#p5-6)). In the figure itself ([fig-4](#fig-4)) Muon does have the narrower box — an interquartile range of about 45–105 epochs against 60–150 for AdamW — but it has markedly more outliers and they run further: Muon's topmost point stands exactly at 1000 epochs, whereas AdamW's furthest outlier is around 872. A narrower box, not a narrower distribution.

**A claim about medians is referred to a figure that has no medians.** The sentence "Figure 4 (a box plot) shows that the median grokking epoch under Muon is markedly lower than under AdamW (see figure 5)" ([p5-6](#p5-6)) refers for the medians to figure 5, which contains only two **means** ([fig-5](#fig-5)). The medians can be read only off the box plot and are nowhere given as numbers — and they diverge from the means considerably (by the figure, about 103 and 78 against 153.09 and 102.89), which itself indicates a strongly right-skewed distribution.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

**Shown — exactly one thing.** On a sample of runs of unnamed size, merged over six tasks and three softmax variants, the mean epoch of the first crossing of the 95 % threshold fell from 153.09 to 102.89 ([fig-5](#fig-5)), and the box plot ([fig-4](#fig-4)) shows a shift of the whole distribution in the same direction. The direction of the effect is the work's only experimental finding.

**Conjectured — the whole mechanism.** Everything said about **why** Muon helps is gathered in a five-row table ([fig-1](#fig-1)), put forward in the introduction as a hypothesis ("We conjecture that **Muon accelerates grokking** thanks to several key differences…", [p2-1](#p2-1)) and in no way checked after the experiment. Not a single measurement is attached to any row: there are no weight norms, no spectra, no logits, no FLOP counts and no ablation of even one of Muon's operations. The only support adduced is NanoGPT, called outright "an informal benchmark suite" ([p2-2](#p2-2)), that is, an argument from a leaderboard on a different task and a different scale. The conclusion meanwhile repeats the mechanism more assertively — "the geometry of the optimiser's update decisively influences the onset of delayed generalisation" ([p5-7](#p5-7)) — although only the epoch was measured.

**Three rows of table 1 are inaccurate in substance.** "Imposes spectral norm constraints": Muon normalises and orthogonalises the update itself rather than constraining the spectral norm of the weights; what keeps the weights from running away is weight decay. "Approximates second-order updates" (and "second-order information" in the abstract, [p1-2](#p1-2)): Muon takes no curvature information — it is a steepest descent in the spectral norm, computed by a Newton–Schulz iteration on the momentum buffer. "Spends fewer FLOPs for the same loss": the Newton–Schulz iteration is performed on every hidden matrix at every step and makes a Muon step **more** expensive than an AdamW step, not cheaper; neither FLOPs nor wall-clock time is measured in the work at all.

**The setting is not recoverable from the text.** Not named are: the number of layers, the width, the number of heads, the dropout rate ([p3-3](#p3-3), [p3-4](#p3-4)), the batch size, the epoch budget, the learning rate — for neither of the two methods — the weight-decay value ([p5-3](#p5-3)) and the number of runs and seeds. Without the link to the code (`github.com/atveit/torch_grokking`, [p8-1](#p8-1)) the work is not reproducible.

**The statistics do not match the data.** Runs of different tasks are merged into one sample and compared by a two-sample $t$-test ([p5-4](#p5-4)) — this is pseudoreplication: runs of different tasks are not interchangeable, and the between-task spread (by the figure, from tens of epochs to a thousand) is plainly larger than the difference between the methods, so the values 153.09 and 102.89 are a mixture of the method effect with the composition of tasks. The distribution is strongly right-skewed and, judging by Muon's point exactly at 1000 epochs, truncated on the right by the training budget; neither the budget nor the fate of the runs that did not grok (excluded? counted at the budget?) is anywhere stated, and the comparison of means depends directly on that choice. No effect size, no confidence interval, no non-parametric test and no correction for multiplicity (six tasks by three softmax variants) is given.

**The bibliography was assembled without checking.** Five of the fourteen sources do not exist: [8] "Thiry, Kerg, Ollivier, Grokking of Modular Arithmetic in Transformers", [9] "Barak et al., Grokking as Phase Transition: A Simple Model", [10] "Chughtai, Chan, Nanda, Grokking Shows Long Optimization Dynamics", [11] "Davies et al., Grokking as Competition of Functions", [12] "Varela, Henaff, Pascanu, Grokking Neural Network Dynamics" ([p7-8](#p7-8) — [p7-12](#p7-12)). The arXiv numbers attached to them belong to unrelated works: 2208.06791 is "On the recent-$k$-record of discrete random variables", 2206.02840 "Spatial Acoustic Projection for 3D Imaging Sonar Reconstruction", 2309.03840 "Generating Minimal Training Sets for Machine Learned Potentials", 2310.03133 "Weinstein presentations for high-dimensional antisurgery", and 2305.11779 "DMDD: A Large-Scale Dataset for Dataset Mentions Detection" (checked directly against arXiv on 2026-08-20). The entries [9]–[12] are not cited in the text at all and appear only in the list. The analysis of errors in the existing references is below, under "Erroneous citations".

**Presentation.** Of the five "figures" only one is an actual figure ([fig-4](#fig-4)); figures 1, 2, 3 and 5 are tables. There are no appendices and no discussion of the results; the paper appeared in a single version and was not peer-reviewed.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2205-10343
**Liu et al. (2205.10343) — the conclusion inverted into its opposite.** `"Prior work has established that weight decay and other regularization techniques are crucial prerequisites for observing the grokking phenomenon [7, 8]."` ([p1-4](#p1-4)). Of the two supports one ([8]) does not exist, and the other says the opposite: in the phase diagrams of [Liu et al.](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) grokking is "sandwiched between comprehension and memorization", and a sufficient weight decay and dropout move the training into the comprehension phase, in which "the grokking phenomenon disappears entirely"; of their own MNIST setting the authors write "with sufficient regularization we can again "ungrok" the training". That is, in Liu et al. regularisation is a means of **removing** grokking rather than a condition for observing it. A claim about the necessity of weight decay does exist in the corpus (Nanda et al. 2301.05217 — "without weight decay or some other form of regularisation our networks on the modular arithmetic task do not grok"), but this paper does not cite it; Nanda's work stands in its list under [6] and is cited for a different purpose, as the source of the choice of tasks.

###### mc-2201-02177
**Power et al. (2201.02177) — the work ascribed to the wrong authors.** Entry [1] of the bibliography reads `"L. Prieto, J. Frankle, et al., *Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets*"` ([p7-1](#p7-1)). The work belongs to [Power, Burda, Edwards, Babuschkin and Misra](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md); the name Prieto is taken from entry [4] (see below), and Frankle has nothing to do with it. Substantively the reference stands correctly — to the primary source of the observation of grokking on small algorithmic tasks ([p1-3](#p1-3)); the error is only in the authorship, but it is such that a citation "(Prieto & Frankle, 2022)" carried onward would point to a non-existent work.

###### mc-2501-04697
**Prieto et al. (2501.04697) — the authors transposed with entry [1], and the line taken up broken off.** Entry [4] reads `"A. Power, B. Lee, et al., *Grokking at the Edge of Numerical Stability*"` ([p7-4](#p7-4)); the work belongs to [Prieto, Barsbey, Mediano and Birdal](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md), and "A. Power" has been carried here from entry [1] — the two references have exchanged first authors. Substantively this paper takes stablemax from them and its motivation through "softmax collapse" ([p3-1](#p3-1), [fig-1](#fig-1)), but it does not continue the line: no result for the softmax variants is reported, ⊥Grad is not tried, and the question of whether Muon's gain overlaps with the fix of the numerical stability — the very question for whose sake the design was made two-factor — is left unanswered. Other entries are corrupted too: [5] (Martins's co-author is Astudillo, not "E. Niculescu-Mizil, A. D. Magalhães", [p7-5](#p7-5)) and [7] (Liu's co-authors are Kitouni, Nolte, Michaud, Tegmark and Williams, not "Y. Wang, J. D. Hopkins, T. Xiao", [p7-7](#p7-7)).

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. For each it is said what exactly is distorted, with references to the authors' paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. The "Erroneous citations" sections on the side of the citing cards have not yet been created (cards for three of the four citing works do not yet exist), so the detailed analyses are not addressed and the citing works are named directly.

**"Muon is the optimiser of Tveit et al." (erroneous).** Hidajat, Stoll and An (2605.15787) twice put this paper in the place of the work introducing Muon: `"To make our convergence proofs agnostic to whether one uses AdamW, decoupled weight decay [Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019], or orthogonalized variants like Muon [Tveit et al., 2025], we abstract the optimizer strictly by its contractive properties."` and `"Optimizers that decouple weight decay from the gradient preconditioning step (such as AdamW [Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019], Muon [Tveit et al., 2025], and Lion) satisfy the norm-contractive property"`. The present paper neither introduces nor describes Muon: it takes it ready-made and refers to Jordan (2024) and to "Muon is Scalable for LLM Training" ([p5-2](#p5-2), [p7-2](#p7-2), [p7-3](#p7-3)); Muon's update formula does not appear in it at all. It cannot be cited for a definition of Muon — this is a reference to a measurement, not to a method.

**"It is shown that Muon accelerates grokking" (widening, later checked and refined).** The claim itself is conveyed accurately across the corpus and is confirmed by later works, but it accretes details absent from here. Exemplary formulations for comparison are given by Wang (2607.20512) — `"Tveit et al. (2025) report that Muon (Jordan et al., 2024) groks in fewer epochs than AdamW on modular arithmetic, crediting both orthogonalized momentum (a Newton–Schulz iteration) and spectral-norm-based update scaling"` — and by [Tian (2509.21519)](../2509.21519.provable-scaling-laws-of-feature-emergence-from-learning-dynamics-of-grokking/2509.21519.provable-scaling-laws-of-feature-emergence-from-learning-dynamics-of-grokking.card.md), who carefully calls this work "evidence" as distinct from "analysis": `"While evidence (Tveit et al., 2025) and analysis exist (Shen et al., 2025) to show that Muon has advantages over other optimizers"`. What is lost in the carry-over. First, the unit of measurement: what is measured here is **epochs**, not time and not compute; Janati et al. (2608.07436) measure that a Muon step costs 1.75–1.80 times more than an AdamW step, whereupon the advantage in steps at depth 1 turns into a loss of about 15 % in wall-clock time — that is, the title's claim of "accelerates" in wall-clock time is not checked by this work ([p2-2](#p2-2) is the only support on efficiency, and that from someone else's leaderboard). Second, the threshold: the measure adopted here counts the **first** crossing of 95 % and does not require it to be held ([p4-2](#p4-2)), whereas in Janati et al. none of the nine Muon settings is stable after the grokking, which is why they take a "stable" threshold. Third, the mechanism: Wang (2607.20512) isolates orthogonalisation rather than spectral scaling as the active principle — of the five rows of the hypothesis table ([fig-1](#fig-1)) one thereby gains support and another is refuted; in the work itself neither is measured.

**"Muon is faster than AdamW on modular arithmetic" (accurate).** Janati et al. (2608.07436) cite the work for exactly that fact and in a pair with a later one: `"Under this routing it reaches the grokking threshold on modular arithmetic faster than AdamW alone [14, 17]."` and `"14 and 17 report faster grokking on modular arithmetic"`. This is the role in which the work lives in the corpus — a source for the direction of the effect; none of its mechanistic arguments is inherited there.
