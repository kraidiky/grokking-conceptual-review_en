# Huang, Mirzasoleiman 2026 — Tuning the Implicit Regularizer of Masked Diffusion Language Models: Enhancing Generalization via Insights from $k$-Parity

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Jianhao Huang**, **Baharan Mirzasoleiman** (Department of Computer Science, University of California, Los Angeles).

arXiv [2601.22450](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22450), June 2026 (v2), 23 pages, 7 figures (9 files), 4 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 2, in the corpus — 0. The first work in the corpus on masked diffusion — and a new lever for controlling grokking: the earlier controlling quantities (the data fraction, weight decay, the initialisation scale, the optimiser) were applied to the training at an unchanged objective, whereas here the objective of the training itself is changed — and the delay disappears. The masked-diffusion (MD) objective is decomposed analytically into a signal mode (which drives feature learning) and a noise mode (which presses the output towards zero on informationally unrecoverable inputs — a built-in implicit regulariser); on the task of $(20,6)$-parity, where ordinary training of nanoGPT shows textbook grokking, the MD objective brings the training and validation curves down almost simultaneously. The applied half: from the signal fraction $P_{S}=(k+1)\mathbb{E}[t(1-t)^{k}]$ a rule for choosing the distribution of the masking fraction is derived, checked at 50M (WikiText, a U-shaped curve) and on LLaDA-8B (pre-training and SFT).

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the removal of the delay by a change of the training objective itself: on $(n,k)=(20,6)$-parity the reference training of nanoGPT with supervision on the label gives textbook grokking (100 % on the training set almost at once, 50 % on the validation set for a long time, with the late convergence confirmed by an extended run in appendix F.2), while the MD objective with the same weight decay brings both curves down almost simultaneously ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). The analysis: the comparison is not levelled by the amount of supervision — the reference run supervises the label only, whereas MD supervises the reconstruction of all the masked symbols, and by the reversibility of XOR each of the $k+1$ elements of the secret set teaches the very same thing (at $k=6$ — a sevenfold supervision); the work contains no experiment separating "a regulariser against memorisation" from "a richer supervision"; and neither the seeds nor the spread bars are given anywhere.

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — the work's proving ground: $(20,6)$-parity with the label glued on as the last symbol of the sequence, training of nanoGPT with a stochastic masking (Bernoulli with a fraction $t\sim U[t_{0},t_{1}]$, the loss taken only on the masked positions with a weight $1/(2t)$), and evaluation on the fixed mask "the input is visible, the parity bit is masked" ([`p6-5`](#p6-5)); the convergence is fastest for ranges near the theoretical optimum $\mathcal{U}[0,0.246]$, while the extreme $\mathcal{U}[0,0.1]$ and $\mathcal{U}[0,0.4]$ are unstable. The analysis: against the line of Barak et al. (hidden progress on the plateau) there are no progress measures here at all — by two accuracy curves one cannot tell whether the plateau is removed or merely shortened; and the paper's own signal optimum of corollary 4.8 ($t=1/7\approx 0.143$ pointwise) is never checked — the experiment uses a third, intermediate order ($t_{0}=0$, maximising over $t_{1}$).

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — a regulariser built into the objective itself: theorem 4.3 decomposes the MD loss into $P_{S}\mathbb{E}_{S}[\ldots(f_\theta-f^{*})^{2}]+P_{N}\mathbb{E}_{N}[\ldots f_\theta^{2}]$, where the second term requires the model to stay silent on informationally unrecoverable inputs — and this, by design, closes off the road to pure memorisation without any explicit penalty on the weights ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)); it is extended to the cross-entropy as well (remark 4.4) so that the mechanism does not look like an artefact of the quadratic loss. The analysis: what is proved is the existence of the regulariser, not the absence of the delay — the theory contains no quantity with the dimension of training time; weight decay is meanwhile fixed at $\eta=0.1$ in all the runs, so the interaction of the two regularisers is not checked (there is no MD run at $\eta=0$, although the survey itself refers to the robustness of MDLM without weight decay).

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — a direct continuation of Tian's energy frame (2509.21519): under the assumption of a lazy readout, minimising the effective loss is equivalent to maximising the energy $E(\boldsymbol{W})={\boldsymbol{c}}(\boldsymbol{W})^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}(\boldsymbol{W})^{\dagger}{\boldsymbol{c}}(\boldsymbol{W})$, and since $E\propto P_{S}^{2}$, the signal fraction works as a variable amplifier of the speed of feature learning; corollary 4.6 — in the limit of pure signal ($P_{N}\to 0$) the energy of a sufficiently expressive network levels off at a constant and feature learning collapses: the noise term is not a hindrance but the thing that keeps feature learning alive ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)). The analysis: the corollary speaks of the unattainable point $P_{N}=0$ and is silent about the small non-zero $P_{N}$ at which the real training happens; the lazy-readout assumption is taken from Tian technically, whereas in the corpus laziness is the very quantity under analysis (the lazy-to-rich transition), and the connection is not worked through.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — a new lever and honest cross-checks.** A change of the training objective as a way of removing the delay is a class of intervention new to the corpus; the signal/noise decomposition with the explicit formula $P_{S}=(k+1)\mathbb{E}[t(1-t)^{k}]$ gives a controllable knob, carried through to practice (the U-shaped curve at 50M; +4.6 and +8.8 points on HellaSwag/ARC-Easy in the 8B pre-training; GPQA 0.402 against 0.344 under SFT). The deliberate cross-checks are apt: the extended run confirms that what the reference model shows is grokking rather than a failure, and the uniform attention confirms that the reduction to a perceptron is legitimate.

**The theory proves something other than what the abstract claims.** What is proved: a regulariser exists (theorem 4.3), the signal sets the pace (theorem 4.5, $E\propto P_{S}^{2}$), and in pure signal feature learning collapses (corollary 4.6). What is not proved: that the ordinary objective must grok, and that the noise term shortens the plateau — the passage from "a regulariser exists" to "there is no delay" is made in words. The chief irreducibility: the MD supervision is $k+1$ times richer than the reference one on the same sample — and there is no separating experiment.

**Internal inconsistencies.** On the generative tasks (GSM8K, MATH) the winning range is $[0.5,1.0]$ — a nearly complete masking, which the paper's own theory assigns to the noise and calls uninformative; in the text this is presented as a separate observation without being reconciled with theorem 4.3. The "prediction" $t^{\star}\approx 0.46$ from the $n$-gram mixture almost entirely restates the predominance of bigrams ($w_{2}=0.74$ reduces the problem to $\arg\max t(1-t)$), and its linearity in $P_{S}$ contradicts the paper's own quadratic theorem 4.5 (this is admitted in a footnote). The gains of "8.8 % and 5.8 %" are percentage points, not per cent. There are neither seeds nor spread bars; and there are traces of unfinished editing (empty keywords, the misprints "quesiton", "unidentifable").

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that masked diffusion removes grokking") — more precisely: on one task $(20,6)$, one nanoGPT build, at one value of weight decay, against one reference run supervised on the label alone and with no number of seeds given, it is shown that the training and validation curves converge almost simultaneously; the theoretical part derives the existence of a regulariser but not the absence of the delay.
