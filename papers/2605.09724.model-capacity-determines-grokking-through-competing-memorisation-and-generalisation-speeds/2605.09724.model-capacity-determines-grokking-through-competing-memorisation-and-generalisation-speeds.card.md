## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work does not explain the mechanism of the phenomenon but answers the question "at what model size does it appear at all", and does so by separating two quantities that had previously merged into one. The first is the capacity threshold $P_{\text{mem}}$, the size from which a memorizing solution is representable; the second is the onset of grokking, the smallest size from which the delay is consistently non-zero. Both quantities are introduced as measurable first-passage times to a threshold and declared the outcome of a race between two processes — a memorizing one and an algorithmically generalizing one ([p. 2, para. 1](#p2-1), [p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)); in the abstract this is put as grokking "emerging as the outcome of a competition between two measurable timescales" ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)). The main claim is that the second is strictly greater than the first: "models with a parameter count substantially above this threshold continue to generalize immediately" ([p. 1, para. 4](#p1-4)), while the entry into the grokking regime coincides with the size at which the two measured time curves cross ([p. 9, para. 3](#p9-3), [fig. 1](#fig-1)); how this looks on the training curves of a single prime is shown separately ([fig. 4](#fig-4)). The formulation of the mechanism is extremely brief: "What governs whether a model groks is not whether table lookup is representable, but whether it is reached before the algorithmic solution." What the work does not have: the mechanism proper — not a single intervention inside the network is made, representations are not analysed, circuits are not isolated, and both speeds are precisely *measured* rather than derived, which the authors state outright ([p. 9, para. 6](#p9-6)). A caveat that matters for citers: the agreement between prediction and observation is not exact but carries a constant offset — the empirical onset comes about 30 % earlier than predicted ([p. 13, para. 5](#p13-5)) — and this offset is recalibrated when the weight decay, the training fraction, the depth or the operation changes.

**[The reference grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting is taken as standard — modular arithmetic is called "a controlled testbed for separating memorization from algorithmic solutions" ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)) — and fixed rigidly: modular division, an input of four tokens $X_{i}=[a,\ \texttt{op},\ b,\ \texttt{=}]$, a vocabulary $V=p+2$, a training fraction $\alpha=1/2$ ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1)); the architecture is a decoder-only transformer of depth $L_{\text{depth}}=2$ with one head, RoPE, a gated FFN of width $4d$ and RMSNorm, with the size varied only by the embedding width $d\in[10,1000]$ ([p. 4, para. 5](#p4-5)); AdamW, lr $10^{-3}$, weight decay $1.0$, batch $512$, at most $5{,}000$ epochs ([p. 12, para. 2](#p12-2)). To the testbed the work adds two new measurable quantities and one measure: the generalization delay $\Delta E(P)$ as the difference of the first epochs at which the thresholds are reached ([p. 6, para. 2](#p6-2)) and the "grokking point" as the smallest size from which $\Delta E$ is consistently positive ([p. 6, para. 3](#p6-3)). The measurement protocols are separated and described apart: the memorization speeds are measured at 44 widths with 10 seeds (42–51) ([p. 12, para. 5](#p12-5)), the grokking runs on their own width grid, truncated to $d\leq 256$ when fed into the crossing plots ([p. 12, para. 6](#p12-6)). It matters that the sweep axis here is model size, not training time: every point in [fig. 1](#fig-1) is a separate run, not a moment of one run. What the work does not have: sweeps over the input encoding, the split and the number of heads; dropout is fixed at $0.2$ in the grokking and speed runs and at $0$ in the capacity runs ([p. 12, para. 2](#p12-2)), and there is no sweep of its own over it — the corresponding subsection C.5 consists of a promise ([p. 19, para. 2](#p19-2)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — there is one main task, division $a/b\bmod p$, and it is taken at once across ten primes in the range 90–140 (97, 101, 103, 107, 109, 113, 127, 131, 137, 139; [p. 12, para. 1](#p12-1)) — a sweep over the modulus rare for the corpus, and it is what allows the scaling of the prediction with $p$ to be checked. A second operation appears in a single appendix: modular addition enumerates all $p^{2}$ pairs instead of $p(p-1)$, whence $K_{\text{mem}}$ and $n_{\text{equiv}}$ are recomputed ([p. 22, para. 3](#p22-3)). A number valuable for the corpus from there: for addition $T_{\text{gen}}(P)$ is consistently faster than for division, and the predictor overshoots the onset by $\approx 2.5\times$ for $+$ against $\approx 1.4\times$ for $/$ ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1), [fig. 10](#fig-10)). What the work does not have: multiplication and subtraction — they are declared planned ([p. 23, para. 2](#p23-2)) — and permutation composition; and there is not a single experiment in which the algorithmic complexity $K_{\text{alg}}(p)$ is computed, although the notion is introduced ([p. 4, para. 4](#p4-4)).

**[The memorization phase / plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work turns memorization from a phase of training into a separately measured speed. $T_{\text{mem}}(P)$ is defined as the number of epochs to 99 % training accuracy on a dataset *with random labels* of the same informational complexity as the modular dataset ([p. 5, para. 7](#p5-7)); memorization is thereby measured in a run where there is nothing to generalize by construction, and is not conflated with generalization. The main empirical finding about this quantity: it is determined first of all by the capacity fraction $f=K/(C_{\text{model}}P)$, not by $P$ and $K$ separately — points for datasets of different complexity fall roughly on one curve ([fig. 3](#fig-3), [p. 6, para. 5](#p6-5)), and the dependence on the capacity fraction is fitted with an exponential on $f\in[0,0.25]$ ([p. 5, para. 10](#p5-10)). What the work does not have: a connection between the memorization time on random labels and the memorization time on the modular task itself — this quantity is not measured at all, and $E_{\text{train}}(P)$ from the definition of the delay is nowhere compared with $T_{\text{mem}}(P)$; the assumption that one stands in for the other remains an assumption.

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — random labels here are neither a nuisance nor an object of study but a measuring instrument, and this is the main thing the work gives the notion. The datasets $D^{\text{rand}}$ are built from uniform inputs and independent uniform labels, "so that there is no structure to generalize" ([p. 5, para. 1](#p5-1)); on them both the capacity (the plateau of the curve $M_{T}(n)$) and the memorization speed are measured. An essential detail of the setting: a fully random dataset of the same *informational* complexity is used instead of the modular dataset with corrupted labels, that is, the noise fraction is not swept at all but taken equal to one. What the work does not have: intermediate noise fractions, a comparison with partial label-corruption settings, and a check that the saturation time on random labels predicts the training-set fitting time in the modular task itself.

**[Kolmogorov complexity](../../concepts/kolmogorov-complexity.md)** — the work translates the discussion of memorization into bits and gives the corpus a working pair of quantities. The notions are carried over from [21] and adapted "to our discrete classification setting" ([p. 4, para. 6](#p4-6)). The data complexity is defined as the worst case under random labels, $K_{\text{mem}}(p,\alpha)=N_{\text{train}}\log_{2}V=\alpha p(p-1)\log_{2}(p+2)$ ([p. 4, para. 3](#p4-3)), and total memorization as the sum of the excesses of the log-likelihood over a uniform baseline, bounded above by $n\log_{2}V$ ([p. 4, para. 8](#p4-8)); it is measured by the curve $M_{T}(n)$, which levels off on a plateau, and this plateau is linear in the number of parameters ([fig. 2](#fig-2)). The algorithmic complexity $K_{\text{alg}}(p)$ is introduced verbally and is not computed ([p. 4, para. 4](#p4-4)) — the notion is in the work, the numbers behind it are not. Hence the only indirect evidence about it: the first models attaining near-perfect accuracy both on training and on validation have a $P$ slightly below $P_{\text{mem}}$, which the authors read as the ability of gradient descent to "find algorithmic solutions more compact than the raw dataset" ([p. 7, para. 3](#p7-3)). What the work does not have: a description or measurement of the most compact solution itself — neither a program length, nor a minimal description of the weights, nor any compression.

**[Data fraction / critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the axis is swept directly and gives one of the two most telling sweeps. The training fraction is introduced as a control quantity right in the setting: the training subset contains a fraction $\alpha\in(0,1)$ of all admissible pairs, and at $\alpha=1/2$ one has $N_{\text{train}}=N_{\text{test}}$ ([p. 4, para. 2](#p4-2)). The central setting is $\alpha=1/2$, with a sweep over $\lbrace0.3,\mkern3mu0.4,\mkern3mu0.5,\mkern3mu0.6,\mkern3mu0.7\rbrace$, and at each $\alpha$ the size of the random dataset $n_{\text{equiv}}$ is recomputed as well ([p. 18, para. 1](#p18-1)). Within each $\alpha$ the prediction holds, but the calibration constant drifts monotonically — from $-0.39$ dex at $\alpha=0.3$ to $+0.21$ dex at $\alpha=0.7$, that is, $+1.23$ dex per unit of $\alpha$ — and adding $\alpha$ to the predictor turns out to be borderline ($F=4.45$, $p=0.044$; [p. 19, para. 1](#p19-1)). The work also gives the notion a second quantity — the critical *parameter count* $P_{\text{mem}}(p,\alpha)=K_{\text{mem}}(p,\alpha)/C_{\text{model}}$ ([p. 5, para. 4](#p5-4)), linking the data fraction to the model size through bits. What the work does not have: a critical fraction as such — no threshold in $\alpha$ below which there is no generalization is sought here; at $\alpha\in\lbrace0.6,0.7\rbrace$ the measurement runs into the edge of the width grid, and the authors acknowledge this outright ([p. 19, para. 1](#p19-1)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the language of phases is present in the work, but it is descriptive and refers to the model-size axis rather than to time: three regimes — insufficient capacity, immediate generalization, and grokking beyond the crossing point ([p. 2, para. 2](#p2-2), developed at [p. 8, para. 3](#p8-3)) — and the change of regimes itself is described by three numbered regularities ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)). The only place where the words "phase boundary" are used in earnest is the statement that the crossing of the two timescales "tracks the empirical phase boundary" ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4)). What the work does not have: an order parameter, critical exponents, finite-size scaling, and any check at all that the transition between regimes is sharp — the boundary is defined operationally, through the first size at which $\Delta E$ becomes positive, and with a resolution equal to the step of the width grid.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the only axis on which the framework is checked at full width and on which it fails in a strong form. Seven values $\lambda\in\lbrace0,\mkern3mu0.01,\mkern3mu0.03,\mkern3mu0.1,\mkern3mu0.3,\mkern3mu1.0,\mkern3mu3.0\rbrace$ are swept, matched between the speed and the grokking runs ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)); the central value is $\lambda=1.0$. The rank predictiveness stays high ($\rho=0.900$), but the offset drifts monotonically by $+0.32$ dex per unit of $\lambda$, and the "skeptic-friendly" comparison of $M_{3}$ against $M_{1}$ is rejected ($F=92.0$): $\lambda$ adds information beyond the crossing alone ([p. 16, para. 3](#p16-3)). The authors' own explanation — that weight decay "reshapes the speed curves rather than merely shifting them" — is a conjecture unsupported by a separate measurement. What the work does not have: an analysis of why at $\lambda=0$ the predictor misses by the largest margin ($-0.535$ dex) — that is, in the one regime where there is no weight decay at all, the framework is calibrated worse than anywhere else.

**[Learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — the only axis on which the calibration holds flat and the sufficiency test passes: the slope of the residual in $\eta$ is statistically zero ($p=0.62$), and $M_{3}$ against $M_{1}$ is not rejected ($F=0.98$, $p=0.34$) ([p. 17, para. 2](#p17-2)). It holds, however, on three values out of five: at $\eta=3{\times}10^{-3}$ not a single model in the swept range of widths groks within the epoch budget, although the predictor yields a finite $\widehat{P}_{\text{cross}}$, and at $\eta=10^{-2}$ training does not reach saturation at all, on either random or modular data ([p. 17, para. 1](#p17-1)). Both cases the authors declare to lie "outside the optimization window in which the speed measurements themselves are well defined" and assign to out-of-scope rather than to failures of the predictor — this is an authorial decision, not a result of a test. What the work does not have: learning-rate schedules and a separation of $\eta$ from $\lambda$, although the product of these two quantities is what sets the rate of norm decay in neighbouring corpus works.

**[Initialization scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — three values $\lbrace0.5,1.0,2.0\rbrace$ are swept, applied as a multiplicative rescaling of all weights after initialization ([p. 15, para. 1](#p15-1)). An observation useful for the corpus: at $1.0$ and $2.0$ the spread of the residuals is tight ($\sigma_{\log}\leq 0.03$), while at $0.5$ it grows by an order of magnitude ($0.260$), and is set by a single prime $p=139$, where the observed onset comes at $\sim\negthinspace2.2\times 10^{6}$ parameters against a predicted $\sim\negthinspace6.8\times 10^{5}$ ([p. 16, para. 1](#p16-1)). The authors' explanation — "the drift of the small-initialization regime into an undertrained corner where the speed measurements are noisier" — is a conjecture. What the work does not have: a connection with the "lazy-to-rich" line, for which the initialization scale is the principal lever: no comparison with that line is made along this axis, and the spread at $0.5$ is not analysed further.

**[Optimizer](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the optimizer is one and the same in all the experiments, AdamW with betas $(0.9,0.98)$ ([p. 5, para. 7](#p5-7), [p. 12, para. 2](#p12-2)), and this is a substantial limitation: both speeds on which the whole framework rests are properties of the bundle "architecture + AdamW + protocol", which the authors fix in the definition, introducing $\Theta$ as "the model architecture (including the optimizer and the training protocol)" ([p. 5, para. 1](#p5-1)). The work does not sweep this axis: there is no comparison with SGD, the effect of adaptivity on the relation between $T_{\text{mem}}$ and $T_{\text{gen}}$ is not measured, and the capacity constant $C_{\text{model}}=2.16$ is likewise tied to this optimizer.

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — the work merely refers to the explanation of Varma et al. and assigns it a definite place: they establish "which solution is preferred at convergence", whereas here it is "which solution gradient descent meets first as a function of model capacity" ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4)). This is stated as a refinement rather than a dispute, and the work makes no check of the claims about circuit efficiency: there are neither circuit norms nor circuit isolation here. On the concept card the work stands under "Citations".

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — mentioned twice and only in the survey: Kumar et al. as controlling grokking through a "laziness" parameter of the output scale and the alignment of the initial NTK with the labels, with grokking "in the Goldilocks regime where feature learning is delayed", and Mohamadi et al. as a static representational analogue — a proof that the kernel regime cannot solve modular addition ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)). There are no measurements of the authors' own along this axis, although the initialization-scale sweep ([p. 15, para. 1](#p15-1)) sits on exactly the lever this line uses — the connection is not drawn.

**[Sparse subnetwork / lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — mentioned once, in the survey: Merrill et al. "in the sparse-parity setting describe grokking as the appearance of a sparse subnetwork through the selective norm growth of a small set of neurons" ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)), and are then assigned to those who answer the question of which solution is preferred at convergence ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4)). Sparsity is not measured in the work.

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — the work names the line of "pattern-learning speeds" as its conceptual ancestor and points to Davies et al., who unite grokking and double descent through a spectrum of speeds, as well as to epoch-wise double descent as an argument that "training time, not only model size, is a meaningful axis" ([p. 3, para. 3](#p3-3)). Its own contribution is claimed to be the replacement of that qualitative picture with two separately measurable times ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4)). What the work does not have: double descent proper — no non-monotonicity of performance in model size is sought or shown here, although the sweep runs precisely over size.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work merely mentions the line: "complexity-based progress measures" (Clauw et al., DeMoss et al.) are assigned to readings of grokking "as an emergent transition in the network's internal organization" ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)). The work introduces no progress measure of its own: both of its quantities are first-passage times to a threshold, that is, outcomes of a run rather than functions of the training state predicting the time remaining; they are measured on *separate* runs and as functions of model size, not over the course of a single training run.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — mentioned once: Thilak et al. as having revealed "late 'slingshot' dynamics in adaptive optimizers, often coinciding with the grokking transition", alongside optimizer-side interventions supporting the picture of different speeds ([p. 3, para. 3](#p3-3)). All the work's experiments run under AdamW, that is, in exactly the class of optimizers in which the slingshot was observed, but neither loss spikes nor norm oscillations are examined here.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The two main quantities of the analysis are counted from different events.** The generalization speed $T_{\text{gen}}(P)$ is defined as the number of epochs until the validation accuracy exceeds a saturation threshold, "we used $99$%" ([p. 5, para. 11](#p5-11)), while the generalization delay $\Delta E(P)$ is defined as the difference between the first epoch with validation accuracy above $98$% and the first epoch with training accuracy above $99$% ([p. 6, para. 2](#p6-2)). The lowering of the threshold is noted in a footnote and explained by a wish to "remove noise from the empirical data", but the consequence is nowhere discussed: the observed onset $P_{\text{onset}}$ and the curve $T_{\text{gen}}$ that predicts it are counted from different events, and the constant offset between prediction and observation ($-0.16$ dex, that is, about 30 %) is analysed as a property of the predictor ([p. 13, para. 5](#p13-5)), although part of it may be created by this mismatch of thresholds.

**The grokking point is defined in two non-equivalent ways.** In section 4.3 it is "the smallest model size $P$ for which $\Delta E(P^{\prime})>0$ for all measured $P^{\prime}\geq P$" ([p. 6, para. 3](#p6-3)); in appendix B the same quantity $P_{\text{onset}}(p)$ is defined as the smallest $P$ for which $\Delta E(P^{\prime})>0$ "for every $P^{\prime}>P$" ([p. 13, para. 1](#p13-1)). The second definition does not require $\Delta E(P)>0$ at the point itself and therefore gives a value one step of the width grid lower. All the work's quantitative checks are carried out on $P_{\text{onset}}$ from appendix B, while the verbal description in the body follows the definition of section 4.3.

**The sufficiency test on the central sweep is run against a feature that does not vary on that sweep.** In the body the result is presented as substantive: "given $\widehat{P}_ {\text{cross}}$, dropout adds no explanatory power beyond the crossing alone ($F\approx 0$, $p=1$)" ([p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2)). Meanwhile appendix A.2 says that "dropout equals $0.2$ in the grokking and speed runs and $0$ in the capacity runs" ([p. 12, para. 2](#p12-2)), and appendix B itself notes that the robustness sub-claim "is immaterial on the central sweep, where dropout is fixed within each grokking cell" ([p. 14, para. 4](#p14-4)). The numbers confirm this: model $M_{2}$ from dropout alone gives an in-sample $R^{2}=0$ exactly and an adjusted $-0.125$ ([p. 14, para. 3](#p14-3)) — the signature of a constant feature. The "sufficiency against baselines" on the central sweep is thereby checked against an empty rival; a genuine check is given only by the sweeps of appendices C–E, and there it passes only for the learning rate.

**The number of primes in the central sweep is given as both ten and eleven.** Appendix A.1 says: "we use all 10 primes in the range from 90 to 140" and lists them ([p. 12, para. 1](#p12-1)). Meanwhile the scope of the weight-decay sweep names six primes per cell, including $p=149$ — outside the declared range — "with the central-sweep primes $\lbrace101,103,109,131,137\rbrace$ also present at $\lambda{=}1.0$" ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)), which gives eleven; the $\lambda=1.0$ row of table 5 carries $n_{\text{primes}}=11$, and the caption of table 9 speaks outright of "the central-sweep grid over 11 primes". The composition of the central sweep — the very one on which the Spearman $\rho=0.976$ and table 1 were obtained — is therefore ambiguous in the paper's text.

**The median logarithmic residual of the central sweep is given as two numbers.** In the body and in appendix B it is $-0.16$ ([p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2), [p. 13, para. 5](#p13-5)), in tables 5 and 9 for the same setting ($\lambda=1.0$, $\alpha=0.5$, 11 primes) it is $-0.171$. The discrepancy is nowhere noted and apparently corresponds to a different composition of the cells, that is, to the same question of "10 or 11 primes".

**Minor.** The caption of table 1 declares a permutation $p$-value "over 10,000 shuffles", while table 8 gives $p_{\text{perm}}<10^{-4}$ — a value below the resolution of such a procedure (the smallest attainable is $10^{-4}$). In the discussion of fig. 3(a) the inverse capacity is typeset as $1/C_{\text{model}P}$ ([p. 6, para. 5](#p6-5)) instead of the $1/(C_{\text{model}}P)$ of the figure caption — a typesetting slip in the formula.

## What is measured, what is fitted and what is conjectured

The work is arranged as a chain "capacity $\to$ speeds $\to$ grokking", and the reliability of its links is very uneven; a citer will do well to distinguish four levels.

**Measured cleanly.** That the capacity threshold is not the onset of grokking: at $P>P_{\text{mem}}$ models still generalize immediately over a wide range of sizes, with zero delay ([p. 1, para. 4](#p1-4), [p. 7, para. 4](#p7-4), [p. 9, para. 3](#p9-3)). This is a negative result, it depends on no fitting except the conversion of bits into parameters, and it is also the most valuable thing the work gives the corpus. Here too belongs the accompanying observation that for several primes the first models with near-perfect accuracy on training and validation have a $P$ slightly *below* $P_{\text{mem}}$ ([p. 7, para. 3](#p7-3)). And a third: the collapse of points of differing complexity onto a single curve when the memorization time is plotted against the capacity fraction ([fig. 3](#fig-3)).

**Fitted.** The capacity constant $C_{\text{model}}\approx 2.16$ is obtained by a linear fit over seven widths $d\in\lbrace10,\dots,22\rbrace$ at a single prime $p=113$, at dropout $0$ and one seed (42) per cell ([p. 12, para. 4](#p12-4)), and is applied to all primes, to models up to $d=1000$ and to parameter counts up to $10^{6}$, that is, it is extrapolated one to two orders of magnitude beyond the fit; the speed and grokking runs meanwhile use dropout $0.2$. In the weight-decay and depth sweeps the capacity is remeasured only at some of the values, and in the remaining cells $C_{\text{model}}$ is "pinned" to $2.16$ ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2), [p. 20, para. 2](#p20-2)). Neither an intercept $b$ nor an $R^{2}$ value is given in the paper — it says only "with a small intercept and a high $R^{2}$" ([p. 6, para. 4](#p6-4)). Through $C_{\text{model}}$ the fit enters both $P_{\text{mem}}$ and the capacity fraction $f$, and through them all the conclusions.

**Conjectured and declared as such.** The exponential form $T_{\text{mem}}(P)\approx be^{af}$ the authors twice call an empirical fit rather than a mechanistic claim ([p. 7, para. 1](#p7-1), [p. 9, para. 5](#p9-5)). It is likewise noted that the framework is "mechanistic, not architecture-agnostically predictive": $T_{\text{gen}}(P)$ is measured rather than derived, and every new architecture or task requires the measurements to be rerun ([p. 9, para. 6](#p9-6)). Separately the authors enumerate what their check is not: not a power analysis, not cross-validation, not a hierarchical model, about ten cells per figure ([p. 14, para. 5](#p14-5)).

**Not carried out where claimed.** The closing formulation "in all six our framework holds within any one matched setting" ([p. 9, para. 1](#p9-1)) is confirmed only partially in the appendices. For weight decay, sufficiency is rejected ($F=92.0$; [p. 16, para. 3](#p16-3)), for the training fraction it is borderline ($F=4.45$, $p=0.044$; [p. 19, para. 1](#p19-1)), and under a change of operation the categorical feature is significant ($F=155$; [p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1)). The heaviest case is depth: at $L_{\text{depth}}\geq 6$ there is no crossing at all in the swept range, $T_{\text{mem}}$ is everywhere above $T_{\text{gen}}$, so $\widehat{P}_ {\text{cross}}$ is undefined, whereas grokking is empirically observed, and the rank relation over the nine surviving cells is not significant ($\rho=0.600$, $p_{\text{perm}}=0.10$) ([p. 20, para. 3](#p20-3), [fig. 9](#fig-9)). The predictor is silent where the phenomenon is present. Finally, the preprint is openly unfinished: subsections C.5, D.2 and E.2 consist of promises ([p. 19, para. 2](#p19-2), [p. 22, para. 1](#p22-1), [p. 23, para. 2](#p23-2)), and among the unfulfilled is precisely the dropout sweep, on which the only sufficiency test of the central sweep rests.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this work restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached.

###### mc-2402-15175

**Huang et al. (2402.15175) — "progression" is called a regime of immediate generalization.** The survey says: `"[9] extend this competition picture to a 2D phase diagram over model size and dataset size that includes a small-model ‘progression’ regime of immediate generalisation"` ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)). In Huang et al. themselves progression is not immediate generalization: in the caption of their figure 1 it is defined as a regime in which the model "first memorizes part of the training data at zero validation performance and only then generalizes" ([Huang et al., fig. 1](../2402.15175.unified-view-of-grokking-double-descent-and-emergent-abilities/original/2402.15175.unified-view-of-grokking-double-descent-and-emergent-abilities.md#fig-1)), and they write outright that progression and grokking "show similar validation-performance dynamics over training", differing in the level of training accuracy at which the generalizing circuits arise, and in the fact that under progression the model is unable to memorize the training set in full. That is, delayed generalization in progression is present; what is absent is only the complete fitting that precedes it. The error is not harmless: it is precisely what one of the two "prior observations recovered as consequences of the framework" rests on — "small models on modular arithmetic do not grok", with a reference to [13, 9] ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4), [p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4)).

###### mc-2205-10343

**Liu et al. (2205.10343) — "decoder capacity" is substituted by parameter count.** It is said: `"[13] earlier mapped a four-phase diagram (comprehension, grokking, memorisation, confusion) on modular arithmetic and noted a small-decoder regime that does not grok"` ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)), and hence in the introduction: `"very small models on modular arithmetic do not exhibit grokking at all, generalising immediately instead [13, 9]"` ([p. 1, para. 4](#p1-4)). In Liu et al. the phase diagrams are built over the representation learning rate, the decoder learning rate and the decoder weight decay ([Liu et al., p. 6, para. 10](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/original/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.md#p6-10), [p. 7, para. 2](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/original/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.md#p7-2)); they never sweep either width or parameter count, and "decoder capacity" in their conclusion is called a quantity set "among other things, by the learning rate and the weight decay". For a work whose whole idea is that what matters is precisely the parameter count rather than the effective capacity, the substitution is substantial: it turns an observation about hyperparameters into an observation about model size and thereby creates a "prior observation" for the framework to recover.

###### mc-2603-25009

**Manir & Rupa (2603.25009) — the conclusion is conveyed without the authors' caveats.** It is said: `"A recent empirical study [18] disentangles depth, architecture, activation, and regularisation effects on modular addition, finding that grokking dynamics are dominated by optimisation–regularisation interactions rather than architectural specifics"` ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)). The retelling is correct by the abstract of the cited work, but it loses three of its caveats: the "architecture" axis there is inseparable from the "optimizer" axis, and the authors admit this outright ([Manir & Rupa, p. 16, para. 4](../2603.25009.a-systematic-empirical-study-of-grokking-depth-architecture-activation-and-regularization/2603.25009.a-systematic-empirical-study-of-grokking-depth-architecture-activation-and-regularization.card.md#p16-4)); their own closing number says that the contribution of architecture does not vanish but merely turns out moderate — $1.90\times$ at each one's optimal regularization ([Manir & Rupa, p. 10, para. 1](../2603.25009.a-systematic-empirical-study-of-grokking-depth-architecture-activation-and-regularization/2603.25009.a-systematic-empirical-study-of-grokking-depth-architecture-activation-and-regularization.card.md#p10-1)); and "on modular addition" is incomplete — the comparison of architectures is carried over there to multiplication as well. The same retelling pattern is observed in other works citing Manir & Rupa.

**Unresolved.** The claim about [21] — `"They argue that grokking begins when capacity is saturated."` ([p. 3, para. 4](#p3-4)) — serves as the target whose refutation the whole work rests on, but it cannot be checked against the stored texts: Morris et al. (arXiv 2505.24832) is not in the corpus. The refutation itself in the paper is meanwhile conducted not against the quotation but against a "natural reading" of the framework ([p. 9, para. 3](#p9-3)) — that is, against a formulation the authors attribute to the source themselves.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns observed in the corpus works that cite this one; in each case it is stated what exactly is distorted, with links to the authorial paragraphs carrying the lost caveat. The citing works (`2607.13749`, `2607.04333`, `2605.20441`) have no cards yet, so detailed analyses from their side have not been set up.

**"It is shown that grokking arises from the intersection of two measurable timescales" (over-extending).** The citing works convey the central claim without the number that qualifies it. Thus `2607.13749` writes: `"they show that grokking emerges from the intersection of two measurable timescales, a memorisation speed and a generalisation speed, both functions of parameter count"` and calls the framework `"the precise capacity framework of Song and Ye 2026"`. In the authors' own text the agreement is not exact: there remains "a small, prime-independent constant offset — the empirical onset comes about 30 % earlier than predicted" ([p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2)), which is also put as "the predictor scales correctly with $p$ but overestimates the absolute onset by $\sim 30$%" ([p. 13, para. 5](#p13-5)). What is also lost is that this offset does not transfer between settings: it is recalibrated with the weight decay ([p. 16, para. 3](#p16-3)), the training fraction ([p. 19, para. 1](#p19-1)), the depth ([p. 20, para. 3](#p20-3)) and a change of operation ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1)). A model of accurate conveyance is the formulation from `2605.20441`: `"Song & Ye 2026 relate grokking on modular arithmetic to competing memorisation and generalisation timescales as functions of parameter count"` — it retains both the task and the fact that what is at issue is timescales as functions of parameter count, not a proven law.

**"As capacity grows the grokking window narrows, and large networks grok after a shorter wait" (erroneous).** `2607.13749` derives from the framework a temporal dependence that is not in the paper: `"As capacity grows, the memorisation timescale shrinks faster than the generalisation timescale, and the grokking window narrows"` and `"Large networks memorise quickly and grok after a shorter wait."` In Song and Ye the measured quantity is the delay $\Delta E(P)$, and beyond the crossing point it becomes *non-zero*; there is not a single statement in the work that it then decreases, and the "grokking point" is defined precisely as the size from which the delay is consistently positive ([p. 6, para. 3](#p6-3)). The three panels of [fig. 1](#fig-1) show the appearance of a non-zero delay and its further growth with the parameter count, not its shrinking: in the $p=113$ panel the delay points rise from zero to the left of the crossing to more than thirty epochs at the largest models. What does become faster with growing $P$ is memorization ([p. 6, para. 5](#p6-5)) — which is precisely why the delay arises.

**"The framework applies only where the network is known to be large enough to memorize" (erroneous).** `2607.13749` twice assigns Song and Ye only the upper end of the scale: `"Existing work, including the precise capacity framework of Song and Ye 2026, studies what happens when the network is at least large enough to memorise"` and `"Their analysis assumes throughout that the network is large enough to memorise the training data; the regime where memorisation is structurally impossible lies outside their framework."` Meanwhile the capacity-deficient regime in Song and Ye is named first of the three and described outright: "the model capacity is substantially below the complexity of the dataset", "neither solution is representable and both training and test accuracy are low" ([p. 2, para. 2](#p2-2)), and in the analysis of the three regimes it is developed as an underfitting regime at $P\ll P_{\text{mem}}(p,\alpha)$ ([p. 8, para. 3](#p8-3)) and shown by training curves — "small models underfit" ([fig. 4](#fig-4)). A caveat in the citer's favour: in [fig. 1](#fig-1) this regime is barely visible, because the width sweep begins already near $P_{\text{mem}}$ — but it is present in the paper's text. The distinction the citing work draws is substantively correct but different: in Song and Ye a shortage of capacity is a shortage of *parameters*, whereas in the citing work the impossibility of memorization is structural; but it is formulated as Song and Ye lacking a whole regime that they in fact have.

**Weaker echoes.** `2607.04333` conveys the work in a single sentence and accurately: `"Song and Ye [16] frame grokking as a race between memorization and generalization speeds."` — it loses no caveats, because it makes no claim to a result.
