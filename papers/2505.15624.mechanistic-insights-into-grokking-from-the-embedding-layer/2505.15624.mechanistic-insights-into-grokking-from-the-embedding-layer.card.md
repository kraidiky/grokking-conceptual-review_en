## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work offers the corpus a cause of the delay rather than one more description of it: the title thesis is that the delay is introduced by the embedding layer ("[embeddings are central to grokking](#p1-2)", abstract) and that an MLP without embeddings "[can generalize immediately](#p1-2)". On four modular-arithmetic tasks an MLP with embeddings gives an exemplary trapezoid — 100 % on training by around $1{,}5\cdot 10^{3}$ steps and a test jump at around $10^{4}$ ([fig. 2](#fig-2)) — and this is a reproduction of grokking on a carrier that has neither attention nor LayerNorm. What the work does not contain and what cannot be credited to its authors: the comparison "with embedding / without embedding" is conducted not against a one-hot input but against a scalar one — the operands are fed as numbers in a four-dimensional vector ([§3.3](#p4-7)); on addition without embeddings both curves reach only ~0.87 and ~0.77 by $10^{5}$ steps, and on multiplication the training accuracy stays at about 0.08, that is, the comparison model does not fit the training set at all ([fig. 2](#fig-2)) — whereas grokking is defined through a delay **after** fitting. There is neither a number of seeds nor confidence bands here: every curve in every figure is a single run; nor is there a carry-over to a transformer, which the authors name as their chief limitation ([§6](#p10-2)).

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work uses the picture "an era of memorisation, then a sudden jump of the test accuracy" as given and calls it a "[trapezoid pattern](#p5-2)" — a name for the shape of the curve that is rare in the corpus. There is no internal analysis of the plateau here: what happens to the embeddings between the fit and the jump the work does not measure, and the gradient heat maps ([fig. 3](#fig-3)) are taken at steps 1, 12, 40 and 68 — within the first hundred steps, two orders of magnitude before the jump. The work proposes no quantity that grows on the plateau.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the set of tasks is extended to four operations at one modulus: $(a+b)$, $(a\div b)$, $(a\times b)$ and $(a^{2}+b^{2})$ modulo $P=97$ ([§5](#p8-8)). The work gives the corpus an argument that the embedding erases the arithmetic difference between the tasks: "[the embedding layer strips the input group elements of their numerical values](#p4-3)", and so training on addition and on multiplication does not differ for a network with an embedding, whereas a network without an embedding fails completely on multiplication. The basis is the isomorphism $\big(\{0,1,\dots,p-2\},+\big)\cong\big(\{1,\dots,p-1\},*\big)$, shown by heat maps at $p=7$ ([fig. 1](#fig-1)). An essential caveat: the claim about generators accompanying the isomorphism is wrong (see ["Inconsistencies"](#p4-2)), and the word "simple" is used in the sense of "cyclic".

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting is taken from [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) and carried over to a two-layer MLP: an embedding dimension of 128, a hidden layer four times wider, an output of $P=97$, ReLU, Adam with a weight decay of 0.001 ([§5](#p8-8)); the tokenisation of $(3+2)(\text{mod }5)=0$ into $[3,5,2,6,0]$ ([§3.3](#p4-6)). The split is their own: 20 % is held out as test, and 30 % of the full set is taken from the remainder for training ([§5.1](#p8-10)). The work extends the proving ground neither with new operations beyond the modular ones, nor with another modulus, nor with a comparison of timings against the original work; it does, however, admit outright that the settings are chosen so that grokking is preserved: "[aggressive tuning of learning rates and batch sizes can suppress or delay grokking](#p16-4)".

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the fraction of training data is fixed here, and what varies is the **structure** of the split: uniform, skewed and random ([§4.2](#p7-1)). This is an independent lever that the corpus almost lacks: at one and the same fraction the skewed split fits the training set and does not generalise, while the uniform one is faster than the random one at small batches, the gap being erased at batches above 512 ([fig. 4](#fig-4), [§5.1](#p9-2)). The work seeks no critical dataset size and constructs no dependence of the time on the fraction; besides, the skewed split changes the composition of the test set too ([fig. 8](#fig-8)), that is, it shifts the task itself rather than only the update frequencies, and the work makes no attempt to separate these two effects.

**[Weight decay / L2 regularisation](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — to the work belongs a distinct formulation of the disproportion: "[gradients reach the tokens only probabilistically, while weight decay acts on all the embeddings uniformly](#p6-1)", on account of which the embedding of a token absent from the batch moves only by decay. Whence an equilibrium $\mathbf{e}_{i}(t)\to-\frac{\eta pg}{\lambda}$ and an estimate of the convergence time $T\geq\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\left(\frac{C}{\epsilon}\right)$ are derived ([§4.3](#p8-2)) — that is, at a weak decay the equilibrium embedding norm is large and the convergence slow. A separate line: weight decay in bilinear models implicitly constrains the nuclear norm of the product, which is taken from Kobayashi et al. 2024 (there is no such work in the corpus) and checked by tracking the ranks ([§C.2](#p15-5)). What is absent: a comparison with runs without weight decay, an analysis of the dependence of the delay on $\lambda$, and any separation of the decay's effect on the embeddings from its effect on the other weights.

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the work offers the cheapest of the corpus's grokking accelerators: Adam-LR differs from Adam by exactly one number — the learning rate of the embeddings is ten times that of the rest ([§5.2](#p9-4)) — and there is no additional computation whatever. The measured benefit is modest and is named honestly: the test's rise to 100 % is accelerated roughly twofold at $lr=0.01$ and a batch of 512 ([fig. 6](#fig-6)). The second line, more valuable for the corpus, is an explanation of why Adam behaves differently on the embeddings than on the other weights: the authors relate the "block heterogeneity" of the Hessian spectrum noticed in transformers to the bilinearity of the weights, and call this a guess outright — "[We conjecture that it stems from the bilinear nature of the weights](#p14-5)". What the work does not contain: a comparison of Adam-LR with [Grokfast](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md), with [NeuralGrok](../2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation/2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation.card.md) or with [Muon](../2504.16041.muon-optimizer-accelerates-grokking/2504.16041.muon-optimizer-accelerates-grokking.card.md), or a comparison at equal computational cost; SGD takes no part in the experiments at all — the analysis of SGD against Adam is taken entirely from others' works ([§5.3](#p9-5)).

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — the work introduces a layerwise learning rate as a remedy against the bilinear coupling and derives a rule for it: the ratio $c=\frac{\eta_{E}}{\eta_{W}}$ must satisfy $c\propto\frac{\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{E})}{\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W})}\cdot\frac{f_{W}}{f_{E}}$ ([proposition 4.1](#p8-5)). It matters for the corpus that in fact the rule is not applied: the value is taken as a constant, $c=10$, "[guided by empirical trends of the singular values](#p8-7)", and neither the singular values nor the frequencies are recomputed over the course of the training — the word "adaptive" in the abstract means a constant chosen once. The sensitivity analysis ([fig. 5](#fig-5)) does not fully bear out the caption: on Amodp the fastest is $c=4$, while $c=32$ does not generalise at all; there are no training curves in the figure, so the delay as such is not visible in it — only the time of the test's rise is measured.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the work gives the initialisation a role within the bilinear pair: at PyTorch's initialisation with a scale of $\mathcal{N}(0,\sqrt{2/d})$ one usually has $\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{E})\gg\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W})$, since $\mathbf{W}$ is higher-dimensional ([appendix B](#p13-5)), and it is precisely this inequality that stands in the rule for $c$. Two trials are reported separately: frozen embeddings give a slow convergence, and small initial embeddings improve it, "[permitting stronger early gradients](#p8-3)". What is absent: neither curves nor numbers for either trial — this is a single sentence of the main text with references to others' works; there is no sweep over the initialisation scale (as in [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md)) here.

**[The loss landscape / basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the work's contribution to this notion is a layerwise measurement of the curvature: the maximum eigenvalues of the Hessian are computed separately over $\mathbf{E}$ and over $\mathbf{W}$ by the power method through Hessian–vector products, and they differ by an order and a half (about 0.13 against about 3.5, [fig. 7](#fig-7)); under Adam-LR the values over $\mathbf{W}$ fall and approach those of $\mathbf{E}$. The bilinear coupling is declared the source of saddle points and of sensitivity to the initialisation ([§1](#p2-2)). What is absent: not a single saddle is found or shown — the phrase "saddle points" stands in a description of the landscape's expected structure, while only two maximum eigenvalues are measured; no correction is made for the different numbers of parameters in $\mathbf{E}$ and $\mathbf{W}$; and the conclusion that "$\mathbf{W}$ leads the early optimisation while $\mathbf{E}$ completes the generalisation" rests on a coincidence in time of two vertical cut-offs in a single run.

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the work tracks the ranks of $\mathbf{E}$, $\mathbf{W}$ and their product in three settings ([fig. 10](#fig-10)) and arrives at a negative conclusion valuable precisely for its honesty: the rank of $\mathbf{W}$ passes through three eras — an early fall, a shelf and a decline coinciding with the grokking ([§C.2](#p15-6)) — but "[the evolution of the rank alone does not capture the onset of grokking](#p15-7)". What the work does not contain: a caveat that the third of the three settings ($wd=0.005$) does not generalise at all and that its training accuracy collapses to zero after ~0.47 — the fall of the rank in a failed run is included in the argument about implicit nuclear regularisation with no mention of this; and that the statement about the stability of the rank of $\mathbf{E}$ holds only for the first setting (see ["Inconsistencies"](#p15-6)).

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the work applies the DFT not to the logits and not to the activations but to the embedding matrix itself — along the input dimension, with an $\ell_{2}$ norm over the embedding dimension ([§C.3](#p16-2)) — and obtains a modest but independent argument: on addition the spectrum is concentrated in a few frequencies, on division it is smeared ([fig. 11](#fig-11)), and under Adam-LR the picture on addition is the same. That is, the accelerator shifts the timing rather than what is learnt. Fourier features are meanwhile considered as a **replacement** for learnt embeddings rather than as a description of them: they "[are able to bypass the need for learnt embeddings](#p16-1)", but require prior knowledge of the periodicity. What the work does not contain: no circuits and no analysis of how the frequencies found add up into an algorithm — from the analysis of [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) only the DFT procedure is taken; nor is there a multiplication panel, although the text speaks of addition and multiplication ([§C.3](#p16-3)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work gives the corpus a case useful precisely as a demarcation: the word "mechanistic" stands in the title, but there are no circuits, no features and no reverse engineering of an algorithm in it — what is analysed is the optimisation dynamics (update frequencies, singular values, curvature, ranks). The authors themselves use the word in the same widened sense, assigning to "earlier works on the mechanistic understanding of grokking" Kumar's thesis and Lyu et al. ([§C.4](#p16-4)), that is, works about training dynamics rather than about the structure of the computation. This work cannot be credited with a mechanistic explanation of grokking in the sense of [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) or [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md): it refers to those works as "high-level phenomena" that miss the role of the individual components ([§1](#p1-4)).

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — the work embeds the sparsity of the embedding updates in the dispute about gradient noise: the heavy-tailedness of the noise, by which Zhang et al. 2020 explain SGD's lag, is related to "[the randomness of the embedding updates for tokens with low $p$](#p14-3)", while the argument of Kunstner et al. that noise alone is insufficient (SGD lags behind Adam under full-batch training too) is adduced in opposition. There are no noise measurements of the work's own: the quantity $p_{i}$ is nowhere measured, and its proxy is the three splitting strategies; the token frequencies in the datasets are shown ([fig. 8](#fig-8)), but the frequencies of appearance in batches are not.

**[Softmax collapse](../../concepts/numerical-stability-fix.md)** — a mention only: [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md) are named as a work relating delayed generalisation to numerical instability and are declared **complementary** — "[offering solutions that complement our attention to the structural coupling and to the disproportion of the gradients](#p3-1)". Neither StableMax, nor $\perp$Grad, nor the phenomenon of softmax collapse itself is reproduced or checked here; the shared motif — "grokking as a trouble of optimisation rather than of cognition" — remains declared rather than shown.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The definition of an embedding makes the work's chief comparison impossible.** [§3.1](#p3-4) says that even with one-hot inputs the first weight matrix "in essence acts as a learnt embedding", and that therefore every such model has embeddings — "explicit or implicit". But the model "without embeddings" of [§3.3](#p4-7) encodes $a$ and $b$ as **numbers** in a four-dimensional input vector, that is, it has neither tokens nor a first matrix over them. Under the definition of §3.1 the comparison of section 3.3 cannot be a comparison "with embedding / without embedding": what are compared are an embedding and a scalar input, and the models differ not in the presence of an embedding but in the way the operands are fed.

**Equations (5) and (6) differ in sign, and solution (9) contains a superfluous factor.** [Equation (5)](#p6-4) has $\nabla_{\theta_{t}}\mathcal{L}^{T}(\theta_{t+1}+\theta_{t})$, whereas [equation (6)](#p6-5), obtained from (5) by substitution, has $(\theta_{t+1}-\theta_{t})$; checked against the published PDF, this is a typo of the paper rather than of the conversion. Separately: [solution (9)](#p8-2) is written as $Ce^{-\lambda t}-\frac{\eta pg}{\lambda}$, whereas the [equation (8)](#p7-4) being solved has no factor $\eta$, and the probability carries the index $p_{i}$ rather than $p$.

**The caption of figure 3 calls rows columns.** The [caption](#fig-3) speaks of "sparse columns in the embedding gradients", whereas on the axes of the heat maps the tokens are plotted vertically (the Y axis is Token ID, the X axis Embedding Dimension), and the empty stripes are rows. The observation itself is direct and vivid; only the word is wrong.

**The claim about generators is false for both groups.** [§3.2](#p4-2) says that in $\big(\{0,1,\dots,p-2\},+\big)$ any element other than $0$ serves as a generator, and in $\big(\{1,\dots,p-1\},*\big)$ any other than $1$. Both claims are false: in $\mathbb{Z}_{6}$ only $1$ and $5$ are generators, and in $\mathbb{Z}_{7}^{*}$ only the primitive roots. The isomorphism of the two groups is meanwhile stated correctly, and the conclusion of §3.2 rests on it alone; the word "simple" is used in the sense of "cyclic" rather than in the accepted group-theoretic one.

**The rank of $\mathbf{E}$ is called stable on the strength of one panel out of three.** [§C.2](#p15-6) asserts that "the rank of $\mathbf{E}$ remains largely stable throughout". In [figure 10](#fig-10) this is true only for the first setting (Adam, $wd=0.001$): under Adam-LR the rank of $\mathbf{E}$ falls from 99 to about 30 at around $5\cdot 10^{3}$ steps and then returns to 99, and at $wd=0.005$ it falls from 99 to about 20 and oscillates thereafter. The settings in question are the same three as are listed in the next paragraph of the same section.

**The dimensions of the bilinear pair do not agree, and the product is written in two ways.** In [equation (2)](#p5-1) the input $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}$ is a concatenation of four embeddings, that is, a vector of length $4d$, and the matrix $\mathbf{W}$ ought to have $4d$ columns; [appendix B](#p13-2) declares $\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{R}^{4d\times d}$, which has $d$ columns. The latter agrees with [figure 10](#fig-10), where the rank of $\mathbf{W}$ starts at 128 rather than at 512. In the same place the product is labelled $EW^{T}$, whereas the text of [§C.2](#p15-5) everywhere writes $\mathbf{E}\mathbf{W}$ and $\text{rank}(\mathbf{E}\mathbf{W})$; at the declared dimensions only $EW^{T}$ is defined.

**Figure 9 repeats figure 7 word for word.** [Figure 9](#fig-9) in appendix C.1 is composed of the same two files as [figure 7](#fig-7) of the main text and carries the same caption word for word (both files in `images/` are byte-identical with the files of figure 7). The paragraph that refers to it likewise repeats the paragraph of §5.3.

**The captions of figures 12 and 13 name one dataset instead of four, and two panels duplicate figure 4.** The [captions](#fig-12) of both figures say "for the modular multiplication dataset", although panels (a)–(d) are four different tasks. Beyond that, panel 12(a) is the same file as [4(a)](#fig-4), at a learning rate of 0.001 against the 0.01 declared in the caption, and panel [13(c)](#fig-13) is the same file as 4(c), at a rate of 0.001 against the 0.005 declared (the files in `images/` are byte-identical).

**One and the same observation is credited to two different works.** In the [introduction](#p1-4) the "phase transition in the linear regions of the input space" is assigned to humayun2024deep, and in the [related-work survey](#p2-7) the same thing — "delayed robustness", local complexity — is assigned to jeffares2024deep. In the bibliography Kumar's work is listed as "PhD thesis, none, 2024".

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

The distinction matters because the work's abstract contains the word "prove", while there is not a single proof in the sense of a theorem in it.

**Proved — nothing.** [Proposition 4.1](#p8-5) is derived in [appendix B](#p13-6) by a chain of proportionalities: $\|\nabla_{\mathbf{E}}\|\propto\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf{W})$, its paired relation and the ratio of update frequencies — with no constants, no conditions and no error estimate. The abstract's claim "We prove that an adaptive learning rate ratio … mitigates bilinear coupling effects, accelerating convergence" relates only the **scales of the updates**; there is no theorem about convergence, about the rate of convergence or about a weakening of the coupling in the work.

**Shown (by single runs, with no seeds and no bands).** An MLP with embeddings groks on all four tasks ([fig. 2](#fig-2), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). The skewed split fits the training set and does not generalise, the uniform one is faster than the random one, and the gap is erased at batches above 512 ([§5.1](#p9-2)) — this is the work's cleanest place, because exactly one lever is varied here. Adam-LR roughly halves the time of the test's rise to 100 % at $lr=0.01$ and a batch of 512 ([fig. 6](#fig-6)); in that same setting the gap between fitting and generalising is small to begin with, that is, the acceleration is measured where there is hardly any grokking. The maximum eigenvalues of the Hessian over $\mathbf{E}$ are far smaller than those over $\mathbf{W}$ ([fig. 7](#fig-7)). The embedding spectrum is concentrated on addition and smeared on division ([fig. 11](#fig-11)).

**Conjectured and called a conjecture.** That the block heterogeneity of the Hessian spectrum in transformers stems from the bilinearity of the weights — "[We conjecture](#p14-5)". That transferring other MLP layers might give the same benefit as transferring the embedding — an observation "[in preliminary trials](#p3-2)", with no data; note that it undermines the exclusivity of the embedding and bears directly on [Xu et al. 2025](../2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model/2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model.card.md), in which transferring precisely the embedding is the essence of the technique.

**Not measured at all.** The probability $p_{i}$, on which the whole of section 4.1 rests, is nowhere measured — its proxy is the three splitting strategies. The relation of the sparsity of the updates to the moment of grokking is not checked: the heat maps are taken at steps 1, 12, 40 and 68 ([fig. 3](#fig-3)), while the test jump occurs at around $10^{4}$. There are no comparisons with other grokking accelerators and no comparison at equal computational cost; SGD takes no part in the experiments. The authors' caveat, which lowers the generality of everything else: "[aggressive tuning of learning rates and batch sizes can suppress or delay grokking](#p16-4)", and the settings are chosen so that grokking is preserved.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns noticed in the citing works of the corpus; each entry says what exactly is distorted and gives references to the authors' paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. The work appeared in May 2025 and is so far cited by few, so there are two patterns and both are built on single formulations.

**"It is shown that the embedding layer is the bottleneck of delayed generalisation" (widening).** Citing works retell the paper as a measurement of the embeddings lagging behind the subsequent layers: 2606.05863 assigns to it and to [Xu et al. 2025](../2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model/2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model.card.md) the thesis that "the embedding layer itself can be a bottleneck for delayed generalization", and 2606.08985 that the embeddings receive "sparse, frequency-dependent, and bilinearly coupled updates, which can cause them to lag behind downstream linear layers". The second is more accurate than the first, but it too credits the work with a measurement of the lag. There is no measurement of the lag in the work: what is shown is the empty stripes in the gradient heat maps within the first hundred steps ([fig. 3](#fig-3)) and the difference of the maximum Hessian eigenvalues across layers ([fig. 7](#fig-7)); not a single quantity comparing the moment at which the embedding is ready with the moment at which $\mathbf{W}$ is ready has been constructed. The chief caveat is lost too: the comparison model is not a network without an embedding but a network with a scalar input ([§3.3](#p4-7)), which on multiplication does not fit even the training set ([fig. 2](#fig-2)).

**"The work showed a two-dimensional circular structure of the solution on modular addition" (erroneous).** 2606.08985 includes this work in a list of sources for the claim that the learnt solution "spontaneously organizes into a two-dimensional circular structure", beside Nanda et al., Zhong et al. and Gromov. There is no such result in the work: the only representational measurement is a DFT of the embedding matrix with an $\ell_{2}$ norm over the embedding dimension ([§C.3](#p16-2)), and it gives a spectrum rather than a geometry; no circle, no two-dimensional projection and no principal components are shown in the work, and on division the spectrum is smeared ([fig. 11](#fig-11)). The work itself formulates its observation cautiously — "[distinct frequency peaks indicate that the model internally captures the periodic structure intrinsic to the task](#p16-3)" — and that hedge is lost in the carry-over.

**An example of accurate citation.** 2607.06639 retells the work preserving both its setting and its limits: the authors "track the rank of the embedding $E$, the first layer $W$, and their product under small weight decay ($0.001$–$0.005$) and report that $E$’s rank stays largely stable while $W$ and $EW$ compress", call the divergence from their own data outright "an open cross-setup question that our clamp law does not resolve", and derive from [§4.3](#p8-2) the consequence $\lVert e_{i}\rVert\propto 1/\lambda$, which the authors themselves do not write out but which follows from their equilibrium. This is a case in which a citing work conveys both the result and its setting.
