# Schiffman 2026 — Transformers Converge to Invariant Algorithmic Cores

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Joshua S. Schiffman** (New York Genome Center, New York).

arXiv [2602.22600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22600), February 2026 (v2), 17 pages, 6 figures (5 files), 6 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. A single-author work (the mechanistic-interpretability workshop at ICML 2026) shifting the target of interpretation from realisations to invariants: the ACE method (Algorithmic Core Extraction) isolates an "algorithmic core" — a low-dimensional subspace of the activations that is causally necessary and sufficient for the task and that recurs across independently trained models. For the corpus the main part is the grokking one: the cores of modular addition "crystallise" exactly during the grokking era, a blindly fitted shift operator acquires eigenvalues on the unit circle (a rotational mechanism with no Fourier form prescribed in advance), and as the training continues with weight decay the cores *inflate* from ~15 to ~60 dimensions — "the minimum norm requires the greatest redundancy" — and the same redundancy yields the inverse law of the grokking time $\tau_{\rm grok}\propto 1/(\omega p)$.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — a double contribution: (1) the crystallisation of the core simultaneously with the delayed generalisation — on $a+b\equiv c\bmod 53$ the cores contract into subspaces that are necessary and sufficient under ablation exactly during the grokking era (epoch 800) ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)); (2) a theory of the time: after the memorisation the drift is set by the weight decay, the margin follows the ODE $\dot m=-\omega m+c\,\omega\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}$, whence the inverse law $\tau_{\rm grok}(p)=-\Omega\log(1-p_{\rm crit}/p)\approx\Omega p_{\rm crit}/p\propto 1/(\omega p)$, checked by sweeps ($\tau\propto\omega^{-1.02}$; a solution of the ODE with $R^{2}>0.99$, $\hat{p}_{\rm crit}\approx 23$). The analysis: the "coincidence" of the crystallisation with the grokking is resolved only to within 100 epochs and over three models at once; the inverse law is measured in steps of *full-batch* descent — with a dataset growing as $p^{2}$, the total work to grokking grows with $p$; the law argues with the $t_{g}\propto p^{2.2}$ of 2603.29262, and not one work in the corpus that measured the dependence on $p$ is cited; and the data fraction — the corpus's main controlling parameter — is absent from the theory (it is 50/50 throughout).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — a conclusion opposite to the common one: weight decay does not simplify the representation but spreads the weight across all the equivalent solutions. After the grokking the task loss is negligible, the training implicitly solves $\min\|\boldsymbol{\alpha}\|^{2}$ subject to $\langle\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\psi}\rangle\geq\delta$, and by Cauchy–Schwarz the minimum is attained at $\boldsymbol{\alpha}\parallel\boldsymbol{\psi}$ — when *all* $\lfloor p/2\rfloor$ modes are active; experimentally the cores inflate from ~15 to ~60 dimensions with the weight decay retained and stay contracted when it is switched off after the grokking ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)); the inflation is decomposed into counters: the sufficient dimensions stay steadily few while the necessary ones keep growing. The analysis: this argues directly with the branch "grokking is compression" — the contracted solution turns out to be an instantaneous state right after the grokking rather than the limit of the training; hence the speculative "window of interpretability" (to switch the weight decay off soon after convergence); and the assumption $\gamma(t)\approx c\,\omega$, which contributes the whole factor $\omega$ to the law, is declared but not measured.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — a blind reconstruction of the clock mechanism with no Fourier form prescribed in advance: a linear shift operator (+1), fitted by least squares in the coordinates of the core, acquires by the grokking era eigenvalues that settle on the unit circle (a rotational dynamics), whereas before the grokking they are scattered inside the disc ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)); the particular rotational modes (pairs of conjugate eigenvalues) differ across three seeds — a functional equivalence without a structural identity — and already at the grokking era there are more modes than the one minimally needed, while as the training continues their number approaches the limit $\lfloor p/2\rfloor=26$. The analysis: three seeds per condition; the cores are extracted once every 100 epochs; and the "Fourier clock algorithm" is attributed to Nanda et al., although the name "clock" comes from Zhong et al., which the paper cites separately.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — a conceptual shift: circuits and sparse features describe the coordinates of a realisation, whereas cores describe the causal subspaces and the dynamics that persist across realisations (an analogy with diagonalisation: the power lies not in the sparsity but in the invariants — the eigenvalues); in Markov chains three models with a near-zero cosine between their weights carry 3-dimensional cores in nearly orthogonal subspaces (an overlap of $0.02$–$0.04$) but with a CCA of $\approx 0.99$ and a spectrum reproducing the non-trivial eigenvalues of the true transition matrix; and in six LLMs (GPT-2 S/M/L, LLaMA-3.1, Gemma-2, Qwen2.5) number agreement carries a one-dimensional steerable axis in the late layers ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)). The analysis: "nearly orthogonal" is at the level of random subspaces (the expected overlap is $3/64\approx 0.047$), so the geometric divergence is the default state of affairs rather than a finding; the spectral gap of GPT-2 Large ($2.8\times 10^{10}$) points to a degenerate fit; and the "systematic violations in all six" in free generation are supported by two selected examples, with only the AUC on the authors' own 1200 prompts being quantitative.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — the blind reconstruction, a measure of redundancy, and the non-monotonicity of simplicity.** The operator in the coordinates of the core recovers the rotational mechanism with no a priori Fourier hypothesis — an automation of what Nanda did by hand; the separation of "sufficient" and "necessary" dimensions is a working measure of redundancy; the inflation of the core under weight decay after the grokking is a rare observation of a non-monotonicity in the simplicity of the solution over the course of the training; the check $\|\boldsymbol{\psi}\|_{2}^{2}=\tfrac{3}{4}p$ comes out right ($39.75=0.75\cdot 53$); and the pedigree from systems theory (the Kalman decomposition, a minimal realisation, balanced truncation) gives the frame a non-trivial foundation.

**The theory of the grokking time rests on unmeasured places.** The assumption $\gamma(t)\approx c\,\omega$ is not measured — and it is precisely what contributes the factor $\omega$; "redundancy speeds grokking up" is not supported by a measurement of the redundancy: the sweep runs over the modulus $p$, that is, the task itself changes, and there is no experiment "one task, different redundancies"; the law is measured in steps of full-batch descent on a single-layer network, whereas the cores and the inflation were studied on a two-layer one; and the two panels of fig. 4 are taken under different conventions (mini-batches against a full batch).

**Internal inconsistencies and limits.** The length of the training is given now as $2\times 10^{3}$, now as $2\times 10^{4}$ epochs (the discrepancy is in the PDF too); there are three seeds per condition almost everywhere; the dimension of the core is determined by an energy threshold and then refined by the very ablations against which it is subsequently assessed; and the cores of GPT-2 Large and Gemma-2 are found in the very last layer — which is not stated.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that transformer mechanisms are universal; the grokking time is inversely proportional to $p$") — more precisely: on three seeds at $p=53$ a cyclic dynamics in the core is shown that coincides with the grokking to within 100 epochs; the inverse law is measured in steps of full-batch descent on a single-layer network in which the redundancy changed together with the task; and in units of total work the time grows with $p$.
