## Concepts covered

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the work **extends** the Fourier analysis of Nanda et al. from addition to the whole of modular arithmetic and obtains its main result: every grokking operation has a Fourier representation of its own ([`p1-1`](#p1-1)). Subtraction learns a sparse embedding, as addition does, but an asymmetric neuron-to-logit map ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)); multiplication engages **all** the frequencies with a bias towards the cosine; and polynomials give a superposition of these pictures ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)). The reverse side of the same result: for the operations that do not grok there simply are no clear Fourier pictures.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — two new task-independent quantities are introduced: **FFD** (the Fourier frequency density — the fraction of frequencies whose norm exceeds a fraction $\eta$ of the maximum) and **FCR** (the Fourier coefficient ratio — how far the components are biased towards the sine or the cosine) ([`p4-5`](#p4-5), [`p5-1`](#p5-1)). The difference from the measures of Nanda et al. is substantial: theirs are derived from the analysed mechanism of addition, whereas these are computed from any weight matrix. Which quantity tracks the progress depends on the operation — for addition the FFD falls, for multiplication the FCR, and for $ab+b$ both ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — both measures are statistics of the spectrum in the Fourier basis rather than in the singular one; the inflection point of the FFD or the FCR coincides with the saturation of the test accuracy ([`fig-8`](#fig-8)). The threshold $\eta=0.5$ is chosen by a sweep: a smaller one lags behind the grokking, a larger one converges too soon ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)).

**[Freezing a subnetwork](../../concepts/freezing-subnetwork.md)** — the work's main methodological device: **pre-grokked models**. A module (an embedding or the whole transformer) trained to grokking on one operation is inserted frozen into the training on another ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)). This separates the question "has a representation been learned" from the question "has an algorithm been learned": if a pre-grokked embedding helps, the representations are similar; if a pre-grokked transformer helps, the algorithms are.

**[Ablation and intervention](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the whole transferability section is built on that device, and the result is negative: the transfer works only from elementary arithmetic to linear expressions and almost never to high-degree polynomials ([`p11-1`](#p11-1), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). A separate frequency ablation shows that subtraction has *dependent* frequencies: not key ones, yet removing them worsens the restricted loss ([`p13-1`](#p13-1) and appendix E).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the notion is widened from one operation to a whole family: addition, subtraction, multiplication, linear expressions, monomial terms, sums of powers and polynomials up to the seventh degree, at $p=97$ (and $p=59, 113$ in appendix J) ([`p1-1`](#p1-1)). The summary table A.5 in appendix I is the corpus's fullest list of which modular operations grok and at what data fraction.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work honestly marks the limit of the method: a complete closed-form analysis, as in Nanda et al. for addition, is present here neither for multiplication nor for polynomials ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)). In its place an "interpretable reverse engineering" through frequency pictures and transferability is proposed — that is, a description rather than a derivation of an algorithm.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the notion acquires a **condition of occurrence** stated algebraically: polynomials with a cross term ($a^2+ab+b^2$, $a^3+ab$) do not grok, whereas those decomposable through addition or subtraction ($(a\pm b)^n$) grok even at high degrees ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). The comparison of $a^2+ab+b^2$ against $(a+b)^2$ — two algebraically equal expressions — shows that what matters is the **form in which the task is written** rather than its complexity.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — mentioned in the survey as one of the conditions of grokking (Power et al., Lyu et al.) ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)) and taken into the setting as $\lambda=1.0$ in AdamW; the work gives no analysis of its role of its own.

**[Semi-grokking](../../concepts/semi-grokking.md)** and **[data fraction](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the fraction $r$ here is a working parameter rather than an object of study: the result for each operation is presented as "the smallest $r$ at which grokking occurs" ([`tab-1`](#tab-1)), and this gives the corpus a convenient scale of the difficulty of the operations.

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — multi-task training introduces the notion of **co-grokking**: under a mixture of addition, subtraction and multiplication the grokking sets in for all the tasks at once, but requires a larger data fraction than each does separately ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)). A suitable mixture may even induce grokking where single-task training could not: $a^2+b^2$ and $(a+b)^2$ help $a^2+ab+b^2$ to grok ([`p12-3`](#p12-3)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no explanation of *why* a cross term is an obstacle: the decomposability is established as an empirical regularity, and the authors derive it neither from the training dynamics nor from properties of the Fourier basis. Nor are there language models: a carry-over to practice is named as a direction of work ([`p13-1`](#p13-1)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original task; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the Fourier analysis of addition and the progress measures generalised here; [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — the analytical solution available only for a narrow class; [Rubin et al. 2024](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.card.md) and [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — the explanations the work leans on in its survey; [Merrill et al. 2023](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md) — the sparsification of the network, named among the driving forces of the late generalisation.

**[Polysemanticity and superposition](../../concepts/polysemanticity-superposition.md)** — the transfer of features between similar operations ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)) is a mild form of the same question: one direction serving several tasks. It can be checked rigorously here because the algorithm is known.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is declared empirical and is exactly that: three hypotheses posed in the introduction are checked by experiment, and two of the three are confirmed only in part — which the authors say themselves.

**The most valuable result is the algebraic criterion.** Decomposability through $(a\pm b)$ separates the grokking polynomials from the non-grokking ones ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)), and this is checked on a pair of expressions equal as polynomials: $a^2+ab+b^2$ and $(a+b)^2$ differ only in their written form, yet only the second groks. In the corpus this is the only criterion of task difficulty stated in the language of the task itself rather than of the training dynamics.

**The transferability is checked and largely refuted.** The hypothesis that "grokked models acquire common transferable features" is confirmed narrowly: from addition to $2a\pm b$, and from multiplication to $ab\pm b$ ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)). On high-degree polynomials the pre-grokked models do not speed grokking up and often hinder it ([`p10-5`](#p10-5), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). The negative result is presented outright and supplied with a full table rather than a single example.

**The asymmetry of subtraction is a strong observation.** A transformer pre-grokked on subtraction transfers to **nothing**, including subtraction itself ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)). This is a strange and therefore valuable fact: it shows that "the same task" and "the same solution" are different things, and that the algorithm a network finds may depend on the run more than on the operation.

**The progress measures measure but control nothing.** The FFD and the FCR coincide in time with the rise of the accuracy ([`fig-8`](#fig-8)), and the threshold $\eta$ is chosen by a sweep. No prediction of the form "this model will grok by step N" is built from them, however, and there is no intervention changing the FFD or the FCR for the sake of accelerating grokking — that is, these are indicator measures rather than levers.

**The weak place is the statistics.** All the experiments run on 3 seeds, and almost everywhere the mean is given; in the multi-task section the best accuracy across runs is given. Appendix M answers this with a single spread check for one figure ([`p27-1`](#p27-1)) — useful, but it does not extend to all the tables.

**The dependence on $p$ is admitted and not analysed.** Appendix J shows that $a^3+ab$ groks at $p=97$ but not at $p=59$ and $p=113$ ([`p25-1`](#p25-1)), and that a smaller modulus — that is, a smaller task — does not help. It follows that some of the conclusions about "grokking" and "non-grokking" operations are tied to the chosen modulus; the authors note this and leave it as it stands.

**The check of the alternative hypothesis is done correctly.** The supposition that the matter lies in a skewed label distribution is checked directly — by the KL divergence and the label entropy averaged over 100 splits — and rejected ([`p26-1`](#p26-1)). Such an explicit check of a "boring" explanation is a rarity in the corpus.

**A caveat about identifiability.** Freezing part of the model could explain the failures of transfer by itself; the authors answer this with an identical seed for the pre-grokking and the subsequent training and with the argument that the incompatible pairs have different Fourier components, while rotations of a weight matrix do not change the Fourier basis ([`p27-2`](#p27-2)). The argument is sound, but it remains an argument.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the broadest empirical map of modular tasks: the summary table A.5 lists dozens of operations with the data fraction at which each groks. For the wiki it is above all a **reference work**: when another paper claims an explanation of grokking, one can look here at the operations on which it ought to have worked. The work proposes no mechanism of its own and does not claim to.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2201-02177
**Power et al. (2201.02177) — weight decay as a precondition, and a conflation of regimes.** The paper twice presents weight decay as a condition of grokking with a reference to Power: `"In simple algorithmic tasks like modular addition, grokking would be observed with proper weight decay and the ratio of train-test splits (Power et al., 2022; Lyu et al., 2023)"` and `"The weight decay is one of the factors inducing the grokking phenomenon"` (with a reference to Power et al.). Meanwhile [the canonical figure of Power was obtained with Adam and no weight decay at all](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-a1-2): in the cited work weight decay speeds the generalisation up and saves data, but is not a condition of it. Grokking without explicit regularisation is demonstrated also by [Thilak et al.](../2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md) (the slingshot mechanism) and by [Gromov](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — under vanilla gradient descent. A separate inaccuracy is the hyperparameter table of the appendix: a configuration referenced as "following Power et al." combines "Weight Decay 1.0" with "Epochs 1e6", whereas the $10^{6}$ budget in Power belongs precisely to the run without weight decay, and the weight-decay-1 experiments ran for $10^{5}$ steps — the result is a regime that does not exist in the cited work.

###### mc-2206-04817
**Thilak et al. (2206.04817) — grokking on computer vision ascribed to them.** `"grokking could occur in more general settings such as teacher-student …, NLP …, computer vision (Thilak et al., 2022)"`: on CIFAR-10 Thilak shows a slingshot and a late maximum of the accuracy, but not grokking in the sense of a passage from chance guessing to generalisation; [their grokking is in the setting of Power et al.](../2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md#p1-2), while the canonical vision demonstration is Omnigrok, which is cited in the same sentence for something else.

###### mc-2310-03789
**Rubin et al. (2310.03789) — "dynamic phase transitions during training".** `"could be explained with the dynamic phase transitions during training (Rubin et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2023)"`: an equilibrium Bayesian theory is bracketed with a dynamical one under the common label "during training": in Rubin [the transition is governed by the sample size, the noise and the width](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/original/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.md#p2-1), and [the learning in the model is a Langevin dynamics run to equilibrium](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/original/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.md#p3-8); there is no time axis in the theory on which "during training" would be meaningful.

###### mc-2306-13253
**Notsawo et al. (2306.13253) — placed within mechanistic interpretability.** `"many works along the mechanistic interpretability have attempted to systematically understand what happened … through extensive reverse engineering (Olah et al., 2020; Olsson et al., 2022; Akyürek et al., 2023; Elhage et al., 2022; Notsawo et al., 2023)"`: the cited work contains neither an analysis of circuits nor reverse engineering: its method is [a spectral analysis of the loss curve of the early steps](../2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens/original/2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens.md#p3-4) and [slices of the loss landscape](../2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens/original/2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens.md#p4-1). The list into which it is placed describes a different genre.
