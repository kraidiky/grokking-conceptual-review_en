# Qchohi, Rossi 2026 — A Bayesian Perspective on the Role of Epistemic Uncertainty for Delayed Generalization in In-Context Learning

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Abdessamed Qchohi**, **Simone Rossi** (EURECOM, France).

arXiv [2604.12434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12434), April 2026 (v1), 27 pages, 7 figures (9 files), 4 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The first Bayesian view in the corpus of delayed generalisation in in-context learning (ICL): on the linear modular arithmetic $z=ax+by\bmod 29$ in the setting of He et al. (the task vector is hidden and the model infers it from the triples in the context), the epistemic uncertainty (EU), estimated by two approximate Bayesian methods (IVON and a last-layer Laplace), is proposed as a label-free indicator of generalisation; the theoretical support is the solvable linear model of Levi et al. plus a Gaussian variational Bures–Wasserstein flow, in which both the delay of the accuracy thresholds and the delay of the EU peaks are derived from a single spectral mechanism — the lower edge of the Marchenko–Pastur law. The paper's main gap: the temporal language ("the EU collapses when the model groks") against the grid nature of the main figures (final values over a grid of settings), and the "collapse" against the "peak" of the EU, which in the paper's own theory are different quantities.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the carrying of the name onto an ICL setting: "to generalise" here means to infer a new task from the examples in the context rather than to learn one table; OOD generalisation (unseen tasks on unseen inputs) arises only at the largest task diversities ($T\geq 256$) and requires a tuning of the data fraction, and in those same settings the final EU is low ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)). The analysis: there is not a single training-accuracy curve in the paper — the gap "memorised → a long nothing → generalised" is not shown on the transformer, and the delay as such is not measured; the main figures are maps of *final* values over a grid $(T,\alpha)$ rather than trajectories, yet they are presented in temporal language ("it collapses when the model groks"); there are two genuine trajectories, and both speak of a *peak* of the EU before the accuracy threshold rather than of a collapse — three different formulations in one paper.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the claim of "the EU as a label-free indicator of generalisation": grokking has so far been defined through the accuracy (which needs labels), whereas the epistemic uncertainty is measured from the posterior without labels; over the grid of settings, at a small task diversity almost all of the total uncertainty is epistemic, while at $T\geq 256$ the EU falls sharply with the aleatoric part remaining high, and this agrees between IVON and Laplace ([`p4-10`](#p4-10)). The analysis: nothing is compared with the existing progress measures (Clauw, Golechha — both cited) on this same task, and no firing threshold for the EU is proposed; "label-free" is half true — the EU is computed on a pre-selected OOD slice, that is, it requires knowledge of the distribution of the tasks; and the comparison of IVON with Laplace is inhomogeneous in substance (two differently trained models, with the weight decay differing by six orders of magnitude: $1$ against $10^{-6}$).

**[Effective theory / statistical physics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — a self-contained derivation of the variational dynamics: for Bayesian linear regression the Gaussian Bures–Wasserstein flow gives closed ODEs and exact solutions; in the limit $n,d\to\infty$ at $\lambda=d/n<1$ both excess losses are averages over the Marchenko–Pastur law with the same late behaviour $t^{-3/2}e^{-\kappa t}$ and a common $\kappa$ set by the lower edge $\nu_{-}=(1-\sqrt{\lambda})^{2}$; the delay is the logarithm of the ratio of the prefactors $K_{\text{gen}}/K_{\text{emp}}=(1-\sqrt{\lambda})^{-2}$ divided by $\kappa$ ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)). The analysis: in this model grokking is a smooth shift between two curves with the same decay rate rather than a sharp transition; the non-monotonicity of the EU is built in by a threshold redefinition (the binary entropy peaks at $p=1/2$ by definition), whereas the regression EU proper at $\Sigma_{0}\prec\Sigma_{\infty}$ grows monotonically and gives no collapse at all (lemma C.3 against lemma 5.1); the delay of the EU peaks literally coincides with the delay of the accuracy thresholds — both are monotone functions of the same excess loss, and theorem 5.1 is tautological in that sense; and $\lambda$ is not measured for the transformer, so there is no quantitative link between the theory and the experiment.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — an ICL variant of the canonical testbed after He et al.: $z=ax+by\bmod 29$, the task vector $(a,b)$ hidden, the model receiving 32 triples $(x,y,z)$ in the context (a window of 96 tokens) and required to infer the task; four slices (ID Train/Val, OOD Train/Val) cross the unseenness of the tasks with that of the inputs; the sweeps at inference are over the length of the context (the EU is non-monotone: it first rises and then falls sharply while the accuracy rises monotonically) and over the noise in the context (corrupting the labels $z_{i}$ raises both the aleatoric and the epistemic components) ([`p4-1`](#p4-1)). The analysis: one modulus ($p=29$), one architecture, one family of tasks; the "number of tasks $T$" of the main text and the "base $n_{\mathrm{task}}$ with a parallelogram extension $4n_{\mathrm{task}}$" of the appendix are nowhere explicitly stitched together; and the rise of the aleatoric component under injected label noise is called "slightly unexpected", although it is the expected outcome.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — the design itself and the derivation.** The idea of measuring delayed generalisation by decomposing the predictive uncertainty into an epistemic and an aleatoric part is a new instrument for the corpus; the non-monotonicity of the EU in the length of the context (a rise at a small context, then a sharp fall), consistent across two methods, is a substantive observation about adaptation at inference time; the derivation of the variational dynamics in appendix C is self-contained and reusable; and the disclosure of the use of an LLM and of the budget (~12 000 accelerator hours) is honest.

**Between the language and the data there is a gap.** "A collapse of the EU at the moment of grokking" is presented as a temporal phenomenon but shown as a grid one (final values over $(T,\alpha)$); there are two training trajectories, and they are about a *peak* of the EU before the accuracy threshold; and there is no training-accuracy curve — the delay is not measured. In the theory a "collapse" exists only for the threshold surrogate (the binary entropy), while the regression EU is monotone; and the delays of the EU peaks and of the accuracy thresholds are one and the same expression, yet the paper insists now on "different observables", now on "two probes of one mechanism".

**The comparison of the methods, and inconsistencies.** IVON against Laplace is a comparison of two differently trained models (variational learning against a post-hoc superstructure over a MAP) with weight decays differing by six orders of magnitude — incommensurable in precisely the main lever of grokking; the decay value of IVON disagrees within the paper ($10^{-6}$ against $10^{-5}$); the notation $d$ collides (layers against dimension); and there are traces of editing (a paragraph repeated word for word with the misprint "we identify a the condition", and the broken phrase "tokenized into three numerical.").

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that the epistemic uncertainty collapses at the moment of grokking") — more precisely: on one ICL task at $p=29$ it is shown that in the settings with a high final OOD accuracy the final EU is low, and that in one training trajectory the peak of the EU comes before the accuracy threshold; the theoretical support is a linear model in which the non-monotonicity of the EU is built in by a threshold redefinition.
