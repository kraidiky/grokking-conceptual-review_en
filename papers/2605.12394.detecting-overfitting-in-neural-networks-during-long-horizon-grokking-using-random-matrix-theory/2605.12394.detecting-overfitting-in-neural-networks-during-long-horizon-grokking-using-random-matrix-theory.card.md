# Prakash & Martin 2026 — Detecting overfitting in Neural Networks during long-horizon grokking using Random Matrix Theory

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Hari K. Prakash** (University of California, San Diego), **Charles H. Martin** (Calculation Consulting).

arXiv [2605.12394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12394), May 2026 (v2), 24 pages, 24 figure panels, 14 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 1, in the corpus — 1. An instrument paper of the WeightWatcher line: to recognise the onset of overfitting from the weights alone — with no data, no gradients and no optimiser state. The technique: shuffle a weight matrix entrywise, fit the spectrum of the covariance of the shuffled matrix with a Marchenko–Pastur distribution and count the outliers beyond the edge — "Correlation Traps", which break self-averaging. On three long-horizon settings (MLP/MNIST, modular addition, GPT2 on knowledge composition) the traps are absent in the pre-grokking and grokking eras and appear in a jump at the entry into the late era of the falling test accuracy, named here **anti-grokking**; weight decay suppresses both the traps and the depth of the fall. Plus a layer-by-layer survey of the open gpt-oss-20b/120b.

## Concepts covered

**[Anti-grokking](../../concepts/anti-grokking.md)** — the post-grokking era of a falling test accuracy at an almost flawless training one is named anti-grokking and presented as an introduction of the term by the authors ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)). The supporting observation: the pre-grokking and the anti-grokking eras are indistinguishable by the train/test curves but differ structurally — the correlation traps appear only in the second of them ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)); their appearance coincides with the minimum of the test loss ([`p6-7`](#p6-7)). The analysis: the direct predecessors with the same experiments and the same notion (2602.02859 by the same first author, 2506.04434) are not cited — plausibly because of the anonymised submission, but in the text as it stands both the term and the traps look as though they were introduced here for the first time.

**[Heavy-tailed self-regularisation (HTSR)](../../concepts/heavy-tailed-self-regularization-htsr.md)** — a direct continuation of the Martin–Mahoney–WeightWatcher line, but with the instrument turned inside out: the earlier diagnostics read the spectrum of the *correlated* matrix $\mathbf{W}$, whereas here the spectrum of the *shuffled* $\mathbf{W}^{\mathrm{rand}}$ is analysed — the shuffling destroys the correlations while preserving the multiset of the magnitudes, and an outlier that survives it speaks of an atypicality of the distribution of the entries itself ([`p2-7`](#p2-7)). The exponent $\alpha$ is not used at all, and the appendix says outright that the construction does not require a tail with an exponent $<2$ ([`p23-1`](#p23-1)) — a weakening of the earlier formulation "many traps $+\ \alpha<2$" from the uncited predecessors.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — taken not as the object of explanation but as a laboratory: a long horizon gives an observable overfitting with the eras already marked out ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)); the main lesson is that "grokking is not always the end of the training": a model can find the generalising rule and then lose it as the optimisation continues ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)). The grokking of the MLP is induced by multiplying the initial weights by 8.0 — a technique of the Omnigrok line, the connection with which is not discussed; and the main MLP run is a single seed.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the controllability of the indicator: at WD=0.01 both the traps (2.0 and 1.0 against $7.5\pm 5.6$ and $1\pm 0$) and the depth of the late fall of the accuracy are suppressed ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)) — "it is not simply a matter of smaller weights but of the suppression of the traps". A cross-check against the growth of the norm is added: under a perturbation of the scale the global $\ell_{2}$ norm wanders through all the eras while the traps stay at zero until the third ([`p21-3`](#p21-3)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — a trap is defined spectrally: $\lambda_{trap}>\lambda_{+}^{\mathrm{MP}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{TW}}$, justified through the Baik–Ben Arous–Péché transition ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)); the interpretation is a breaking of self-averaging by two mechanisms: a geometric localisation (the mass of an eigenvector on a small set of coordinates) and a spectral condensation (one large eigenvalue with a delocalised vector) ([`p5-5`](#p5-5)). It is stated that a violation of MP is not a sign rule: the harmfulness is decided by an intervention or by a JSD probe on random inputs ([`p5-13`](#p5-13)).

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**Three supports of the indicator hold up under checking.** The outcome is the same in all three settings: zero traps in the first two eras against a jump in the third (modular addition: $0.00/0.00/4.88\pm 1.55$ over eight matrices). Weight decay suppresses both the traps and the depth of the fall — the indicator is controllable rather than merely coincident. The cross-check against the growth of the norm: under a perturbation of the scale the $\ell_{2}$ norm wanders through all the eras ($17.52\to 16.80\to 27.64$) while the traps do not. Plus the single genuine intervention: replacing a trap in the first layer of the MLP changes the confusion matrix and weakens the bias towards the pre-image class.

**"Harmfulness without data" does not square with the paper's own labelling.** A distinction between harmful and harmless traps "without any data" is claimed, yet the labelling of harmfulness itself is obtained from the change of the test error; what is computed without data is only the JSD probe, and its agreement with the harmfulness is checked against test data. The very definition of a harmful trap ("the replacement improves or worsens the test accuracy") means only that it is active — the sign of the effect does not follow from it.

**The claimed contribution about localisation is not supported.** There is not a single measured quantity of localisation (IPR, mass fraction, support size) in the work; the only reported outcome is negative ("a limited predictive signal, the relation changing from experiment to experiment") and contradicts appendix D.3 ("it is precisely the localisation that matters"). There are traces of an excised appendix: references to a "proof" and notations introduced nowhere; and the procedure for mapping a trap into the coordinates of a layer is called "set out below" but is not set out.

**The boundaries of the eras and the cross-checks disagree.** The boundaries of the MLP eras in the main part and in appendix C differ by an order of magnitude; the "pre-grokking" era in table 10 has a training accuracy of 0.73 — contrary to the paper's own definition of the era; appendix E on the seeds and the repeated shufflings contains not a single number; and the statement that "a removed trap returns as the training continues" comes with neither a figure nor numbers. There is a single negative sample (the pre-grokking era): neither ordinary overfitting without grokking, nor random labels.

**gpt-oss is a survey only.** The traps in gpt-oss-20b/120b are counted layer by layer but related to no quality of the model whatever; the conclusion of "possibly a harmful overfitting in large models" remains a conjecture (admitted in the limitations), while the broader-impact section meanwhile proposes the traps as a criterion for selecting open models and as an instrumental argument for regulatory bodies.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that correlation traps cause harmful overfitting, in large language models included") — more precisely: on three settings a coincidence is shown between the appearance of the traps, the third era and the minimum of the test loss; one intervention on one trap of the MLP changes the confusion matrix; "harmfulness" is defined as any change of the test accuracy under a replacement; and in gpt-oss the traps are merely counted.
