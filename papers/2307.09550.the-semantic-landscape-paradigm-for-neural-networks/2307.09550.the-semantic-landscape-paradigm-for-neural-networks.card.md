# Gokhale 2023 — The semantic landscape paradigm for neural networks

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Shreyas Gokhale** (the physics department of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology).

arXiv [2307.09550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09550), July 2023 (v1), 14 pages, 4 figures (all of them schemes). A purely conceptual work by a statistical physicist: learning is described as a walk over a graph whose nodes are the internal algorithms that emerge ("heuristic models"), the landscape being set by an unobservable "fallibility" $\mathcal{F}$ (the sum of KL divergences over *all possible* inputs) and the transitions being Arrhenius ones — across "semantic barriers". Within this frame grokking is declared a **percolation** transition: the data are the edges of the graph (the size $D$ of the set is proportional to the mean degree), below $D_{c}$ the component of the memorising model is cut off from the generalising one, and generalisation is impossible under arbitrarily long training; emergence with scale is the same percolation but in $N$ (the parameters add nodes); the scaling laws are derived from Bouchaud's trap model for glasses. This is the only work in the corpus in which grokking is claimed to be precisely a percolation — a geometric transition, in the connectivity of the solution space; there is meanwhile no experimental part at all, and the percolation itself is nowhere built as a model.

## Concepts covered

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the only reading in the corpus of grokking as a **percolation** transition: the transition is geometric, in the connectivity of the solution space — at $D<D_{c}$ the graph of heuristic models falls apart, and a path from the memorising model $\mathcal{M}_ {M}$ to the generalising one $\mathcal{M}_ {G}$ does not exist ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)); emergence with scale is the same percolation but in $N$: the parameters add nodes, the data add edges ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)). The analysis: the percolation is nowhere built as a model — there is no explicit graph, no fraction of occupied edges, no order parameter, not a single critical exponent; and two transitions are conflated — the percolation sets only the *possibility* of grokking (in $D$), while the sharpness *in time* at $D>D_{c}$ is a first-passage problem, which is not solved.

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the known critical size $D_{c}$ is redescribed as a percolation threshold: the training examples are the edges of the graph of models (they do not add models — they add transitions between them), and in the simplest approximation $D$ is proportional to the mean degree of the graph ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)); above the threshold the speed of grokking is proportional to the excess degree, $t_{g}\propto 1/(D-D_{c})$, with a claimed agreement with the representation theory of Liu et al. ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). The analysis: the identification of $D$ with the mean degree is in no way justified and carries the whole weight of the conclusion; the agreement with 2205.10343 is established at the level of the form of the dependence rather than of the exponent.

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — a coarse-graining of the loss landscape into a "semantic landscape" is proposed: the relief is set by the fallibility $\mathcal{F}$ over the space of algorithms, improvement is possible only by crossing *semantic barriers*, and the transitions are Arrhenius ones, $\tau_{ij}=\tau_{ij}^{0}e^{\beta(\mathcal{F}_ {j}-\mathcal{F}_ {i})}$ ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)); hence the prediction that the test loss at grokking ought to rise temporarily before falling, and that the size of the spike carries information about the height of the barriers ([`p5-3`](#p5-3)). The analysis: the spike is presented as a prediction but is backed by references to already published observations — this is a retrodiction; and what is useful for the corpus is the fork itself — where to place the barrier: in the loss landscape over the weights (as later in the activation-escape line) or on the graph of algorithms, as here.

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — the appearance of new abilities with scale is explained by a percolation in $N$: the growth of the number of parameters widens the network's "vocabulary" and adds model-nodes, and the path to models of low fallibility opens only at a sufficiently large $N$ ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)); in the dispute over the "mirage of emergence" a clear position is taken — emergence must be defined by the appearance of an *ability*, independently of loss metrics, and it can be established only by looking inside the network through a mechanistic analysis ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)). The analysis: there is no experimental part at all — all four figures are schemes, and the word "show" of the abstract refers to an argument rather than a measurement.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — a vocabulary and one clear fork.** The division of the roles of $D$ and $N$ — the data are edges, the parameters are nodes — is a compact image from which the critical dataset size, the emergence with scale and the known saturation under the growth of one parameter at the other fixed all follow at once. The question of where the barrier lives that the network crosses at grokking is posed explicitly — in the loss landscape over the weights or on the graph of algorithms: the Arrhenius form (3) anticipates the kinetics of the activation escape (2606.17120) but places the barrier in a different space. Against the "mirage of emergence" there is an articulate position: the ability, not the shape of the curve.

**There is no experiment, and the percolation is not built.** No numerical computation on an explicit graph, no training, no fit to the author's own data; the "we show" of the abstract is about an argument. The claim "this is a percolation transition" is a renaming of the known fact about $D_{c}$: there is no explicit graph, no fraction of occupied edges, no order parameter (the size of the giant component), no exponents, no finite-size analysis — the objection of 2603.24746 to "phase-transition rhetoric without falsifiable finite-size quantities" strikes exactly such a presentation. The abstract promises "first passage and percolation", but the first-passage problem is nowhere solved — and it is precisely that problem which is responsible for the sharpness of the transition in time at a fixed $D>D_{c}$.

**There are many free quantities, and all of them are unobservable.** $\beta$, $\tau^{0}$, $\mu$, $\alpha$, $d_{f}$, $\ell_{0}$, $\ell_{\infty}$, $D_{k}$ — the exponent $\mu/(d_{f}\alpha)$ can be fitted to almost any measured value, which is presented as a virtue. Conjecture **C1** is nearly irrefutable (a Gödel numbering makes the mapping exist all but automatically), and the empirical prop creates the appearance that something trivial has been checked. Two misprints survive even in the published PDF: formula (2) is written with three summation signs and no addition; in §4.2 the cases $d_{f}=2$ and $d_{f}=1$ are given one and the same $\mathcal{F}(N)\propto 1/\sqrt{N}$, whereas formula (7) at $d_{f}=1,\alpha=1$ gives $1/N$.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that grokking is a percolation transition") — more precisely: a vocabulary is proposed in which the known critical dataset size is redescribed as a percolation threshold on a graph that is nowhere built; the work contains neither a computation nor an experiment. An echo of the image returned to the corpus independently: 2602.18523 describes a "percolation-like" threshold in the geometry of the optimisation three years later and without a reference.
