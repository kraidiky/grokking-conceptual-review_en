## Concepts covered

**[The slingshot](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — the subject of the work, and it reinterprets it wholesale. In Thilak et al. the slingshot was a property of the optimisation itself; here it is shown that it suffices to move the computation of the logits and the loss into float64 — and the spikes disappear, while the parameters are still stored in float32 ([`p5-1`](#p5-1), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). The claim is perfectly clear: the slingshot is a trace of the bounded precision of the mantissa rather than a dynamics of the landscape.

**[Softmax collapse](../../concepts/softmax-collapse.md)** — the point of departure. Prieto et al. showed that at a margin $z_{m}-\max_{k\neq m}z_{k}>23\ln 2\approx 16$ the gradient of the correct class is rounded exactly to zero, whereupon the rise of the test accuracy ceases ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). This work points out a **second** effect of the same failure: the gradients of the incorrect classes remain non-zero, and hence the zero-sum condition over the classes is broken ([`p4-6`](#p4-6), [`p4-7`](#p4-7)).

**[Numerical stability](../../concepts/numerical-stability-fix.md)** — the mechanism is named and measured. The breaking of the zero sum imparts to the global mean of the separating layer $\boldsymbol{W}_ {G}$ a drift in the direction $-\boldsymbol{\mu}_ {G}$, which in its turn drifts the feature mean towards $-\boldsymbol{W}_ {G}$; the loop closes, both norms grow exponentially, and the cosine between them goes to $-1$ ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). The authors call this *numerical feature inflation* ($\mathcal{NFI}$). A loss of ordering is separately excluded: with the logits clamped so that $z_{min}\geq z_{max}-100$ the slingshots persist ([`p15-7`](#p15-7)).

**[Neural collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — the second support of the conclusion. The analysis is conducted on the assumption that the model has already reached the NC state, and uses all three of its conditions: the collapse of variability, the simplex ETF and the self-duality ([`p4-9`](#p4-9)). It matters that the drift of $\boldsymbol{W}_{G}$ requires the self-duality to be redefined: the ETF is now formed not by the weights themselves but by the centred $\boldsymbol{W}_ {k}-\boldsymbol{W}_ {G}$ ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)).

**[Optimisation methods](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — Adam here is not the cause but an amplifier. While the gradients are near zero the second-moment accumulator decays and the effective learning rate approaches the limit $\eta/\varepsilon_{\text{Adam}}$; when the gradient of the correct class returns, the step turns out to be about 50 times larger than usual ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). The check is direct: switching to ordinary gradient descent with $\eta=10^{5}$ gives a spike at roughly the same epoch ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)).

**[The edge of stability](../../concepts/edge-of-stability.md)** — the demarcation is drawn meticulously and with a proof. Under the cross-entropy with hard labels the largest eigenvalue of the Hessian tends to zero, and hence the late instability does not come from the curvature ([`p16-3`](#p16-3), [`p16-5`](#p16-5)). Moreover, a coexistence is shown on a narrow network: in float64 the early EOS oscillations remain while the late slingshots disappear ([`p16-7`](#p16-7), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the connection is twofold. On the one hand, grokking under the cross-entropy without weight decay is held to be theoretically infeasible (Lyu et al. estimate a requirement of $10^{100}$ steps), and it was observed in Thilak et al. precisely together with slingshots ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). On the other, it is shown here that in modular division every spike is accompanied by a stepwise rise of the test accuracy up to $100$%, and this is read as an implicit perturbation knocking the model out of its current state ([`p7-5`](#p7-5), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — hence the answer to the question of why slingshots are rare. An explicit regularisation bounds the magnitude of the parameters ($\Vert w\Vert_{\infty}\lesssim 1/\lambda$ under AdamW), the logits stay within finite bounds, and the margin almost never crosses the absorption threshold ([`p17-2`](#p17-2)). All the work's experiments are set up at zero weight decay.

**[Implicit bias and margin maximisation](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — the work points out a divergence from the known theory. The gradient-flow analysis (Soudry et al., Lyu & Li) predicts only a logarithmic growth of the norms in separable models, whereas in fact the norms and the logits grow fast ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)). The explanation offered is this: the gradient-flow analysis does not account for the finite precision of the loss computation, and once $\mathcal{NFI}$ is removed the growth of the parameters is markedly reduced.

**[The basins of the loss landscape](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — two unexpected observations here. The first: a **smaller** learning rate triggers slingshots more often, since large steps inject noise and prevent settling into sharp basins ([`p14-3`](#p14-3), [`p14-4`](#p14-4)). The second: small batches suppress $\mathcal{NFI}$ both by randomly delaying the convergence and by an implicit bias towards flat minima ([`p17-4`](#p17-4), [`p17-5`](#p17-5)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the setting of Thilak et al. is reproduced: modular division at $p=97$, a two-layer transformer and a six-layer MLP, a 50/50 data split ([`p6-4`](#p6-4), [`p27-2`](#p27-2)). It is on this task that the slingshots are most distinctly visible, since it is structured and almost noiseless ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[Vision data and real tasks](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the check on CIFAR-10 covers an MLP, VGG11, a ViT and a ResNet18, as well as dataset sizes from 200 to 50 000 examples ([`tab-1`](#tab-1), [`p14-1`](#p14-1)). The one exception is the ResNet18, and it is explained within the theory: the residual mass $\epsilon$ there decays too fast, so the factor $(1+\eta\epsilon/\sqrt{K})^{t}$ has no time to drive the growth ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)).

It is worth saying separately what the work contains beyond what it promises. Section A.4 reports an **unsuccessful reproduction** of a known result: the authors did not see the divergence of the logits to minus infinity from the work of Wortsman et al. either in PyTorch or in NanoDo, and they note that in the three years since that paper appeared they could find no independent confirmations ([`p17-8`](#p17-8), [`p17-9`](#p17-9)). The divergence they do observe goes to **plus** infinity ([`p18-2`](#p18-2)). For the corpus this is a separately valuable record.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon; ## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). A work by four authors (Tsinghua University and The University of Tokyo), 29 pages, 11 of them main text.

**The main argument is checkable in a single line of code, and that is its strength.** Moving the computation of the logits and the loss into float64 while keeping the parameters stored in float32 removes the slingshots ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)). Such an experiment separates a numerical cause from all the others at once, and anyone can repeat it.

**The intervention is chosen to fit the theory rather than the outcome.** If the loop is started by the breaking of the zero sum, then a projection of the logit gradient $g\leftarrow g-\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k}g_{k}$ ought to remove the slingshot — and it does ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)). This is a check not of a consequence but of a link of the derivation itself.

**The demarcation from the edge of stability is made by a proof rather than by an argument.** It is shown that under the cross-entropy with hard labels the largest eigenvalue of the Hessian tends to zero ([`p16-3`](#p16-3)), and then a coexistence of the two phenomena is shown on a narrow network: in float64 the early oscillations remain and the late spikes go away ([`p16-7`](#p16-7)). In the corpus this is a rare case in which two outwardly similar phenomena are separated by experiment.

**The exception is explained within the theory rather than discarded.** The ResNet18 gives no slingshots — and this is derived from a condition of theorem 3.7: its residual mass $\epsilon$ decays too fast, so no exponential growth accumulates ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)). The prediction is checkable: it suffices to look at the course of $\epsilon$ ([`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**An unsuccessful reproduction of somebody else's work is set out openly.** The authors write outright that they were unable to reproduce the divergence of the logits to minus infinity from the work of Wortsman et al. either in PyTorch or in NanoDo, and that in three years they found no independent confirmations ([`p17-8`](#p17-8), [`p17-9`](#p17-9)). For a preprint this is a rare directness.

**The whole conclusion, however, rests on the assumption of neural collapse.** Theorems 3.4, 3.6 and 3.7 assume that the model is already in the NC state, and beyond that that $\boldsymbol{W}_{G}$ is orthogonal to the discriminative subspace ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)) and that $\epsilon$ is nearly constant ([`p5-11`](#p5-11)). No measure of the deviation from NC is given in the experiments, and the orthogonality condition is taken as an assumption, although lemma C.1 proves it only for the feature means in a ReLU network and not for the weights.

**The unconstrained-features model is admitted to be a weak point by the authors themselves.** The analysis is conducted only for the penultimate layer and the classifier, on the assumption that the backbone is capable of producing any features whatever ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)). For shallow networks this assumption may fail — which, as is noted, is consonant with the difficulties Nanda et al. had in observing slingshots in shallow networks.

**The connection with grokking is claimed but not shown.** "Every spike is accompanied by a stepwise rise of the accuracy" ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)) is an observation on one task and one figure; what exactly the benefit of the perturbation consists in is stated cautiously ("may help"), and there is no check. Meanwhile the title poses the question "grokking or glitching?", and the text gives no answer to it in either direction.

**The numerical estimates are approximate in places.** The estimate of the size of the Adam update is made on a scalar toy model ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)), and the agreement with the experiment (a 40-fold growth against a predicted 50-fold) is called close. The order of magnitude does indeed agree, but this is an order-of-magnitude estimate rather than a computation.

**Some of the checks are made at a single setting.** The experiments with the dataset size, the learning rate, the bias and the loss of ordering are given as single runs with seed 42 ([`p27-1`](#p27-1) and appendix D); there are no seed-to-seed deviations anywhere in the work.

**The claim about low precision is for now a conjecture.** The statement that under BF16, FP8 and FP4 $\mathcal{NFI}$ will become the decisive source of instability ([`p17-6`](#p17-6)) is framed as a supposition and is not backed by experiment: the large-language-model experiments themselves are set up in BF16, but there the precision **did not** reduce the growth of the logits but increased it ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)).

**Its place in the corpus.** The work is not about grokking but about the numerical failure that accompanies grokking. Its value for the corpus lies in three things: the slingshot is declared and shown to be a trace of the arithmetic rather than a property of the optimisation; a mechanism ($\mathcal{NFI}$) is given with a checkable intervention; and the edge of stability is separated from the slingshot by a proof and by an experiment. The price: the whole conclusion leans on an unmeasured assumption of neural collapse, the connection with grokking remains an observation on one task, and the question carried in the title receives no answer.
