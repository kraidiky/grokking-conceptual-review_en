## Concepts covered

**[The compression of the representation manifold](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the work gives the corpus the earliest direct observation that the dimension of the activations falls during the grokking era, and it does so in a pure form of variant B (the geometry of the representations): the order parameter lives in activation space rather than in the weights or in the complexity of the input-output map. The measure is taken ready-made from Ansuini et al. 2019 — the TwoNN estimator based on the ratio of the distances to the two nearest neighbours ([p2-4](#p2-4), [p3-1](#p3-1)) — and two quantities are made from it: the LLID (the ID of the last layer's activations on a validation set) and the PID (the largest ID across the layers); "simpler" for the authors means "a lower LLID", "more plausible" means "a higher training accuracy and PID" ([p1-5](#p1-5)). The main observation: in 16 transformer runs on division modulo $97$ the fall of the LLID coincides in time with the surge of the validation accuracy, and the mean minimum LLID of the grokking models is $8.53$ against $16.16$ for those that did not grok ([p6-1](#p6-1), [fig-3](#fig-3)). What the work does not contain: not a single intervention — the LLID is nowhere lowered by force to check whether generalisation will follow, and optimisation by the LLID is named a direction for future work; the notions of "neural collapse", "the information bottleneck" and "low-rank weights" are absent altogether; and the PID is never once measured in the transformers, so that the claimed PID/LLID trade-off is never applied to grokking. Moreover, the measure itself is declared internally inconsistent by the authors ([p3-4](#p3-4)) — of which more below.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work takes the phenomenon as given and hangs a new measurement on it, neither contesting nor redefining it. The setting is reproduced after Power et al. 2022 through the unofficial reimplementation of Snell 2022, but narrowed: one operation instead of twelve, a budget of 40 thousand steps, a 4×4 sweep over the data fraction and the weight decay ([p5-4](#p5-4), [p11-2](#p11-2)). The contribution to the notion is not a mechanism but an accompanying quantity: "long after the training accuracy has saturated… there occurs a sudden fall of the LLID coinciding with it in time" ([p1-3](#p1-3), [p2-2](#p2-2)). One observation stands apart, barely analysed in the corpus's later works: in a late grokking at a small data fraction the LLID first spikes and then settles below its previous plateau ([p6-1](#p6-1)). What the work does not contain: the language of phase transitions, progress measures, circuits, or an analysis of what exactly happens to the representation — only a scalar per layer and its course over time; the number of seeds is not stated and there are no spread bands in any figure.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — it appears in two roles, both taken from predecessors. The first: a paraphrase of the finding of Power et al. — "a stronger regularisation made grokking set in substantially earlier in training" ([p3-5](#p3-5)); it is for this reason that weight decay is taken as one of the two sweep axes. The second: a measurement of the authors' own — strengthening the weight decay monotonically lowers the LLID in a ResNet-18, a Pearson $-0.88$ on CIFAR-100 and $-0.77$ on CIFAR-10 ([p4-4](#p4-4), [fig-1](#fig-1)). What the work does not contain: the weight norm — it is never measured, so that the link "weight decay → a low LLID" is not decomposed into any intermediate mechanism; nor is there a separation of what weight decay sets in the transformer runs — the time of grokking or the level of the LLID — while the groups $8.53$ and $16.16$ differ precisely in weight decay and data fraction.

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — the work compares two techniques, dropout and weight decay, by a consequence common to them and finds it: both lower the LLID uniformly ([p4-4](#p4-4), [fig-1](#fig-1)). Hence too its own principal thesis, the two-sidedness of regularisation: the LLID cannot be lowered without limit, because regularisation lowers the ID of the early layers too, that is, the PID, hindering the learning of sufficiently diverse features ([p2-1](#p2-1)); beyond the optimal regularisation the PID and the validation accuracy collapse together, more markedly on CIFAR-100 ([p5-3](#p5-3), [fig-4](#fig-4)). The work ends with a call for layer-wise differentiated regularisation techniques ([p4-1](#p4-1)) — no such technique is proposed or checked here. What the work does not contain: a separation of how exactly dropout and weight decay differ in their effect on the PID; the high values of the PID/LLID ratio in figure 2 come exclusively from the weight-decay runs ([fig-2](#fig-2)), so the relation may be between families rather than within them.

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work takes no position in this dispute and answers no such question, but its sweep supplies material: grokking is observed in it at weight decay $=0$ too ([fig-3](#fig-3), the panels $p=0.25$ and $p=0.3$), that is, regularisation is not obligatory for grokking in this setting (ADAM, a constant learning rate, division modulo $97$, a budget of 40 thousand steps). The authors themselves draw and discuss no such conclusion: they paraphrase Power et al. to the effect that weight decay speeds grokking up ([p3-5](#p3-5)) and take it as a sweep axis rather than as a condition. What the work does not contain: a "with regularisation / without" comparison as a separate experiment, or indeed any statement about necessity.

**[Vision and real data](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — half of the work rests on a ResNet-18 and CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, but there is no grokking there and none is sought: the images serve as a proving ground for the link "regularisation — LLID — generalisation", and all the trained models reach a perfect training accuracy at once ([p4-3](#p4-3)). What is useful to the corpus from this are the orders of magnitude and the signs of the correlations: the LLID under stronger regularisation falls from $\approx 24$ to $\approx 7$ ([fig-1](#fig-1)), the PID under ordinary regularisation stays roughly within $80\text{–}110$ and collapses to $40\text{–}60$ under an excessive one ([fig-4](#fig-4)), and the relation of the accuracy with the LLID is negative ($r=-0.90$ on both datasets) and with the PID positive ($r=0.82$ and $r=0.86$) ([p5-2](#p5-2), [fig-5](#fig-5), [fig-6](#fig-6)). What the work does not contain: a delayed generalisation on images, an analysis of the dynamics over the course of the training (all the CIFAR points are taken at the end of training), and a carry-over of the grokking conclusion to real data.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — of the set of Power et al. exactly one operation is taken, division modulo $97$, and it is taken as a ready-made dataset from Snell's reimplementation ([p5-4](#p5-4), [p11-2](#p11-2)). There is no addition, no multiplication and no composition of permutations in the work, the dependence on the modulus is not checked, and the structure of the learned solution is not analysed. The notion is touched, then, only as a carrier: the proving ground is needed in order to obtain a sudden generalisation, not in order to learn anything about arithmetic.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives that phase one precise observation: the training accuracy in all 16 runs reaches $100$% very quickly, and the training LLID follows the same course as the validation one ([fig-7](#fig-7)). That is, the fall of the LLID falls in an era in which the training part has long been saturated, and the whole rearrangement described happens inside the plateau ([p1-3](#p1-3)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of the plateau itself — what the network is doing while the LLID is high is not asked here; and the notions of a "memorising solution" and a "generalising solution" as two distinct mechanisms do not appear in the text.

**[Data fraction / critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the training-data fraction is taken as the second sweep axis, $p\in\lbrace0.15,0.2,0.25,0.3\rbrace$, precisely because "these parameters strongly affect when models begin to grok and how long the grokking era lasts" ([p5-4](#p5-4)). The observed consequence: three runs — $p=0.15$ at $WD=0$ and at $WD=10^{-4}$, and $p=0.2$ at $WD=0$ — did not grok within the budget of 40 thousand steps and are shown in red ([fig-3](#fig-3)). What the work does not contain: neither a threshold, nor a name for it, nor an attempt to compute it — the data fraction here is a control knob of the sweep rather than an object of study.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the notion is touched only enough to make it visible how the LLID differs from a progress measure. By the corpus's definition a progress measure *precedes* the jump and is causally related to it; the authors' LLID *coincides* with the jump in time ("co-occurrent"), there is no causal check, and the proposal to optimise by the LLID is deferred to future work ([p6-1](#p6-1)). The term "progress measure" does not occur once in the paper, although Barak et al. appeared earlier; a framing in terms of progress measures cannot be ascribed to the authors. The one thing here closer to a harbinger is the spike of the LLID *at the beginning* of the grokking, but it is described on a single panel and interpreted by argument.

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the two halves of the work run on different optimisers, and this is inherited from the predecessors with no discussion: SGD with a momentum of $0.9$, a step learning-rate schedule and $200$ epochs on CIFAR ([p11-1](#p11-1)); ADAM with betas $(0.9,0.98)$, a constant rate of $0.001$ and $40k$ steps on the transformer ([p11-2](#p11-2)). What the work does not contain: a comparison of the optimisers, a discussion of their contribution to the LLID, or indeed any analysis of what slingshots, adaptivity or the schedule might do to the measured quantity.

**[Over-parametrisation and depth](../../concepts/overparameterization-depth.md)** — the work removes an apparent contradiction: the success of extremely over-parametrised networks looks counter-intuitive ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)) only while complexity is counted by the number of parameters rather than by the dimension of the learned representation.
## Points we could not reconcile

**The headline "suddenness" does not hold across all the panels of figure 3.** The text asserts "a sudden fall of the LLID coinciding in time with a sudden rise of the validation accuracy" ([p6-1](#p6-1)), but in the figure itself ([fig-3](#fig-3)) the course differs from panel to panel. At $p=0.2$, $WD=10^{-4}$ the green curve falls gradually throughout the run: the descent begins in the first thousands of steps, long before the rise of the accuracy at around 15–22 thousand steps, and continues for another ten thousand steps after it. At $p=0.15$, $WD=0.01$ — the very panel the authors refer to as an example of a spike — the LLID has by the moment of the jump already fallen from $\approx 25$ to $\approx 12$, that is, most of the fall occurs in the era *before* the grokking. The "coincidence in time" holds for part of the sweep rather than for the sweep as a whole.

**In two runs the LLID returns upwards after its fall while the validation accuracy is $1.0$.** In the panel $p=0.3$, $WD=0$ ([fig-3](#fig-3)) the LLID falls to $\approx 13.5$ around 10 thousand steps and then grows to $\approx 20$ and stays there to the end of the run, while the accuracy had reached $1.0$ even earlier. In the panel $p=0.25$, $WD=0.01$ a minimum of $\approx 11$ is attained exactly at the point of the jump, after which the LLID returns to $\approx 15$ and remains there. The link "a low LLID ↔ generalisation" is not supported by these two runs, and they are not mentioned in the text. Directly connected with this is the choice of measure in the summary: the numbers $8.53$ and $16.16$ ([p6-1](#p6-1)) are computed from the *minimum over the run* LLID rather than from the final one — that is, from a quantity that in both of the runs named is a brief dip rather than a settled level.

**The measure on which the whole work rests is declared internally inconsistent by the authors themselves.** In [p3-4](#p3-4) it is said outright: the intrinsic dimension cannot grow along the layers of a network, since a layer is a function of the preceding activations alone — yet the estimate does grow, and it is precisely that growth which gives the "humped" shape from which the PID is taken. The discrepancy is put down to estimator error, after which the estimator is retained as adequate, since "all we require is a consistency of trends and comparisons". This is an honest caveat, but it means that the PID is a quantity that exists only thanks to an admitted measurement error, and that the whole PID/LLID trade-off is built on it.

**The record of the weight-decay sweep in appendix A does not match figure 1.** In [p11-1](#p11-1) the weight-decay values are given as $ae^{-b}$ with $a\in\lbrace1,2,5,6,8\rbrace$ and $b\in\lbrace-6,-5,-4,-3,-2\rbrace$; taken literally this would give values from $e^{2}\approx 7$ to $8e^{6}\approx 3200$. In [fig-1](#fig-1), however, the weight-decay axis runs from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-2}$, that is, $a\cdot 10^{b}$ was meant. Moreover, [p4-4](#p4-4) does not say by which quantity the Pearson correlation is computed — by the weight decay itself or by its logarithm; over a sweep of four orders of magnitude this changes the number, and in the CIFAR-100/dropout panel the dependence is non-monotone besides (the LLID rises up to a dropout of $\approx 0.15$ and only then falls), while $r$ is computed over the whole curve.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

**What is shown** (by measurement, on the authors' own data): regularisation of both kinds lowers the LLID in a ResNet-18 ([fig-1](#fig-1)); the validation accuracy is negatively related to the LLID and positively to the PID, and the PID/LLID ratio is related to the accuracy more strongly than either quantity separately ([fig-2](#fig-2), [fig-5](#fig-5), [fig-6](#fig-6)); beyond the optimal regularisation the PID and the accuracy fall together ([fig-4](#fig-4)); and in 16 transformer runs the fall of the LLID falls in the same era as the surge of the accuracy, with the training accuracy long at $100$% by then ([fig-3](#fig-3), [fig-7](#fig-7)).

**What is not shown but reads as though it were.** All of the above are coincidences in time and correlations over one point per run; that is how it is named in the conclusion, where the work's result is formulated as "a strong correlation between the intrinsic dimension of the activations and generalisation" ([p7-1](#p7-1)). There is not a single intervention in the work: the LLID is nowhere lowered by force, the PID is nowhere held fixed, and the authors themselves name optimisation by the LLID a direction for future work ([p6-1](#p6-1)). The comparison of $8.53$ against $16.16$ runs between groups differing precisely in weight decay and data fraction — that is, in quantities of which the paper itself says that they set the time of grokking ([p3-5](#p3-5)) and the level of the LLID ([fig-1](#fig-1)); a common cause is not excluded here. The number of seeds is stated for no sweep, and there are no spread bands in any figure.

**What is claimed by argument.** The reading of the LLID spike as "a hint of an escape from a local minimum", and the conclusion that a higher weight decay "helps such a jump", are conjectures supported by no measurement whatever: neither the landscape, nor the weight norm, nor any other quantity is computed in the process ([p6-1](#p6-1)). Conjectural too is the explanation of why the picture on CIFAR-100 is sharper ([p5-3](#p5-3)), and the explanation of the similarity of the IDs after the first and the second transformer blocks by the small size of the network ([p5-4](#p5-4)).

**What is promised and not delivered within the paper.** The PID for the transformer runs is not measured at all — the claimed PID/LLID trade-off, the main conclusion of the work's first half, is never once applied to grokking. The code is promised "upon acceptance" ([p2-3](#p2-3)), with no link to back that promise.
