# Kumar, Bordelon, Pehlevan, Murthy & Gershman 2024 — Do Mice Grok? Glimpses of Hidden Progress During Overtraining in Sensory Cortex

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Tanishq Kumar** (Harvard College), **Blake Bordelon**, **Cengiz Pehlevan**, **Venkatesh N. Murthy**, **Samuel J. Gershman** (Harvard; the Kempner Institute).

arXiv [2411.03541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03541), November 2024, version v2 (December 2024), 24 pages, 10 figures (15 panels), 11 display formulas. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 1. The only transfer in the corpus of the grokking vocabulary to data from a living cortex: a reanalysis of someone else's recordings from the posterior piriform cortex of mice (Berners-Lee et al. 2023) shows that while the behaviour stays at the ceiling, the representations of the odour classes go on separating and the margin of a maximum-margin classifier keeps growing — a "hidden progress" beneath a behavioural shelf, explained through a late maximisation of the margin. The biological sequel to the same authors' work on the lazy→rich transition ([2310.06110](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md)).

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the paper's frame is taken wholesale from deep learning: grokking after Power et al. 2022 and "hidden progress" after Barak et al. 2022 are declared the pattern under which the neural data are brought ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). Of the canonical phenomenology only one feature survives in the biological part — the internal changes while the external does not; an abrupt transition in generalisation is shown only in a synthetic MLP ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)), and the question of the title, "Do Mice Grok?", gets no answer in the text.

**[Lazy to rich](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the mechanism proposed: a late rich feature learning under a saturated behaviour, in the terms of the lazy and the rich regime ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)). The key honest caveat: there are no data from the initial period of training, so it cannot be said whether the cortex starts in the lazy or the rich regime — the very link "the lazy→rich transition" is untestable in the biological data ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)).

**[Margin maximisation](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — the central measure and hypothesis: the representations of the piriform cortex evolve so that the margin of a maximum-margin SVM grows over the days of overtraining ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)); of three distinguished notions of margin maximisation the one adopted is the one in which the features $\phi_{t}$ themselves change while the decoder is retrained to convergence at every step (formula (1), [`p6-2`](#p6-2)). The causal argument is a single loss ablation in the synthetic model: a hard-margin hinge kills the late gain ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — three independent internal measures on the neural data at a flat behaviour: the accuracy of LDA decoding by day ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)), the between-class anticorrelation of the population responses ([`p4-2`](#p4-2), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)) and the SVM margin ([`fig-4`](#fig-4)); in the synthetic model the Fisher discriminant is added as a measure of the signal-to-noise ratio ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)).

**[Feature emergence](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the predictive power of late feature learning: the learned features are reusable under a flip of the labels $y\mapsto-y$, whence an explanation of the reversal effect after overtraining — the kernel alignment is indifferent to the sign of the label, $K_{y}=K_{-y}$ ([`p10-2`](#p10-2), [`p11-4`](#p11-4)); in the lazy regime there is nothing to reuse.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**There are no new experiments — acknowledged outright.** The work is a reanalysis of someone else's recordings (Berners-Lee et al. 2023) plus a synthetic model; both starting observations (a rise of the decoding accuracy under overtraining; that mice overtrained for longer do better on held-out probes) are taken from the original work rather than obtained here. A co-author of the original data is among the authors, so the "novel reanalysis" is not an external one.

**There is no generalisation curve over time in the biological data at all.** The probes are presented only at the end of the overtraining, so the whole temporal dynamics of the argument is the internal measures. The "suddenness" of the abstract is an inference from the growth of the SVM margin rather than an observation on individual probes. An abrupt grokking-like transition is shown only in the synthetic MLP, and its "abruptness" is on a logarithmic epoch axis: in linear time the transition takes up almost the whole of training.

**The scale of the data is small and the statistics informal.** Four mice, about fifteen neurons per session (in the appendix, "about 20"); there are no formal hypothesis tests on the slope — only standard errors and a comparison with a chance baseline; the last days of mouse T are discarded, whereas the authors of the original data kept them; and for mouse S the anticorrelation falls slightly over the overtraining. The authors themselves call the findings "suggestive rather than conclusive".

**The causality is one ablation.** The argument for the causal role of margin maximisation rests on replacing the cross-entropy by a hinge in one synthetic setting, with no intermediate values of the hard margin, no repetitions across seeds and no error bands. An architecture ablation (a biologically plausible network) supports the conclusion that "it is the objective function rather than the architecture". The biological drivers of late learning are listed outright as conjectures (metabolic constraints as an analogue of weight decay, rare errors as a weak signal, a penalty on uncertainty).

**The theoretical part is detached from the rest.** The explanation of the reversal after overtraining is built on a two-layer linear network with a squared loss and whitened data, whereas the whole of the remaining argument rests on cross-entropy, ReLU and margin maximisation; the connection is verbal. The effect being explained is itself contested in the literature across species and tasks — a footnote acknowledges this, but the explanation proceeds as if the effect were established.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that mice grok") — more precisely: in four mice, at an unchanged behaviour, three internal measures grow (decoding, between-class anticorrelation, the SVM margin), while an abrupt transition in generalisation is shown only in a synthetic MLP.
