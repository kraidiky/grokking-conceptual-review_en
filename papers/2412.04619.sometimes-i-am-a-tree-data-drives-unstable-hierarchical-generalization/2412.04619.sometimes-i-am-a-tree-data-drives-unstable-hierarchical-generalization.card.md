# Qin, Saphra & Alvarez-Melis 2024 — Sometimes I am a Tree: Data Drives Unstable Hierarchical Generalization in LMs

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Tian Qin**, **Naomi Saphra** (Harvard University), **David Alvarez-Melis** (Harvard University and Microsoft Research).

arXiv [2412.04619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04619), December 2024 (v4 of September 2025 is analysed), 19 pages, 15 figures. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 11, in the corpus — 0. A direct argument with 2404.16367 (Ahuja et al.) within a shared setting: the question is moved from the architecture of the network and the training objective onto the **data**. Two quantities: the *complexity* (the fraction of sentences with a centre embedding) decides **which** rule is acquired — centre embeddings lead to the hierarchical one, their absence to the linear one (not one of 50 seeds takes the hierarchy without them — a confirmation of Wexler's 1980 linguistic prediction); the *diversity* (the number of distinct syntactic trees) decides whether **any** rule is acquired at all — a small one gives a stable memorisation, a large one an adherence to a general rule, and an intermediate one an unstable region with oscillations of the out-of-domain accuracy and a divergence of the seeds. Structural grokking is declared to be produced by a **competition** between the linearly- and hierarchically-inclining subsets of the data; 1 % of the examples of one kind switches the outcome of the training.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the data as the controlling variable of structural grokking: the delay is reproduced on the original QF data, and immediately after the training loss converges *all* the runs stand at 0 % out-of-domain accuracy (a strictly linear rule) and only then begin to rise, with the strongest oscillations and divergence of the seeds occurring precisely "during the grokking" ([`p17-2`](#p17-2)); the main explanatory claim is that structural grokking is produced by a competition between the linearly- and hierarchically-inclining subsets of the data: without competing subsets the model acquires the rule at once, with no gradual transition ([`p9-6`](#p9-6)). The analysis: the claim is not supported by a single measurement of time — the time to generalisation is nowhere measured, and the total variation catches the oscillations rather than the delay ("competition = instability = delay" is an identification, not a result); and the carry-over to classical grokking is an argument by correspondence, without a single experiment on modular arithmetic.

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — memorisation as a *stable* regime rather than a transitional phase: at a data diversity below 5 trees the training stabilises with no adherence to any rule — the model applies the hierarchical rule to the syntactic structures seen in training and does not carry it over to distant ones ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)); the check by the tree edit distance — at a diversity of 1 the out-of-domain accuracy falls as the TED grows, at a diversity of 5 the relation disappears ([`p18-2`](#p18-2)); the concluding frame is that "memorisation is merely one more rule for capturing the training distribution", which unites grokking with other phase transitions ([`p9-7`](#p9-7)). The analysis: at a fixed number of examples a small diversity is at the same time a many-fold repetition of the same trees — the setting does not separate "few distinct" from "many repeats".

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the size axis is replaced by an axis of **diversity**: at a fixed 50k examples the outcome is decided by the number of distinct syntactic trees — fewer than 5 gives a stable memorisation, 5–20 an unstable region, and from 50 upwards a stable adherence to a rule; the triple of regions is reproduced in the linearly-inclining setting too ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)); the sensitivity is extreme — mixing in 1 % of right-branching statements sharply lowers the probability of the hierarchy, and removing 1 % of the centre-embedded ones collapses the behaviour not to the linear rule but to a random one ([`p16-2`](#p16-2)). The analysis: the result of Zhu et al. that "grokking requires a sufficiently large dataset" is directly reinterpreted here — a large dataset is effective in so far as it is diverse.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — an argument with 2404.16367 within a shared setting: the training objective is fixed (it is the language-modelling one throughout) and the outcome is decided by the data — the complexity (centre embeddings) selects the rule, the diversity selects "a rule against memorisation" ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)); stability is equivalent to adherence: under any mixture of data the final out-of-domain accuracy of the stable runs is either 100 % or 0 %, and the "horseshoe" curves show that the less stable a run, the less general its rule ([`p6-5`](#p6-5)). The analysis: two unexplained things are put to the Bayesian explanation of Ahuja et al. (why the hierarchy arrives *later* than the linear rule, and why the seeds diverge) — the work answers only the second by measurement; there are no internal measurements at all (neither tree-ness nor ablations) — the conclusion is purely behavioural.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — two axes of the data and a measure of instability.** The quantity "data diversity", with a definition through the number of syntactic trees and a check of the transferability of the rule by TED; the total variation of the out-of-domain accuracy as a ready measure of the instability of delayed generalisation; and the evidence that 1 % of the examples of one kind switches the outcome of the training. Wexler's linguistic prediction ("degree 2" suffices for the hierarchy) is checked on a language model with a clean outcome: without centre embeddings not one of 50 seeds takes the hierarchy, and Lightfoot's position is supported too — the model carries the hierarchy over to degree-1 data if it has seen enough degree-2 examples in *another* task (the copying of statements). The terminology is separated carefully: classical grokking against structural, with an explicit bridge through the diversity.

**The claim about grokking is stronger than what is shown.** "Structural grokking is produced by a competition of subsets" is not supported by a measurement of time: the time to generalisation (the steps to the turning point) is nowhere measured, the dynamics curve is shown only for the original — already mixed — sample, and what is shown implies "homogeneous data give a stable and predictable outcome" but not "an immediate one". Instability and delay are brought together without being separated: the TV measures the oscillations over the whole training, and a stable but delayed run is indistinguishable within that frame. The carry-over to classical grokking is an argument by correspondence.

**Internal inconsistencies.** Section 4.2 ("under any mixture all the stable runs end at 100 % or 0 %") contradicts section 5.2 (at a small diversity the runs are stable and adhere to nothing) — the first holds only at the original diversity. The horseshoe relation is built from two quantities derived from the same series of out-of-domain accuracies (the oscillations and the endpoint), so part of the relation is built into the definitions. The number of seeds wanders: "all the experiments on the same 50 seeds" is promised, appendices C and D count 30 each, the caption of fig. 14 says "50" and the text says "30". Minor blemishes: "(Section XX)" in the caption of fig. 6, "OOD accuracy accuracy", "greate"; and the v4 title on the title page carries the tail "in LMs", which is dropped in the arXiv listing.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that grokking is caused by properties of the data"; "centre embeddings produce hierarchical generalisation") — more precisely: on two artificial grammatical tasks with one architecture and one training objective it is shown behaviourally that the fraction of centre-embedded sentences decides the choice of rule, and the number of distinct syntactic trees decides whether there will be a general rule instead of memorisation; intermediate values of both quantities give oscillating runs; and the cause of the delay itself is not measured.
