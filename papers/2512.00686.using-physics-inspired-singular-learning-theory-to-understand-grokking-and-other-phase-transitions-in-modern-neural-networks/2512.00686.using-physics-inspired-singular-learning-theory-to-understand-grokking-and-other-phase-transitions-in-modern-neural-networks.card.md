# Lakkapragada 2025 — Using physics-inspired Singular Learning Theory to understand grokking & other phase transitions in modern neural networks

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Anish Lakkapragada** (the Department of Statistics, Yale University).

arXiv [2512.00686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00686), December 2025 (v3), 9 pages, 7 figures, 1 table. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 1, in the corpus — 1. A single-author empirical test of singular learning theory (SLT) on toy settings: the Arrhenius hypothesis — that the time of a transition is exponential in the change of the free energy $\mathcal{F}_{n}$ — is tested on the grokking of modular addition (168 grokked runs out of 500) and on Anthropic's toy models of superposition (declared unconvincing), while the scaling of the local learning coefficient (LLC) with the complexity of the task is measured on three controllable families: polynomial regressors, low-rank linear networks and autoencoders. A statistic rare for the corpus: the distribution of the LLC jump at grokking over 168 runs ($\Delta\lambda\approx-22$).

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — taken not in its own right but as a special case of a phase transition and as a testbed for SLT: the Arrhenius hypothesis $r_{i\to j}\propto\exp(\beta_{\text{eff}}\Delta\mathcal{F}_{i\to j})$ ought to predict when a model will grok ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)). On modular addition ($p=53$, 500 runs, of which 168 grokked) the fit gives a stably negative slope but with a low $R^{2}$ ([`p3-4`](#p3-4), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)); the mechanics of grokking — Fourier features, norms, representations — the paper does not touch. What is valuable: the histogram of the complexity jump $\Delta\lambda\approx-22$ over 168 runs — the grokked solution is simpler than the memorising one ([`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the second setting is chosen precisely for the multiplicity of transitions within one run: Anthropic's toy models of superposition (TMS), where the transitions are sought as falls of a smoothed loss curve ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). The result is honestly declared unconvincing: the slope of the fit changed sign when the method of detecting the transitions was changed ([`p4-3`](#p4-3), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)) — a negative result described outright, which is rare for the corpus.

**[Effective theory / statistical mechanics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — the physical frame here is Watanabe's SLT: the free energy $\mathcal{F}_{n}\approx\min_{\alpha}[nL_{n}(w^{*}_{\alpha})+\lambda_{\alpha}\log n]$ as the model's internal choice between the loss and the complexity ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)), and the hypothesis about the transition time borrows the form of the Arrhenius equation from chemical kinetics ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)). The analysis notes: how exactly $\mathcal{F}_{n}$ was computed is not said in the text, the sign of $\Delta\mathcal{F}$ twice conflicts with the paper's own picture of minimisation, and the estimation of the barrier — the very thing that sets the exponent in Arrhenius — is relegated to the open questions.

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — the motivation of the work: PAC bounds are vacuous because networks are singular — one function is given by many sets of weights, and the Fisher matrix is degenerate at the true point ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)); the LLC $\lambda_{\alpha}$ is proposed as a complexity measure that accounts for the singularity. The measurements give both agreements with the theory (the quadratic $\lambda_{r}\approx\frac{1}{2}r(2d-r)$ for the low-rank networks, [`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`fig-6`](#fig-6)) and disagreements: for the formally regular polynomial regressors the LLC is an order of magnitude below the expectation $d/2$ and comes to a shelf — explained by singularities from a compressed input domain ([`p5-8`](#p5-8), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)).

**[Singular learning theory](../../concepts/singular-learning-theory.md)** — the paper brings the frame into the corpus: networks are *singular* statistical models ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)), and so the classical asymptotics do not apply to them. The evidence for the Arrhenius link between the free energy and the rate of the transition comes out mixed ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)), and that caveat is preserved.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The main hypothesis rests on $R^{2}=0.062$.** The claim that it "is consistent with our hypothesis" for modular addition rests on a fit $\Delta\mathcal{F}=-26.8\log r-133$, which explains about six per cent of the spread; the number is not given in the text — it says only "albeit with a low $R^{2}$ score". The fit is done the wrong way round ($\Delta\mathcal{F}$ is regressed on $\log r$ rather than the reverse), the value of $\beta_{\text{eff}}$ is nowhere given, and from the negative slope it follows that a larger fall of the free energy corresponds to a longer time to grokking.

**The sign of $\Delta\mathcal{F}$ fails to add up twice.** By figure 3 the free energy at the grokked point is higher than at the memorising one — against both the introduction ("the network consistently tries to lower the free energy") and the paper's own components: since both the loss and $\lambda$ fall at grokking, $nL_{n}+\lambda\log n$ ought to fall. Neither discrepancy is qualified. How exactly $\mathcal{F}_{n}$ was computed from $\lambda$ and $L_{n}$ is never said.

**The selection and comparability of the runs.** Of the 500 runs 168 grokked (33.6%), and the rest are discarded without discussion; what the runs differed in — seeds or hyperparameters — is not said, and $\beta_{\text{eff}}$ depends on the hyperparameters by definition. The settings of the SGLD sampler differ between the experiments by two orders of magnitude in the step size, so the LLC values are not comparable between experiments; and there is no code, no data, no seeds and no number of chains.

**Question 2: the agreements and the shelf.** The quadratic for the low-rank networks is reproduced with $R^{2}=0.999$ (the coefficients about 13% below the expected ones); the linearity for the autoencoder ($R^{2}=0.998$) is declared not to have appeared in the literature, although it follows from the linear growth of the number of weights in $r$. For the polynomial regressors the discrepancy is not "below expectation" but more than an order of magnitude: at $d=100$ the expectation is 50 and about 3.9 is measured — a shelf with no growth, explainable equally by singularities from the compressed domain and by a saturation of the SGLD estimate itself; the second possibility is not tested. The conclusion about a "greater capacity to generalise" of the compressed models follows from nothing: generalisation was not measured in the experiment.

**An honest negative result.** The experiment on TMS is admitted by the author himself to be unconvincing — under a cruder method of detecting the transitions the slope came out reversed; the evidence is called "mixed" in the conclusion, and the first open question is how to estimate the free-energy barrier at all.

**How the work will be cited** ("SLT predicts the grokking time", "an Arrhenius law of grokking") — more precisely: on one task a fit with $R^{2}=0.062$ was obtained over 168 selected runs, and the repetition on TMS was declared unconvincing by the author himself and changed sign when the method of detecting the transitions was changed.
