## Concepts covered

**[Neural collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — the work directly contests the conjecture that NC is necessary for generalisation ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). The argument is built as an intervention: a term $-\lambda\cdot\mathrm{NCC}$ is added to the cross-entropy loss, which **pushes apart** the class means and increases the within-class variance ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`p7-2`](#p7-2)). The network meanwhile generalises just as it does without the intervention: the training and validation accuracies do not change, and the NCC measure, after a brief fall, steadily **rises** ([`p7-3`](#p7-3), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)). It is separately checked that the NCC measure captures the second feature of the collapse as well: under its penalty the pairwise angles between the clusters depart from the optimal 10-simplex ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)).

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the work's central quantity: the **relative flatness** of Petzka et al. (2021), that is, the trace of the Hessian weighted by the inner products of the rows of the weight matrix ([`p5-6`](#p5-6), [`p5-7`](#p5-7)). Its advantage over the classical sharpness measures is its invariance to a per-neuron rescaling and to orthogonal transformations ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)), and it is computable in large networks thanks to the closed-form bound of Walter et al. (2024) for the penultimate layer under a cross-entropy loss ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — it is not the object of study here but a **measuring instrument**: since the generalisation sets in much later than the memorisation ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)), the two events are separated in time and one can see which quantity follows which ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). The outcome of that measurement is the work's main result, announced already in the abstract ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)): both quantities fall at the onset of the generalisation, but the NCC begins to fall **already in the memorisation phase**, whereas the relative flatness stays high and falls sharply exactly at the onset of the generalisation ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the reverse side of the same device: if flatness is necessary, then by **forbidding** it one can induce a delayed generalisation where it does not occur. The regulariser $-\lambda\cdot\kappa^{\phi}_{Tr}(\mathbf{w})$ drives the network into sharp minima ([`p2-3`](#p2-3), [`p8-6`](#p8-6), [`p8-7`](#p8-7)); when it is removed at epoch 200 (CIFAR-10) or 150 (ImageNet-100) the validation accuracy shoots up ([`p9-1`](#p9-1), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). This is grokking induced on demand, on a ResNet, a ViT and pre-trained language models.

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work makes a precise distinction on it, useful to the corpus: a collapse-like clustering of the representations is a property of a **memorising** network rather than of a generalising one ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)). The NCC thereby turns out to be closer to the training accuracy than to an indicator of generalisation.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — of two candidates for the role of an indicator of generalisation one is eliminated. And the elimination is made not by the strength of the relation (both quantities are related to generalisation) but by the **time of onset** and by the response to an intervention ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). This is an argument of a causal kind, rare in the corpus: it is not enough to ask whether a quantity follows the generalisation — one must ask whether it causes it.

**[The manifold and the compression of the representation](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the theoretical contribution: under the classical assumptions about collapse the relative flatness is bounded above by a quantity decaying exponentially in $\lambda$ — the scale of the last layer's weights ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)). Hence a reading of the whole picture: collapse is **one** geometric road to flatness rather than a synonym for it ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)). The appendix states this more cautiously than the claim itself: the NCC is invariant to a rescaling of the weights and therefore does not by itself entail flatness — flatness arises only because training under a cross-entropy loss goes on increasing $\lambda$ ([`p16-5`](#p16-5)).

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — the theory of Petzka et al. does not work unconditionally, and the authors stress it: locally constant labels and a **representativeness** of the features are needed ([`p5-9`](#p5-9), [`p10-2`](#p10-2)). A network that has perfectly contracted the training set and assigns the test objects to the wrong means will exhibit both collapse and flatness yet will not generalise ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)). The representativeness is measured by a kernel density estimate and improves exactly at the onset of the rise of the validation accuracy ([`p29-2`](#p29-2), [`p29-3`](#p29-3), [`fig-20`](#fig-20)).

**[The edge of stability](../../concepts/edge-of-stability.md)** — the work joins the dispute about sharpness with a clear position: the objections to flatness (Andriushchenko et al., 2023) are directed at the classical measures, sensitive to reparametrisation, rather than at the relative flatness ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)). An asymmetry is admitted meanwhile: penalising flatness reliably delays the generalisation, whereas encouraging flatter minima does not substantially improve it ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)).

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — the flatness regulariser is itself unstable, and appendix C analyses why: the cross-entropy loss lowers the uncertainty of the prediction, whereas the regulariser is effective only at a high uncertainty, so that a multiplicative term $2USW$ drives the weight norm up ([`p19-5`](#p19-5)). Two crutches hold the training together: a normalisation of the penultimate-layer representation ($\Vert\phi\Vert=1$) and a constraint on the Frobenius norm of the weights ([`p20-8`](#p20-8)).

**[Real data](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the coverage of the experiments is unusually broad for the corpus: a ResNet-18 on CIFAR-10 ([`p16-6`](#p16-6), [`p16-7`](#p16-7)), a ViT-tiny on ImageNet-100 ([`p24-2`](#p24-2)), TinyBERT and DistilGPT2 on SST-5 ([`p25-3`](#p25-3), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). It is precisely this that lends weight to the claim that the induced delayed generalisation is not a peculiarity of algorithmic tasks ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the original observation is made in the corpus's customary setting: a two-layer transformer, AdamW at a rate of $10^{-4}$ and a weight decay of $1.0$, $10^{6}$ steps, a 50/50 split, averaged over three seeds ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)).

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — a difference of settings is worth noting: the grokking is observed under AdamW in the algorithmic task ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)), whereas all the interventions are carried out under SGD with a momentum of 0.9 and no weight decay ([`p16-7`](#p16-7), [`p24-2`](#p24-2)). The return to generalisation after the regulariser is removed is secured by a change of optimisation regime: the learning rate is returned to a large value, a cosine annealing is switched on and weight decay is added ([`p21-3`](#p21-3)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no proof of the necessity of flatness — there is experimental evidence in four settings, and the authors write carefully "possibly necessary" ([`p9-5`](#p9-5)). Nor is there an explanation of why the induced delayed generalisation repeats ordinary grokking: the resemblance is outward, and the authors say outright that the extent of that resemblance is not yet understood ([`p10-3`](#p10-3)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the setting from which the grokking is taken; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the weight norm as an explanation, replaced here by flatness; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the progress measures named in the survey; [Merrill et al. 2023](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md) — the sparse and dense subnetworks from the same survey; [Minegishi et al. 2025](../2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks/2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks.card.md) — "grokked tickets", named in the survey as Gouki et al. ## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is experimental, with one proved statement and two interventions: one removes the neural collapse, the other the flatness.

**The design of the work is stronger than its individual results.** Grokking is taken not as an object but as a **separator in time**: it pulls the memorisation and the generalisation apart, so that one can see which quantity arises when ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). This one observation suffices to demote the NCC: a quantity that already falls during the memorisation cannot serve as an indicator of the onset of generalisation ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)).

**The rejection of the necessity of NC is shown by an intervention, and that is the right argument.** A penalty on the clustering drives the NCC up while the validation accuracy does not suffer ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)). There are two caveats. The first: the intervention suppresses the NCC — the NC1 feature and in part NC2 — but the work does not measure the full set of Papyan et al.'s four conditions ([`p4-4`](#p4-4)), although the check of the angles ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)) partly makes up for this. The second: the sensitivity to $\lambda$ is narrow — at $10^{-2}$ the training collapses, at $10^{-4}$ and below the regulariser does nothing, and only at $10^{-3}$ is the claimed picture obtained ([`p17-2`](#p17-2), [`p17-3`](#p17-3)).

**The finding most awkward for the work is buried in appendix B.** Under ordinary data augmentation (a random crop and a flip) the NCC regulariser **ceases** to raise the NCC at the same $\lambda=10^{-3}$ ([`p17-6`](#p17-6)). That is, "the network generalises without collapse" is obtained in a setting without augmentation, in which the validation accuracy is 78 %, whereas with augmentation the same network gives 94 % — and the collapse cannot be suppressed ([`p17-7`](#p17-7)). It does not follow that the conclusion is wrong, but the claim "NC is not necessary" rests on a training regime in which real models are not trained.

**The necessity of flatness is shown more weakly than it sounds.** What is shown: if flatness is suppressed, the generalisation is delayed and does not reach the baseline level — 60 % against 78 % on CIFAR-10 and 38 % against 43 % on ImageNet-100 ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)). This is a relation of the form "no flatness, no full generalisation" in four settings. No logical necessity follows from it, and the authors themselves write "possibly necessary" ([`p9-5`](#p9-5)); the section heading "Relative flatness is necessary" ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)) sounds more categorical than its own text.

**Switching the regulariser off changes more than one variable.** After the penalty is removed the learning rate is returned to $10^{-1}$, a cosine annealing is switched on and a weight decay of $2\times 10^{-4}$ is added ([`p21-3`](#p21-3)). The recovery of the accuracy is ascribed to the return of flatness, but three optimisation settings change at the same time, each of which is by itself related to generalisation. There is no comparison with a run in which only they are changed and the regulariser is left alone.

**The flatness numbers are given with no spread and differ by orders of magnitude.** $2209.94\to 1.91$ for CIFAR-10 and $22212.75\to 313.22$ for ImageNet-100 ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)) are one measurement per setting, with no bars. And the appendix says in the same place that averaging over seeds is **meaningless**, since different seeds take the training into different sharp minima ([`p21-2`](#p21-2)) — an honest admission, but one that also deprives the experiment of statistics.

**The theorem proves something other than what its paraphrase says.** Proposition 5.3 bounds the relative flatness by a quantity decaying exponentially in $\lambda$ ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)), and $\lambda$ is the scale of the classifier's weights rather than a measure of collapse. The appendix spells this out directly: the NCC is invariant to a rescaling, does not by itself entail flatness, and flatness arises only because training under the cross-entropy goes on increasing $\lambda$ ([`p16-5`](#p16-5)). So "collapse leads to flatness" is true as "collapse plus further training under the cross-entropy leads to flatness", and in the main text this caveat is barely visible ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)).

**The flatness regulariser is held in place artificially.** It fights the cross-entropy loss by construction: the latter lowers the uncertainty, while the former is effective only at a high one ([`p19-5`](#p19-5)). To keep the training from diverging, a normalisation $\Vert\phi\Vert=1$, a Frobenius-norm constraint and a temperature $\tau=2$ in the softmax are introduced ([`p20-8`](#p20-8)). The analysis of the weight constraint shows how narrow the corridor is: at WC = 20 everything is stable, at 100 the flatness is four times higher, and at 150 and 200 the training collapses entirely ([`p22-4`](#p22-4)).

**An inconsistency in the description of the model.** Section 6 says that a **ResNet-10** is trained on CIFAR-10 ([`p8-7`](#p8-7)), whereas both appendix B and appendix C speak of a ResNet-18 ([`p16-8`](#p16-8), [`p21-3`](#p21-3)). By the rest of the text this is a slip, but anyone reproducing the work should know of it.

**Representativeness is the most underrated part.** The condition of feature representativeness turns flatness from a coincidence into a theoretically grounded indicator ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)), and the experiment shows that it improves exactly at the onset of the rise of the validation accuracy ([`p29-3`](#p29-3)). But it is measured by a kernel density estimate with a bandwidth of 1.0 and a weight of 0.02 per object, chosen **empirically** ([`p29-2`](#p29-2)), and is given in a single figure with no analysis of the sensitivity to those numbers.

**Its place in the corpus.** The work does what almost nobody in the corpus does: it takes two quantities, both related to generalisation, and **separates** them by an intervention in both directions. The outcome is a demotion of neural collapse to a side consequence and a promotion of relative flatness to a candidate for a necessary condition. No less valuable is the side contribution: it is shown that a delayed generalisation can be **induced** on a ResNet, a ViT and language models, that is, that grokking is not a peculiarity of small algorithmic tasks. The price: settings without data augmentation and without dropout, single measurements with no spread, and an admitted impossibility of averaging over seeds.

###### mc-2407-12332
**Mohamadi et al. (2407.12332) — a two-phase dynamics as a ready explanation.** `"Recent work explains grokking through two-phase dynamics: an initial kernel regime focused on memorization, followed by feature learning that supports generalization (Mohamadi et al., 2023; Lyu et al., 2024; Kumar et al., 2024)"`: in Mohamadi what is proved is [an impossibility for kernel methods](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/original/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.md#p3-4) and [the existence of generalising solutions with a small l∞ norm](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/original/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.md#p3-5), while the junction of the phases itself — that the training really does pass from one to the other — is shown only by experiment. "Explains … through" presents the link as proved. On top of that the workshop version "2023" is cited, and there is no Mohamadi entry in the paper's bibliography at all — a dangling reference.
