# Hu, Chen, Saphra & Cho 2023 — Delays, Detours, and Forks in the Road: Latent State Models of Training Dynamics

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Michael Y. Hu**, **Angelica Chen**, **Naomi Saphra**, **Kyunghyun Cho** (New York University; Cho is also with Prescient Design, Genentech and CIFAR LMB).

arXiv [2308.09543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09543), August 2023, current version v3 (January 2024), TMLR 12/2023. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 21, in the corpus — 3. The title in the arXiv metadata is shorter than the title of the paper itself ("Latent State Models of Training Dynamics"); the corpus uses the paper's title. The work builds a hidden Markov model (HMM) over the training run from 14 weight metrics and extracts from it a "map of the training" — a graph of latent states; on the grokking tasks the map branches by random seed, and **detour states** are discovered, visiting which lengthens the convergence. ## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — not the subject but the main arena: modular addition and sparse parities are taken as settings with explicit phase transitions, against the smooth CIFAR-100, MNIST and MultiBERTs ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)). Two contributions to the discussion of grokking: a map of paths of different lengths from memorisation to generalisation with detour states on the slow paths ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)), and a demonstration that stabilising the training (restoring layer normalization, reducing the batch) speeds up convergence by roughly 30 times and removes the sensitivity to the seed ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). Separately there is the authors' conjecture, hedged with "we conjecture": that grokking is a consequence of a sharp optimisation landscape ([`p12-2`](#p12-2)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the claimed difference from the line of hand-picked measures: instead of one carefully chosen metric, a set of 14 general weight metrics is computed, and the structure among them is found by unsupervised learning ([`p12-4`](#p12-4)). The labels "memorisation" and "generalisation" are, however, assigned to the states by matching against the loss curves rather than by the make-up of the computation — the states have no mechanistic content.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the paper gives an operational definition: a phase transition is a period of training with an inflection of the concavity of the loss which is then sustained, with no return to chance quality ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)). The memorisation transition is identified with state 1 of the modular-addition map, and the time of the transition to generalisation is linked to how quickly the $L_{2}$ falls immediately after the memorisation ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)).

**[The canonical grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — both settings are reproduced verbatim: modular addition after Nanda et al. (a one-layer transformer without layer norm, 30 % of the pairs, weight decay 1.0, a batch of 2048) and sparse parities after Barak et al.; 40 runs per setting ([`p5-7`](#p5-7), tables 5–6).

**[Weight-norm minimization](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** and **[the Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — the central qualitative observation: what distinguishes the fast path to generalisation is a fall of the $L_{2}$ that is "just right" — ↓1.68 σ against ↓0.59 for the average path and ↓2.08 for the slowest ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)); in §5.2 this is directly linked to the Goldilocks-zone hypothesis in the exposition of Liu et al. ([`p12-3`](#p12-3)). A caveat for the corpus: the non-monotonicity rests on a fast branch of 2 runs out of 40, and the top feature of the fast path is not the $L_{2}$ but the spread of the weights.

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the divergence of the runs by seed arises at the very start of training, immediately after the memorisation transition — both in the grokking tasks and in a destabilised CIFAR-100 ([`p12-5`](#p12-5)); hence the practical advice to judge a setting by the early variability of a few seeds.

**[The loss landscape](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the support of the closing conjecture: layer norm and a small batch are known in the literature to smooth the landscape, so the removal of grokking by those knobs is read as an argument that grokking is a consequence of a sharp landscape ([`p12-2`](#p12-2)). There are no direct measurements of the landscape in the paper.

**[Seed spread and reproducibility](../../concepts/seed-variance-reproducibility.md)** — the forty seeds are taken not for the sake of reporting but in order to measure the sensitivity of the training to randomness itself ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)): the subject of the paper is the forks of the trajectories, and without a large number of runs they cannot be seen.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The method is the main contribution, and it is honestly described.** An HMM over 14 weight metrics, the number of states chosen by BIC on held-out trajectories, and feature importance through the partial derivatives of the log-posterior (the method was corrected after an error in the early versions was pointed out — the authors expressly thank the finder; the numbers of v1/v2 in this part are void). The semantics of the states comes from a linear regression of the convergence time on the "bag of states", with the honest caveat "we are not forecasting": this is a description of a relation, not a prediction.

**What rests on small branches.** The fast path of modular addition is 2 runs out of 40; the "fast" branch of the parities is 1 run out of 40. Both the "just-right" fall of the $L_{2}$ and the isolation of the detour states rest on them. The $R^{2}$ of the regressions (0.977, 0.961, 0.905) are given with no held-out check, at 5–6 features on 40 observations.

**A definitional subtlety about the detours.** In a linear map every run passes through all the states, and the first condition of the definition of a detour is unsatisfiable in principle — the authors say so outright, yet the conclusion "stabilisation removes the detour states" is presented as substantive. The detour singled out is not the state with the largest regression coefficient: the selection is decided by an "optionality" read off the map.

**Internal roughnesses.** The caption of fig. 5 explains the stabilisation by "layer normalization and a smaller learning rate", whereas §3.3 and table 6 speak of reducing the batch (the learning rate is 1e-3 in both cases). The comparison with the baselines (appendix C) is incomplete: the BIC is not comparable between classes of models, and the ability of k-means to find detours was not tested. The conclusion about MultiBERTs rests on five runs with no regression.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that grokking has detour states") — more precisely: it is shown on two algorithmic settings of 40 seeds each and one destabilised ResNet setting, and the fast branches by which the definition of a detour is set contain 2 and 1 run respectively.
