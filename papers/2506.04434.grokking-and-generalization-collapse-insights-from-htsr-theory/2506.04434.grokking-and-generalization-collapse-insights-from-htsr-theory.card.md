# Prakash & Martin 2025 — Grokking and Generalization Collapse: Insights from HTSR theory

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Hari K. Prakash** (University of California, San Diego, Data Science and Engineering), **Charles H. Martin** (Calculation Consulting, San Francisco).

arXiv [2506.04434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04434), June 2025 (the sole version v1), 15 pages, 7 figures. There is no citation count for it in the corpus table. The work extends the training to $10^{7}$ steps and finds a **third era** — anti-grokking, a late collapse of the generalisation at a flawless training accuracy — together with a measure that distinguishes that era: the heavy-tail exponent $\alpha$ from the theory of heavy-tailed self-regularisation. ## Concepts covered

**[Heavy-tailed self-regularisation (HTSR)](../../concepts/heavy-tailed-self-regularization-htsr.md)** — the prism of the whole work. For each layer weight matrix a correlation matrix $\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{W}^{T}\mathbf{W}$ is built, its empirical spectral density is fitted by a power law, and the exponent $\alpha$ of that tail serves as a measure of the layer's quality ([`p3-1`](#p3-2), [`p3-8`](#p3-9)). The classes are known from the earlier works of Martin and Mahoney: $\alpha\gtrsim 5{-}6$ — the layer is nearly random, $2\lesssim\alpha\lesssim 5{-}6$ — the correlations have formed, $\alpha=2$ — the optimal value, $\alpha<2$ — an excessive correlation and overfitting ([`p4-1`](#p4-1)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the subject, but taken over an unusually long horizon: the training is extended to $10^{7}$ steps, and there a **third era** is discovered ([`p2-1`](#p2-1), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). Pre-grokking: the training accuracy is flawless, the test accuracy low. Grokking: the test accuracy shoots up. **Anti-grokking**: the test accuracy collapses to 0.5 while the training accuracy stays flawless and the weight norm goes on growing.

**[Ungrokking](../../concepts/ungrokking.md)** — an explicit demarcation. In Varma et al. ungrokking names a loss of generalisation under **retraining on a smaller dataset** ($D<D_{crit}$) and is explained by a change in the efficiency of circuits under weight decay; here, by contrast, the collapse occurs on the **original** dataset, without weight decay and simply from long training ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)). The explanation of Varma et al. does not extend to this case, since it leans on weight decay.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the main claim: $\alpha$ marks out all three eras, whereas the best rivals mark out only the first two ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The activation sparsity, the entropy of the weight magnitudes and the approximate local circuit complexity of Golechha are compared against it ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)); all of them catch the pre-grokking and the grokking, but at the collapse they either continue in their previous direction or stay flat ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — **correlation traps**: a weight matrix is shuffled entrywise into $\mathbf{W}^{rand}$, its spectrum is fitted by a Marchenko–Pastur distribution, and the outliers beyond the edge of the bulk $\lambda^{+}_{rand}$ are the traps ([`p4-8`](#p4-8), [`p5-2`](#p5-2), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)). The argument is checkable: in a well-trained model the shuffled spectrum ought to follow MP, and a departure from it is significant.

**[Correlation traps](../../concepts/correlation-traps.md)** — they appear **only** in the anti-grokking era and never earlier: 0 in the pre-grokking, 0 in the grokking, 7.5 ± 5.6 in FC1 at the collapse ([`tab-2`](#tab-2), [`p8-6`](#p8-6)). A Kolmogorov–Smirnov test confirms it: a P-value of $\approx 1.0$ in the first two eras and $1.877\cdot 10^{-5}$ at the collapse ([`tab-4`](#tab-4), [`p13-1`](#p13-1)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the controlling knob, and the work deliberately runs both cases. At WD=0 the $l^{2}$ norm grows steadily, the grokking happens all the same, and then the collapse sets in; at WD=0.01 the test accuracy after the grokking falls slightly and settles on a long plateau, while $\alpha$ stabilises near 2 ([`p12-7`](#p12-7), [`p12-9`](#p12-9), [`fig-6`](#fig-6)).

**[The weight norm](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the explanation through it is rejected outright: grokking happens at a growing norm too, and at anti-grokking the norm goes on growing while the generalisation collapses ([`p2-1`](#p2-1), [`p8-3`](#p8-3)).

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — a new explanation of all three eras through layer-wise convergence is proposed: in the pre-grokking only some of the layers have converged ($\alpha\approx 4$) while the rest are nearly random ($\alpha\approx 5$); in the grokking all the important layers converge to $\alpha\approx 2$; in the anti-grokking one or several layers overfit, dropping below $\alpha<2$ ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[Rank collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — the conjectured mechanism of the collapse: the traps are perturbations of rank one or higher that cause a large shift of the mean of the entries $\mathbb{E}[W_{ij}]$ and make the weight distribution **atypical**; an atypical estimator will not generalise ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)).

**[Real-task data](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the setting: a fully connected MLP of depth 3 and width 200, ReLU, a 1000-example subsample of MNIST (100 per digit), an MSE loss over one-hot targets, AdamW with a rate of $5\cdot 10^{-4}$, the weights multiplied by 8 at initialisation, $10^{7}$ steps, about 11 hours on a Quadro P2000 ([`p11-1`](#p11-1), [`tab-3`](#tab-3)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the explanation through the weight norm, contested here; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — circuit efficiency and ungrokking, from which anti-grokking is demarcated; [Merrill et al. 2023](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md) — the competition of a sparse and a dense subnetwork; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — progress measures through circuits; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — an effective theory of representation learning.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is short: 9 pages of main text, 5 appendices, 7 figures, 4 tables.

**The third era is found, and found cheaply.** All it took was to extend the training to $10^{7}$ steps where the predecessors stopped at $10^{5}$–$10^{6}$ ([`p2-5`](#p3-1)). This is the sort of case in which the finding lay on the surface and nobody looked.

**The demarcation from ungrokking is drawn precisely.** In Varma et al. the loss of generalisation requires retraining on a smaller dataset and weight decay; here there is neither ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)). This is not a renaming of somebody else's phenomenon but a different one.

**The comparison with the rival measures is set up as a negative check.** The activation sparsity grows in the pre-grokking and in the anti-grokking alike; without knowing the threshold one cannot tell these eras apart by it, whereas $\alpha=2$ is a cut-off given by the theory in advance ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)). The argument is framed exactly so — as a test of the ability to **discriminate** rather than merely to "correlate".

**The correlation traps are confirmed not by eye but by a test.** The Marchenko–Pastur fit, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic and the P-value are given for all three eras ([`tab-4`](#tab-4)); the transition from $P\approx 1.0$ to $1.877\cdot 10^{-5}$ is striking.

**The explanation of all three eras through layer-wise convergence is coherent.** Pre-grokking — some layers are under-trained; grokking — all the important layers are at $\alpha\approx 2$; anti-grokking — some layers have dropped below 2 ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). Such a description also explains why the pre-grokking and the anti-grokking look alike from outside (flawless training, poor test) yet are distinguishable by $\alpha$.

**The whole work, however, is a single setting.** One MLP of depth 3, one dataset, one subsample of 1000 examples, one seed (the settings table gives `Random Seed 0`), two values of weight decay ([`tab-3`](#tab-3)). The limitation is admitted first in the list ([`p14-5`](#p14-5)), but the claim of a "universal layer convergence target at $\alpha\approx 2$" does not hold on such a foundation.

**The spreads are given in the tables, their source is not.** Table 1 gives $0.9\pm 0.4$ and table 2 gives $7.5\pm 5.6$; the caption says "different seeds were applied" ([`tab-1`](#tab-1)), whereas the settings list a single seed ([`tab-3`](#tab-3)). How many runs were averaged is said nowhere.

**The initialisation is multiplied by 8, and this is not discussed.** The weights and biases at initialisation are multiplied by 8.0 ([`tab-3`](#tab-3)) — the very Omnigrok technique by which grokking is deliberately induced. How this bears on $\alpha$ and on the collapse itself is not examined.

**The converse implication is admitted by the authors to be false.** It is conceivable that a layer has $\alpha<2$ and generalises poorly for some other reason, and well-trained models do have layers with large $\alpha$ ([`p14-6`](#p14-6)). Thus $\alpha<2$ is an indicator rather than a criterion.

**The mechanism of the collapse is declared a conjecture.** The words "in some as yet unclarified way" and "it is conjectured that layers with many traps are overfitted" stand in the text outright ([`p9-1`](#p9-1), [`p9-2`](#p9-2)); the chain "traps → atypicality → poor generalisation" is set out as an argument rather than checked by an intervention.

**The text is unproofread.** The native arXiv HTML renders **all** the literature references as unresolved keys (`power2022grokking`, `martin2021implicit`, `golechha2024progressmeasuresgrokkingrealworld`) — that is, in the published HTML of the paper the link between the text and the bibliography is lost entirely; the end of the third contribution item carries a double full stop ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)); and in appendix D the variance for the final state is called $\sigma_{mp}\approx 2$ ([`p13-1`](#p13-1)) against the $0.949$ of table 4 ([`tab-4`](#tab-4)).

**Its place in the corpus.** The work brings two new things into the corpus: a third era — a collapse of the generalisation after the grokking under long training without weight decay — and a measure that distinguishes that era from an outwardly similar pre-grokking. It is also valuable that the measure is taken **from the weights alone**, with no access to test data. The price: one tiny setting on MNIST, no number of runs given, an initialisation multiplied by 8 with no analysis of the consequences, and a mechanism of the collapse declared a conjecture. For the corpus this is a pointer to an unexplored region rather than a closed question.
