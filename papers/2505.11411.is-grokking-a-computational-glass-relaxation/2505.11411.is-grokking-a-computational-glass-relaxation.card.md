## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the corpus the first measurement of the Boltzmann entropy of a network in which grokking is observed: the landscape $S(ln(L_{train}),A_{test})$ is taken on a one-layer transformer with hundreds of thousands of parameters ([p5-8](#p5-8)), and on that landscape grokking is interpreted as a computational glass relaxation — memorization corresponds to the rapid cooling of a liquid into a non-equilibrium glass-like state, the subsequent generalization to slow structural relaxation ([p2-2](#p2-2), [p6-4](#p6-4)). Hence two claimed corollaries: there is no entropy barrier between memorization and generalization ([p6-3](#p6-3)), and there is a "high-entropy advantage in grokking" — the gap between the AdamW training curve and the equilibrium curve ([fig. 1](#fig-1)). What the work does not have: a single quantitative glass measure — no relaxation time, no two-step correlation function, no ageing; the glassiness remains an analogy, and the methodology of Winter & Janssen, which the authors themselves cite, they defer to future work ([p4-1](#p4-1), [p10-3](#p10-3)). Nor is there a temperature: the "rapid cooling" is attributed to ordinary AdamW descent, whereas $k_{B}T=1$ exists only inside WLMD and WanD ([p9-3](#p9-3)), and the cooling rate is nowhere measured. There is one testbed: a one-layer transformer on modular arithmetic, and the authors state outright that the work includes no other grokking testbeds ([p10-2](#p10-2)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work's principal negative claim: grokking is NOT a first-order phase transition. There are two arguments, and both are taken from the entropy landscape: on the vertical part of the L-shaped trajectory there is neither an entropy barrier nor a training-loss barrier, and the equilibrium curve in the low-loss region is continuous, whereas a first-order transition would require a discontinuity of the order parameter ([p6-3](#p6-3)). Hence the conclusion that grokking is a kinetic process rather than a transition in the thermodynamic sense ([p6-4](#p6-4)). The objection is addressed directly to Rubin et al. (2310.03789) and is repeated in the conclusions ([p9-1](#p9-1)). What the work does not have: the opponent's setting — neither the tasks of Rubin et al. nor their two-layer teacher — student models ([p10-2](#p10-2)); the absence of a barrier is shown in a two-dimensional projection and inside an artificially bounded volume of parameters, whose boundaries are chosen so as to "cover the range typically reached in ordinary training" ([p4-5](#p4-5)). The authors themselves add in the discussion that the divergence from Rubin et al. is "probably" explained by a different architecture and different data ([p10-1](#p10-1)).

**[Order parameter](../../concepts/order-parameter.md)** — the test accuracy is designated the order parameter explicitly and by analogy with the density at a liquid—gas transition ([p6-1](#p6-1)). It matters that this quantity has a double role in the work: it also serves as the second sampling coordinate $y_{WL}$ in WLMD ([p5-4](#p5-4)), and in order for it to have a gradient with respect to the parameters it is approximated by a sigmoid ([p5-5](#p5-5)) — a smoothed accuracy with slope $s=7$ ([p14-4](#p14-4)). What the work does not have: a justification of the choice beyond the analogy, and a check of the sensitivity of the conclusions to the smoothing slope and to the bin resolution ([p4-6](#p4-6)); the continuity of the equilibrium curve is a property of the smoothed quantity, not of the original accuracy.

**[Effective theory / statistical mechanics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — the work carries the mapping "a network is a physical system" through to a computable quantity: the parameters are the degrees of freedom, the training loss is the energy, and the Boltzmann entropy $S=k_{B}\ln V$ is the logarithm of the volume of parameter space corresponding to a given pair (training loss, test accuracy) ([p4-5](#p4-5)). The sampling method is Wang-Landau molecular dynamics, whose computational cost grows linearly with the number of parameters, which is what allowed entropy sampling to be raised from models of a few hundred parameters to transformers ([p5-8](#p5-8)). What the work does not have: a meaningful absolute value of the entropy — the authors openly admit that only differences between bins are meaningful ([p4-6](#p4-6)); the finiteness of the volume is imposed by hand, with reflecting boundaries per layer or a hard hypersphere ([p4-5](#p4-5)); nor is there a formal convergence criterion for WLMD — the run lengths were chosen as the points where the map stopped changing noticeably ([p6-2](#p6-2)).

**[Loss landscape / basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the work proposes to replace the local geometry with a global quantity: flatness and weight norm together are, on its claim, the entropy — the logarithm of the volume of solutions with the same generalization ability ([p3-1](#p3-1)). Grokking is read from this as a failure of exploration: "a dynamical process caused by the optimizer's inefficient exploration of the basins of the loss landscape" ([p7-3](#p7-3)). What the work does not have: measurements at the level of individual basins — neither the width of a minimum nor the barriers between them; the quantity the work operates with is a two-dimensional projection of a volume, not the structure of the landscape.

**[The Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — both sides of the dispute are reproduced in one work, and that is its strong point. On the one hand, the Omnigrok recipe is confirmed: a hard rescaling of the weights to a norm of 30 after every step largely removes grokking ([p7-1](#p7-1), [fig. 6](#fig-6)). On the other, WanD finds generalizing solutions at a norm growing to roughly 175, with no constraints at all, which is declared a counterexample to explaining grokking through the evolution of the norm toward a Goldilocks zone alone ([p8-3](#p8-3), [fig. 3](#fig-3)). What the work does not have: the counterexample is narrowed by the authors themselves with the caveat "in a more strictly defined sense, since we do not scale the NN output" ([p8-3](#p8-3)); the Omnigrok tasks (MNIST and others) are not reproduced ([p10-2](#p10-2)), and the comparison of the zone is conducted under conditions different from the opponent's.

**[The spherical norm constraint](../../concepts/spherical-weight-norm-constraint.md)** — the constraint is used as an instrument rather than as a subject: after every iteration step the weight norm is recomputed and the weights are scaled to a fixed value of 30 ([p7-1](#p7-1)). The result is twofold — grokking almost disappears ([fig. 6](#fig-6)), and the entropy landscape under this constraint ceases to reach the states of strong memorization, that is, the "computational glass" ([p7-2](#p7-2)). What the work does not have: a sweep over norm values — a single value of 30 is tested, taken from Omnigrok; the work does not construct the dependence of the high-entropy advantage on the norm.

**[Weight norm minimization](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work objects to the norm as a measure of a solution's "efficiency" and proposes the entropy instead: an efficient solution has more "free" parameters and therefore a larger volume ([p10-1](#p10-1)). The direct experimental evidence is the growth of the WanD weight norm over the course of training to roughly 175 while generalization is attained ([fig. 3](#fig-3)). What the work does not have: a measurement of the entropy of the WanD solutions — the entropy landscape is taken for AdamW, the high norm is shown separately, and the link "higher entropy — higher norm" is nowhere checked.

**[When regularization is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — WanD is declared to remove grokking "without any explicit regularization (such as weight-norm constraints)" ([p2-5](#p2-5)), and this is read as agreement with Kumar et al. (2310.06110), but "in a more strictly defined sense" — without scaling the output ([p8-3](#p8-3)). What the work does not have: the reference AdamW curve of section 4.1 is taken at weight decay 1 (table 1, [A.2](#p14-2)), that is, the "far from equilibrium" trajectory is measured under strong explicit regularization, and there is no comparison at other values of it; the interaction of glass-like dynamics with explicit regularization the authors themselves place among the unexamined ([p10-2](#p10-2)).

**[Optimizer](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the work redescribes the role of the optimizer in the language of the analogy: gradient and especially adaptive optimizers such as Adam carry out a "rapid cooling", driving the parameters insistently toward low loss with insufficient perturbations, whence a computational glass forms ([p9-3](#p9-3)); Adam's non-uniform scaling of gradients additionally damps the updates in the region of small gradients ([p9-4](#p9-4)). Hence the positive part: a toy optimizer WanD on one-dimensional WLMD, comparable to AdamW in step count (about 2000 steps) and faster in wall-clock time (50 s against 72 s per 1K steps) ([p8-3](#p8-3)). What the work does not have: the cooling rate is neither measured nor varied; WanD is acknowledged to be a proof of concept, sensitive to hyperparameters and unstable near generalization ([p10-2](#p10-2)), and in appendix B.3 it is annealed together with AdamW with a weight $\alpha=(2A_{test}-1)^{2}$ ([B.3](#p16-3)).

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — perturbation is the operative part of the proposed mechanism: a lack of perturbations (a large learning rate, a small batch size, the precision of the data) is named as the cause of getting stuck in non-generalizing minima, while the Langevin dynamics inside WanD is what avoids the formation of a computational glass ([p9-3](#p9-3)). The AdamW training is meanwhile full-batch ([A.2](#p14-2)), that is, there is no batch noise in the reference curve at all. What the work does not have: a decomposition of WanD into parts — the contribution of the Langevin term is not separated from that of the entropy gradient, and there is no experiment with added noise alone.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — three operations at a single modulus $p=67$: $x+y$ (easy), $x^{2}+y$ (hard) and $x^{3}+xy^{2}+y$ (unlearnable), with a 50/50 split at a fixed seed ([p4-3](#p4-3), [p4-4](#p4-4)). The third operation is a valuable control experiment: on it even the equilibrium state does not generalize, although some states give about 20 % against roughly 1.5 % for random guessing ([p6-3](#p6-3)), that is, high entropy by itself does not produce generalization. What the work does not have: neither the group $S_{5}$ nor other moduli — 67 is taken for the sake of sampling cost, and the justification is confined to the remark that grokking persists at 67 ([A.1](#p14-1)).

**[The reference grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting is taken from Nanda et al. (2301.05217) as is: the same one-layer transformer model, 4 attention heads of width 32, model dimension 128, MLP of width 512 ([A.1](#p14-1)); one and the same model serves for AdamW training, WanD training and entropy sampling. The one deliberate departure is the modulus 67 instead of 113 ([A.1](#p14-1)). What the work does not have: a check that at 67 the testbed behaves as at 113, beyond the assertion that grokking persists.

**[The memorization phase / plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work makes its strongest claim about the memorization phase: the memorization state "is in neither an equilibrium nor a metastable state" ([p9-1](#p9-1)), and the length of the plateau is attributed not to a barrier but to kinetics — to very small gradients in the low-loss region ([p9-4](#p9-4)). The reason for landing in that state is overparameterization, which allows the training data to be fully memorized and the loss to be dropped, which in the analogy amounts to lowering the temperature below the glass transition temperature ([p9-3](#p9-3)). What the work does not have: the absence of a barrier in the chosen projection is not the absence of metastability, and there is no experiment distinguishing these two states; the work's own figures show that on the easy task the test-accuracy plateau sits around 50 %, not at random guessing ([fig. 5](#fig-5)), whereas in the text the memorization phase is described as "the test accuracy remains very low".

**[Sparse subnetwork / lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the work rewrites the finding of Merrill et al. (2303.11873) in the language of volume: in a sparse subnetwork only a few neurons take part in memorizing the rules, while the remaining degrees of freedom contribute to the entropy, so a sparse solution has a larger volume ([p10-1](#p10-1), [C](#p18-1)). What the work does not have: this is an argument, not a measurement — the work measures neither the sparsity of solutions nor subnetworks, and the entropies of a dense and a sparse solution are nowhere compared side by side.

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — a resolution is proposed for the dispute between Varma et al. (2309.02390) and Kumar et al. (2310.06110) about "efficiency": the dispute stems from defining efficiency through the parameter norm alone, and the entropy is declared the better indicator ([p10-1](#p10-1)). What the work does not have: neither the setting of Varma et al. nor that of Kumar et al. is reproduced; "efficiency" through entropy is not measured on a single circuit, and the word "circuit" goes no further in the work than a retelling of someone else's claim.

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the proposal of Kumar et al. (2310.06110) that the key determining quantity of grokking is the feature-learning rate is identified with the cooling rate of the glass analogy: the limited rate of free motion of the particles is an important cause of glass formation and of long relaxation times ([p10-1](#p10-1)). What the work does not have: neither the kernel, nor its change over time, nor any measure of the feature-learning rate — the correspondence is drawn in words.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — a mention only: the work of Thilak et al. (2206.04817) is named among the mechanisms with which the entropy analysis agrees ([p3-2](#p3-2)), and in the discussion it is reduced to the thesis "perturbation is useful for the learning process in grokking", supported by WanD's removing grokking directly ([p10-1](#p10-1)). The work measures neither norm jumps, nor loss spikes, nor any numerical phenomena of the optimizer.

**[Data fraction / critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — increasing the training fraction is named as an effective way to remove grokking, and on this the experiment of appendix B.3 is built: fractions of 50, 60, 70 and 80 % at a single learning rate of 0.003 on two tasks ([B.3](#p16-3), [fig. 7](#fig-7), [fig. 8](#fig-8)), and WanD attains generalization earlier than AdamW at all four fractions ([B.3](#p17-1)). What the work does not have: it neither seeks nor names a critical value of the fraction, and the main landscape is taken only at 50 %.

**[Learning structured representations](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — a mention with a redescription: the line of works on the transition from poor to good representation learning is called consistent with the glass analogy — as a relaxation from a badly organized "glass-like" structure to a more ordered one ([p10-1](#p10-1)). The work analyses no representations: there is no Fourier structure, no embeddings, and no measures of feature organization in it.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — in the work it is both a support and a target. The target: it is claimed that grokking "is not owed exclusively to weight decay, as some earlier theory proposed" ([p9-1](#p9-1)). The support: three of the six rows of the hyperparameter table are runs at weight decay 1 (table 1, [A.2](#p14-2)), and it is precisely they that give the reference AdamW curves of all three landscapes of section 4.1. What the work does not have: an analysis of how explicit regularization interacts with glass-like dynamics — this is relegated to the limitations ([p10-2](#p10-2)).

## Points we could not reconcile

**The WanD algorithm reads the test labels, whereas the text declares it to have access only to the training data.** Section 5 says that WanD samples the entropy landscape by the training loss alone "because of the fundamental constraint that it has access only to the training data" ([p7-4](#p7-4)). In algorithm 1 itself the stopping condition is "the transformer model $T$ attains generalization: $Accuracy(T(X_{test}),y_{test})\approx 100\%$", and the "Check" line computes the test accuracy at every epoch ([algorithm 1](#p8-2)). Either the constraint holds and stopping by test accuracy is not part of the optimizer, or WanD as written uses the test labels. For the claim "WanD removes grokking without any constraints" this matters.

**"Removes grokking" / "largely removed" / "unstable right at generalization".** The abstract says that WanD "is able to remove grokking without any constraints" ([p1-2](#p1-2)); the caption of figure 3 softens this to "grokking in training with WanD is largely removed" ([fig. 3](#fig-3)); in the limitations it is said that WanD "exhibits instability near generalization" ([p10-2](#p10-2)), and in appendix B.3 it is annealed together with AdamW for stability ([B.3](#p16-3)). Figure 3(a) itself shows not a plateau but oscillations of the test accuracy roughly between 0.9 and 1.0 with dips to 0.2 — that is, exactly what the limitations call instability. One should cite the caption and the limitations, not the abstract.

**"Our results overturn this conclusion" and "probably it is a different setting" stand in the same paragraph.** Of the work of Rubin et al. it is said that "our results overturn this conclusion", and immediately: "probably the matter is that their network architecture and training data differ from ours" ([p10-1](#p10-1)). Either a refutation or a different setting — together this does not hold; in the limitations the authors openly admit that they did not reproduce the tasks of Rubin et al. ([p10-2](#p10-2)).

**The number of steps to generalization for AdamW on one and the same task differs threefold between two figures.** In figure 3(d) AdamW on $x+y\ mod\ 67$ at a fraction of 50 % reaches generalization in roughly 2000—2500 steps, whereas in figure 7(a) — on the same task and at the same fraction — in roughly 700 ([fig. 3](#fig-3), [fig. 7](#fig-7)). The learning rate of figure 7 is named (0.003, [B.3](#p16-3)), that of figure 3 is not: the caption says only that "both WanD and AdamW use the same learning rate". The reference comparison "WanD against AdamW" from the main text is therefore not tied to table 1, and the threefold difference between the two AdamW runs is not explained in the text.

**The definition of the memorization phase conflicts with the work's own figures.** The introduction describes the state before grokking as one in which "the test accuracy remains very low" ([p1-3](#p1-3)), and the survey as a transition "from random guessing to (almost) perfect test accuracy" ([p3-2](#p3-2)). On the easy task the work's own curves show a test-accuracy plateau around 50 % ([fig. 5](#fig-5), [fig. 7](#fig-7)), that is, far from random guessing (about 1.5 % at $p=67$); on the hard task the test accuracy, instead of a plateau and a jump, grows continuously over three decimal orders of magnitude in steps ([fig. 5](#fig-5)). This is not a typo but a divergence of the description from the material: the "memorization state" whose entropy the work measures is, on its easy task, halfway generalizing.

**Equation 4 is printed with a lost parenthesis.** The second term reads $\frac{\partial Sln(L_{train}),A_{test},t)}{\partial A_{test}}$ instead of $\frac{\partial S(ln(L_{train}),A_{test},t)}{\partial A_{test}}$ ([eq. 4](#p5-5)). It looks the same in the PDF, so this is the paper's typo rather than the conversion's.

## What is shown, what is conjectured and what is untested

The work is made up of a measurement and an interpretation, and they hold up differently. A reader taking a claim from it should know which half it belongs to.

**Measured.** The entropy landscape $S(ln(L_{train}),A_{test})$ is taken for three tasks at 60M, 80M and 20M WLMD epochs ([p6-2](#p6-2), [fig. 1](#fig-1)) and for three widths at a fixed norm of 30 at 60M epochs ([p7-1](#p7-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)); the cost is named honestly — about 100 GPU-hours per 1M epochs and almost 4000 GPU-hours for one experiment of section 4.2. On that landscape are measured: the absence of an entropy barrier on the vertical part of the trajectory, the continuity of the equilibrium curve at low loss, and the gap between the AdamW curve and the equilibrium one ([p6-3](#p6-3), [p6-4](#p6-4)). There is a control experiment: on the unlearnable task the equilibrium state does not generalize either ([p6-3](#p6-3), [fig. 1](#fig-1)) — high entropy by itself does not give generalization. There is also a check of robustness to the choice of the second coordinate: internal experiments with a cross-entropy test loss instead of the smoothed accuracy give the same conclusion ([C](#p17-2)). Also measured are the step count and the wall-clock time of WanD against AdamW on modular addition and the growth of the weight norm to roughly 175 ([p8-3](#p8-3), [fig. 3](#fig-3)).

**Derived from what is measured, with caveats that should be carried along with the conclusion.** The absence of a barrier is shown in a **two-dimensional projection** and inside a volume bounded by hand — with reflecting boundaries per layer, arranged so as to "cover the range of parameters typically reached in ordinary training" ([p4-5](#p4-5)); the sensitivity to the choice of boundaries, to the bin resolution and to the smoothing slope $s=7$ is not shown ([p4-6](#p4-6), [p14-4](#p14-4)). The convergence of WLMD is established by the sign "the map stopped changing noticeably" ([p6-2](#p6-2)); there is no formal criterion and no error estimate for the entropy, and the method itself is designed to push the system out of visited states ([p5-1](#p5-1)) — and is used to prove the absence of a barrier. The conclusion that "the memorization state is neither equilibrium nor metastable" ([p9-1](#p9-1)) is stronger than what is shown: the absence of a barrier in the chosen projection is not the absence of metastability.

**Declared to be analogy and argument.** All the glass content of the work is a mapping rather than a measurement: there is no relaxation time (Arrhenius or VFT), no two-step correlation function and no ageing in the work, and the authors themselves relegate these measurements to future work, citing the methodology of Winter & Janssen ([p4-1](#p4-1), [p10-3](#p10-3)). The resolution of the "efficiency" dispute through entropy ([p10-1](#p10-1)) and the explanation of the higher entropy of sparse subnetworks through "free" parameters ([C](#p18-1)) are arguments; neither of them is backed by a measurement on solutions. The identification of the "feature-learning rate" of Kumar et al. with the cooling rate ([p10-1](#p10-1)) is made in words.

**Untested, and part of this the authors acknowledge.** One architecture, one class of tasks, the opponents' tasks absent ([p10-2](#p10-2)); the interaction of glass-like dynamics with explicit regularization not examined; WanD sensitive to hyperparameters and declared a proof of concept. To this should be added what is not in the list of limitations. The reference AdamW curve of section 4.1 is taken at weight decay 1 ([A.2](#p14-2)), that is, the "far from equilibrium" trajectory is measured under strong explicit regularization, and there is no comparison at other values of it. The comparison of WanD with AdamW in appendix B.3 is not on equal terms by construction: there WanD is annealed together with AdamW with a weight $\alpha=(2A_{test}-1)^{2}$, that is, the update weight is computed from the **test** accuracy ([B.3](#p16-3)). The connection between the high-entropy advantage and the network width is called non-obvious, contrary to the expectation from the work on which the whole entropy frame rests ([B.2](#p16-2)) — and that work (arXiv 2503.13145) is written by the same group of authors and is absent from the corpus, that is, the key support is a self-citation.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2310-03789
**Rubin et al. (2310.03789) — "two free-energy minima" and a free-energy barrier are attributed to them.** [`"They used the Langevin dynamic optimizer to train two two-layer nonlinear teacher-student models and found that the memorization and generalization states of the NN were located at two free-energy minima, thus believing that grokking is a first-order phase transition that requires crossing a free-energy barrier."`](#p6-2). The words `free energy`, `barrier`, `metastable` do not occur in the original of Rubin et al. at all: their language is that of saddles of the action $\hat{S}$, and [the saddles exist separately and in the mixed phase remain degenerate](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/original/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.md#p6-6), while the kinetics of the transition is not considered there. This matters for reading the present work, not only for reading Rubin et al.: the title objection "there is no barrier" ([p6-3](#p6-3), [p9-1](#p9-1)) is directed against a quantity the opponent does not have, and refutes the retelling made here. On the Rubin et al. side the pattern has already been described as "attributing attributes of a first-order transition that are not in the work", and the "entropy barrier" attributed there is traced by the citing works precisely to the present operationalization (see `mc-2310-03789` in the card [2606.17120](../2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking/2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking.card.md#mc-2310-03789)). The other references of this paragraph have been checked and are given accurately — the setting of Rubin et al. is indeed teacher — student with two-layer non-linear networks and Langevin dynamics.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the corpus works that cite this one (the `original/` folders of all `papers/` directories were surveyed; five works cite it, all in the body text). Each entry says what exactly is distorted and leads to the authorial paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. Cards exist for two of the five citing works ([2603.24746](../2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition/2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition.card.md) and [2606.17120](../2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking/2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking.card.md)), but they have no `mc-2505-11411` anchors yet, so there are no "details" links here; the candidates have been passed to the coordinator.

**"It was found that there is no entropy barrier" (over-extending).** In [2606.17120](../2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking/2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking.card.md) (Ersoy & Wiesner) the claim is conveyed without a single caveat: `"Rubin et al. [11] proposed a first-order phase transition analogy involving an entropy barrier. This was then challenged by Zhang et al. [23], who found no entropy barrier and proposed glassy relaxation instead."` What is lost is everything that makes the finding a finding about a particular setting: there is no barrier **in the two-dimensional projection** $S(ln(L_{train}),A_{test})$ and **inside a volume bounded by hand** ([p4-5](#p4-5), [p6-3](#p6-3)), on a **one-layer transformer at $p=67$**, whereas the work does not reproduce the opponent's tasks and architecture and admits this itself ([p10-2](#p10-2)), and itself adds that the divergence is "probably" explained by a different setting ([p10-1](#p10-1)). It is worth noting separately that the "entropy barrier" is attributed here to Rubin et al. as well: they have no barrier of any kind, and the retelling about a "free-energy barrier" originates in the work under analysis — see ["Erroneous citations"](#mc-2310-03789) above.

**"Generalizing solutions are abundant and accessible" (over-extending).** In `2608.01833` — a direct continuation of this line — it is said: `"Although the generalizing solutions are abundant and accessible in principle (35), the optimization process has difficulty exploring alternative parameter configurations."`, and in the same place: `"Results show that generalization states occupy a much larger solution space than the memorization one"`. The work shows something else: what it measures is the **difference of entropies between bins at one and the same training loss** ([p4-6](#p4-6), [fig. 1](#fig-1)), while "accessibility" does not follow from the absence of a barrier in the chosen projection — this is exactly the place where the authors themselves take a step stronger than what is shown ([p9-1](#p9-1)). The same work also gives an accurate assessment of the general situation: `"this perspective has remained largely at the theoretical on macroscopic level without direct empirical validation on training dynamics"` — and the warning here concerns two formulations, not the work as a whole.

**The setting "as in the original glass work" is reproduced with a different split.** In the same `2608.01833`: `"we follow the previously reported setup in the original glass theory work and experiment on a 1-layer transformer on the arithmetic task of $(x^{2}+y)\bmod 67$ (35)"`, but that same work says that 30 % of the sample is held out for estimating the test accuracy, whereas in the work under analysis the split is 50/50 at a fixed seed ([p4-4](#p4-4)). Since the training fraction is one of the levers by which the work under analysis itself removes grokking ([B.3](#p16-3)), this divergence of settings is worth keeping in mind when comparing step counts.

Formulations exemplary for comparison. In `2605.20441` the work is conveyed together with its genre: `"Zhang et al. 2025 frame grokking as a glass-physics relaxation analogically (memorisation as rapid cooling into a glassy state, generalisation as slow relaxation), which is a different abstraction from the mechanistic AdamW-update relaxation argument we deploy"`, and in the summary table of that work the row is marked `analogical` — exactly the status the analogy has ([p4-1](#p4-1)). In [2603.24746](../2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition/2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition.card.md) — `"Zhang et al. [51] argue for computational glass relaxation rather than a phase transition"`, with the "argue" preserved. In `2607.29503` the work is called a continuation of the entropy line of Yang et al., and it is said that the high-entropy advantage is especially substantial in this setting — which is what is claimed ([p2-5](#p2-5)).
