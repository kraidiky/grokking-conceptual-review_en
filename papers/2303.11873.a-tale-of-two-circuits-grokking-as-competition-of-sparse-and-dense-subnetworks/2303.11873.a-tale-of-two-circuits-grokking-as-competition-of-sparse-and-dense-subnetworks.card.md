## Concepts covered

**[The sparse subnetwork / lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the work gives the notion an **operational definition and a measurable dynamics**. The "active subnetwork" is found by magnitude pruning down to the smallest size at which the predictions do not change on any input; the authors call the number of its neurons the *effective sparsity* ([`p3-1`](#p2-5)). After the grokking the network becomes orders of magnitude sparser — and this transition coincides in time with the transition in the loss.

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — the task on which everything is built: $(n,k)$-parity with $n=40$, $k=3$, $N=1000$, taken from Barak et al. Its advantage here is that **exact constructions** are known for it — how many neurons are needed to compute the parity — and so the size of the subnetwork found can be set against the theoretical minimum.

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — the work gives a **different mechanism of competition** from that of Varma et al.: there the generalising circuit wins because it is more efficient in parameter norm, here because its neurons grow selectively in norm while the competitor "dies out" ([`p4-2`](#p4-1)). It matters that the direction is the opposite: in Varma the solution with the **smaller** norm wins, here the one with the larger. The authors state this outright in a footnote: the growth of the selective weights yields effective sparsity because large weights dominate in linear layers.

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work **refines the notion itself**: in that phase the network is not quite memorising. On addition the accuracy before the grokking is exactly what commutativity affords — that is, one property of the operation has already been generalised; on division (which is non-commutative) the generalisation before the transition is near zero ([`p4-4`](#p4-3)). "Memorisation" turns out to be a dense subnetwork that generalises somewhat.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the transition receives an **internal signature computable from the training data**: the effective sparsity falls by orders of magnitude simultaneously with the jump in test accuracy, yet it is measured without a test set. This is a rare property for a progress measure.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the effective sparsity is precisely a candidate for such a measure, and of a different kind from the Fourier measures of Nanda et al.: it requires no knowledge of the task's algorithm.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — a direct comparison: Nanda et al. found the sparsity **in the Fourier domain**, here it is found in the ordinary structure of the network ([`p3-5`](#p3-4)). Two independent ways of seeing one and the same compression.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — the work starts from the observation of Thilak et al. about the sigmoidal growth of the last layer's norm and refines it: the growth of the norm is not general but **selective** — explosive in some neurons and decaying in others. This answers a reproach the authors themselves formulate: Thilak et al. give no description of the behaviour of individual neurons.

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — grokking is proposed as a **controllable test bench** for the emergent abilities of large language models. In the conclusion this is brought down to a concrete hypothesis: in Dettmers et al. a small subset of the weights of pre-trained language models has a large magnitude and overwhelmingly explains the predictions — that is, the same picture of "a directed growth of the norm → sparsity".

**[Effective theory / the statistical mechanics of learning](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — the work notes that similar phase transitions have long been studied in statistical physics, but in a different setting (online descent, large limits), and formulates the requirements for an "effective theory" of such phenomena: to understand the causal mechanisms, to predict where the transition will occur, and to disentangle the statistical from the computational side.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the notion receives an explanation through a change of the dominant structure rather than through a change within one. The work's limitation is named by the authors themselves: it is shown "at least in a specific setting" — one task, one architecture, five seeds.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the binary-operator task on which the commutativity argument is checked; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — sparsity in the Fourier domain against sparsity in the structure; [Slingshot](../2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md) — the observation about the growth of the norm, refined here into a selective one; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — the same idea of a competition of two circuits, but with the opposite sign in parameter norm.

**[Sparsity](../../concepts/activation-sparsity.md)** — the work links two of its forms: the sparsity in the Fourier domain noticed earlier, and the sparsity of the ordinary structure of the network ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). It matters that the sparsity here is not postulated but derived from a selective growth of the norm in a small set of neurons.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is short (workshop format) and cautious in its formulations: "we believe", "at least in a specific setting". It is usually cited more firmly than it is written.

**What is measured is measured firmly and elegantly.** The effective sparsity — the number of neurons of the smallest subnetwork reproducing the full network's predictions — falls by orders of magnitude exactly when the transition in the loss occurs ([`p3-1`](#p2-5)). The fidelity of the sparse subnetwork jumps to full while the quality of its complement falls at the same time: this is a measured change of the dominant structure rather than a paraphrase of a training curve. A separate value of the measure is that it is computable **from the training data** while correlating with the test transition.

**The causality is not established, and the authors say so.** "We believe this is likely a causal effect of norm growth" — the work contains no intervention that would suppress the norm growth in the selected neurons and check whether the transition disappears. The order "a selective growth of the norm → the dominance of the sparse subnetwork" is shown by a coincidence in time.

**The strongest place concerns the memorisation phase.** On addition the accuracy before the grokking is exactly what commutativity affords: the network has already generalised one property of the operation although it looks as though it is memorising. On division, where there is no commutativity, the generalisation before the transition is near zero ([`p4-4`](#p4-3)). This is a checkable distinction over two operators, and it changes the meaning of the word "memorisation" for the whole corpus.

**The conclusion about DNFs is weaker than it looks.** The subnetwork size over five runs is 6 or 8 neurons, which coincides with the sizes of two DNF constructions for the parity of three bits. But only the **number of neurons** coincides: there is no matching of the weights to the construction. And most importantly, there exists a 4-neuron construction through a threshold gate which the networks **do not find**. The size of the subnetwork therefore speaks rather of which solutions are reachable for SGD than of which circuit the task requires. The authors give the fact but do not work through its consequences for their own conclusion.

**The role of the regularisation is not isolated.** The sparsification is put down to the norm regularisation "coming into force" once the bulk of the loss has been zeroed. Meanwhile a run at a smaller weight decay gives the same picture, and there is no run **without** weight decay at all — so the necessity of the regularisation for the mechanism is not checked.

**The support over which the sparsity is measured is not specified.** The definition of the active subnetwork requires the predictions to coincide "for all $x\in\mathcal{X}$", but which $\mathcal{X}$ is taken in the experiments is not said. The measured quantity depends on this directly.

**Its place in the corpus.** The mechanism is opposite in sign to the explanation of Varma et al.: there the circuit with the **smaller** parameter norm wins, here the subnetwork with the growing one. Both works speak of a competition of two circuits and disagree about what confers the victory; in the corpus this disagreement remains open. The observation of Thilak et al. about the growth of the last layer's norm is refined here into a selective one, and the Fourier-domain sparsity of Nanda et al. receives an analogue in the ordinary structure of the network.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns of misreading or overstatement of this work, as observed in the citing papers of the corpus. Each entry says what exactly gets distorted in the restatement and leads to the authors' own paragraphs, where the qualification that goes missing still stands. The specific wordings are examined in the citing works' cards, in their "Common misreadings and overstatements" sections, where the "details" links lead.

**Merging "Merrill; Varma" into a single thesis (an over-extension).** Four works of the corpus cite this paper and [Varma et al.](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) as a pair under the common statement "grokking is a competition of a sparse and a dense subnetwork". The merger erases an open disagreement: here the subnetwork with the [growing norm](#p4-1) wins, in Varma the circuit with the smaller norm (efficiency); the signs are opposite, and not one of the citing works notes it. Echoes: [2504.13292](../2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model/2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model.card.md), [2512.03437](../2512.03437.grokked-models-are-better-unlearners/2512.03437.grokked-models-are-better-unlearners.card.md), [2603.15492](../2603.15492.grokking-as-a-variance-limited-phase-transition-spectral-gating-and-the-epsilon-stability-threshold/2603.15492.grokking-as-a-variance-limited-phase-transition-spectral-gating-and-the-epsilon-stability-threshold.card.md), [2604.13123](../2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation/2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation.card.md) — their formulations are analysed in the mc-2309-02390 items of the citing works' cards.
