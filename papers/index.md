# Index of the corpus papers

168 works. The texts of the works that have cards are stored in the folders of those cards (`original/`); the txt extractions of the source repository were not copied here. Cards have been created for all one hundred and sixty-eight — links point to them, and every such work has a description; the import queue is empty.

The format of a line in the first list: `arxiv-id` · total: citations · in corpus: citations · title, with a description below the line: what the corpus inherited from the work, how it differs from its neighbours and what it does not have. The second list is the same selection in chronological order, as a table and without descriptions.

The "total" figure is the work's own citationCount from Semantic Scholar (a single source, decision D1-A of task-008), taken from [citations-2201.02177.txt](citations-2201.02177.txt). Every work of the corpus has a "total" figure (and so do those moved out to the externals — their rows remain with the verdict =ext); the snapshots of the two sections are ten days apart, so neighbouring figures are comparable with that correction in mind.

The "in corpus" figure is how many works of the corpus cite this one, computed from the stored `original/` texts ([citations-in-corpus.txt](citations-in-corpus.txt); the counting rule and the lists of citing works are in its header; task-012). It measures the weight of a work inside the conversation about grokking rather than its general fame: the second work of the list by "total", 2212.07677, has an "in corpus" figure of only 4. Every work of the list now has a folder in `papers/`, and the figure has been computed for each.

## By citation count

The corpus's founding work is pinned first, outside the sort order: it introduces the term itself, and the corpus is built around it. Its own "total" figure (719, snapshot of 2026-08-24) is taken from the supplementary section of the table; it is worth noting that by this counter it is no longer first — Nanda et al. 2023 has more citations (941, snapshot of 2026-08-14).

- `2201.02177` · total: **719** · in corpus: **164** · [Power et al. 2022 — Grokking: Generalization Beyond Overfitting on Small Algorithmic Datasets](2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md)

  The founding work of the corpus: it introduces the term **grokking** and sets the subject itself — generalization arriving orders of magnitude later than overfitting. The corpus inherited its testbed from it: tables of binary operations with uninformative symbols (modular arithmetic at a prime modulus and the composition of permutations), the training-data fraction as a control quantity, weight decay as the most effective of the interventions swept. The search here proceeds by sweeping interventions rather than by analysing a mechanism: the work proposes no mechanism, introduces no progress measures and does not use the language of phase transitions — all of that appears in later authors, and a noticeable part of the corpus is written as an answer to the gap left here. The numbers — the composition of the task set, the hyperparameters, the caveat about the canonical plot of figure 1 — are in the card.

What follows is in decreasing order of citation count.

- `2301.05217` · total: **941** · in corpus: **141** · [Nanda et al. 2023 — Progress measures for grokking via mechanistic interpretability](2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md)

  A complete reverse-engineering of a one-layer transformer on modular addition: the network maps the inputs onto a circle and adds the rotations through a discrete Fourier transform and trigonometric identities. From the mechanism understood, two progress measures are derived — the restricted and the excluded loss — and training is divided into three phases: memorization, circuit formation, cleanup. The main conclusion contests the abruptness: the jump in test accuracy falls on the cleanup, already **after** the generalizing mechanism has been learned.

- `2205.10343` · total: **281** · in corpus: **100** · [Liu et al. 2022 — Towards Understanding Grokking: An Effective Theory of Representation Learning](2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md)

  The first work explaining grokking through **representation learning**. The structure is defined operationally — through $\delta$-parallelograms $\mathbf{E}_{i}+\mathbf{E}_{j}=\mathbf{E}_{m}+\mathbf{E}_{n}$ and their fraction RQI — and the critical dataset size is derived as the point at which the representation becomes uniquely determined: the prediction $r_{c}\approx0.4$ was made before the experiment and confirmed. It introduces the **Goldilocks zone** and the four phases of training (comprehension, grokking, memorization, confusion), from which it follows that grokking is a region in hyperparameter space rather than a property of the task. Here too grokking is for the first time induced outside algorithmic data, on MNIST. The effective theory is meanwhile postulated: the derivation in appendix L holds only for a linear decoder in the adiabatic limit, whose conditions are not met in the experiments.

- `2207.08799` · total: **208** · in corpus: **59** · [Barak et al. 2022 — Hidden Progress in Deep Learning: SGD Learns Parities Near the Computational Limit](2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit/2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit.card.md)

  The progenitor of the **hidden progress** paradigm: from it the corpus took both the term itself and the general definition of a progress measure — any function of the training state that predicts the time to convergence. The testbed is inherited too: sparse parity is the corpus's only task in which the transition point is tied to a proven computational limit (a lower bound for statistical-query algorithms) rather than to an empirical threshold. The search here is not for an explanation of grokking but for an answer to the question of what SGD is busy with on the plateau; the answer — the gradual amplification of a sparse solution through a Fourier gap in the population gradient — is set against a Langevin random search, and the whole work rests on that contrast. The difference from its neighbours is that the plateau here is not only measured but proved: for an idealized architecture the share of the time spent reaching "slightly better than trivial" tends to one in **deterministic** gradient flow, where there is no batch noise at all — which separates explanations of the transition through the structure of the task from explanations through a noise-driven escape from a metastable state. What the work does not have: an explanation of grokking, and indeed any connection of hidden progress with it. The main results are taken in the online setting, where a fresh batch at every step excludes overfitting and hence the gap between training and validation error; grokking is a separate finite-sample experiment of appendix C.5, on which none of the three proposed progress measures is computed. Nor is there causality here: "causal" is the citers' word, not the authors'. The numbers — the grid of experiments, the batch sizes, the conditions of the theorems — are in the card.

- `2306.17844` · total: **178** · in corpus: **28** · [Zhong et al. 2023 — The Clock and the Pizza: Two Stories in Mechanistic Explanation of Neural Networks](2306.17844.the-clock-and-the-pizza-two-stories-in-mechanistic-explanation-of-neural-networks/2306.17844.the-clock-and-the-pizza-two-stories-in-mechanistic-explanation-of-neural-networks.card.md)

  The work that took from the corpus the assumption that the mechanism is unique: on modular addition a network implements not only the "clock" of Nanda et al. but also a previously undescribed "pizza", and sometimes several copies of one algorithm at once. The corpus inherited from it above all a measurement kit — gradient symmetricity, distance irrelevance and circularity, three quantities by which a trained network can be assigned automatically to an algorithm — the technique of isolating circles, and the attention-rate parameter, which continuously turns a transformer into an MLP. The search here is not for an explanation of the delay but for a map: which solutions arise at which hyperparameters — and the transition between solutions is called representational, because both phases give perfect accuracy and only the internal machinery changes. In this the work differs from its neighbours twice over: the axis of its transition is a hyperparameter rather than training time, and the performance does not change at the transition at all. What the work does not have: the word "grokking" — it stands only in four bibliography entries; curves of delayed generalization; and indeed any possibility of seeing them, because runs that did not reach perfect validation accuracy are discarded from the analysis. Nor is there an analysis of the non-circular solutions, which in deep networks make up the majority of the runs. The numbers — the modulus, the training-data fraction, the fractions of explained variance, the thresholds of the measures — are in the card.

- `2302.03025` · total: **164** · in corpus: **34** · [Chughtai et al. 2023 — A Toy Model of Universality: Reverse Engineering how Networks Learn Group Operations](2302.03025.a-toy-model-of-universality-reverse-engineering-how-networks-learn-group-operations/2302.03025.a-toy-model-of-universality-reverse-engineering-how-networks-learn-group-operations.card.md)

  The work from which the corpus traces its **representation-theoretic** language for describing algorithmic solutions: group composition through the matrices of irreducible representations and characters, with modular addition a special case, composition in a cyclic group. From it too come the testbed of seven groups on two architectures and the names of the $S_{5}$ representations that the corpus still uses. The difference from its neighbours lies in the object of the search: the analysis is carried out not in order to explain grokking (the mechanism and both progress measures are taken ready-made from Nanda et al. 2023) but in order to test the **universality hypothesis**, for which the task was deliberately chosen so that all the solutions could be enumerated and what was learned checked against the complete list. The answer obtained is twofold and split into two forms: weak universality holds, strong universality does not, and the latter is shown in three independent ways. Here too the corpus gains an unusually strong argument from ablation — a loss **worse than random** — and a prediction of the authors' own about a preference for simple features, refuted by them. What the work does not have: a proof that networks converge to a single solution — the authors' conclusion is directly the opposite, and the extended thesis was later contested by an analysis through cosets on the same setting; there is no experiment without regularization, no sweep over the data fraction and no transfer to real models. The numbers — the hyperparameters, the coverage of the runs, the values of the ablations — are in the card.

- `2210.01117` · total: **159** · in corpus: **118** · [Liu et al. 2022 — Omnigrok: Grokking Beyond Algorithmic Data](2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md)

  Explains grokking through **loss landscapes**: the reduced training and test losses as functions of the weight norm have the shapes "L" and "U", and their mismatch at $w>w_{c}$ is the cause of the delay. From this the law $t\propto\gamma^{-1}$ is derived — the time to generalization is inversely proportional to weight decay — confirmed over about two orders of magnitude. Grokking here is **reversible in both directions**: it is induced by increasing the initialization scale (MNIST, IMDb, QM9) and removed by training at a constant weight norm in the original setting of Power et al. The Goldilocks zone moves from hyperparameter space into weight space — a spherical shell of radius $w_{c}$.

- `2310.06110` · total: **112** · in corpus: **65** · [Kumar et al. 2023 — Grokking as the Transition from Lazy to Rich Training Dynamics](2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md)

  Explains grokking by the transition from **lazy dynamics to feature learning**: the network first fits the sample as a linear model with the initial NTK and rebuilds its features later — and the gap between these stages is the delayed generalization. It is governed by the output scale $\alpha$ (the laziness) and the alignment of the initial kernel with the task; with both, grokking is induced and removed, including in the transformer setting of Nanda et al. The key thing for the corpus is a **counterexample**: a two-layer MLP on modular arithmetic groks without weight decay at a **growing** weight norm, which directly contradicts the explanations of Omnigrok and of Varma et al. Appendix 16 shows that "grokking in accuracy" on a regression may be an artefact of the choice of threshold.

- `2309.02390` · total: **110** · in corpus: **83** · [Varma & Shah et al. 2023 — Explaining grokking through circuit efficiency](2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md)

  Explains grokking by a competition between two families of circuits: the generalizing one is learned more slowly but is more efficient — it produces larger logits at the same parameter norm, and weight decay prefers it. From this a critical dataset size $D_{\textrm{crit}}$ is introduced, at which the efficiencies are equal. From the theory, **before the experiment**, two new phenomena were predicted and then found: ungrokking (a fall back to memorization when fine-tuning on a small sample) and semi-grokking (a transition to intermediate accuracy). The authors themselves note that the explanation is incomplete: grokking is observed without weight decay as well.

- `2206.04817` · total: **98** · in corpus: **69** · [Thilak et al. 2022 — The Slingshot Mechanism: An Empirical Study of Adaptive Optimizers and the Grokking Phenomenon](2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md)

  Explains grokking by an **anomaly of adaptive optimizers**. After perfect accuracy is reached, the weight norm of the last layer behaves cyclically: rapid growth, an abrupt transition with a loss spike, a plateau — and the authors call the full cycle the "slingshot effect". The features change in a jerk **only** at the transition. Without explicit regularization grokking arises almost exclusively at the moment the slingshot begins; the slingshot is present in Adam, AdamW and RMSProp and absent in SGD and Adagrad, and it is governed by the $\epsilon$ in the denominator of the adaptive step. The most heavily illustrated work of the corpus — 175 figures. The authors admit outright that **they have no theoretical explanation of the mechanism**, and show in appendix C that weight decay gives the same generalization faster and without a slingshot.

- `2303.11873` · total: **91** · in corpus: **54** · [Merrill et al. 2023 — A Tale of Two Circuits: Grokking as Competition of Sparse and Dense Subnetworks](2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md)

  Explains grokking by a **competition between two subnetworks**: before the transition the predictions are governed by the dense one, after it by the sparse one, and they barely overlap. The mechanism is selective norm growth: in the neurons of the future sparse subnetwork the norm grows explosively, in the rest it decays and they "die out". A measurable **effective sparsity** is introduced — the size of the smallest subnetwork reproducing the predictions of the full one; it falls by orders of magnitude simultaneously with the transition and is computable from the training data. Separately valuable is the observation that the memorization phase **does not quite memorize**: on addition the accuracy before grokking is exactly what commutativity alone gives. The size of the subnetwork (6 or 8 neurons) matches the DNF constructions for parity, but the networks do not find the 4-neuron construction.

- `2405.15071` · total: **90** · in corpus: **16** · [Wang et al. 2024 — Grokked Transformers are Implicit Reasoners: A Mechanistic Journey to the Edge of Generalization](2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners/2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners.card.md)

  Carries grokking over into the domain of **reasoning over a knowledge graph**: a model is trained on a mixture of atomic facts and facts derived from them by hidden rules, and tested on unseen inferences. The main result for the corpus is an **objection about the critical dataset size**: the speed of grokking is determined by the *ratio* of derived to atomic facts and does not depend on the sample size at all. The mechanism is explained through the circuit efficiency of Varma et al., and in a quantitative form. Systematicity depends on the configuration of the circuit: for composition it is sequential, the second-hop facts are stored in a separate copy — and there is no OOD generalization even after 2 million steps; for comparison the circuit is parallel — and systematicity is present. The architectural cause is verified by intervention: sharing parameters across layers unlocks OOD generalization.

- `2301.02679` · total: **86** · in corpus: **51** · [Gromov 2023 — Grokking modular arithmetic](2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md)

  The corpus's only work in which the task is solved **analytically**: the weights of a two-layer perceptron with a quadratic activation computing $n+m\,\text{mod}\,p$ are written out in closed form as cosines with frequencies $2\pi k/p$ and three sets of phases, tied by the condition $\varphi^{(1)}+\varphi^{(2)}=\varphi^{(3)}$. The Fourier structure here is not discovered in a trained network but derived from the task: the solution is a $\delta$-function assembled by the constructive interference of waves. The main contribution to the corpus is a **counterexample**: grokking is obtained with ordinary gradient descent and MSE, with no regularization at all, without an adaptive optimizer and without a trainable encoder, which at a stroke bounds the domain of the explanations through weight decay, through the slingshot and through the competition of encoder with decoder. A measure is introduced — the inverse participation ratio (IPR) of the Fourier transform of the weights — which grows from the very start of training and is computable without a test set. The author declares his own explanation deliberately simple: once the training loss has bottomed out, the only way to reduce it further is to start learning the right features.

- `2311.18817` · total: **85** · in corpus: **53** · [Lyu et al. 2024 — Dichotomy of Early and Late Phase Implicit Biases Can Provably Induce Grokking](2311.18817.dichotomy-of-early-and-late-phase-implicit-biases-can-provably-induce-grokking/2311.18817.dichotomy-of-early-and-late-phase-implicit-biases-can-provably-induce-grokking.card.md)

  The work from which the corpus received the **dichotomy of the early and late implicit biases** — the most cited theoretical frame for grokking here — and with it a testable number: the time of the transition $\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$, logarithmic in the initialization scale and inversely proportional to weight decay. From it too *misgrokking* entered the corpus: a dataset built by inverting the condition, on which a network first generalizes and after long training falls back almost to chance — the most widely used counterexample to identifying grokking with the acquisition of simplicity. The direction of the search sets it apart from its neighbours: not a mechanism and not a progress measure, but the **sharpness** of the transition as the object of proof; one common reproach is put to its predecessors — the absence of a rigorous analysis of the training dynamics. The recipe "large initialization plus a small non-zero weight decay" is taken wholesale from Liu et al. 2023, but the explanation through the Goldilocks zone is rejected by an argument from homogeneity: for a homogeneous network the norm does not bound expressivity, and a narrow zone in norm itself calls for an explanation. What the work does not have: (1) grokking is proved only on two constructed examples — sparse linear classification with diagonal networks and matrix completion — while for modular addition, the only task from the grokking literature shown here, nothing is proved; (2) the late side of both theorems yields a KKT point, not a global optimum; (3) the sharpness is a property of the limit $\alpha\to+\infty$, with no estimates at finite values; (4) what is analysed is gradient flow, not the GD, SGD or Adam with which the experiments themselves are run. The numbers of the experiments are in the card.

- `2305.18741` · total: **78** · in corpus: **19** · [Murty et al. 2023 — Grokking of Hierarchical Structure in Vanilla Transformers](2305.18741.grokking-of-hierarchical-structure-in-vanilla-transformers/2305.18741.grokking-of-hierarchical-structure-in-vanilla-transformers.card.md)

  The work that carried grokking out of algorithmic tables into language: the corpus inherited from it the name **structural grokking** and with it a setting in which what is delayed is not memorization but the choice between two rules — the hierarchical and the linear — that explain the training sample equally well. The testbed is taken not from Power et al. but from McCoy et al. 2020: ambiguous data plus a separate out-of-distribution set that only a model having acquired the hierarchical rule passes. The search here is not for the machinery of the delay but for the internal marker by which an architecture capable of surviving it can be recognized — and the answer is negative for two markers and positive for a third: the weight norms and the sparsity of attention grow in all models and do not distinguish the successful depths from the unsuccessful ones, whereas the tree-structuredness of the computation is greatest precisely at the suitable depth. From it too the corpus takes two things it has nowhere else: the control quantity is the model's **depth** rather than the data fraction or weight decay, and the dependence on it is an inverted U — the intermediate depths generalize, not the deep ones. What the work does not have: a memorizing dip on held-out data from the same distribution — the in-domain validation accuracy is near 100 % early and for a long time; the machinery of the phenomenon; an intervention that would switch it on through tree-structuredness; a check of whether the deep models grok beyond the allotted step budget; and real language data — all three sets are built from context-free grammars, which the authors state outright. The value of the weight decay is nowhere named. The numbers — the composition of the sets, the depths, the step budget, the hyperparameters — are in the card.

- `2211.12316` · total: **78** · in corpus: **6** · [Bhattamishra et al. 2023 — Simplicity Bias in Transformers and their Ability to Learn Sparse Boolean Functions](2211.12316.simplicity-bias-in-transformers-and-their-ability-to-learn-sparse-boolean-functions/2211.12316.simplicity-bias-in-transformers-and-their-ability-to-learn-sparse-boolean-functions.card.md)

  The corpus's only work comparing **two architectures** on one testbed: from it the corpus inherited Boolean sensitivity as a measurable notion of simplicity and the observation that on sparse parity a transformer and an LSTM swap places — on full parity the recurrent model generalizes, on sparse parity only the transformer does, and at 5-20 % flipped labels at that. The search is not for the machinery of the delay but for the inductive bias: the complexity of the functions is measured on randomly initialized models, then at every epoch of training, and only afterwards checked on tasks of low sensitivity. The difference from its neighbours on this testbed lies in two things: the target function is known, so generalization means agreement with the target rather than a rise in validation accuracy; and sparse parity acts not as a testbed for a theory but as a task that separates architectures. Grokking here is a side observation of appendix E, and that is exactly what is valuable to the corpus: it is obtained under Adam **without** weight decay, and the window in training-set size is shifted by the choice of positional encoding. What the work does not have: an explanation — asked directly whether a bias toward low sensitivity explains the success on sparse functions, the authors answer "No", and why the LSTM overfits they likewise declare unknown; there is no theory, no data fraction as a control quantity, no measurement of the grokking delay; three negative results are named by the authors themselves — variable length, the SST/IMDB texts and arbitrary $k$-juntas at $n=100$, $k=5$. The numbers — the hyperparameter grid, the set sizes, the noise fractions — are in the card.

- `2303.06173` · total: **63** · in corpus: **42** · [Davies, Langosco, Krueger 2023 — Unifying Grokking and Double Descent](2303.06173.unifying-grokking-and-double-descent/2303.06173.unifying-grokking-and-double-descent.card.md)

  The origin of the corpus's unifying line: from it comes the thesis that grokking and double descent are not two phenomena but one, together with the vocabulary that carries the thesis — **patterns**, learned at different speeds and generalizing differently. From it too the corpus took a second axis of grokking: the term *model-wise grokking* (grokking in model size) is introduced here, and later works refer to it precisely as introduced here. The search is not for the machinery of a network but for a conceptual frame: a toy model of sigmoids with the limiting predictiveness and the learning speed decoupled reproduces both shapes of curve, and the passage between them is obtained by changing a single kind of parameter. The difference from its neighbours in the unifying line lies in direction: in Huang et al. 2024 the same claim is carried through a competition of circuits with measurable quantities, whereas here there are no quantities at all, only the posing of a question and one experiment. What the work does not have: a fit of the model to data — the parameters of the sigmoids are set by hand, and so what is shown is only that both shapes of curve are generable by one family of sigmoids, not that they are generated by it in a real network. There is neither a second task, nor a second architecture, nor seeds and spreads, nor an analysis of the objection of Power et al. — it is quoted verbatim and left unanswered. The title is assertive while the authors' own formulation is hedged ("we conjecture"), and in citation that hedge is the first thing to go. The most solid place in the work is a prediction made before the experiment and confirmed quantitatively: an early pattern with $0/b=0$ produces a spike in validation accuracy above chance. The numbers — the settings, the sizes, the peak at 96 examples — are in the card.

- `2402.15555` · total: **61** · in corpus: **28** · [Humayun et al. 2024 — Deep Networks Always Grok and Here is Why](2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md)

  The work to which the corpus owes **delayed robustness**: if instead of a validation set one looks at adversarial examples regenerated after every training step, a late rise in quality is found where the validation curves show none. From it too the corpus took **local complexity** — the density of the non-linearities of the spline partition around a point, the only progress measure that depends neither on the dataset, nor on the labels, nor on the loss function, and is therefore measurable around randomly drawn points — and **region migration**: the observation that late in training the non-linearities move away from the training points toward the decision boundary. The search here goes wide rather than deep: instead of one task, six families of settings from MNIST to the text of Shakespeare are swept; instead of a mechanism, a geometry is proposed; and the main lever turns out to be not regularization but batch normalization, which damps the phenomenon. In this the work differs from its neighbours: its interventions change the geometry of the input space rather than the weights, and weight decay in it is zero almost everywhere. What the work does not have: causality — a coincidence in time is shown, and there is no intervention changing the measure independently; a definition distinguishing grokking from any late rise in any metric; and the universality claimed by the title — in the one large setting (a ResNet18 with batch normalization on full ImageNet) there is no region migration at all. The numbers — the measurement settings, the attack parameters, the region densities near the decision boundary — are in the card.

- `2310.03789` · total: **52** · in corpus: **27** · [Rubin et al. 2024 — Grokking as a First Order Phase Transition in Two Layer Networks](2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.card.md)

  The corpus's most theoretical work: grokking is not compared to a phase transition but **mapped** onto one — an order parameter, an action, the saddles and the condition of their degeneracy are written out, and for the modular model the transition point is found analytically. From a first-order transition a mixed phase follows: some of the neurons already fluctuate around the saddle that knows about the teacher (the authors call such neurons **droplets**), while others still fluctuate around the zero one — and this prediction is checked on an ensemble of trained networks. The most valuable thing for the corpus, however, is not the explanation but the join with Gromov: his cosine solution is derived here by a second, independent route, and one of its restrictions is removed along the way. It must be read with two of the authors' caveats: what is called "grokking" in the main part is not the attainment of near-perfect accuracy but a jump in a different quantity, and the training throughout is equilibrium Langevin rather than SGD — the transfer to the trajectories the rest of the corpus studies is not justified. The formulas, the phases and the numbers of the experiments are in the card.

- `2406.06158` · total: **43** · in corpus: **2** · [Kunin et al. 2024 — Get rich quick: exact solutions reveal how unbalanced initializations promote rapid feature learning](2406.06158.get-rich-quick-exact-solutions-reveal-how-unbalanced-initializations-promote-rapid-feature-learning/2406.06158.get-rich-quick-exact-solutions-reveal-how-unbalanced-initializations-promote-rapid-feature-learning.card.md)

  The point at which the corpus gains a **second axis** for describing the training regime. Before it, a network's position between lazy and rich was reduced to the overall initialization scale; here a layerwise imbalance is added to it — the difference between the weights of neighbouring layers, conserved under gradient flow — and unlike earlier treatments it has a direction: the early layers may learn faster (upstream) or the late ones may (downstream), and these are two different worlds, not one "imbalance".
