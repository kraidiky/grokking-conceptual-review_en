## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work asks not "why does it happen" but "is it worth it". The answer is decomposed into three findings: the reasoning pathways of a grokked and a non-grokked model are **identical**; the behavioural grokking and the formation of the circuit are **decoupled**; and the transferability even of a mature circuit is **limited** ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The concluding reading: grokking is not the acquisition of a new way of reasoning but the process of embedding memorised atomic facts into an already formed pathway ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)).

**[Reasoning over knowledge graphs](../../concepts/reasoning-knowledge-graphs.md)** — the setting is taken from Wang et al. (2024): a synthetic graph of 2000 entities and 200 relations, 40 000 atomic facts, 5 % held out as OOD ([`p2-5`](#p2-5), [`p9-2`](#p9-2)). The training sees all the atomic facts but the composite queries only from ID facts; the test requires composing two hops through an OOD fact ([`p2-5`](#p2-5)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the working method: the logit lens decodes the hidden states of all the layers, and an inference trace is marked "the bridge entity is found" if the decoded state coincides with the true intermediate entity ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)). The generalisation circuit is defined precisely so — through an explicit recovery of the intermediate entity in the middle layers.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — natural grokking is decomposed into three eras: the saturation of the training objectives (~0.1 M steps), a stable recovery of the intermediate entity for OOD (+0.2 M), and learning how to use that entity (established after 1 M steps) ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)). It matters that the "transition" here is not a single jump but three separate events.

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the main objection to the corpus: under a full augmentation of both hops the circuit forms within 0.3 M steps **without** a long grokking era, and the transferability of such a model is the same ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). Hence the conclusion that long training merely embeds the sparsely supervised OOD facts into an already ready circuit ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)).

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — the conjecture of Varma et al. is taken into the work as a working foundation: a high ratio of inferred queries to atomic facts ($\phi=18$) increases the relative complexity of the memorising circuit and prompts the model to pass to the generalising one sooner ([`p9-7`](#p9-7)).

**[Semi-grokking](../../concepts/semi-grokking.md)** — a new distinction of the same kind is introduced: **fake grokking**. Under an augmentation of the second hop alone the training curve answers the behavioural description of grokking, yet the final answer arrives **not** through a recovered intermediate entity ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). Such models then fail at transfer ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)).

**[Freezing a subnetwork](../../concepts/freezing-subnetwork.md)** — the transfer question is posed as fine-tuning on 2000 new atomic facts while retaining 8000 old ones ([`p4-2`](#p4-2), [`p4-3`](#p4-3)). The outcome is twofold: when a new fact is needed at the **first** hop the circuit copes instantly; when at the **second** it does not cope, despite a correctly recovered intermediate entity ([`p4-4`](#p4-4), [`p4-5`](#p4-5)).

**[Catastrophic forgetting](../../concepts/catastrophic-forgetting.md)** — accounted for by the construction of the fine-tuning data: 8000 of the original ID facts are deliberately retained so that the earlier knowledge does not crumble ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)).

**[The emergence of abilities](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — the practical conclusion is formulated as a trade-off: the decision to pursue grokking is a choice between a scarcity of data and computational resources; with sufficient composite data one may stop at the saturation of the training ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)).

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — an explanation of why ordinary transformers fail at this task: a representational mismatch between independent layers, in which the deep layers are not trained to take the hidden representation of the intermediate entity as input ([`p1-6`](#p1-6)). Shared parameters remove that mismatch.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — it is absent here altogether: the task is composite and factual rather than algebraic. For the corpus this is a rare case in which grokking is studied on reasoning over facts.

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no analysis of where exactly the boundary lies between the formation of a circuit and fake grokking: a sweep over augmentation strategies is admitted to be computationally infeasible ([`p5-2`](#p5-2)). Nor is there a carry-over to ordinary transformers: all the results are confined to architectures with shared parameters, and this is stated ([`p5-3`](#p5-3)). There is no number of runs for any experiment.

**[Behavioural against true grokking](../../concepts/behavioral-vs-true-grokking.md)** — the distinction here is measured rather than postulated: grokked and non-grokked models follow identical reasoning pathways ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)), that is, a jump in accuracy does not guarantee that a circuit has formed.

**[Negative results](../../concepts/negative-results.md)** — the work names outright the reason why negative checks are few in the corpus: an investigation of "fake grokking" is expensive and therefore not exhaustive ([`p5-2`](#p5-2)). The caveat is kept as it stands, because it delimits the scope of the positive conclusion.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is short (five pages of main text) and wholly mechanistic: three findings, one setting, not a single theorem.

**The question is posed correctly, and it should have been posed long ago.** The corpus is full of techniques for accelerating grokking, but almost no one asked **what comes out at the end** and whether the wait is worth it. Here it is asked — and the answer is sobering.

**Finding 1 is the strongest and the most checkable.** Given a full composite augmentation of both hops, the circuit forms within 0.3 M steps instead of 1 M, and in transferability the two models are indistinguishable ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). The argument is built on a measurement of the mechanism rather than on a comparison of accuracies: in both models every correct prediction goes through a recovered intermediate entity.

**Hence a reformulation of grokking worth remembering.** The reasoning pathway forms **early** and for ID facts; the long era is needed only to draw into it the facts that were sparsely supervised ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)). This does not refute the circuit picture but changes its meaning: grokking turns out to be a property of the **labelling of the data** rather than a property of the optimisation.

**Finding 2 is useful in that it introduces a discriminating check.** "Fake grokking" is the case in which the curve looks like grokking while the mechanism is different ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). For the corpus this matters: almost all the works on accelerating grokking recognise it **by the curve**, and not one checks the circuit.

**Fake grokking, however, is shown in exactly one augmentation regime.** It is admitted outright: a sweep over strategies is computationally infeasible, and the boundary conditions remain an open question ([`p5-2`](#p5-2)). This is therefore an existence result rather than a measure of prevalence.

**Finding 3 is asymmetric, and that is interesting.** A new fact at the first hop is assimilated instantly, at the second it is not ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)). No explanation is given outright, but it suggests itself from the structure of the circuit: the first hop is a retrieval of an atomic fact, whereas the second requires the layers to be able to take the hidden representation of the intermediate entity as input for that particular new relation.

**The claim of an "incomplete mastery of composite logic" is broader than what is shown.** From the fact that the transfer requires either another grokking era or more data ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)) a limited transferability follows, but not that the logic has not been mastered. The human yardstick ("a rule once learned is applied at once") is introduced in section 5 as an expectation and is nowhere justified.

**The number of runs is not stated and there are no spread bands.** All the curves are single runs, and the distinction "grokking against fake grokking" rests on the appearance of the curves and on the fraction of traces with the intermediate entity found. For a work whose main argument is mechanistic this is a notable gap.

**The setting is artificial and small, and this is stated.** The knowledge graph is synthetic, the ratio $\phi=18$ is chosen deliberately for a quick appearance of the circuit ([`p9-6`](#p9-6)), and all of it is confined to transformers with shared parameters ([`p5-3`](#p5-3)). A carry-over to real language models is neither shown nor claimed.

**The practical conclusion is stated honestly and narrowly.** Not "grokking is unnecessary" but "grokking is the price of sparse supervision": with sufficient composite data one may stop at saturation ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)). This is directly opposite to the way grokking is usually presented in the corpus — as a desirable event to be hastened.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the wiki's first work assessing grokking **by its outcome** rather than by its mechanism: what kind of model results and whether what has been gained transfers. Two contributions are valuable: the distinction between behavioural and genuine grokking — a check that all the works on acceleration lack — and the reformulation of grokking as the embedding of sparsely supervised facts into an early-formed circuit. The price: one artificial setting, one family of architectures, no number of runs, and no analysis of the boundary between the genuine and the fake cases.

(2024); …"`: Grokfast does not induce grokking but accelerates one already under way; the induction of grokking in non-algorithmic tasks within the work itself is [a borrowed Omnigrok recipe: an inflated initialisation and truncated samples](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md#p7-3).
