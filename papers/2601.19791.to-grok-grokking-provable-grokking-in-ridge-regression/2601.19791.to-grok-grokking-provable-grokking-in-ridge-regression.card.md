## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work proves it **end to end**, from beginning to end, in ridge regression: (i) the model overfits early, (ii) a poor generalisation holds for a long time thereafter, (iii) the generalisation error eventually becomes arbitrarily small ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The priority claim is formulated precisely: these are the first rigorous quantitative bounds on the generalisation delay — on what the authors call the **grokking time** — expressed through the training hyperparameters ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The definition of the grokking time is made operational: $t_{1}$ is the last step at which the training loss is still above $\epsilon$, $t_{2}$ the first step at which the generalisation loss is already below $c$; grokking is a large $(t_{2}-t_{1})$ at $c\geq\epsilon$ ([`p4-9`](#p4-9)).

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — three theorems laid out over three eras. Theorem 4.4 gives a fast convergence of the training error, theorem 4.5 a lower bound on the generalisation loss that holds for a long time, theorem 4.6 the final guarantee $L(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}_{\lambda})\leq 2L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}_{\lambda})+\epsilon$ through an ordinary uniform convergence by Rademacher complexity ([`p6-7`](#p6-7), [`p7-4`](#p7-4), [`p7-5`](#p7-5)). The sample-size requirement comes from there too: $n=\Omega(b^{4}\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}\rVert_{2}^{4}\epsilon^{-2})$ ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the chief controlling lever of the whole picture. The convergence of the training loss **does not depend** on $\lambda$, whereas the time to generalisation is inversely proportional to it: $t_{2}\propto 1/\lambda$, whence $(t_{2}-t_{1})\rightarrow\infty$ as $\lambda\rightarrow 0$ ([`p6-5`](#p6-5)). The mechanism is set out plainly: at $m\gg n$ gradient descent effectively updates only the projection of the weights onto the data subspace, whereas the orthogonal component decays only under the action of the weight decay, at a rate of $(1-\eta\lambda)^{t}$ ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)). In other words, weight decay here is not a condition of the generalisation but its **clock**.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the second lever: increasing $\nu^{2}$ increases **both** times logarithmically, $t_{1},t_{2}\propto\ln(\nu^{2})$, but $t_{2}$ grows faster, whereupon the gap widens and the grokking is strengthened ([`p6-5`](#p6-5)). On a nonlinear network the same increase does not lengthen the grokking substantially, but it does widen the gap between the losses in the overfitting era ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)).

**[The critical data size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — a third lever, acting from the other side: decreasing the sample size $n$ strengthens the grokking by accelerating the convergence of the training loss, that is, by decreasing $t_{1}$ ([`p6-5`](#p6-5), [`p7-11`](#p7-11)). The theory gives no clean dependence of $t_{1}$ on $n$ and $m$, and the reason is stated outright: there simply is no quantitative bound on $\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Phi})$ in terms of $m$ and $n$ ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the whole sharpness of the bounds rests on the smallest **non-zero** eigenvalue $\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Phi})$: it is precisely its presence that separates the fast rate of the training loss from the slow rate $(1-\eta\lambda)^{2t}$ ([`p5-6`](#p5-6)). Remark 4.3 honestly caveats that only the limiting Marchenko–Pastur law is known and that there is no non-asymptotic bound ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)).

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the overfitting era here has an exact expression: the training error decays at a rate of $(1-\frac{1}{n}\eta\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Phi})-\eta\lambda)^{2t}$, whereas the generalisation error decays only at a rate of $(1-\eta\lambda)^{2t}$; the divergence of these two rates **is** the grokking ([`p5-6`](#p5-6)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — there is no transition here in the customary sense at all. Both quantities decay smoothly and predictably, and the appearance of an abrupt transition is given only by the logarithmic time axis ([`p1-2`](#p1-2), figure 1). This is an objection of substance for the corpus: grokking is obtained with no rearrangement of representations whatever.

**[The lazy-to-rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — a line from which the work demarcates itself outright. Lyu et al. (2024), Mohamadi et al. (2024) and Kumar et al. (2024) are analysed, and of each it is said what exactly is lacking: convergence to a KKT point does not give global optimality; the smallness of the weight norm is not proved; the delay is not shown beyond the first iteration ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)). The question of whether grokking can be proved **as** a lazy-to-rich transition is left open ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — named, with an explanation of why it is insufficient: an NTK analysis guarantees that every neuron stays near its initialisation, but the aggregate contribution of $m$ neurons is still capable of driving the network to the zero function, and hence does not secure a persistently poor generalisation ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — an interpretation carried into the discussion: the overfitted solution corresponds to training **without** weight decay, and the limiting one to the minimum-norm interpolating predictor; grokking is thereby a transition from a misspecified prior to a well-specified one ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)).

**[Varieties of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — the setting is deliberately classical: a squared loss plus an $\ell_{2}$ penalty, ordinary gradient descent at a fixed step size ([`p4-3`](#p4-3), [`p4-4`](#p4-4)). No dropout, no early stopping, no adaptive optimisers; the authors note that the analysis should go through for a decaying step size and for the gradient flow as well ([`p4-4`](#p4-4)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** and **[sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — named as the places where grokking was discovered and studied ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)), but they do not appear in the work itself at all. All the experiments are synthetic: ridge regression, a network with random ReLU features, and a two-layer ReLU network with a zero teacher ([`p7-7`](#p7-7), [`p7-11`](#p7-11), [`p8-2`](#p8-2)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is not a single real dataset: no MNIST, no text, no algorithmic tasks ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)). Nor is there the unrealisable case — it is named first among the open questions, with a note that under agnostic learning an arbitrarily small weight decay is capable of preventing generalisation altogether ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)). And there is no test-error plateau in its customary form: in regression the continuous loss decays smoothly, and the familiar plateau returns only on passing to a thresholded surrogate measure of accuracy ([`p9-3`](#p9-3), [`p22-7`](#p22-7)).

**[Grokking outside neural networks](../../concepts/grokking-in-non-neural-models.md)** — complete, end-to-end results on grokking are proved for overparameterised linear regression with weight decay ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The aim is stated outright — a full guarantee rather than a reproduction of a curve ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)); the solvability of the setting allows the lazy→rich explanation to be compared with other mechanisms directly ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)).

**[Grokking time](../../concepts/grokking-time.md)** — the time here enters the proof itself: the statements cover the whole path of the training rather than only the fact of the transition ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)).
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is theoretical: six theorems, three experimental settings, not a single real dataset.

**The chief contribution is not an explanation of grokking but a proof of it in a toy mechanism where everything can be computed.** The value lies precisely there: linear regression allows both convergence rates to be written out in closed form and lets one see that the delay is a difference of two exponents rather than a consequence of a rearrangement of features ([`p5-6`](#p5-6)). For a corpus in which nearly every explanation leans on a change of learning regime, this is an objection of substance: grokking is reproduced where there is nothing to change.

**The priority claim has been checked and appears to be correct.** The earlier works are analysed by name, and of each it is said which part of the complete result is lacking ([`p1-4`](#p1-4), [`p2-1`](#p2-1)). The closest is Xu et al. (2024), but they do not show that the delay extends beyond the first iteration — an exact and checkable argument.

**The bounds are quantitative, and that is a rarity.** $t_{1}$ is bounded above, $t_{2}$ below, both through explicit quantities ([`p5-13`](#p5-13), [`p6-1`](#p6-1)). More importantly, the bounds **predict the scaling rates** rather than only the sign of an effect, and the experiment confirms this: the dashed theoretical lines coincide with the solid experimental ones ([`p7-10`](#p7-10)).

**The bound on $t_{1}$ is not, however, closed with the bound on $t_{2}$ through the quantity $\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{+}$.** This is admitted outright: there is no quantitative estimate of that quantity in terms of $m$ and $n$ under any assumptions ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). The explicit bounds of equation (8) are obtained only under a Gaussian assumption on the features ([`p7-8`](#p7-8)) — that is, the handsome picture of figure 2 rests on an additional assumption absent from the theorems.

**The requirements on the hyperparameters are strong, and they are worth reading together.** Theorem 4.2 requires at once: $n=\Omega(b^{4}\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}\rVert^{4}/\epsilon^{2})$, $m=n+\Omega(\ldots)$ and $\lambda=O(\ldots)$ ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)). At $\epsilon=c=0.01$, as taken in the experiments, the first requirement gives very large $n$; in the experiments, meanwhile, $n=100$. The theory and the experiment, then, agree qualitatively rather than within the region where the theorems apply.

**The upper bound on $t_{1}$ is loose at large $\lambda$ — and the authors say so themselves.** A tight bound ought to carry $\eta\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}^{+}(\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Phi})+\eta\lambda$ in the denominator ([`p6-5`](#p6-5)). The caveat is honest, but it means that the control of the grokking is proved only from the small-weight-decay side.

**The realisability of the teacher is not a minor technical detail.** The whole proof rests on $L_{n}(\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*})=0$, and without it both the norm estimate $\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}_{\lambda}\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}\rVert_{2}$ and the connection with theorem 4.6 collapse. The authors acknowledge this, placing the unrealisable case in the very first open question and noting that there a small weight decay is capable of preventing generalisation altogether ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)).

**The experiments on nonlinear networks confirm the dependences but not the theory.** The agreement with figure 2 is qualitative ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)); no bounds are proved for the nonlinear case, and the authors do not claim any. The abstract's claim that the bounds "capture the grokking behaviour on nonlinear networks too" is easy to widen, when cited, into more than is shown.

**The zero-teacher setting deserves a separate caveat.** In the warm-up (theorem 4.1) and in the experiments with a two-layer network the teacher is taken to be the **zero function** ([`p5-1`](#p5-1), [`p8-2`](#p8-2)). Generalisation then means the convergence of the weights to zero, and "learning" reduces to decay. This makes the proof transparent, but it also makes the task further from the one for whose sake the notion of grokking was introduced.

**The test-error plateau is restored by a device worth remembering.** It is shown that in regression there is no plateau in the substance of the measure, and that the familiar picture is recovered if one counts as "accuracy" the fraction of examples with an error below a threshold ([`p9-3`](#p9-3), [`p22-7`](#p22-7)). The observation is valuable and double-edged: it explains why the plateau in Power et al. is so sharp — and reminds one at the same time that part of the sharpness of grokking is a property of the chosen measure.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the corpus's most rigorous work on the delay: the only one in which both eras are proved together and in which the grokking time is expressed through the hyperparameters by closed formulas. It is valuable too that it manages **without** any feature learning: no lazy-to-rich transition, no neural collapse, no compression of the description. The price: a realisable teacher, the absence of real data, a zero teacher in two settings out of three, and a gap between the region where the theorems apply and the region in which the experiments were run.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2201-02177
**Power et al. (2201.02177) — "long after" written into the definition of the phenomenon.** The introduction presents the delay as an unconditional property: `One of the most striking examples is “grokking” (Power et al., 2022), a phenomenon where generalization only starts improving long after the model achieves perfect training performance`; §1.1 extends the same unconditional formula backwards in time too: `the underlying behavior—where generalization emerges long after overfitting—had already been noted in earlier work by Blanc et al. (2020)`. In Power the formula is not unconditional: [the validation accuracy "sometimes" begins to rise abruptly](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#p2-3), [the size of the delay is governed by the fraction of training data](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-3-1-1), and [the canonical plot with the longest delay is run on Adam with no weight decay and a stretched budget — even though the paper's default is AdamW with a weight decay of 1](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-a1-2). That at a weight decay of order 1 there is no long delay is shown in [Omnigrok, figure 2](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#fig-2): the number of steps to generalisation is inversely proportional to the weight decay, and under a strong regularisation the generalisation is fast. The irony is that the whole of the rest of the paper proves exactly this conditionality: [the abstract already asserts that "grokking can be amplified or eliminated by a judicious choice of hyperparameters" and that it is "not an inherent defect of deep learning but a consequence of particular training conditions"](#p1-2) — only the definitional sentence with the reference to Power is erroneous, not the work's position.

###### mc-2206-04817
**Thilak et al. (2206.04817) — grokking unconditionally attributed to adaptive optimisers.** `"Thilak et al. (2022) attributed grokking to the use of adaptive optimizers—an optimization anomaly named the 'Slingshot Mechanism' (cyclic phase transitions)"`: the condition ["without explicit regularisation"](../2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md#p1-2) is dropped, as is the empirical character of the coincidence — the authors observed a concomitance, not a cause. The widened reading is refuted within the corpus: [Kumar et al.](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) and [Gromov](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) obtain grokking on vanilla gradient descent, where there is neither adaptivity nor a slingshot.

###### mc-2303-11873
**Merrill et al. (2303.11873) — assigned to the "non-neural-networks".** `"was also encountered in models that are different from neural networks (Blanc et al., 2020; Humayun et al., 2024; Merrill et al., 2023)"`: Merrill's setting is a [single-layer ReLU network](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/original/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.md#p1-3) (a transformer in the appendix) — these are neural networks precisely; grokking beyond neural networks is the subject of Miller et al. Elsewhere the same paper cites the target exemplarily: "attributed grokking **therein** to the competition…", with the scope retained.

###### mc-2405-15071
**Wang et al. (2405.15071) — assigned to the "LLMs".** `"and was also encountered … as well as in large language models (Zhu et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Xu et al., 2025)"`: Wang's models are GPT-2-like transformers of 8 layers trained from scratch on synthetic facts; [the work's setting](../2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners/2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners.card.md#p2-2) is neither large language models nor pretraining.
