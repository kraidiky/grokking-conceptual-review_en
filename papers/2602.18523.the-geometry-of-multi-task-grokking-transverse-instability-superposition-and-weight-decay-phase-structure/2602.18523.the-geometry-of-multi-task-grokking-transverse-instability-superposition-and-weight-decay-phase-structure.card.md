## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work carries the phenomenon over into a multi-task setting: one shared transformer trunk learns two operations (addition and multiplication modulo 97) or three (plus $x^{2}+y^{2}$), and each task groks at its own moment ([p. 1, para. 3](#p1-3)). From this the corpus gains an observation of a **staged** order: the tasks pass their transitions not simultaneously but one after another ([fig. 1](#fig-1)). Worth keeping alongside [Furuta et al. 2024](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md), where for some multi-task mixtures the opposite outcome was cautiously conjectured — simultaneous grokking of all tasks; here there are only two mixtures and both give a staircase. The definition of grokking here is twofold, and that matters: grokking proper is defined as reaching 98 % test accuracy held over three consecutive measurements, while the "grokking step", on which ALL the task-order tables are built, is the first attainment of 90 % ([p. 3, para. 7](#p3-7)). What the work does not have: any check of whether the task order is robust to the choice of threshold — the two thresholds are used interchangeably, and the sensitivity to that choice is not measured.

**[The reference grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the frame is taken from Power et al. unchanged (modular arithmetic at $p=97$, a random 50/50 split, a small transformer, AdamW) and extended in exactly one place: there are several classification heads but a single trunk ([p. 3, para. 4](#p3-4), [p. 3, para. 5](#p3-5)). The model is an encoder, not a decoder, $d_{\text{model}}=128$, 4 attention heads, $d_{\text{ff}}=256$, two layers, about 303 thousand parameters for two tasks and 315 thousand for three. This is the corpus's first use of the testbed in which the controlling quantity is not the data fraction but the **number of simultaneous tasks**.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — there are only three operations and all at one modulus: $(x+y)\bmod 97$, $(x\cdot y)\bmod 97$ and $(x^{2}+y^{2})\bmod 97$ ([p. 3, para. 5](#p3-5), [p. 3, para. 6](#p3-6)). The claimed order of mastery — multiplication, then the quadratic sum, then addition — is justified speculatively: $x^{2}+y^{2}$ "shares multiplicative structure" with $x\cdot y$, while addition is algebraically different ([p. 4, para. 5](#p4-5)). What the work does not have: an analysis of the structure of the tasks themselves. The difference between the operations is measured only by the time to reach an accuracy threshold; neither the symmetries of the task, nor group representations, nor any features of the solution are compared across the operations.

**[The memorization phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives it a geometric picture, named the **duality of scaffolding and solution**: the leading PCA directions of the trajectory are the "scaffolding" of memorization, while the generalizing solution lives in their orthogonal complement ([p. 28, para. 7](#p28-7)). The first three components carry more than 98 % of $\left\|\Delta\theta\right\|^{2}$ and, on reconstruction, give chance-level accuracy; the directions that produce generalization carry less than 1 % of the variance. From this follows a direct conclusion, shown by measurement: **the fraction of explained variance is not a measure of a direction's usefulness** ([p. 23, para. 4](#p23-4)). The controls without weight decay meanwhile give a **higher** concentration in the first component (~70–80 % against ~50–55 %), that is, memorization proceeds along a simpler low-rank trajectory ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work assigns weight decay the role of a **phase parameter** and builds a picture of regimes along it: fast grokking at $\lambda\geq 0.5$, slow at $\lambda\leq 0.3$, a boundary around $\lambda\approx 0.3$, and a separately standing $\lambda=0$ ([p. 29, para. 7](#p29-7), [p. 9, para. 7](#p9-7)). A second transition is measured by the number of reconstruction components: accuracy jumps from chance level to nearly flawless in the step from $k^{*}-1$ to $k^{*}$, with no intermediate regime ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1)). A third is spectral: the degenerate spectrum of queries and keys gives way to the dominance of a single mode ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)). What the work does not have: the exponent. It says the grokking time grows roughly as $t_{\text{grok}}\propto\lambda^{-\alpha}$, but the value of $\alpha$ is nowhere given and nowhere fitted ([p. 9, para. 5](#p9-5)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — a sweep over five non-zero values plus a control shows that four different quantities change in concert with $\lambda$: the grokking time (13 thousand steps at $\lambda=1.0$ against 98 thousand at $\lambda=0.1$ for two tasks), the depth of the negative curvature ($-63$ against $-17$), the reconstruction threshold and the lead time of the defect ([p. 29, para. 7](#p29-7), [table 1](#tab-1), [p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2)). The interpretation the work offers is **compression pressure**: strong regularization packs the algorithms more densely into fewer directions ([p. 24, para. 3](#p24-3)). What the work does not have: a separation of weight decay's contribution to the weight norm from its contribution to the geometry of the trajectory; the weight norm is not measured at all.

**[When regularization is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the $\lambda=0$ control is in place and gives the work's cleanest negative result: neither the two-task nor the three-task models generalize within a budget of up to 250 thousand steps ([p. 5, para. 1](#p5-1)), even though negative curvature is present in them and is far from small ($\lambda_{\text{min}}\approx-50$ against $-63$ at $\lambda=1.0$) ([p. 9, para. 2](#p9-2)). The conclusion is stated cautiously and exactly as shown: negative curvature of the Hessian is **necessary but not sufficient**. A caveat for the corpus: this is shown in a single setting — encoder, cross-entropy, AdamW — and the claim "weight decay is necessary for grokking" does not follow from it.

**[Loss landscape / basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — curvature is measured as the smallest eigenvalue of the Hessian by power iteration, separately per task and on the total loss ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)). The picture of the transition is a **saddle** one: weight decay supplies a pressure that pushes the trajectory off the memorization saddle into the generalizing basin ([p. 9, para. 2](#p9-2)). Separately, the work proposes a reinterpretation of the robustness of overparameterized models: it comes not from the flatness of the minimum but from the **geometric redundancy of optimization paths** — several center manifolds exist alongside one another, and when the main escape route is blocked, optimization can switch to a spare one ([p. 27, para. 10](#p27-10), [p. 27, para. 2](#p27-2)). What the work does not have: measurements of the flatness of the minimum. Neither the trace of the Hessian, nor its top eigenvalues, nor any measure of sharpness is given, so the contrast with flat minima remains an argument. Cross-task Hessian interactions the authors explicitly exclude from the computation ([p. 30, para. 7](#p30-7)).

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the most substantial part of the work. The spectral gap $g_{12}^{(\ell)}=\sigma_{1}(W_{Q}^{(\ell)})-\sigma_{2}(W_{Q}^{(\ell)})$ opens during or immediately before the collapse of the commutator, and the passage from an almost degenerate regime to the dominance of a single mode is declared a necessary precursor of generalization ([p. 16, para. 4](#p16-4)). In the plane (spectral gap, commutator norm $\left\|[W_{Q},W_{K}]\right\|_{F}$) every grokking run traverses a loop of "competition — instability — alignment", while runs without weight decay produce no loop at all and wander diffusely ([p. 17, para. 1](#p17-1), [fig. 20](#fig-20)). A complementary view is given by the sliding Gram matrix of the updates: the lower gap $g_{23}=\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{3}^{2}$ drops before grokking in 18 runs out of 18 with weight decay and in only 1 of 18 controls ([p. 20, para. 2](#p20-2)). What the work does not have: definitions of the quantities it itself relies on — the "curvature threshold $\omega$" and "remark 12.22" are taken from a preprint by the same author, are not defined in this paper and are absent from the corpus ([p. 21, para. 1](#p21-1)).

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the work offers an observation worth singling out: the commutator transition is a property of the **trunk**, not of the heads. In the three-task setting all three tasks grok at different points of ONE shared trajectory of the attention operators — a commutator peak at step 17,600, then multiplication (17,900), then $a^{2}+b^{2}$ (24,800), then addition (30,500) ([p. 17, para. 4](#p17-4)). The observation rests on the text: the only illustration for it is built on a two-task run and does not show three tasks (see the section on inconsistencies). Separate circuits per task and separate phase transitions are, by this observation, not required. The weights of the task heads are meanwhile almost orthogonal ($|\cos|<0.08$, in the three-task setting all three pairs lie in $[-0.01,+0.008]$) — the readout of the tasks is geometrically separated ([p. 6, para. 2](#p6-2)). A layer asymmetry is also found: the lower layer develops noticeably greater non-commutativity than the upper one, and the difference grows with the number of tasks ([p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2), [fig. 21](#fig-21)).

**[Gradient orthogonality](../../concepts/orthogonal-gradient-perp-grad.md)** — the work's central causal quantity. A fraction $f$ of the gradient's component orthogonal to the PCA manifold is removed: $\mathbf{g}_{\text{new}}=\mathbf{g}-f\cdot\mathbf{g}_{\perp}$ ([p. 14, para. 2](#p14-2)). This component carries less than 1 % of the gradient variance and is declared causally necessary for grokking ([p. 26, para. 7](#p26-7), [p. 15, para. 1](#p15-1)). A notable difference from the same author's single-task line: there the measure $\rho=\left\|\delta_{\perp}\right\|/\left\|\delta\right\|$ was compared against a random subspace, whereas here there is no random-subspace control and the claim is strengthened to "universal integrability" ([p. 6, para. 3](#p6-3)).

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the intervention is carried out with a detailed dose response, and this is the best experimental part of the work. Removing 1–7 % of the orthogonal component delays grokking (up to $+203\%$ for two tasks, $+124\%$ for three), removing 10 % or more kills it outright in both settings — a break between 7 % and 10 % ([p. 14, para. 3](#p14-3), [p. 15, para. 1](#p15-1), [fig. 18](#fig-18)). The threshold near 10 % is called the fragility boundary and interpreted as a percolation phenomenon ([p. 26, para. 8](#p26-8)). A separate finding is non-monotonicity: at 50 % removal in the two-task setting grokking returns, with three tasks it never returns ([p. 30, para. 4](#p30-4)). The caveat is acknowledged by the authors themselves: this whole section is built on a single seed (42) and a single value of $\lambda$ ([p. 30, para. 7](#p30-7)).

**[Compression of the representation manifold](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the work gives the corpus a pair of opposite measurements on one model. On the one hand, reconstruction from the first $k$ uncentered PCA components of the trajectory requires only $k^{*}$ directions at more than $3\cdot 10^{5}$ parameters ([p. 12, para. 4](#p12-4), [p. 22, para. 3](#p22-3)). On the other, the very same solution is compressed by nothing: layerwise SVD gives chance level at rank 64 and nearly flawless accuracy at rank 128 (that is, at full rank, equal to $d_{\text{model}}$), magnitude pruning that keeps the 50 % largest weights (99.3 % of the norm) gives chance level, and even a uniform rescaling by $\pm 5\%$ destroys the solution ([p. 13, para. 4](#p13-4), [p. 13, para. 5](#p13-5), [p. 14, para. 1](#p14-1)). This combination is named **holographic incompressibility** ([p. 25, para. 8](#p25-8)). What the work does not have: intermediate ranks. Nothing was tried between 64 and 128, so "there is no low-rank structure" is shown by two points.

**[Sparse subnetwork / lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — a direct negative result for pruning: keeping the larger half of the weight increments by magnitude, carrying 99.3 % of the squared norm, gives chance accuracy, and partial recovery begins only at 80 % kept ([p. 13, para. 5](#p13-5)). From this the work derives a prediction for practice: pruning more than ~10 % of the transverse capacity should sharply degrade generalization ([p. 28, para. 3](#p28-3)). A caveat: no retraining after pruning was carried out, whereas the lottery-ticket hypothesis is precisely about retraining the discovered subnetwork — the comparison in the work passes this distinction by, and the citation to the original source misspells the second author's surname ("Frankle and Carlin" instead of Carbin).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the commutator defect is used as an early-warning signal, and the count is given in full: its onset precedes generalization in 42 conditions out of 42 — 15 two-task and 27 three-task ([p. 10, para. 1](#p10-1), [p. 10, para. 2](#p10-2)). The size of the lead behaves differently in the two settings: for two tasks the lead fraction is V-shaped with a minimum of 4.9 % at $\lambda=0.5$, for three it reaches 96.7 % at $\lambda=0.1$ ([table 5](#tab-5), [table 6](#tab-6)). It matters that the defect is called a **shared** signal of the whole model rather than a per-task one: the mean lead across the three tasks is almost identical (60,622, 57,144 and 57,078 steps) ([p. 10, para. 2](#p10-2)). What the work does not have: an advance separation of grokking from non-grokking runs by this measure — the $\lambda=0$ controls do not generalize at all here, so the measure is tested only where grokking occurs.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work introduces the notion of **superposition in parameter space** and explicitly separates it from superposition in activation space, studied by feature dictionaries and sparse autoencoders ([p. 21, para. 4](#p21-4), [p. 30, para. 5](#p30-5)). Under it three different observations are gathered: overlap in parameter space (full-rank matrices encode several circuits), PCA compression ($k^{*}\ll P$) and the coexistence of tasks in one subspace without destructive interference. What the work does not have: a single isolated circuit. Neither Fourier features, nor trigonometric identities, nor an analysis of what exactly the heads compute is sought here; the correspondence between PCA directions and features in the sparse-autoencoder sense is named as a matter for future work ([p. 30, para. 6](#p30-6)).

## What is shown and what is conjectured

Notes by the card's editor following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is arranged as a sweep: one setting, six measurement techniques, six claimed phenomena. Its strength and its weakness sit in the same place — in the breadth of the sweep under a single author, a single architecture and reliance on four of the author's own preprints, of which one is verifiable inside the corpus.

**The dose response of the transverse ablation is the most reliable thing in the work.** Here there is a monotone stretch, a break, and a control: a projection onto a random subspace of the same strength produces nothing ([p. 14, para. 3](#p14-3)). The break near 10 % is reproduced in both settings at different parameter counts and different time scales, and the conclusion "less than 1 % of the gradient variance is causally necessary" does follow from what is shown ([p. 26, para. 7](#p26-7)). The limitation the authors name themselves: one seed, one value of $\lambda$ ([p. 30, para. 7](#p30-7)).

**The shared trunk trajectory is the most valuable observation and at the same time the least supported by its illustration.** Three tasks grok at three different points of one loop of the attention operators, and this is the only place where the task order acquires a mechanism rather than a guess ([p. 17, para. 4](#p17-4)). The observation is checkable and substantive: from it follows that multi-task grokking requires neither separate circuits per task nor separate phase transitions. But it is made on a single seed and a single layer, and the figure to which both formula (3) and the whole of section 9.3 refer shows a two-task run with two grokking marks instead of three ([fig. 19](#fig-19)) — that is, the reader does not see the claimed picture in the paper.

**"Universal integrability" is presented without a null model, and this is a direct loss compared with the predecessor.** $\rho\approx 1.000$ is claimed in all conditions ([p. 6, para. 3](#p6-3)). But at $P\approx 3\cdot 10^{5}$ parameters and a basis of $k\leq 100$ directions a random vector gives $\rho\approx\sqrt{1-k/P}\approx 0.9998$: the measured value is indistinguishable from chance level. In the same author's single-task work the random-subspace control was carried out and gave an excess of 1.8–2.9 times; here the control has vanished while the claim has been strengthened to "universal". The author himself wrote in that work that the measure is "identically equal to 1" and therefore distinguishes nothing on its own.

**The order in which the tasks grok is the most cited part and the most weakly supported.** The claimed order "multiplication → quadratic sum → addition" holds in full in only 5 of 15 three-task conditions ([table 2](#tab-2)). At $\lambda=0.3$ and $\lambda=0.1$ the last to grok is the quadratic sum, not addition; at $\lambda=0.1$, seed 2024 the quadratic sum groks **first** (145,900 against 149,200 and 149,300). Exactly one thing holds firmly: multiplication groks first or simultaneously with the others in 13 of 15 three-task conditions. The text limits the claim correctly — "the order is consistent across three seeds at $\lambda=1.0$" — but the caption of table 2 and the abstract remove the limitation. The time resolution is meanwhile coarser than the differences claimed: at $\lambda=0.3$, seed 2024 all three tasks grok at the same step (47,800), and the two-task gaps of 67–200 steps amount to 0.2–1.5 % of the grokking time at a measurement step of 100–200 steps, with no paired statistics given for them.

**The Hessian-based predictor of the order distinguishes nothing.** Section 6 proposes the rule "the task with deeper negative curvature groks first" and itself reports a hit in 2 seeds out of 3 ([p. 9, para. 1](#p9-1)). But multiplication groks first in all three seeds, that is, the outcome is constant: a rule that always predicts the same thing is untestable on a constant outcome.

**The lead statistics are computed as for independent trials where there are fewer of them.** The sign test $p=2^{-27}$ for the three-task part treats 27 conditions as independent, whereas these are 3 tasks $\times$ 3 seeds $\times$ 3 values of $\lambda$ = 9 runs ([p. 10, para. 2](#p10-2)). The paper itself asserts that the defect spike is a shared signal of the model rather than a per-task one — then there are 9 independent trials, and $p=2^{-9}$. The observation itself, "the onset of the defect precedes generalization in 42 of 42", is not overturned by this.

**What is shown directly and without reservations.** The first principal component carries most of the trajectory variance (by tables 14 and 15 — from 89.6 % to 96.8 %) and on reconstruction gives chance-level accuracy ([table 14](#tab-14), [p. 23, para. 4](#p23-4)); the $\lambda=0$ control gives curvature without grokking ([p. 9, para. 2](#p9-2)); all three ways of post-hoc compression fail ([p. 13, para. 4](#p13-4)); removal of the orthogonal component kills grokking at 10 %, whereas a random projection of the same strength does not ([p. 14, para. 3](#p14-3)). These four measurements stand on their own, without references to the author's preprints.

**What is declared to be argument.** Speculative are: the justification of the order through "shared multiplicative structure" ([p. 4, para. 5](#p4-5)); the explanation of the difference between the V-shaped and the monotone lead fraction through "the geometric slack eaten up by the third task" ([p. 11, para. 2](#p11-2)); the placement of the regime boundary at $\lambda\approx 0.3$ ([p. 9, para. 7](#p9-7)); the interpretation of the 10 % threshold as percolation ([p. 26, para. 8](#p26-8)); and the whole chapter on redundant center manifolds, which rests on the single point $\alpha=0.50$ ([p. 27, para. 2](#p27-2)).

**Reliance on the author's own preprints.** The single-task PC1 % values (70–94 %), the 1.1× ratio between layers in the single-task setting, the causal interventions accelerating grokking by 32–60 %, the value $k^{*}_{\mathrm{terminal}}$ "10 of 12" and "remark 12.22" are taken from the works Xu 2026a, 2026c and 2026d ([p. 2, para. 3](#p2-3), [p. 20, para. 2](#p20-2), [p. 21, para. 1](#p21-1), [p. 29, para. 1](#p29-1)). Of these, only 2602.16746 (Xu 2026b) is in the corpus, and the single-task support given there is 68–83 %, not 70–94 %. Part of the chain is unverifiable for a wiki reader.

**Place in the corpus.** The work gives the corpus a multi-task testbed in which several algorithms share one trunk, and two measurements no one else has: the shared trajectory of the attention operators along which the tasks pass their alignment thresholds one after another, and a detailed dose response of the transverse ablation with a break near 10 %. Its main weakness is the vanished null models: where the predecessor compared against a random subspace and a random walk, here the claim is strengthened and the comparison removed.

## Points we could not reconcile

Below are the places where two fragments of the paper say different things. The section is for whoever cites the paper: the numbers in the abstract, in the text and in the tables disagree here, and the choice of source changes the statement.

**Five phenomena or six.** The abstract announces "five consistent phenomena" and immediately lists six, as items (1)–(6) ([p. 1, para. 3](#p1-3)). The same in the PDF. A trace of an unfinished edit: the sixth item — the spectral geometry — was added after the others.

**Two-task gaps: 100–400 steps or what is in the table.** The text twice asserts that the tasks separate by 100–400 steps ([p. 4, para. 4](#p4-4), [p. 18, para. 1](#p18-1)), whereas table 1 gives gaps of 200, 67, 200, 100 and $-866$ steps ([table 1](#tab-1)). Two of the five values lie outside the stated range, one of them negative.

**The negative gap at $\lambda=0.1$ is explained by an exception from which it does not follow.** The text: the order holds everywhere, "with one exception at $\lambda=0.1$, seed 2024, where addition led by 100 steps" ([p. 4, para. 4](#p4-4)). A mean of $-866$ steps over three seeds cannot arise from a single exception of 100 steps ([table 1](#tab-1)).

**The reconstruction threshold $k^{*}$: four mutually inconsistent statements.** Section 10.5 — "two-task models systematically require more components than three-task ones" ([p. 24, para. 4](#p24-4)); section 11.3 — "three-task models require a larger minimal superposition dimension ($k^{*}\approx 5$–$8$ against $k^{*}\approx 5$–$9$ for two-task ones)", where the ranges given contradict the sentence itself ([p. 27, para. 3](#p27-3)); section 8.2 — "three-task models require a comparable or *smaller* number of PCA components" ([p. 13, para. 2](#p13-2)); section 12.5 — "$k^{*}$ grows with the number of tasks (from $\sim 5$ for two-task to $\sim 7$ for three-task)" ([p. 30, para. 6](#p30-6)). The first and the last are directly opposite. The tables do not confirm "systematically" either: at $\lambda=0.3$ the mean $k^{*}$ is 5.7 for two tasks against 6.3 for three ([table 8](#tab-8), [table 9](#tab-9)).

**The monotonicity of $k^{*}$ in weight decay is refuted by the paper's own table.** Section 8.1: "a clear monotone trend: lower weight decay requires more components" ([p. 13, para. 1](#p13-1)). Table 8 gives means of 6.3, 5.0, 5.7, 7.7, 8.3 at $\lambda=1.0,\,0.5,\,0.3,\,0.2,\,0.1$: the largest weight decay requires more components than $\lambda=0.5$ ([table 8](#tab-8)).

**The lead fraction for three tasks is called monotone and given as non-monotone.** Section 7: the lead fraction "grows monotonically as weight decay decreases", and immediately the values $82\%\to 77\%\to 97\%$ are written out at $\lambda=1.0,\,0.5,\,0.1$ ([p. 10, para. 3](#p10-3), [p. 10, para. 4](#p10-4)). It is then said that three tasks have no "dip at $\lambda=0.5$" characteristic of two tasks ([p. 11, para. 2](#p11-2)) — but the dip is there in those very numbers.

**The arithmetic of tables 5 and 6 does not add up.** In table 5 at $\lambda=1.0$ the lead fraction is given as 52.9 %, whereas the same rows yield $6533/13133=49.7\%$ ([table 5](#tab-5)). In table 6 at $\lambda=1.0$ the mean lead of 8,900 does not agree with the difference of the means $12\,089-1\,833=10\,256$ ([table 6](#tab-6)). The mean of fractions and the fraction of means are mixed up.

**The dose response is called monotone and is non-monotone in the three-task table.** Section 11.1 and section 8.3 speak of a monotonically growing delay up to 7 % ([p. 15, para. 1](#p15-1)), whereas table 11 gives $+62\%$ at 2 % against $+45\%$ at 3 % and $+43\%$ at 5 % ([table 11](#tab-11)).

**The non-commutativity ratio between layers is given as four different numbers.** The abstract and the introduction — $1.5$–$2.4\times$ ([p. 1, para. 3](#p1-3), [p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)); section 9.4, formula (4) and the caption of table 12 — $1.3$–$2.4\times$ ([p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2), [table 12](#tab-12)); table 12 itself gives $1.6$–$2.4\times$; the end of that same section 9.4 — $\sim\!1.7$–$2.4\times$ ([p. 18, para. 4](#p18-4)).

**In table 13 the third task is a different one.** The three-task setting is labelled "add + mul + sub" — subtraction — whereas everywhere else in the paper the third task is $x^{2}+y^{2}$ ([table 13](#tab-13)). Subtraction is meanwhile present in the same table as a separate single-task row.

**The title figure 19 shows something other than what its caption says.** The caption announces a phase portrait for the *three-task* arithmetic and names three grokking points — multiplication 17,900, $a^{2}+b^{2}$ 24,800, addition 30,500 ([fig. 19](#fig-19)). The figure itself is titled "ModAdd+ModMul phase portrait (seed 42, layer 0)" and carries exactly two grokking marks — mul@16300 and add@16400 — with the commutator peak at step 15,200. That is, the only illustration for formula (3) and for the thesis of section 9.3 that three tasks pass their thresholds along one shared trajectory is built on a two-task run and does not show three tasks. The steps marked on it match neither table 1 at $\lambda=1.0$ (13,333 and 13,133) nor the baseline of table 10 (14,100 and 14,000); they are closest to the means at $\lambda=0.5$ (16,267 and 16,200). The same in the PDF.

**The conclusion about the size of the $g_{23}$ drop contradicts the paper's own numbers.** It is said that the size of the drop grows with the complexity of the task; the numbers given in the same sentence — 28–50× (one task), 11× (two), 63× (three) — are non-monotone ([p. 20, para. 3](#p20-3)).

**Superposition or geometric separation.** The whole of section 10 is devoted to "superposition in parameter space": several algorithms coexist in overlapping directions ([p. 24, para. 1](#p24-1)). The thesis of section 12.2 is stated the opposite way: "shared-trunk models accommodate several circuits through geometric separation, not superposition" ([p. 29, para. 2](#p29-2)).

**The cross-task gradient cosines are named twice differently.** Section 5 at $\lambda=1.0$ — 0.24–0.38 ([p. 7, para. 3](#p7-3)); section 12.2 — 0.15–0.28 ([p. 29, para. 3](#p29-3)).

**The variance fraction in the first component is named as three different ranges.** Section 10.2 — "PC1 typically captures 70–90 % of the trajectory variance" ([p. 23, para. 4](#p23-4)); the caption of table 14 — 89–94 % ([table 14](#tab-14)); section 12.4 — 94 % ([p. 30, para. 3](#p30-3)). Tables 14 and 15 themselves give 89.6–96.8 %.

**Module ablation is mentioned among the experiments performed but was not performed.** Section 12.4 lists "layerwise SVD, magnitude pruning, uniform rescaling, module ablation" as the compression methods that failed ([p. 30, para. 2](#p30-2)), and section 8.3 says that "every module is necessary" ([p. 15, para. 2](#p15-2)); there is no experiment with module removal in the paper — section 8.2 describes three methods, not four.

**The quantity $\omega$ is defined and used in two incompatible senses.** In section 9.5 $\omega_{j}=\sigma_{j}/\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}$ is the spectral weight of position $j$ ([p. 19, para. 2](#p19-2)); three paragraphs below $\omega$ is called a "curvature threshold", in the absence of which the spectral transition does not begin ([p. 21, para. 1](#p21-1)). The second sense is not defined in the paper and is taken from the preprint Xu 2026d.

**The "Geometric picture" box anticipates the observations it refers to.** It stands before section 9.1 and opens with the words "these observations show", although not a single observation of section 9 has yet been given ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)).  The same in the PDF.

**The number of runs is nowhere totalled.** The abstract says "up to 90 runs" ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)); there is no itemized accounting in the paper, and the training budgets and sets of $\lambda$ values differ across experiments.
