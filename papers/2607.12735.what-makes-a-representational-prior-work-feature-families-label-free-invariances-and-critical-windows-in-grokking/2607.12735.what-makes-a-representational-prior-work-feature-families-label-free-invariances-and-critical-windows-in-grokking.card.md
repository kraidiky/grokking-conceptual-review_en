# Howe 2026 — What Makes a Representational Prior Work? Feature Families, Label-Free Invariances, and Critical Windows in Grokking

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Gunner Levi Howe** (independent researcher; Truong Xuan Khanh appears in the acknowledgements).

arXiv [2607.12735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12735), July 2026 (v1), 7 pages, 2 figures. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 1 (the companion 2607.04333). A continuation of the author's own companion work: there it was shown that the grokking delay is causally the time it takes task-structured representations to form; here, on 188 new runs, it is established what distinguishes a prior that works from one that does not. Three answers: whether it "works at all" is decided by a match of the **feature family** (a connected but Fourier-circuit-alien band partition locks generalisation out just as a random one does, 1/15 against 0/20); for a speed-up a **label-free invariance** suffices — a prior of commutativity $(a,b)\negthinspace\sim\negthinspace(b,a)$ groks 15/15 at $2.7\times$, more reliably than the supervised one ($p=0.038$), and paired with a norm clamp gives $17\times$; and almost all of the benefit is had for an early window of 4 % of the budget. A sweep of the clamp measures the exponent of the delay law of 2606.13753 in both regimes: the prior flattens it by a factor of ${\sim}17$ ($31\times$ per $+10$ of norm against $1.22\times$), and the exponent itself is redescribed as a function of the representational forces present. An honest limit: tasks in which the generalisation arrives before the memorisation have no delay — there the prior has nothing to govern.

## Concepts covered

**[The norm-separation delay law](../../concepts/norm-separation-delay-law.md)** — the exponent of the clamp law of 2606.13753 is measured for the first time in both regimes: a pure cross-entropy follows a steep exponential (a slope of $0.344$ per unit of norm, $31\times$ per $+10$ — a lower bound, since its high-norm cells are censored entirely), whereas with the structural prior the slope is $0.020$ ($1.22\times$ per $+10$) — a flattening of the exponent by a factor of ${\sim}17$ ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)); whence the redescription: the delay law is conditional on structureless training, and "the exponent is a function of the representational forces present" ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). The analysis: this is a ratio of two slopes under censoring in opposite directions (the top is censored for CE, the bottom breaks down for the prior), with no confidence intervals and 3 seeds per cell; and the location of the floor is stated in the paper in two incompatible ways.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — a three-level answer to the question of what makes a prior work: *whether it works* is decided by a match of the feature family (the true and the related structure, built on the same periodic features, preserve the generalisation, 22/30 and 14/15; the band structure, built on alien threshold features, locks it out, 1/15, as a random partition does), *how fast* by the alignment together with the norm side effect, and *when* by an early window: applying the prior only in the first 2000 epochs (4 % of the budget) gives 10/10 at $2.7\times$, better than applying it throughout ([`p3-1`](#p3-1), [`p5-2`](#p5-2)). The analysis: "the band condition is indistinguishable from the random one" is an argument from a failure to reject the null hypothesis (at 1/15 against 0/20 Fisher's test has almost no power, $p=0.43$).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — a demonstration that *self-supervised* priors work: positive pairs from commutativity alone — with no information whatever about the answer — grok 15/15 at a median of $2.7\times$, more reliably than the supervised true prior ($p=0.038$), and paired with a norm clamp give $17\times$ ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)); an honest limit of the phenomenon is that tasks in which the generalisation arrives before the memorisation (synthetic grammars, small parity) have no delay for the prior to govern, and continuing the training there *destroys* the generalisation already achieved ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)). The analysis: the $17\times$ is obtained at the optimal value of the clamp, at which by the authors' own measurement a pure cross-entropy runs at the same speed — so the prior's own contribution to that number is a robustness to the tuning of the norm rather than speed.

**[Fourier features](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the feature family of the Fourier circuit is made the defining criterion of a prior's suitability: the band partition $(\lfloor a/10\rfloor,\lfloor b/10\rfloor)$ is connected and learnable by threshold features but inexpressible by the periodic features of the circuit — and it locks the generalisation out at exactly matched class sizes ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)); commutativity, by contrast, is a sparse label-free sample from the true equivalence relation (transposed pairs have the same sum) and is therefore expressible by the features of the circuit ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)). The analysis: the "match of the feature family" rests on external knowledge about the circuit (Nanda et al.) and, as the author admits, is not operationalisable outside algorithmic tasks.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — the cleanest control of the programme and a measurement of the exponent in both regimes.** The band control is a separating prediction stated in advance in the companion work and checked here: the connectedness and learnability of a partition are not enough, the feature family decides; and the triple "true/related/band" separates a match of features from a match of the combination. The sweep of the clamp closes the companion work's chief missing arm ("the clamp under a pure CE, without the prior") and yields a rare "correction of emphasis" against the author's own claim: the speed at the optimal norm belongs to the clamp, and what remains to the prior is robustness. The reproducibility is above average: a single RTX 3080, with all the numbers generated by `gen_numbers.py` and checked byte for byte by `verify_regen.py`.

**The headline $17\times$ is a pairing at an optimum where the prior is not needed for the speed.** The strongest method (commutativity $+$ a clamp at $\Vert W\Vert{=}45$) is taken at exactly the value of the clamp at which, by the paper's own conclusion (i), a pure cross-entropy runs just as fast; the row "CE $+$ clamp 45" is absent from the summary table, and no paired comparison of these arms is given — the caveat "strongest means reliably fast" is carried into the abstract, but the table reads as a superiority of the prior in speed. The location of the floor is contradictory: the caption of figure 2 speaks of a common floor of both curves below ${\sim}40\text{–}45$, while the text of point (iii) speaks of a divergence below ${\sim}35\text{–}40$ (the prior breaking down, the CE generalising within hundreds of epochs).

**Statistical caution is required in three places.** The delay itself ($t_{\mathrm{gen}}-t_{\mathrm{fit}}$) is not measured — everywhere only $t_{\mathrm{gen}}$ is, although in the companion work the shuffled prior pushed the fitting out as well. Both conclusions about the critical window ("brief beats long", "early beats late") are differences of medians over 10 runs with no significance tests, at window boundaries chosen in advance; the literature on critical periods of learning is not drawn on at all (nor is the neighbouring 2605.04396 with its weight-decay windows), the grokking accelerators are not mentioned, and composition with them is not checked. Reliability and speed are summarised on different bases: the 15/15 is pooled over three values of $\lambda$, while the speed-up of the true prior holds only at $\lambda{=}1.0$.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that label-free priors speed grokking up by 17 times"; "grokking has a critical window") — more precisely: on modular addition at $p=97$ with a single-layer transformer it is shown that the outcome is governed by a match of the feature family; that the content of commutativity alone suffices for a reliable speed-up ($2.7\times$) with no labels; that the $17\times$ is a pairing with the clamp at its optimum, where the CE is just as fast; and that the "window" rests on differences of medians with no significance. Step 5 is separately valuable: generalisation before memorisation followed by its destruction — a case rare in the corpus, adjacent to the anti-grokking line but analysed only in passing.

###### p2-3

**Band structure** (content control): positives share the magnitude-band pair $(\lfloor a/10\rfloor,\lfloor b/10\rfloor)$ — 100 classes of $\approx$28 train examples, closely matching the true structure’s 97 of $\approx$29. The partition is coherent and learnable (threshold features), but magnitude bands are *not* expressible by the periodic features the task’s Fourier circuit uses [4]. The companion’s feature-family account predicts band patterns with the random partition; a coherence account predicts it patterns with the sibling.

###### p2-4

**Commutativity** (supervision control): positives are the commuted pairs $\{(a,b),(b,a)\}$ only. This uses *no answer information whatever* — commutativity is a property of the task family knowable before training — and it is sparse: only 31% of anchors have their partner in the train split (one positive each). Because commuted pairs share their sum, this is a sparse sample of the true equivalence structure obtained label-free.

###### p3-1

The band prior blocks generalization: 1/15 runs grok, statistically indistinguishable from the random partition (0/20; Fisher exact $p=0.43$ between them) and decisively different from the true structure (22/30; $p=2.3\times 10^{-5}$). Coherence and learnability are therefore *not* sufficient: a prior whose demanded geometry cannot be built from the generalizing circuit’s feature family behaves like noise-structure, even at matched class sizes and strengths. This confirms the discriminating prediction stated in the companion paper — sibling structure (same features, wrong combination: 14/15) preserves generalization while band structure (different features) abolishes it. Two honest nuances: one band run of 15 did grok (it is a strong tendency, not an absolute wall), and several censored band runs show substantial terminal test accuracy on some seeds — the block is best described as pushing the transition far beyond budget rather than destroying it.

###### p3-2

The commutativity prior generalizes in 15/15 runs (vs 0/20 random, $p=3.1\times 10^{-10}$) at a median $2.7\times$ speedup over same-seed baselines — and it is *more* reliable than the label-supervised true-structure prior itself (22/30; $p=0.038$). Sparsity is the plausible mechanism — one positive per covered anchor supplies correctly-aligned structure (commuted pairs share sums) at gentler optimization pressure — and the norms point the right way, though not decisively: the commutativity arms’ median peak weight norm is 103 versus 130 for the supervised prior (baseline $\approx$57), a reduction rather than an absence of the inflation side-effect; as in the companion, the held level is an incomplete summary and trajectory likely matters too. Combining the label-free prior with the companion’s norm clamp ($\|W\|{=}45$) yields the strongest method in this program: 5/5 grokking at a median $17\times$ speedup — sub-1,000-epoch generalization on some seeds — using no label information beyond the task loss itself. This is the proof-of-concept that *self-supervised* representational priors work: the structure can come from known invariances rather than answers.

###### p4-2

Clamping the weight norm to constants from 35 to 70 with and without the prior maps the delay-law [3] in both regimes (Fig. 2). Three findings. (i) At the optimal clamp (${\sim}45$), plain cross-entropy generalizes as fast as the prior does — the *speed* of low-norm training belongs to norm control alone, consistent with Liu et al. [5], and we state this plainly as a correction of emphasis for the companion’s accelerator section. (ii) Away from the optimum the regimes diverge dramatically: plain CE slows $31\times$ per $+10$ norm units (and is fully censored by $+20$), while the prior slows only $1.22\times$ — a ${\sim}17\times$ flattening of the exponent. Structure injection buys *robustness to the norm setting*, which is what matters in practice since the critical norm is not known a priori. (iii) Below the floor (${\sim}35$–$40$) the relationship inverts: plain CE generalizes in hundreds of epochs **[p. 5]** where the prior stalls — at very low norm the contrastive gradient interferes with the lean circuit’s formation. The delay law is thus conditional on structure-agnostic training in a precise, measurable sense: the exponent is a function of the representational forces present.

###### p5-1

Synthetic subject–verb agreement grammars (attractor and conjunction variants) never grok under matched conditions: they generalize immediately (median peak 0.90 by epoch 500) and then *degrade* with continued training (median final 0.73). The memorize-first precondition of grokking is absent — the rule is easier than memorization for this model class — so there is no delay for a representational prior to control. The prior still shapes representations there (number-clustering roughly doubles) and lifts accuracy levels (peak 0.95, final 0.76) but does not prevent the degradation trend. Small sparse parity behaves the same way. The representation-timing account is therefore an account of the *memorize-first regime*; where generalization precedes memorization, different dynamics (and possibly different uses of priors, e.g. as regularizers) apply.

###### p5-2

*Whether* a prior permits generalization is decided by feature-family alignment: structures expressible in the generalizing circuit’s features (true, sibling, commutativity) preserve or enable grokking; structures requiring foreign features (bands) or memorization (random) block it. *How fast* is decided jointly by alignment and the norm side-effect: the exponent measurements show the norm sets a steep default timescale that aligned **[p. 6]** structure almost entirely neutralizes. *When* matters less than expected: the prior’s work is done early, and removing it after the seeding window strictly helps.
