# Ali 2026 — Critical Windows of Complexity Control: When Transformers Decide to Reason or Memorize

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Sarwan Ali** (Columbia University, Irving Medical Center, New York, USA).

arXiv [2605.04396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04396), May 2026 (the sole version v1), 27 pages, 12 figures. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 1, in the corpus — 1 (2605.20441). A single author, with all the experiments run on CPUs (about 20 CPU-hours). A work on compositional generalisation in the anchor-function task of Zhang et al.: the fate of "memorisation against reasoning" is decided within a sharp identifiable window of the training — a weight decay imposed over 25 % of the training inside the window equals a full-training one ($0.93$ against $0.91$ OOD), at a strictly equal budget $\int\lambda\mkern3mu dt$ the middle is $5{-}9\times$ better than the beginning, the boundary of the window is sharp to within 100 steps, and the basin of the reasoning solution *narrows* at a small $\gamma$ — contrary to the recommendation "the smaller the initialisation, the better". A two-scale theory is provided: a memorisation rate of $\Theta(1)$ and a reasoning rate of $\Theta(\gamma^{2})$. For the grokking corpus the pivotal place is appendix C.5: a rare direct experiment of "windowed against constant weight decay" on modular arithmetic, where the phenomenon does not carry over — a constant WD groks $5\times$ faster than a windowed one; the analysis below shows that this experiment is set up without equalising the budget and that its figure reads rather as "the moment weight decay is switched on sets the moment of grokking".

## Concepts covered

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the time of application is singled out as an axis in its own right, separate from the magnitude: at a strictly equalised budget $\int\lambda(t)\mkern3mu dt=20$ the mid placements of the window give an OOD of $0.847\text{–}0.908$ and the early ones $0.103\text{–}0.151$, a gap of $5{-}9\times$ ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)); a window covering 25 % of the training equals a full-training weight decay, and "a weight decay imposed during the first 25 % of the training does literally nothing"; under SGD and at 4 layers a constant WD is even harmful against a correctly placed window. On grokking the dependence is reversed: a constant WD groks $5\times$ faster than a windowed one ([`p26-1`](#p26-1)). The analysis: in the grokking experiment the budget is precisely not equalised (the window receives half), and $\lambda^{ * }$ is tuned for the constant schedule.

**[Initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — a correction to the recommendation "the smaller the better": the peak of the OOD lies at a moderate $\gamma$ ($0.8$), and a measurement over 12 seeds shows the basin of the reasoning solution narrowing at a small $\gamma$ — $12/12$ seeds at $\gamma{=}1.1$ against $8/12$ at $\gamma{=}0.5$, where four failures fall to chance ([`p22-2`](#p22-2)); the theoretical support is a growth rate of the reasoning pathway $\mu_{r}=c_{r}\gamma^{2}\sigma_{e}$ against a $\gamma$-independent memorisation rate, whence a "sweet spot" in $\gamma$ at a finite $T$ ([`p14-6`](#p14-6)). The analysis: the claimed shift of the position of the window with $\gamma$ is not shown in experiment E5 — only the height of the plateau differs.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — a rare direct experiment of "windowed against constant weight decay" on modular arithmetic ($p{=}67$, a fraction of 40 %): both schedules reach an OOD of $\approx 1.0$, but the constant one groks at step $\approx 700$ and the windowed $[0.1T,0.6T]$ at $\approx 3500\text{–}3600$, and the critical-window phenomenon is declared non-transferable, the tasks "phenomenologically different" ([`p26-1`](#p26-1)); the author's reading follows Omnigrok: the solution lives on a weight-norm manifold and requires a continuous pull ([`p26-2`](#p26-2)). The analysis: the budget here is not equalised (the windowed schedule receives half of the constant one), $\lambda^{ * }{=}0.01$ is the best for the constant schedule and the extreme point of the search grid, and by figure 10 the window opens at step 3000 and the grokking occurs at $\approx 3400$ — immediately after the switch-on; what is shown is rather a shift of the moment of grokking following the moment of the switch-on than an irrelevance of timing. The thesis that "modular arithmetic has a single reachable basin" runs against the line of competition between the memorising and the generalising solutions (Nanda et al.).

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — a basin formulation of the choice of solution: reasoning and memorisation are two basins, the choice between them is made within a window of a few thousand steps, and the width of the basin is for the first time in this line measured by a direct count of seeds (12 per point); the condition of applicability is outlined honestly — the critical-window dynamics appears only where both basins are structurally distinguishable and reachable by the model ([`p27-2`](#p27-2)): on SCAN add_prim_jump the compositional basin is unreachable altogether (a zero sequence-level accuracy in every regime), and on grokking, by the author, there is only one basin. The analysis: the bimodality at 4 layers (some seeds at $\geq 0.70$, some at chance) is a direct sign of the narrow basin predicted by theorem 6.

**[Selective weight decay](../../concepts/selective-weight-decay.md)** — the token and position embeddings are excluded from the decay "following standard practice" ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)); and $\lambda$ itself is taken outside the AdamW step so that it can be switched on and off on a schedule. Selectivity by parameter and selectivity by time stand side by side here.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — two transferable protocols and honest negative results.** The protocol of a windowed weight decay with an equalised budget ($\int\lambda\mkern3mu dt$ as a control — the chief defence against the objection "the window simply receives less regularisation") and the measurement of the basin width by a direct count over 12 seeds are transferable to grokking settings. Three negative results are presented outright: the condensation diagnostic works only categorically (a Spearman of $+0.25$, a band of $[28,36]$ with a caveat about recalibration), the SCAN experiment is admitted to say nothing about the phenomenon (the compositional basin is unreachable), and grokking is declared outside the domain of applicability. The theory writes its constants out explicitly, and the predicted onset of the window ($200\text{–}500$ steps) coincides with the measured breakdown ($100\text{–}400$) — the paper's only quantitative agreement between the theory and the experiment.

**The pivotal item for the corpus is E4, and it is set up impurely.** On the anchor task the budget was equalised deliberately ($\lambda$ was raised fourfold inside the window); in the decisive negative experiment on grokking that defence is removed: the windowed schedule $[0.1T,0.6T]$ applies the same $\lambda^{ * }$ and receives half of the constant one's budget. $\lambda^{ * }{=}0.01$ was chosen by a search as the best for the constant schedule and carried over to the windowed one with no recomputation, and it is the extreme point of the grid at that. By the axis of figure 10, $T{=}30\mkern3mu000$: the window opens at step $3000$ and the transition under the windowed schedule comes at $\approx 3400$, that is, immediately after the weight decay is switched on. What is shown is a dependence of the moment of grokking on the moment of the switch-on, not a timelessness of the regularisation. The abstract meanwhile softens this: "a tuned constant weight decay is compared with a schedule" — whereas the body of the paper speaks of a fivefold superiority of the constant one.

**Internal inconsistencies.** The late window gives $0.735$ (under SGD, $0.96$) — theorem 4(3) ("after the window there is no effect") directly contradicts this, and the discrepancy is called a "graceful degradation" in the text. The arithmetic of §5.4 is wrong: "the gap $s{=}0\to 500$ is fewer than 40 batches, $\leq 5000$ examples", whereas 500 steps are 500 batches of 128 over a sample of 720 examples. Remark 18 counts $t_{r}^{1/2}(0.5)\approx 640$ "steps", forgetting the author's own factor $\eta^{-1}\approx 333$ from corollary 5; with it one gets $\sim 2\cdot 10^{5}$ steps $\gg T$, and the four failures out of twelve are explained by a shortage of time (regime b) rather than by an unlucky initialisation (regime a), to which they are ascribed. §4 announces "eight experiments" with twelve listed; §3.4 carries a broken sentence; §5.1 asserts a collapse to chance where the author's own figure shows $0.61$ and $0.46$; and "the same 2-layer transformer" covers $d{=}32$, $64$ and $128$ across different experiments.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is discovered that the timing of the regularisation matters more than its magnitude") — more precisely: in general form the effect was shown already by Golatkar et al. for image classification; here it is carried over to a compositional task, where at an equal budget the gap reaches $5{-}9\times$ and the boundary of the window is sharp to within 100 steps; on grokking, however, the dependence is reversed, and that experiment is set up without equalising the budget.
