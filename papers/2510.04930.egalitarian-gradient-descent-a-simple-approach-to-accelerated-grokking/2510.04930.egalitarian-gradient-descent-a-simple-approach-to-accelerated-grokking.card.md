## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work undertakes not to explain it in general but to name **one** sufficient cause of the delay and remove it. The cause is named thus: asymmetric rates of gradient descent along the different singular directions of the gradient ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The remedy is to equalise those rates; on modular addition, multiplication and sparse parity the plateau disappears almost entirely ([`p10-3`](#p10-3)).

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — an **egalitarian gradient descent** (EGD) is proposed: a layer's gradient matrix is replaced by $\tilde{G}=(GG^{\top})^{-1/2}G=UV^{\top}$, that is, the singular vectors are preserved and all the singular values are set to one ([`p7-6`](#p7-6), [`p7-9`](#p7-9)). The remedy is indifferent to the optimiser and is laid over SGD, Adam, AdamW, RAdam and RMSprop ([`p24-2`](#p24-2)).

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the connection with natural gradient descent is written out precisely: $F=GG^{\top}$ is the empirical Fisher information matrix, and EGD relates to NGD as $\tilde{G}=F^{1/2}\bar{G}$, that is, EGD is a **whitened** NGD ([`p8-4`](#p8-4), [`p8-5`](#p8-5)). From the same place comes a constant of the motion: the Fisher norm of the modified gradient is identically one.

**[Low-pass filtering of the gradient](../../concepts/gradient-low-pass-filtering.md)** — a direct comparison with Grokfast. It is shown that EGD incidentally inherits its bias: dividing by the singular values lowers the weight of the components along large $s_{j}$ ([`p8-8`](#p8-8)). The differences are named by name: EGD has neither a store of past gradients (zero memory cost) nor hyperparameters, and in place of a heuristic there is a spectral analysis ([`p8-9`](#p8-9)). The experiment is more moderate than the claim: on MNIST the RSVD variants outperform Grokfast in both steps and time, while on the transformer Grokfast is **faster** than EGD with an exact SVD ([`p22-2`](#p22-2), [`p22-3`](#p22-3)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the whole work rests on the condition number of the gradient matrix: figure 4 shows that in training addition modulo 97 the largest singular value is far greater than the smallest from the beginning of the optimisation to its end ([`fig-4`](#fig-4)). The cost of the SVD is removed by a randomised SVD, and it turns out that the accuracy of the decomposition does not matter: at a well-chosen rank the RSVD groks even faster than an exact SVD ([`p18-2`](#p18-2), [`p19-2`](#p19-2)).

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — in the toy setting the length of the plateau depends on it directly: at a large initialisation $k_{*}\asymp 1/\varepsilon$, at a small one $k_{*}\asymp\log(1/\varepsilon)$ ([`p6-3`](#p6-3), [`p6-4`](#p6-4)). It matters that EGD turns out to be **wholly insensitive** to the initialisation scale ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)).

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — theorem 1 gives the test error in closed form through the angle between the current weight vector and the true decision boundary: $E(w(k))\simeq\min(1,\arccos(r_{k})/\arccos(r))$ ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)). This is where the plateau comes from: the error stays at 100 % until $r_{k}$ falls below $r$.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — experiments on $\operatorname{Mod}(p,o)$ with $o\in\{+,\times\}$ and $p\in\{79,97,127\}$, a two-layer ReLU network, a data fraction of 0.5 ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)). The acceleration is measured in the number of epochs to 95 % accuracy: up to $53\times$ against ordinary SGD with an exact SVD ([`p18-4`](#p18-4)).

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — the second family of tasks: $(n,k)\in\{(400,2),(100,3),(50,4)\}$, a hinge loss with weight decay ([`p9-5`](#p9-5), [`p9-6`](#p9-6)). Here too is the largest gain: $187\times$ in epochs with a rank-40 RSVD against ordinary SGD ([`p19-2`](#p19-2)).

**[Real data](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the check is carried into the appendices: a three-layer MLP on MNIST in the setting of Omnigrok and Grokfast ([`p22-2`](#p22-2)) and a convolutional network on CIFAR-10 with label shuffling at fixed intervals ([`p25-1`](#p25-1)). The second experiment is not about grokking: it measures adaptability to non-stationarity, and adding EGD increases the area under the curve for all three optimisers.

**[Flatness and the loss landscape](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — appendix F checks whether EGD arrives at the same solution: on the modular tasks the paths of EGD and ordinary SGD diverge and lead into different regions of parameter space, while on sparse parity SGD turns towards the region in which EGD grokked ([`p25-2`](#p25-2), [`p25-4`](#p25-4)).

**[The lazy-to-rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)**, **[the Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)**, **[the slingshot](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** and **[softmax collapse](../../concepts/softmax-collapse.md)** — named in the survey as competing explanations ([`p3-3`](#p3-3), [`p4-1`](#p4-1)), but not compared with the mechanism proposed: the work does not claim that ill-conditioning is the **sole** cause of grokking.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the survey notes separately a long-standing line about plateaux arising near singular regions of the parameters, where the Fisher information degenerates ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). The present explanation is of the same kind: a slow mode rather than a rearrangement of representations.

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no theory for the nonlinear case: theorems 1 and 2 are proved for a linear model on two-dimensional Gaussian data, and an analysis of EGD's convergence for MLPs and transformers is called highly difficult and left for the future ([`p10-7`](#p10-7)). There is no number of runs and no error bars on any figure. And there is no explanation of the chief experimental oddity: on the transformer EGD with an exact SVD is extremely unstable, whereas the randomised SVD — an approximation of the very same thing — is stable ([`p22-3`](#p22-3)).

**[Singular learning theory](../../concepts/singular-learning-theory.md)** — the work recalls that the observation is older than the term: plateaux arise near singular regions of the parameters generated by permutation symmetries and redundancies ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)). The connection with grokking here is a conjecture, not a result.

**[Seed variance and reproducibility](../../concepts/seed-variance-reproducibility.md)** — reproducibility is understood more broadly than the number of seeds: as the completeness of the report on the low-level settings — the learning rate, the size of the weight decay and the rest ([`p9-6`](#p9-6)).
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is applied, with a theoretical warm-up: one remedy, two theorems for a toy model, three arithmetic settings and extensive appendices.

**The remedy is simple, and therein lies its strength.** One formula $\tilde{G}=UV^{\top}$, not a single hyperparameter, no memory beyond the current gradient ([`p8-9`](#p8-9)). For a corpus in which grokking accelerators customarily require tuning (Grokfast — a window and a filter coefficient), this is a notable simplification.

**The acceleration is measured generously and honestly.** Three quantities are given at once: epochs to 95 %, wall-clock time and time per epoch ([`p18-4`](#p18-4)). This is a rarity: usually only the number of steps is reported, which makes any method with an expensive step look better than it is. Here one can see that $45\times$ in epochs turns into $10$–$12\times$ in time.

**The chief discovery is hidden in appendix C.** A randomised SVD — a crude approximation — is not merely cheaper but at a well-chosen rank gives **more** acceleration than the exact decomposition ($187\times$ against $72\times$ at $k=2$) ([`p19-2`](#p19-2)). The work notes this but does not explain it; meanwhile it directly contradicts its own theory, by which the gain comes precisely from an exact equalisation of the singular values.

**The RSVD rank brings back the very hyperparameter whose absence the work is proud of.** This is admitted outright: "the RSVD rank has to be tuned for the greatest gain" ([`p24-1`](#p24-1)). At $k=2$ a rank of 40 gives $187\times$ and a rank of 30 only $18.6\times$; a spread of an order of magnitude ([`p19-2`](#p19-2)).

**The comparison with Grokfast is not as one-sided as the abstract makes it sound.** On MNIST EGD wins ([`p22-2`](#p22-2)); on the transformer with Adam it loses on both quantities, and the authors report it ([`p22-3`](#p22-3)). The argument for EGD there comes down to memory rather than speed, and it is an honest one: Grokfast stores a copy of the gradients.

**The instability of the exact SVD on the transformer is the work's most troubling observation.** It is said outright: "EGD in this setting is extremely unstable", whereas the approximate RSVD is stable and more stable than Grokfast ([`p22-3`](#p22-3)). There is no explanation. The natural conjecture — that the rank truncation damps the noise in the small singular values, division by which is what blows the update up — is neither stated nor checked in the work.

**The theory covers a case other than the one being checked.** Theorems 1 and 2 are proved for a linear least-squares model on two-dimensional Gaussian data ([`p4-5`](#p4-5), [`p17-15`](#p17-15)); the experiments use two-layer ReLU networks, transformers and convolutional networks. The connection between them is an argument by analogy rather than a derivation, and the authors acknowledge this ([`p10-7`](#p10-7)).

**The toy setting is arranged to fit the answer, and this is stated openly.** The feature covariance is deliberately ill-conditioned ($1/\varepsilon\gg 1$), and the training and test distributions differ ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)). Appendix A.3 also analyses the case of coinciding distributions and obtains the same order $1/(\eta\varepsilon)$ — an addition that substantially strengthens the argument.

**The connection with natural gradient descent is written out precisely and is useful.** The relation $\tilde{G}=F^{1/2}\bar{G}$ ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)) explains why pure NGD is not taken here: it would suppress the directions too strongly. EGD turns out to be a middle ground between the ordinary gradient and NGD, and that is a sensible place to stand.

**The kinship with Muon is acknowledged with no attempt at priority.** It is noted that both methods arrive at an approximately orthogonal update, and the differences are named in substance: Muon has Newton–Schulz iterations with no access to the singular values, EGD an SVD with an explicit spectrum ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). The remark that the convergence of two independently motivated methods on one operation is itself evidence for spectral normalisation ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)) is apt.

**The non-stationarity experiment bears on the matter only indirectly.** The label shuffling on CIFAR-10 measures the speed of recovery rather than grokking ([`p24-2`](#p24-2), [`p25-1`](#p25-1)). The results are good ($1.1$–$2.3\times$ in area under the curve), but what they support is the broad claim about the usefulness of spectral normalisation rather than the narrow claim about grokking.

**The number of runs is nowhere named.** Not one figure carries error bars, and the tables give a single number per cell. For a work whose chief claim is a factor of acceleration, this is the same gap as in Grokfast and NeuralGrok in this corpus.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the wiki's third work on accelerating grokking by intervening in the gradient — after Grokfast (a fixed filter) and NeuralGrok (a learnt transformation) — and the simplest of the three: one formula with no parameters and no memory. Its explanation of the delay — the ill-conditioning of the gradient matrix — is akin to the long-standing line about plateaux near singular points and requires neither feature learning nor a rearrangement of representations. The price: a theory only for a linear toy model, the return of a hyperparameter through the RSVD rank, an unexplained instability of the exact SVD on the transformer, and the absence of error bars.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2210-01117
**Omnigrok (2210.01117) — the caveat about inducement.** `"It has been observed in some computer vision and natural understanding tasks (Liu et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2024)"`. "Observed" drops the authors' caveat: grokking on those tasks was ["induced"](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p1-3), and moreover ["in a slightly non-standard setting where it is relatively weak"](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p1-9). In the appendix, meanwhile, the same paper reproduces the MNIST setting of Omnigrok word for word — "the same parameters, optimizer (AdamW), and model as reported by Omnigrok" — that is, in practice it observes the conditions of the inducement; the caveat is dropped only in the survey sentence.

###### mc-2201-02177
**Power et al. (2201.02177) — "only much later" as the definition of grokking.** The related-work section opens with a definition: `Grokking—the phenomenon where models first overfit the training set and only much later undergo a sharp jump in test accuracy—was first documented by Power et al. (2022)`. The obligatory "only much later" does not belong to Power's paper: there [validation accuracy "sometimes" suddenly begins to rise after the overfitting](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#p2-3), [the time to generalisation grows rapidly only as the fraction of data falls, and at larger datasets the curves run more closely together](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-3-1-1), and [the most dramatic delay is obtained in a deliberate setting without weight decay at an increased budget, whereas the default setting is a weight decay of 1](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-a1-2). Under a strong regularisation there is no delay — [Omnigrok, figure 2](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#fig-2): a large regularisation gives a fast generalisation, and the number of steps to it is inversely proportional to the weight decay. In its introduction the same paper is more careful — networks "at times exhibit" such a dynamics, test performance "may hold for a long time" near chance — and its own results only confirm the conditionality of the delay: by the caption to figure 6 the proposed EGD ["groks at once: the generalisation error jumps to its perfect value after only a few iterations"](#p6-2), that is, the authors treat the delay as an artefact of the conditioning of the gradients rather than as a defining property of the phenomenon. The warning concerns the formulation of section 2, not the work as a whole.

###### mc-2309-02390
**Varma et al. (2309.02390) — the dominance of the generalising circuit with no conditions.** `"When both circuits are formed, the generalizing circuits will be dominant in generating outputs"`. Both conditions of the theory are dropped: in Varma the dominance of the generalising circuit requires [weight decay — the authors call their explanation incomplete without it](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md#sec-7) — and a volume of data above the critical one; at small data the memorising circuit wins. The framing "Aligned with this view" next to Kumar et al. is questionable in the same place — that work directly contests Varma's central thesis about efficiency.

###### mc-2501-04697
**Prieto et al. (2501.04697) — citation by title.** `"Prieto et al. (2025) argue that operating near the edge of numerical stability can induce grokking-like delays and propose remedies that restore or accelerate test performance"`: in Prieto [the delay is explained by NLM — the scaling of the logits, an optimisation phenomenon](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p7-1), and proximity to the edge of stability ends not in a delay but in [a halt of the training due to SC](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p4-4). The paper's title has been read as its thesis.

###### mc-2205-10343
**Liu et al. (2205.10343) — a chimera of two works.** `"Liu et al. (2022) develop an effective theory of representation learning, showing that generalization emerges in a specific zone for the weights of a network called the 'Goldilocks Zone'. Grokking occurs when the weights enter this region, a narrow phase between memorization and confusion"` — a hybrid: the phase terminology ("between memorisation and confusion") is indeed [from here](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/original/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.md#p1-2), but the zone here lives in hyperparameter space, and "the weights entering the zone" as the mechanism of grokking is the weight-space picture of [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md). By the paper's own bibliography "Liu et al. (2022)" is precisely 2205.10343, so a weight zone is ascribed to a work that does not contain it.

###### mc-2405-20233
**Grokfast (2405.20233) — "consistently across tasks and architectures".** `"Grokfast amplifies slow (low-frequency) gradient components via simple optimizer-side filters, consistently accelerating grokking across tasks and architectures (Lee et al., 2024)"`: in Grokfast the acceleration is shown in iterations on a single task, [and the chief demonstration is chosen together with a tuned weight decay](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md#p4-3); "consistently" is refuted within the corpus: [2603.01968](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md) observes task-dependent behaviour with a slowdown in some settings, and [2504.17243](../2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation/2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation.card.md) the instability of the EMA variant and the sensitivity of the MA one to hyperparameters. The method's category ("optimizer-side filters") is named correctly, though, and in its appendix the same paper compares itself with Grokfast honestly.

###### mc-2410-04489
**Beck et al. (2410.04489) — the mechanism of Levi et al. ascribed to them.** `"Recent theoretical analyses have studied grokking in high-dimensional linear and logistic regression models under Gaussian assumptions, relating delayed generalization to spectral properties of the empirical covariance near interpolation thresholds (Beck et al., 2025; Levi et al., 2024)"`: the spectral properties of the empirical covariance are the mechanism of Levi et al. (2310.16441, the same group); in Beck the mechanism is a different one: [generalisation occurs if and only if the training set is not linearly separable from the origin](../2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability/2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability.card.md#p5-5), with a critical slowing down at the threshold. Two similar-sounding works by one group have been merged.
