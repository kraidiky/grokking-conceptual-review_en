## Concepts covered

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work introduces three quantities and calls them progress measures outright: activation sparsity, absolute weight entropy and approximate local circuit complexity ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)). It takes the notion from Barak et al. 2022 by a single reference and formulates its own contribution as a replacement: the existing measures are analysed on mathematical tasks, whereas "this common metric" — the $L_{2}$ weight norm — does not explain real tasks ([p. 2, para. 2](#p2-2)). The three quantities are cheap to compute and require neither an analysis of the representations nor knowledge of the correct solution: sparsity is the fraction of a layer's activations below a threshold $\tau$ ([p. 3, para. 3](#p3-3)), entropy is the sum $-|w_{ij}|\log|w_{ij}|$ over a layer's matrix ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1)), and circuit complexity is the KL divergence between the model's logits and those of a copy of it with a random 10 % of a layer's weights zeroed ([p. 5, para. 3](#p5-3)). What the work does not contain: causality in the sense of Barak et al. — not one of the three quantities is subjected to an intervention that would change it independently of the grokking; no correlations are computed anywhere, although the abstract claims "a stronger correlation" ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)), and the whole comparison with the weight norm is done by eye, from the curves of two figures; the sparsity threshold $\tau$ is not given as a number, although the measure is entirely determined by it; and the behaviour claimed for the first measure — a plateau right before the onset of the grokking ([p. 3, para. 6](#p3-6)) — marks a moment rather than tracking a hidden movement towards the solution.

**[The Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — the work's only target named by name. The retelling of the target is reduced to a single quantity: grokking arises in a "Goldilocks zone" of weight norms ([p. 1, para. 4](#p1-4)), the norm is declared the cause ([p. 2, para. 2](#p2-2)), and the paper undertakes to refute this "by showing that grokking can occur far outside this „Goldilocks zone“" ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4)). What the work does not contain: a measurement of the zone itself. Neither the critical norm $w_{c}$ nor the landscape of the reduced test loss in the norm — the only things by which the zone is defined in Liu et al. — is constructed for this setting. What is shown is that the norm grows from about 100 to 400 ([fig. 2](#fig-2)) and that grokking sets in nonetheless; the conclusion "far outside the zone" does not follow from this, because the zone's boundaries are unknown in the work. The converse matters too: its own [figure 1](#fig-1), in the unmodified setting, shows the norm falling from about 85 to 40 simultaneously with the generalisation, that is, it confirms the target — and this is not mentioned in the conclusions.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work gives the corpus a device that drives the norm and the generalisation in opposite directions: a term with a negative sign, $-\delta\sum_{w\in\theta}|w|^{2}$, is added to the loss to push the norm up, and at $\delta=2e-10$, found by "a simple manual sweep", grokking sets in with the norm steadily growing ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)). This is an intervention rather than one more observation of a correlation, and therein lies the work's chief value for the notion. Whence the conclusion: a decrease of the norm is not required for grokking, "and we thereby prove that weight norms are not a causal explanation of grokking" ([p. 5, para. 7](#p5-7)). What the work does not contain: a sweep of $\delta$ — one value is shown, chosen by hand to fit the desired outcome, and the bounds beyond which the device stops working are not named; an analysis of why an objective unbounded below does not diverge over 100 000 steps and of what holds the norm at a level of 400; and a distinction between "a decrease of the norm is not necessary" (shown) and "the norm plays no part in the mechanism" (declared with the word "prove", but not possibly shown from a single run of a single setting).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work stands entirely inside the setting of Omnigrok and inherits its caveats: the grokking on MNIST is obtained by the same two non-standard decisions — the sample cut down to 1000 training examples and the initialisation scale raised to 8.0 ([table 1](#tab-1)) — so what is shown is the inducibility of the phenomenon on real data rather than its occurrence. There is no setting of the work's own: "We keep the setting the same as in Liu et al. 2022b" ([p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)). What the work does not contain: not a single repetition, seed or confidence band — there is one run per setting; not a single table of results and not a single number apart from the hyperparameters and $\delta$; and there is no link to code or to a reproducing repository either.

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — both of the work's settings are non-algorithmic: a three-layer MLP of width 200 with ReLU on MNIST ([p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)) and an LSTM-based model on IMDb ([p. 9, para. 9](#p9-9)), both "with exactly the same settings and hyperparameters as Liu et al. 2022b". The work claims the carry-over of its measures from one architecture to another as a separate contribution. What the work does not contain: the author himself calls the IMDb results "limited" ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)); the step axis in [figure 4](#fig-4) breaks off at around 4000 against 100 000 on MNIST, the overfitting and generalisation zones are not marked on it, the training accuracy stands at one from the very beginning, and the validation accuracy oscillates with no late jump — that is, no delayed generalisation is visible in that figure, although the caption calls it grokking. The legend caption in the PDF is besides truncated to "local circuit comple".

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the design of the check is right and valuable for the corpus: instead of looking at a coincidence of curves, the work intervenes in the training so that the norm and the generalisation go in opposite directions ([p. 2, para. 6](#p2-6), [p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)). The intervention is caveated correctly and to the point: under AdamW the added term goes through the adaptive gradient and therefore does not reduce to "weight decay with the opposite sign" ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)). What the work does not contain: an intervention on its own three measures — none of them is varied independently, and so the status of the three new quantities remains exactly the one for which the weight norm is arraigned: a coincidence in time rather than causality. Zeroing 10 % of the weights ([p. 5, para. 3](#p5-3)) is a way of *measuring* measure 3 rather than an intervention on its value: the number of masks, the averaging and which layer exactly is taken are not stated in the work.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work separates weight decay from the weight norm as a distinct lever: it stays switched on and equal to 0.01 ([table 1](#tab-1)) in every run shown, while the norm grows on account of the added term. The conclusion is formulated as a pair: "a high weight-norm initialisation and weight decay are not the cause of grokking" ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)). What the work does not contain: a run at zero weight decay — nowhere does the work remove it and nowhere does it claim to, although that is exactly how it is most often retold (see the section on erroneous retellings below).

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — AdamW stands as part of the inherited setting and enters a substantive argument once: the added term is realised differently under AdamW from weight decay ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)), and so the intervention cannot be read as a simple sign flip of the weight decay. What the work does not contain: a comparison of optimisers, a check that what is shown is not a property of AdamW, and any runs with another optimiser whatever.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — an inherited lever: the device of Liu et al. — to train with weight decay starting from a high weight initialisation ([p. 2, para. 3](#p2-3)) — is reproduced at the value 8.0 ([table 1](#tab-1)) and unchanged. What the work does not contain: a sweep of the scale, any dependence on it, and a check of whether what is shown holds at an ordinary initialisation.

**[The sparse subnetwork / the lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the lottery-ticket hypothesis is named once and serves as the support of a verbal argument: the critical weights that grow in magnitude are few, since the hypothesis "holds to a good degree for MLPs trained on MNIST" ([p. 4, para. 6](#p4-6)), and so the total weight entropy must fall. What the work does not contain: any ticket — no subnetwork is isolated, no pruning is performed, no weight sparsity (as distinct from activation sparsity) is measured, and the hypothesis is nowhere related to grokking beyond this one step of argument.

**[Varieties of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — appendix C gives an intervention that extinguishes the phenomenon rather than shifting it: with a dropout of $p=0.3$ on every linear layer the delayed generalisation disappears entirely, and the overfitting zone with it ([p. 9, para. 8](#p9-8), [fig. 3](#fig-3)). What the work does not contain: a prediction and a check. The relation of the experiment to measure 3 is asserted by the words "this is consistent with our experiments", although the measure zeroes weights "similarly to dropout", and hence training with dropout directly optimises it — a conclusion neither drawn nor measured; there is no sweep of $p$; and the disappearance of grokking under dropout is consistent with the corpus's other explanations too.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work uses the word "circuit" in the name of its third measure and takes its start from expensive ways of computing circuit complexity, calling its own proposal simple and scalable ([p. 5, para. 2](#p5-2)). What the work does not contain: circuits. Not one circuit is isolated, ablated or matched with an algorithm; the author himself describes the quantity as "the sensitivity of the model's output to small perturbations of the weights" ([p. 5, para. 5](#p5-5)), and no relation to definitions of circuit complexity is drawn. The name and the content of the measure diverge, and this is worth keeping in mind when citing.

---

**[Sparsity](../../concepts/activation-sparsity.md)** — the subject here is its third form, sparsity of response: the work leans on the observation that well-generalising networks carry sparse MLP layers and introduces a measure of effective sparsity ([`p3-6`](#p3-6)). It differs from weight sparsity in that the quantity is defined relative to a sample.

**[Polysemanticity and superposition](../../concepts/polysemanticity-superposition.md)** — a caveat about the applicability of the whole analysis: some of the model's components represent features in superposition ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)), and then reading them off individual neurons ceases to be reliable.
## Points we could not reconcile

**The loss function: the whole text speaks of cross-entropy, the table of MSE.** The abstract speaks of classification "under a cross-entropy loss" ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)), the introduction writes out $L_{\text{CE}}$ as a formula, and the analysis of the entropy's behaviour twice leans on "minimising the cross-entropy loss" ([p. 4, para. 3](#p4-3), [p. 4, para. 5](#p4-5)) — whereas [table 1](#tab-1) gives the loss function as `MSELoss`. One of the two is wrong. The circumstantial evidence favours the table: the work declares that it keeps the setting of Liu et al. 2022b unchanged ([p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)), and in Liu et al. the grokking on MNIST is obtained precisely on MSE with one-hot targets, while their [appendix E](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#sec-e) states separately that under cross-entropy the Goldilocks zone is wider and that inducing grokking requires an initialisation scale of order $\alpha=100$ ([Liu, p. 15, para. 8](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p15-8)) — whereas here it stands at 8.0. The discrepancy touches more than presentation: the derivation of measure 2 is built entirely on which loss is being minimised, and the corpus has already split, citing the work now as a demonstration on cross-entropy and now as one on MSE.

**The word and the sign diverge.** The text says "We then add the square of the $L_{2}$ norm of the weights to the original cross-entropy loss function" ([p. 2, para. 6](#p2-6)), whereas the formula that follows subtracts: $\mathcal{L^{\prime}}=\mathcal{L}-\delta\sum_{w\in\theta}|w|^{2}$. The formula is right — only subtraction pushes the norm up, which is what the next paragraph says ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)).

**The abstract hedges, the conclusion proves.** The abstract: "weight norms, though they might usually correlate with grokking and with our progress measures, are not causative" ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)). The conclusion: "and we thereby prove that weight norms are not a causal explanation of grokking" ([p. 5, para. 7](#p5-7)). Between "might usually correlate … they are not causative" and "prove" lies a difference of status, and the work should be cited by the first formulation: one intervention on one setting is shown.

**The uniform distribution is written in two ways.** [Section 4.2.1](#p4-4) has $w_{ij}\sim U\left(a,a\right)$ — a degenerate interval — whereas appendix B, to which that same sentence refers, sets $w_{ij}\sim U(-a,a)$ and states the interval $[-a,a]$ explicitly ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)). The typo is present in the PDF too; the computation of appendix B, meanwhile, goes through and gives the closed form declared.

**A reference to algorithmic tasks leads to a work about non-algorithmic ones.** In [section 2](#p2-1) grokking on algorithmic tasks is credited to a pair of references, the first of which is Liu et al. 2022b, that is, Omnigrok, a work about non-algorithmic data precisely — and three lines below that same work is named as the source of the measure for real tasks.

## What is shown, what is conjectured and what is merely declared

The marks of the analysis add up into a distinct figure, and it is worth naming separately.

**Shown.** That grokking on MNIST in the setting of Liu et al. sets in even when the $L_{2}$ weight norm grows: the added term $-\delta\sum_{w\in\theta}|w|^{2}$ at $\delta=2e-10$ drives the norm from about 100 to 400, and the generalisation happens all the same ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). This is one intervention, one run, one setting — but it is a genuine intervention, and everything strong in the work rests on it. It is shown too that the three proposed quantities change in the same way in both settings — under a falling and under a growing norm ([fig. 1](#fig-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)) — and that with a dropout of $p=0.3$ the delayed generalisation disappears ([fig. 3](#fig-3)).

**Conjectured.** That the absolute weight entropy must fall over training: an argument about critical and non-critical weights with a reference to the lottery-ticket hypothesis ([p. 4, para. 6](#p4-6), [p. 4, para. 8](#p4-8)) — a verbal argument rather than a derivation or a measurement, and the author himself frames it as an expectation ("one can expect"). That a low entropy is a sign of a robust generalisation ([p. 4, para. 9](#p4-9)) — a reading unverifiable within the work. That a low KL divergence indicates more stable and reliable internal representations ([p. 5, para. 5](#p5-5)) — likewise a reading, introduced by the word "suggesting".

**Declared without being shown.** That grokking occurs "far outside the Goldilocks zone" ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4), [p. 1, para. 8](#p1-8)): the zone is not measured, its boundaries are unknown, and only a growth of the norm is shown. That the three measures "exhibit a stronger correlation with grokking… compared with weight norms" ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)): not a single correlation is computed in the work, and the comparison is wholly by eye. That weight norms are not a causal explanation ([p. 5, para. 7](#p5-7)): what is shown is that a *decrease* of the norm is not necessary, which is a different claim.

**Acknowledged limitations.** The IMDb results are called limited by the author himself ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)). The weights do not form a probability distribution, and the quantity $H$ is unnormalised — this is stated outright ([p. 4, para. 2](#p4-2)); the consequence, not analysed in the work, is that $H$ depends arithmetically on the scale of the weights, that is, on the very norm declared non-causal, and the independence of the two quantities is nowhere checked.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this work restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached.

###### mc-2210-01117

**Liu, Michaud, Tegmark (2210.01117) — the cause named in Omnigrok reduced to a single quantity.** The formulation: `"Liu et al. 2022b introduce the $L_{2}$ weight norm as the cause of grokking on real-world tasks"` ([p. 2, para. 2](#p2-2)); and further, `"a high weight norm initialization and weight decay is not the cause for grokking as hypothesized by Liu et al. 2022b"` ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)).

In Omnigrok the cause named is not the norm but the mismatch of the reduced training and test losses as functions of the norm — the "LU mechanism": the test loss has a "U" shape with a minimum at the critical norm $w_{c}$, the training loss an "L" shape, and grokking arises from the gap between them ([Liu, p. 2, para. 3](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p2-3), [Liu, p. 3, para. 2](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p3-2)). The norm in that picture is a control parameter of the landscape rather than a cause in itself. The collapsed formulation makes refutation easier: to show that grokking occurs at a growing norm is not the same as to show that there is no gap between the "L" and the "U". It is telling too that the critique passes by the target's weakest place: Omnigrok contains an admission of its own that on IMDb the training loss has a "U" shape rather than the required "L", that is, the mechanism's central premise is empirically unfulfilled — and that move is not used here, although IMDb is taken by the same work into its appendix D.

Separately: the declaration "We keep the setting the same as in Liu et al. 2022b" ([p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)) sits ill with the cross-entropy declared. In Omnigrok the MNIST grokking is obtained on MSE with one-hot targets, and their [appendix E](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#sec-e) states outright that cross-entropy requires an initialisation scale of about $\alpha=100$ against the 8.0 standing here ([Liu, p. 15, para. 8](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p15-8)).

In other respects the work cites Omnigrok accurately: the device of a high initialisation ([p. 2, para. 3](#p2-3)), the authors' name for the zone, and the provenance of the setting are all named correctly.

**Kumar (1704.08863) — a uniform initialisation credited to the wrong work.** The formulations: `"weights are initialized using schemes such as the Glorot uniform initialization (Kumar 2017)"` ([p. 4, para. 4](#p4-4)); and `"such as the commonly used Xavier initialization scheme (Kumar 2017)"` ([p. 8, para. 4](#p8-4)). Kumar 2017 is an analysis of initialisation in general rather than the primary source of the Glorot–Xavier scheme; the primary source is absent from the paper's bibliography.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. Each entry says what exactly is distorted and gives references to the author's paragraphs where the lost caveat stands.

**"Grokking obtained without weight decay, at a growing norm" (erroneous).** The commonest retelling: the work is credited with a run with weight decay switched off, in which the norm grows of itself — and on that basis is called a confirmation of the citing authors' own results obtained at WD=0. So in Prakash et al. ([2602.02859](../2602.02859.late-stage-generalization-collapse-in-grokking-detecting-anti-grokking-with-weightwatcher/2602.02859.late-stage-generalization-collapse-in-grokking-detecting-anti-grokking-with-weightwatcher.card.md)): `"Golechha introduced progress measures (e.g., Activation Sparsity, Absolute Weight Entropy) and notably observed grokking without WD, resulting in increasing $\ell_{2}$ norms, similar to our setup."`; the same word for word in Prakash & Martin ([2506.04434](../2506.04434.grokking-and-generalization-collapse-insights-from-htsr-theory/2506.04434.grokking-and-generalization-collapse-insights-from-htsr-theory.card.md)), and in the same authors, `"We observe that grokking occurs even without weight decay (leading to an increasing norm), suggesting weight norm alone is not a complete explanation, confirming the weight-norm related findings by Golechha"`. In the same vein 2606.13753: `"Golechha 2024 reports grokking on real-world data (MNIST, IMDb) outside the expected norm range — even without weight decay, with the norm increasing"`. In the author, weight decay is nowhere switched off: it equals 0.01 in [table 1](#tab-1), is named as part of the setting ("AdamW optimizer with weight decay", [p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)) and stays switched on in both runs shown. The norm grows not because the weight decay has been removed but because a term with a negative sign has been added to the loss ([p. 2, para. 6](#p2-6), [p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)) — and the author specifically stipulates that under AdamW this is realised differently from weight decay. The difference is not cosmetic: "grokking without regularisation" and "grokking under regularisation plus a countervailing term" are different claims, and the second is far weaker.

**"The demonstration is set up at an MSE loss and without explicit regularisation" (erroneous, but generated by the paper itself).** Liu, Cao, Li and Zhou (2605.06152) write: `"growing empirical evidence shows that grokking can also occur without explicit regularization, although many of these demonstrations are conducted under mean-squared error (MSE) loss Gromov (2023); Kumar et al. (2024); Golechha (2024); Prakash and Martin (2025)"`. Both halves diverge from the paper's text: explicit regularisation is present in it (weight decay 0.01), and the loss is everywhere called cross-entropy in the text ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2), [p. 4, para. 3](#p4-3)). The citing authors appear to have read [table 1](#tab-1), which has `MSELoss`. The fault here is not the citing authors': the work itself speaks of the loss in two ways (see the section on inconsistencies), and the corpus has already split in two, referring to different halves of it.

**"The work reads grokking as the sudden emergence of a winning ticket" (erroneous).** In the survey of 2605.15787 the paper is assigned to the family of structural and circuit explanations: `"characterizing grokking as the sudden emergence of specific “winning tickets” [Minegishi et al., 2025, Golechha, 2024, Nanda et al., 2023]"`. The lottery-ticket hypothesis appears in the author exactly once and in an auxiliary role — as an argument for why the weights growing in magnitude are few and why the entropy must therefore fall ([p. 4, para. 6](#p4-6)). No ticket is extracted in the work: no subnetwork is isolated, no pruning is performed, no weight sparsity is measured — what is measured is activation sparsity, a different quantity. There are no circuits in the work either, despite the word in the name of the third measure ([p. 5, para. 5](#p5-5)).

**"Grokking occurs outside the Goldilocks zone" (widening, checked later).** The formulation of [Notsawo et al. (2506.05718)](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/original/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.md#p16-2): `"Golechha (2024) show that $\ell_{2}$ norm alone can not explain grokking, as it occurs even outside the typical “goldilocks zone”(Liu et al., 2023a), and propose alternative measures like activation sparsity and weight entropy that better track generalization dynamics."` This is word for word the author's own claim ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4), [p. 1, para. 8](#p1-8)) — but it has no support: the zone is not measured in the work, neither $w_{c}$ nor the landscape of the test loss in the norm is constructed, and only a growth of the norm is shown. The citing authors carry forward an unsupported step of the paper rather than distorting it; and they are the only ones to have checked the thesis — on their own theory and their own settings, though, rather than inheriting this one.

**The three measures were checked against a negative control and failed it.** Prakash & Martin ([2506.04434](../2506.04434.grokking-and-generalization-collapse-insights-from-htsr-theory/2506.04434.grokking-and-generalization-collapse-insights-from-htsr-theory.card.md)) extended the training to $10^{7}$ steps and ran all three quantities through the phase of late accuracy collapse: activation sparsity `"fails the negative control in the anti-grokking phase because it continues to increase in the same way as in pre-grokking"` (the same formulation is repeated in [2602.02859](../2602.02859.late-stage-generalization-collapse-in-grokking-detecting-anti-grokking-with-weightwatcher/2602.02859.late-stage-generalization-collapse-in-grokking-detecting-anti-grokking-with-weightwatcher.card.md)), absolute weight entropy `"also decreases sharply during the collapse, thus not reliably distinguishing this anti-grokking phase"`, and circuit complexity `"remains low throughout the collapse, failing to reflect the performance degradation"`. This is the corpus's only direct experimental check of the three measures, and it is negative; the same authors are also responsible for the most erroneous retelling of the setting (see the first pattern above).

**Examples of accurate citation.** 2604.12434: `"Golechha (2024) investigates grokking on real-world classification tasks and proposes progress measures such as activation sparsity, absolute weight entropy, and approximate local circuit complexity that correlate with grokking more strongly than weight norms in that study"` — the caveat "in that study" preserved, and the claim not turned into a general law. 2410.04489: `"the role of activation sparsity, weight entropy, and circuit complexity in real-world tasks (Golechha, 2024)"` — the contribution named rather than a conclusion ascribed.

Milder echoes not warranting separate analysis: [2504.17243](../2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation/2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation.card.md) (the absolute weight entropy taken as a ready-made measure of model complexity), [2512.13568](../../externals/2512.13568.superposition-as-lossy-compression-measure-with-sparse-autoencoders-and-connect-to-adversarial-vulnerability/original/2512.13568.superposition-as-lossy-compression-measure-with-sparse-autoencoders-and-connect-to-adversarial-vulnerability.md)↗ and [2510.25966](../2510.25966.grokking-in-the-ising-model/original/2510.25966.grokking-in-the-ising-model.md) (mentions in literature surveys), [2603.01968](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md) (the work stands only in the bibliography and is not retold in the text).
