## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work takes from Power et al. 2022 only the shape of the curve (a long shelf, then a sharp rise) and carries it over to a task that has not been part of the corpus's proving ground: the one-step Collatz map $T(n)$ on an encoder-decoder transformer. The contribution is claimed to lie not in the presence of the delay but in its address: the authors explicitly restate the question — [not whether the internal structure precedes the behaviour, but where exactly the gap that arises is located](#p8-2) — and by two interventions assign the delay to the decoder's read-out rather than to the formation of the representations ([p. 5, para. 4](#p5-4)). The gap itself, between a linearly readable feature and the behaviour, receives from them a name — [the *shadow knowledge gap*](#fig-1) — and the conclusion is formulated as [learnt representations may outrun the behaviour](#p9-2). What the work does not contain: the training accuracy. It is shown in no plot and in no table, so the classic feature of grokking — a fit to the training data long before generalisation — is not measured here at all, and the "plateau" is described only on a held-out set. Nor is there weight decay as a lever, a fraction of training data as a control quantity or any sweep around the transition whatever: the hyperparameters are fixed, and what is varied is the task, the base, the architecture and the scale.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives a plateau of a new kind: not "the training is fitted, the test stands still" but "the feature is linearly readable, the output stands still". In base 8 the parity probe reaches 99.7 % by step 2000, whereas the exact sequence accuracy at that moment is 38 % and stays low until the kink at around step 44k ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). Separately from this, a classic memorisation episode is described in base 2: a peak of 56.0 % at step 16 000, then a collapse to zero with no recovery through 500k steps ([p. 19, para. 8](#p19-8)). What the work does not contain: a measurement of memorisation as such — the training accuracy is not reported, and the words [the model memorises its 1000 training examples](#p19-8) conflict with the online sampling from a range of 10 000 integers described in the same place ([p. 13, para. 3](#p13-3)); see the section on inconsistencies.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the work gives the notion an observation on arithmetic with branching: the encoder acquires the structure of the low-order residues early and in a definite order. The probes for mod 2, 4, 8, 16 all reach the ceiling at around step 2k, with the coarse ones slightly earlier than the fine ([p. 4, para. 2](#p4-2), [fig. 3](#fig-3)), and conditional probes of the 2-adic filtration show that the order is indeed sequential: $k_{2}$ and $k_{3}$ are above 0.99 from step 2000, while $k_{4}$ rises from 0.676 and reaches a peak of about 0.96 only by step 130k ([p. 18, para. 3](#p18-3), [fig. 10](#fig-10)). What the work does not contain: the geometry of the representations. Neither the embeddings nor their structure is inspected here — the whole argument is built on linear readability, and the only geometric quantity in the entire paper is the participation ratio, computed for the binary failure.

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the work's strongest observation on this score concerns a pretrained model: in Pythia-1.4B the probes for $T(n)\bmod 8$ and $n\bmod 16$ read 1.00 already at step 0, before a single gradient of the task, at an exact match of precisely 0.000, and the gap closes within ~500 steps of fine-tuning ([p. 7, para. 4](#p7-4), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). Whence the authors conclude that fine-tuning [installs the read-out, not the features](#p7-4). What the work does not contain: any training at step 0 — and hence no delayed generalisation in the sense of Power et al.: the gap observed there is a gap between the linear readability of the input in a pretrained model and the ability to produce a string, and it is presented in one row with the grokking plateau of the small model although it measures something else.

**[Linear probing](../../concepts/linear-sparse-probing.md)** — the work's chief instrument, and it pushes it to its limit: the probe is a logistic regression with L2 regularisation on standardised, position-averaged states of the encoder's last layer ([p. 14, para. 3](#p14-3)), trained on 5000 examples at an internal 80/20 split. The same protocol is applied to Pythia-160M and Pythia-1.4B, and in the decoder-only baseline it yields an extreme case: an exact match of 0.000 at probes $\geq 0.999$ in the early layers ([p. 25, para. 2](#p25-2), [fig. 12](#fig-12)). What the work does not contain: a control of the probe against an untrained model or against a probe on the input embeddings. This matters precisely here, because the paper itself proves ([p. 19, para. 7](#p19-7)) that in an even base the low-order digits of the output are determined by the two low-order digits of the input — that is, the saturation of the probe is compatible with the required arithmetic being performed by the probe itself rather than by the model.

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the work brings the corpus two devices. The first is the erasure of a probe direction with a control by a random subspace of the same rank: on the plateau removing a single parity direction costs 8.2 percentage points of accuracy, by step 22 000 it costs 0.3 points, and at convergence it is negligible ([p. 28, para. 3](#p28-3), [fig. 16](#fig-16)); at convergence the only load-bearing axis left is a "magnitude" one (0.141), while all the residue subspaces are redundant ([p. 28, para. 4](#p28-4)). The second is the carry-over of the same intervention into a pretrained language model: in Pythia-1.4B erasing a rank-15 subspace for $n\bmod 16$ at layer 4 drops the accuracy 0.845 → 0.043, whereas a random subspace of the same rank costs 0.005 ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)). What the work does not contain: an intervention on the read-out path within a single trained model. Transplantation and rewinding are also called causal, but they act differently: they change the initial conditions of the optimisation ("which half is taken ready-made") rather than a component of a working network.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work uses a kit of MI devices (probes, direction erasure, an analysis of attention heads) but does no reverse engineering: the algorithm the network executes is not recovered here, and not one circuit is named. The only mechanistic identification is a matching of the dispersal of the parity-routing head with the cleanup phase of Nanda et al.: [an encoder-decoder analogue of the *cleanup* phase](#p6-1). The value for the corpus lies elsewhere: the erasure trajectory of six features across 50 checkpoints shows how the support of the read-out passes from a single simple cue to a distributed code ([p. 29, para. 1](#p29-1), [fig. 17](#fig-17)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the authors themselves call this trajectory [a mechanistic *progress measure* in the sense of Nanda et al.](#p29-1), and this is the paper's only use of the term. The quantity does indeed change over a flat region of the loss where the accuracy registers nothing. What the work does not contain: a check of the measure against what a progress measure must be. No prediction of the time to the transition, no comparison with the restricted and excluded losses of Nanda et al., and no reproduction on other bases: the trajectory is taken on a single run in base 8.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the work gives an observation on the cross-attention of an encoder-decoder model that the corpus did not have: during the plateau the share of attention from the BOS position to the last input digit is gathered in a single head (0.89 at step 66 000), which disperses to 0.21 by step 70 000, within the second kink, and does not recover ([p. 20, para. 2](#p20-2)). Which (layer, head) pair becomes the routing one depends on the seed. What the work does not contain: an intervention — the head is neither disabled nor replaced, and the measure is taken only from the forward pass at a checkpoint ([p. 20, para. 1](#p20-1)); and the pattern is pronounced in three seeds out of four, whereas the leading sentence says [it holds in all three](#p20-2).

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work stands outside the proving ground of Power et al. and does so deliberately. There is no binary operation table: examples are generated procedurally from a deterministic map, each step drawing a fresh 1000 integers from $[1,10{,}000]$, and the evaluation runs on a fixed 5000 unseen integers ([p. 3, para. 6](#p3-6), [p. 13, para. 1](#p13-1)). Whence a consequence important for comparison with the corpus: there is no fraction of training data as a control quantity here at all, there is by construction almost no overfitting, and the place of the control parameter is taken by the number base. The default model is different too — not a small decoder-only transformer but an encoder-decoder of 6+6 layers.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the work opposes itself to it outright: unlike the benchmarks of modular arithmetic, [governed by a single global rule](#p2-1), the Collatz step mixes easy and hard cases within one task. Yet all the paper's probes target precisely residues ($\bmod\,2,4,8,16$), so the modular structure enters here not as the task but as a measured feature. What the work does not contain: the modular task proper — not a single run on $(a+b)\bmod p$, and hence no possibility of comparing its numbers with the corpus's directly.

**[Compression of the representation manifold](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the work gives the corpus a rare observation of the opposite sign: the compression accompanies not the generalisation but the failure. In base 2 the participation ratio of the encoder's representations falls from 5.2 to 1.0 within one checkpoint, the accuracy collapses to zero and does not recover through 500k steps, and neighbouring checkpoints at step 66 000 have a cosine similarity of $-0.98$ ([p. 19, para. 8](#p19-8)). What the work does not contain: the participation ratio anywhere else. It is computed only for the binary failure and is not given for the successful bases, so the compression at the collapse cannot be compared with the compression at the grokking from this data.

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — merely mentioned. The coincidence of the head's dispersal with the kink of the loss is called [consistent with explanations of grokking through a competition of circuits](#p20-2), with references to Varma et al. 2023 and Merrill et al. 2023, and in the main text the same works are assigned to competing explanations that place the gap in the formation of the structure ([p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2)). Neither the efficiency nor the capacity of circuits is measured here, and no circuit is isolated in the network.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — merely mentioned and never swept: it is fixed at $10^{-2}$ in every run ([p. 23, para. 2](#p23-2)). Its only substantive appearance is in the explanation of the binary failure, where the collapse is ascribed to [a brittle memorised solution that is later destroyed by the regularisation](#p19-8). There is no check of this explanation in the work — no run in base 2 without weight decay or at another value of it.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The batch size is named twice and differently.** §3 says that [each training step takes 1000 integers](#p3-6), and appendix C.1.1 repeats it ([p. 13, para. 3](#p13-3)); appendix G says that [the batch size was 512 unless otherwise stated](#p23-2). There are no such statements in the text. When citing the training settings one should remember that this work gives the number of examples per step as two incompatible values.

**The binary failure is described as the memorisation of a fixed set that the setting does not contain.** Appendix D.2 says: [the model memorises its 1000 training examples](#p19-8) — and the main text likewise, [the model briefly memorises the training sample](#p7-1). But the training sample here is generated online, a fresh 1000 integers at each step from a range of 10 000 ([p. 13, para. 3](#p13-3)); over the 16 000 steps up to the peak the model sees the range many times over, and no fixed set of 1000 examples that could be memorised exists. This changes the reading of the whole binary episode: what is called "a brittle memorised solution" cannot, by the setting, be one.

**The abstract promises the level of chance, the body of the paper gives 38 %.** In the abstract: [the output accuracy holds near the level of chance for tens of thousands of steps more](#p1-2). In §4.1, in that same setting: [the sequence-level output accuracy at that moment is only 38 %](#p4-1). At an exact match on four or five digits in base 8 the chance level is practically zero, so 38 % is not it. The discrepancy matters for citing works: "near chance" and "38 %" describe different phenomena.

**The probe as a stopping signal contradicts the paper's own checkpoint sweep.** The conclusion asserts: [once the encoder's residue probes saturate, further training of the encoder is unproductive, and the probe accuracy gives a stopping signal](#p9-3) — and appendix B repeats this as the first practical conclusion ([p. 11, para. 8](#p11-8)). The probes saturate by step 2000 ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1)). But the checkpoint sweep gives a fresh decoder trained against encoder snapshots from 2k to 50k steps [an accuracy of 0.65–0.80 with no gain from additional encoder computation](#p5-5), whereas against the converged encoder the same procedure gives 0.978 ([p. 16, para. 1](#p16-1)). Between step 50k and convergence the encoder therefore adds about 0.18 of final accuracy. The authors note this fact ([the encoder goes on being refined after step 50k](#p16-2)) yet retain the conclusion about the unproductivity of further encoder training and about the probe as a stopping signal.

**Table 2 runs against the chief result, and the leading sentence does not reflect it.** On the additional training-data seeds the encoder transplant gives 54.4 % against 94.4 % for training from scratch (seed 10) and 93.6 % against 96.4 % (seed 11); rewinding the decoder on seed 10 gives 62.0 % against the same 94.4 % ([p. 14, para. 10](#p14-10), table 2). Both additional seeds, then, run against the effect, and on one of them it is reversed. The text immediately after the table says the opposite: [across all conditions the individual seeds agree in direction, even where the final accuracy differs, which supports the qualitative claims of the main text](#p14-11). Besides, the "main" rows are left with no numbers, so the comparison of seeds within a condition is incomplete.

**"Collatz from scratch at the same setting" is named by three different numbers.** In §4.2 and in appendix F it is 86.1 % ([p. 5, para. 4](#p5-4), [whereas Collatz from scratch reaches 86.1 % at the same budget](#p22-4)); in the format-matched control it is [~84 percentage points below Collatz from scratch (≈93 %) at the same setting](#p22-6); and in appendix C.3 joint end-to-end training at the same compute budget is called 0.969 ([p. 16, para. 1](#p16-1)). The three numbers stand as the support of three different comparisons, and which of them is comparable with which does not follow from the text.

## What is measured, what is shown and what is conjectured

The work is uneven in the strength of its arguments, and its halves rest on different things. This section separates them — the numbers by themselves do not say where they come from.

**Predicted and checked.** Rewinding the decoder is a direct check of a consequence of the read-out explanation: if the delay lies in the read-out, then freezing the converged encoder and returning the decoder to its step-2k weights ought to remove the plateau. They do remove it: 91.9 % by step 22 000, a final 97.6 % ([p. 5, para. 4](#p5-4), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). Both erasures are built the same way: the trajectory of the drop from erasing parity is predicted to peak on the plateau and to be negligible at convergence — and so it is ([p. 28, para. 3](#p28-3)); and the carry-over of the same intervention into Pythia-1.4B is furnished with a control by a random subspace of equal rank, the difference being two orders of magnitude ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)). This is the work's soundest part.

**Shown, but without the necessary control.** The asymmetry of the transplants — the central argument of §4.2 — has no control with an *untrained* frozen decoder: a fresh encoder trained against any fixed output is already poorly conditioned, and the contribution of the decoder's being trained in particular is not isolated ([p. 5, para. 2](#p5-2)). The local-predictability measure $H^{(r)}$, declared the support of the conclusion about bases, does not distinguish bases at all: [the conditional entropy is zero for every base we checked](#p19-1) on the odd branch, and on the even branch it is 1.00 for all even bases and 0.00 for purely odd ones, so base 2 (0 %) and base 24 (99.8 %) have coinciding values ([p. 23, para. 6](#p23-6), table 6). The authors caveat this themselves: [we use $H_{b}^{(r)}$ as a heuristic measure of the exposure of local structure rather than as a full measure of computational difficulty](#p18-1). The ordering of the bases therefore rests on the direct sweep and on the one-digit-lookahead computation ([p. 19, para. 7](#p19-7)) rather than on the entropy measure.

**Called a delay where no delay is measured.** In the decoder-only baseline the generalisation does not set in in any of the five bases (an exact match of 0.000) at probes $\geq 0.999$ in the early layers ([p. 25, para. 2](#p25-2)): a gap is shown, but not a delay — it has no second end. In Pythia-1.4B the probes give 1.00 at step 0, that is, before any training on the task ([p. 7, para. 4](#p7-4)); this is a gap between the linear readability of the input and the ability to produce a string, not a delayed generalisation in the sense of Power et al., although it is presented in one row with the small model's plateau.

**Conjectured.** Appendix B ("practical conclusions": the probe as a stopping signal, a warm start from an encoder on a convenient base, investment in the read-out, the choice of base) is carried over to the fine-tuning of language models by an expectation — [we expect these conclusions to carry over](#p12-2) — and is not checked experimentally; the first of the four conclusions besides conflicts with the checkpoint sweep (see above). The identification of the head's dispersal with the cleanup phase of Nanda et al. ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1), [p. 20, para. 2](#p20-2)) is an analogy rather than a measurement: the mechanism of the two phases is not compared, only a coincidence in time is. The transitional instability of the depth-4 run is called consistent with a circuit-replacement-like reorganisation, and the authors themselves caveat that [we do not rely on this reading in our main claim](#p22-2).

**Caveats that the authors do make but that are easily lost.** The cross-base comparisons by exact match are confounded with output length, and the authors write outright that [comparisons between distant bases should not be read as a pure measure of algorithmic difficulty](#p12-9); the argument against that confound is given separately, by recomputation into per-digit accuracy and equal-length bins ([p. 19, para. 9](#p19-9)). Part of the cells of the $k$-Collatz sweep are taken on one or two seeds ([p. 27, para. 1](#p27-1)), the main text's principal quantities on three, and only the cross-base transplant is carried to six. There is a single author. And the authors themselves draw the limit of the carry-over: [the mechanism is cleanest in the encoder-decoder setting](#p9-4), and the largest check is a controlled arithmetic task rather than a natural language workload.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

Editorial notes on the places where this work conveys others' results inaccurately.

###### mc-2205-10343

**Liu et al. (2205.10343) — circuit replacement ascribed to a work about representation learning.** In appendix E the transitional instability of the depth-4 run is described thus: `"This pattern is consistent with a circuit-replacement-like reorganization (Liu et al., 2022)"` ([p. 22, para. 2](#p22-2)). Liu et al. 2022 is [an effective theory of representation learning](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md): it introduces $\delta$-parallelograms, RQI, the Goldilocks zone and four phases of training, but there are no circuits and no replacement of them — the language of circuits came into the corpus from other works. The pedigree of the error is visible within the paper itself: in appendix D.3 the very same phenomenon — the displacement of one solution by another at a kink — is assigned to the right address, [consistent with explanations of grokking through a competition of circuits (Varma et al., 2023; Merrill et al., 2023)](#p20-2). That is, the work knows the correct reference and in one place out of two puts it wide. The claim is meanwhile hedged twice ("consistent with", "-like") and furnished with a caveat that the main claim does not rely on this reading, so the price of the error is small; but when citing appendix E the reference should be corrected.

Milder, of the same kind: in §5 "progression through harder subtasks" is assigned to Barak et al. 2022 and Michaud et al. 2023 together ([p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2)). In [Barak et al.](../2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit/2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit.card.md) the mechanism is a different one — a gradual amplification of a sparse solution through a Fourier gap in the population gradient rather than a passage through subtasks in increasing order of difficulty; this does not warrant a separate entry, because it stands in an enumeration of competing explanations rather than in the support of an argument.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. There are so far two citing works, and both convey the chief thesis correctly; below is the one place where a formulation is stronger than the authors'.

**"Transplanting the encoder accelerates grokking 2.75-fold" (widening, requires a caveat).** Citing works take the number 2.75 as a coefficient of grokking acceleration. The formulation observed is in Janati et al. (2608.07436; there is as yet no card for that work in the corpus): `"transplanting a trained encoder accelerates grokking by $2.75$ times while transplanting a trained decoder hurts"`. The authors' quantity is different and narrower: [the encoder transplant reaches 70 % accuracy at step 24 000 against step 66 000 for training from scratch](#p5-2) — that is, one chosen accuracy threshold is reached 2.75 times earlier, rather than a grokking transition setting in 2.75 times sooner, and this is taken on three seeds ([fig. 2](#fig-2)). In the body of version v2 a different quantity has been made the chief one for the transplant: a doubling of the accuracy on six seeds in the hardest setting (0.751 against 0.365, [p. 5, para. 3](#p5-3)), while 2.75 remains only in §4.2 and in the caption of figure 2. Besides, the additional training-data seeds give, under that same condition, 54.4 % against 94.4 % and 93.6 % against 96.4 % ([p. 14, para. 10](#p14-10)) — that is, the coefficient is not stable across seeds. The pedigree of the formulation is traceable: the turn "accelerates grokking by 2.75 times" stands word for word in the arXiv metadata abstract, left over from version v1 and not updated for v2, whereas the abstract in the body of v2 no longer contains that number. The other halves of the same citing sentence — about the 99.7 % probe at an accuracy of about 38 %, and about freezing the converged encoder with a retrained decoder removing the plateau — match the authors' text exactly.

An example of accurate citation, for contrast — Verma 2026 (2605.20441; likewise no card as yet): `"attribute delayed generalization in encoder-decoder Collatz prediction to a representation-access bottleneck rather than feature-acquisition failure"`. Here both the setting and what exactly is being opposed are named, and no quantity is carried out of the work.

Separately — a risk absent from the citations observed so far, but one this work attracts by its construction: citing it as having shown that structure arises before behaviour. It has not shown that and does not claim it: the gap as such is described in Barak et al. 2022 and Nanda et al. 2023, and the paper writes outright that it asks [not about that but about the location of the gap](#p8-2). What is new here is the attempt to name the address, and it is that which should be cited.
