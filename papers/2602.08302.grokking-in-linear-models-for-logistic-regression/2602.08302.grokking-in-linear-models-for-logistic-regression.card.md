# Das, Vedantam, Lakshminarayanan 2026 — Grokking in Linear Models for Logistic Regression

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Nataraj Das**, **Atreya Vedantam** (equal contribution), **Chandrasekhar Lakshminarayanan** (Indian Institute of Technology Madras, Chennai).

arXiv [2602.08302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08302), February 2026 (v1), 15 pages, 4 figures, 1 table. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. An extremely simple solvable model of grokking: a linear model with the logistic loss on data linearly separable through the origin, where the learnable bias $b$ is knowingly superfluous — and it is precisely its slow support-vector dynamics that produces the delay. Three phases (the population one; the support-vector "unlearning" that drives $b$ away from zero; the generalisation, in which the distance of the hyperplane from the origin $|b(t)|/||\mathbf{w}(t)||$ decays as $\mathcal{O}(1/\log t)$ at the expense of the growing weight norm), a closed-form solution for $b(t)$ and a characterisation of the delay time through the support-vector skew $\delta=\sqrt{A_{S}^{-}/A_{S}^{+}}$. The key caveat: on the same distribution as the training one there is no grokking — it arises only on a test set squeezed into a band near the boundary, or on an adversarial one.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the claim "grokking requires neither depth nor representation learning": the delay arises in a single-layer linear model through the dynamics of one scalar — the bias $b$ — and decomposes into three phases; the paper's own definition (definition 3.4) measures the times at which a fraction $(1-\epsilon)$ of the *suprema* of the accuracies is reached and requires $T_{te}>\zeta\cdot T_{tr}$ with a threshold $\zeta$ of order $100$ ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)). The analysis: the definition counts the fraction of the supremum attained rather than the absolute quality — a model with a test-accuracy ceiling of $0.77$ formally groks if it took it late, and this widening against Power et al. is not stated aloud; grokking is present here only under a mismatch of the training and test distributions (on the standard test $\zeta=4$ and $1$, by the paper's own admission), so the title thesis reads with a caveat.

**[Margin maximisation / implicit bias](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — the whole mechanics is built on the apparatus of Soudry et al.: $\mathbf{w}(t)=\hat{\mathbf{w}}\log t+\rho(t)$ with $\rho(t)\to\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$, the direction converges to the max-margin separator early (phase 1), and the late dynamics of the bias under the exponential loss is solved in closed form: $\frac{db}{dt}=\frac{1}{t}(A_{S}^{+}e^{-b}-A_{S}^{-}e^{b})$ with the limit $b_{\infty}=-\log\delta$ ([`p3-17`](#p3-17)). The analysis: in the parameters the model does not converge to the generalising solution at all ($b\to-\log\delta\neq 0$) — convergence exists only in the normalised form $|b|/||\mathbf{w}||$; the theorem is proved for the exponential loss, while the experiments run on the logistic one with no estimate of the error of the substitution; and Soudry's condition "the support vectors span the data" is not checked for $d=1024$ (the number of support vectors is not reported).

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — the non-monotonicity of the test curve (a rise of the accuracy → a collapse in the unlearning phase → a late rise) receives here a closed-form explanation for a linear classifier: the asymmetry of the support vectors drives $b$ away from zero, the test accuracy on the concentrated test set falls to $0.5$, and then the growth of $||\mathbf{w}||$ returns the normalised distance to zero ([`p7-6`](#p7-6)). The analysis: the "unlearning" down to exactly $0.5$ is built in by construction — the test points lie in the band $|\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}|\le\alpha\gamma=10^{-2}$, while the hyperplane moves away from the origin as far as $0.07$, so the whole balanced sample ends up on one side.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — a counterpoint to the corpus's norm picture: generalisation sets in here not because the norm falls but because it *grows* — $||\mathbf{w}(t)||\sim(2/\gamma)\log t$ dilutes the stuck bias, and $|b(t)|/||\mathbf{w}(t)||\to 0$ as $\mathcal{O}(1/\log t)$; weight decay is absent from the setting altogether, its role being played by the logarithmic growth of the norm ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). The analysis: there is no lazy-to-rich transition in a linear model by construction, which separates the bias mechanism from the mechanism of the transition to the rich regime; the quantitative prediction of the delay time (theorem 4.6) is not checked against the experiment by a single number — at $\delta\approx 0.87$ the formula gives units of epochs against the observed $10^{6}$–$10^{7}$.

**[Grokking time](../../concepts/grokking-time.md)** — the term is derived from the geometry of the sample, from the support-vector skew, rather than from the settings of the optimisation ([`p6-9`](#p6-9)). The prediction is checked by the fact that a purpose-built sample lengthens the delay exactly as the theory implies.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong point — the closed-form solution and the controlling parameter.** The whole non-monotonicity of the test curve is explained by the dynamics of one scalar with the explicit solution $b(t)$ through $g(t)=2\sqrt{A_{S}^{+}A_{S}^{-}}\log(t/t_{0})$; the support-vector skew $\delta$ is named as the controlling parameter of the length of the delay, and the experiment with the planting of support vectors (fig. 2) confirms the sign of the dependence: the larger the skew, the higher the plateau of the bias, the higher the peak of the distance from the origin, and the later the test accuracy rises. Definition 3.4 with the threshold $\zeta\geq 100$ honestly calibrates other people's observations ($\zeta=1000$ in Power, $4$ in Gromov, $100$ in Liu on MNIST, $300$ in Thilak). The "delayed robustness" of Humayun et al. is reproduced on a single hyperplane.

**The quantitative part of the theory is not checked against the experiment.** The formula $T_{te}=\exp(|\log\delta|/(1+2\epsilon(2\alpha-1)))$ at $\delta\approx 0.87$ predicts units of epochs against the observed $10^{6}$–$10^{7}$; $\gamma$, $t_{0}$ and the $A_{S}^{\pm}$ themselves have dropped out of it; the quantity $B$ in the bounds is not defined, and the linear model of the accuracy $Q(t)$ is taken without derivation and sets $\sup Q=1$, which the experiments do not reach. The sharpness of the phase boundaries is built into an idealisation (assumption 4.4) rather than derived.

**Internal inconsistencies.** The training curves in the figures level off at $0.82$–$0.88$ rather than "nearly perfect" — only the supremum in the definition saves the case; the phase boundaries in the shading of the figures ($10^{2}$, $10^{4}$) disagree with the text ($5\times 10^{3}$, $5\times 10^{4}$); section 6 reports the grokking of adversarial data "both with and without a learnable bias" — but without the bias the claimed mechanism is not necessary, and the case is left unexplained; $\mathbf{w}_{\text{true}}$ is normalised now by $1/\sqrt{n}$, now by $1/\sqrt{2}$ (at $d=1024$ neither gives a unit norm); the internal references are astray (contribution 1 attributes Soudry's result to theorem 4.5; "Theorem (6)"; the skew towards $-1$ is written as the inequality of the skew towards $+1$); there is a single run, with a fixed seed.

**How the work will be cited** ("grokking is proved for logistic regression; depth is not needed") — more precisely: with data separable through the origin, a knowingly superfluous learnable bias and a test sample squeezed into a band $\alpha\gamma$ near the boundary, the delay arises and decomposes into three phases; on the training distribution itself there is none, and the quantitative prediction of the delay time is not checked against the experiment.
