## Concepts covered

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — the work **builds on** the theory of Varma et al. by adding a second axis to it. In Varma the efficiency of the memorising circuit fell as the training set grew while that of the generalising one stayed constant, whence the critical dataset size $D_{crit}$ ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)). Here the same reasoning is carried out along the model size: the critical size decreases as the model grows while the memorisation capacity grows, and the two curves intersect ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). What the work does not contain: a measurement of the efficiency itself — no circuit is isolated anywhere here, and all the discrimination proceeds by validation quality ([`p3-7`](#p3-7)).

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the notion acquires a dependence on the model: $D_{crit}$ becomes $D_{crit}^{M}$, a function of the network size. A model with a hidden size of 64 groks at 3000 examples, one with a hidden size of 32 only at 4000 ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). This is the first of the frame's two assumptions, declared confirmed by experiment.

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — a second quantity of the same kind is introduced: the **memorisation capacity** $D_{mem}^{M}$, measured directly — by training on random labels over all $12{,}769$ pairs, with identical labels for $(a+b)$ and $(b+a)$ so that commutativity does not inflate the result ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)). The capacity grows with the model size, and the seed-to-seed spread is small.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work turns grokking from a phenomenon into **one of four regions** on the plane "model size × data volume" ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)). Its neighbours on the plane are *ungrokking* (less data than $D_{crit}$), *semi-grokking* (data around $D_{crit}$) and a new *progression* (more data than the model is able to memorise). Grokking thereby ceases to be anything special: it occupies the band between the memorisation capacity and the critical size.

**[Ungrokking](../../concepts/ungrokking.md)** and **[semi-grokking](../../concepts/semi-grokking.md)** — both notions of Varma et al. are reproduced here and placed on the same diagram ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). For semi-grokking a measurement that Varma did not have is added: the interval in which it is observed spans about 1000 training points and, by the authors' observation, is the same for all the models considered ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)).

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — the work's main result on this notion: double descent is explained as a **sequence of four regions** through which a model passes at a fixed data volume below the intersection point — progression, ungrokking, semi-grokking, grokking ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)). Hence two checkable predictions. The first: at 2600 examples there is a dip (ungrokking at hidden sizes 24–40), at 4000 and 5000 there is none ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). The second: by making the task harder, to $(a+b)^{2}\bmod P$, one can raise the $D_{crit}$ curve and obtain a dip where there was none ([`p7-4`](#p7-4), [`p7-7`](#p7-7)).

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — the work **manufactures** an emergent ability instead of observing one: mixing a pure memorisation task (random labels on subtraction) into the modular addition pushes the appearance of the generalisation out to a model about 1570 times larger ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)). From this a hypothesis about LLM pre-training as multi-task learning is drawn ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)). What the work does not contain: a check on language models — the whole construction is built on modular addition.

**[Sparse subnetworks](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — appendix B sets up a proof by contradiction: the feed-forward layer is divided into two parts by the intermediate dimension (in the spirit of an MoE), one processing the algorithmic data and the other the memorisation data. The model size at which the ability appears falls sharply ([`p12-2`](#p12-2)). This is an argument that the matter really is one of competition for parameters.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the parameter norm serves here as a **discriminating indicator** rather than as a mechanism: *grokking* and *progression* give identical-looking quality curves, but under grokking the parameter norm falls while under progression it grows ([`p5-5`](#p5-5), [`fig-9`](#fig-9)). This is the only way the work distinguishes the two dynamics apart from the level of training accuracy at the moment of generalisation.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the setting is standard (addition modulo 113, a single-layer decoder-only transformer with 4 heads, AdamW), but the range is unusually wide: hidden sizes from 4 to 100 and from 2000 to 5000 training points, with 11 seeds per configuration ([`p3-8`](#p3-8)). The second task — $(a+b)^{2}\bmod P$ — is introduced as knowingly harder to generalise at the same memorisation capacity.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — mentioned as a condition of the setting: AdamW is chosen precisely because weight decay markedly affects grokking ([`p3-8`](#p3-8)). The work gives no separate analysis of its role — it inherits it from the theory of circuit efficiency.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** and **[representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — both are named in the survey among the explanations of grokking and are not built into the frame ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)): the work picks one line — the competition of circuits — and builds only on it.

A word should be said separately about the name *progression*. This is a **new** notion: a dynamics in which there are more training data than the model is able to memorise, and in which the generalisation forms **before** a perfect training accuracy, because the cross-entropy loss cannot be reduced otherwise ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)). Such a dynamics had no name in the corpus until now, and what distinguishes it from grokking is not the shape of the curve but the cause: not the efficiency of a circuit but the impossibility of memorising everything.

Cards of corpus papers: [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — the theory this work extends along a second axis; [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original task; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the way of isolating the generalising circuit, which the authors deliberately do not use; [Merrill et al. 2023](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md) — the competition of a sparse and a dense subnetwork, the nearest relative of appendix B; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — the phase diagram in hyperparameter space, to which this diagram is closest in conception.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work rests on two empirical dependences and one picture derived from them; everything else is consequences.

**Both supporting dependences are measured.** The critical dataset size falls with the model size ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)), the memorisation capacity grows ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)). The second is measured directly and carefully — with random labels and a correction for commutativity. The first is obtained from a grid of "dataset size × hidden size" with 11 seeds per point. Both curves are then drawn on one plot, and **the point of their intersection** is already a construction rather than a measurement: where exactly it lies the work does not determine.

**The prediction about double descent is a genuine prediction.** From the frame it follows that a dip will arise at small datasets and disappear at large ones; the check confirms this — 2600 gives a dip to zero, 4000 and 5000 give nothing of the kind ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). The second prediction is stronger: if the $D_{crit}$ curve is raised by making the generalisation harder, a dip will appear where there was none. The task $(a+b)^{2}\bmod P$ does indeed give zero quality at a hidden size of 24, which at the same data volume on addition it did not ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)).

**No circuits are measured in the work.** The whole frame speaks of a competition between memorising and generalising circuits, yet not one circuit is isolated: the method of Nanda et al. is mentioned and declined "for simplicity", and the discrimination proceeds by validation quality ([`p3-7`](#p3-7)). "Circuit" here is therefore a language of description rather than an observed quantity; strictly speaking, what is shown is the behaviour of the quality, not the behaviour of the circuits.

**Progression is distinguished from grokking by one indicator.** Their quality curves look alike, and all the discrimination rests on two things: the level of training accuracy at the moment of generalisation and the sign of the change of the parameter norm ([`p5-5`](#p5-5), [`fig-9`](#fig-9)). The norm is measured, moreover, on a single pair of configurations (hidden size 8 against 64 at 3000 points), and figure 9 shows individual runs rather than statistics.

**The emergence is produced, but its magnitude is an artefact of the setting.** The number "1570 times larger" is obtained by comparing the model size at which the ability appears in the multi-task setting with the size in the single-task one ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)). It depends on which memorisation task was mixed in and on how much of its data there was; the authors note that the volume of memorisation data affects the threshold only weakly, but they tried no other memorisation tasks. The carry-over of the conclusion to LLM pre-training ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)) is an analogy, and the work presents it as such.

**The limitation is named by the authors themselves.** The whole investigation is on algorithmic tasks; an extension to realistic tasks and models is declared necessary for further work. To this should be added what the work does not contain: not one of the four regions is checked outside modular arithmetic, and the third phenomenon — the emergent abilities of LLMs — is mentioned but measured on no language model.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the corpus's only work uniting grokking, double descent and emergence within **one** construction. The price of the unification is the same as for any frame of the kind: the entities (circuit efficiency, memorisation capacity, critical size) are taken from somebody else's theory and are not rechecked, only their consequences are. With Merrill et al., in which the sparse and the dense subnetworks are isolated and measured, a mutual complementarity results: there it is shown that competing subnetworks exist, here that from their competition one can derive the shape of the quality curve in the model size.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2309-02390
**Varma et al. (2309.02390) — "ungrokking" applied to training from scratch, and semi-grokking as a stable state.** The paper leans on the circuit-efficiency theory more heavily than any other in the corpus, and both inaccuracies are at its edges. (1) `"Insufficient data boosts memorization efficiency over generalization, causing model to choose pure memorization without generalization, which is consistent with *ungrokking* stated by Varma et al. (2023)"`: in Varma ungrokking is a regression [of an already grokked network under fine-tuning on a small dataset](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md#p2-1); training from scratch in which the generalisation never sets in is not ungrokking but its absence. The regime thesis of the theory does correspond — the error is precisely in the term. (2) `"This balance prevents the model from fully transitioning from memorization to generalization, resulting in *semi-grokking*"`: Varma's caveats are dropped — that [semi-grokking does not follow strictly from their theory and is probably transient](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md#sec-5-3) — many runs would have grokked fully under longer training. The attribution of priority for the terms is meanwhile correct.
