## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the corpus its first proof of grokking in a neural-network setting and, with it, an exact mechanism: grokking arises from a "dichotomy" of early and late implicit biases — a large initialisation sets an early inclination towards a kernel predictor, a small non-zero weight decay sets a late inclination towards the minimum-norm / maximum-margin solution, and between them lies a provably sharp transition ([p. 2, para. 2](#p2-2)). Both gaps in the predecessors are named outright: "No prior work has rigorously proved grokking in a neural network setting" and "No prior work has provided a quantitative explanation as to why the transition from memorization to generalization is often sharp" ([p. 1, para. 6](#p1-6)). What the work does not contain: grokking is proved only on two *constructed* examples — sparse linear classification by diagonal linear networks and overparameterised matrix completion; for modular addition, the only task from the grokking literature shown in the paper, nothing is proved ([fig. 2](#fig-2) is a reproduction, not a theorem). Nor is there an analysis of how to remove the delay: "our work focuses on understanding the cause of grokking but does not study how to make neural nets generalize without so much delay in time" ([p. 9, para. 9](#p9-9)).

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the work gives this transition rigorous quantitative bounds on both sides: up to the time $\frac{1-c}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ the predictor after normalisation converges to the maximum-$L^{2}$-margin linear classifier on the NTK features (theorem 3.4, [p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1)), and by the time $\frac{1+c}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ there is a time at which the direction of the parameters lies along a KKT point of the margin-maximisation problem (theorem 3.5, [p. 6, para. 7](#p6-7)). The authors' terms are *kernel regime* and *rich regime*, taken from Moroshko et al. 2020, rather than *lazy*/*rich*: the word "lazy" appears in the paper only in the title of the work of Chizat et al. and in a retelling of the concurrent work of Kumar et al. ([p. 16, para. 4](#p16-4)), in which the similarity of conclusions is acknowledged and the difference is claimed as "rigorous analyses for both lazy and rich regimes with quantitative bounds". What the work does not contain: the late side of both theorems yields only a KKT point rather than a global optimum, and the authors stipulate themselves that for non-convex $f_{i}$ this is only a first-order necessary condition ([p. 6, para. 8](#p6-8)); the statements about the late regime take the form "there exists a sequence of times $t_{k}$ within the window" rather than "for all sufficiently large $t$".

**[The neural tangent kernel (NTK)](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the kernel regime *under weight decay* is declared a result in its own right, of interest beyond the grokking problem, and the difference from an ordinary NTK analysis is named outright: there the norm changes little, here it changes by orders of magnitude, and the kernel regime holds on account of a large multiplier ([p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)). The technical finding on which it all rests: under weight decay ${\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t;\alpha\bar{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_ {\mathrm{init}})\approx\alpha e^{-\lambda t}\bar{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_ {\mathrm{init}}$ — the norm falls exponentially while the direction stays close to the initial one, and "remoteness" is measured not by the absolute displacement $\Vert{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(0)\Vert_{2}$, as in existing analyses, but by the normalised quantity $\Vert\frac{e^{\lambda t}}{\alpha}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)-\bar{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_ {\mathrm{init}}\Vert_{2}$ ([p. 6, para. 3](#p6-3)). What the work does not contain: any measurement of the empirical NTK, any width limits and any appeal to infinite width whatever — an "NTK feature" here is defined as $\nabla f(\bar{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{init}};{\boldsymbol{x}})$ at a finite model, and the asymptotics runs in the initialisation scale $\alpha$ rather than in the width.

**[Margin maximisation / implicit bias](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — this is the primary source of the very phrase "dichotomy of early- and late-phase implicit biases", which the concept card so far quotes at second hand, through Mohamadi et al. 2024. The late bias is set by two problems: margin maximisation (R1) for classification and norm minimisation (R2) for regression; in the diagonal linear case the global optimum of (R1) equals the maximum-$L^{1}$-margin classifier, and "our theory suggests a sharp transition at time $\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ from max $L^{2}$-margin linear classifier to max $L^{1}$-margin linear classifier" ([p. 7, para. 5](#p7-5)). What the work does not contain: a proof of global optimality — for diagonal networks the authors merely "believe one may be able to" ("we believe that one may be able to obtain the global optimality", [p. 7, para. 6](#p7-6)), and for matrix completion globality is replaced by the "positive evidence" of theorem 3.12 ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4)).

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work gives the notion a rigorous form on the regression side: the late bias is a KKT point of the problem $\min\frac{1}{2}\Vert{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\Vert_{2}^{2}$ subject to $f_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})=y_{i}$ (R2, theorem 3.10), and in matrix completion the global optimum of that problem is the minimum-nuclear-norm solution ([p. 9, para. 3](#p9-3)). It matters that the norm falls neither gradually nor "along a zero-loss manifold": in the kernel phase it falls exponentially in time, $\Vert{\boldsymbol{\theta}}(t)\Vert_{2}\approx\alpha e^{-\lambda t}$, at a nearly unchanging direction, and the whole turn of the direction fits into a narrow window around $\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$. The norm is placed on the second axis of all four illustrations, and in them one can see that under weight decay the sharp test transition coincides with a collapse of the norm ([fig. 1](#fig-1), [fig. 3](#fig-3)), and without weight decay, on the contrary, with its monotone growth by several orders of magnitude ([fig. 4](#fig-4)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of those curves — the norm is plotted as an illustration, and no threshold, no law and no measure is built on it, nor does the language of a "zero-loss manifold" appear in the paper.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work gives weight decay the role of the source of the late bias and derives from it the quantitative transition time $t^{ * }=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$: inversely proportional to $\lambda$ and logarithmic in the initialisation scale. An experiment on modular addition confirms the direction of the shift on both sides and adds a price: "when weight decay surpasses a small threshold, the regularization strength can become so strong that it even hinders the optimization of the training loss and causes the training accuracy to collapse eventually", which the authors note as consistent with Lewkowycz and Gur-Ari 2020 ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1)). What the work does not contain: a quantitative check of the law itself — only the direction of the shift is shown, and no fit of a coefficient or a slope is given; the whole theory is conducted for a gradient flow on $\mathcal{L}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\Vert{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\Vert_{2}^{2}$, that is, for $L^{2}$ regularisation rather than for the decoupled AdamW variant.

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work gives the corpus a rare answer of "not necessary, but necessary for *sharpness*". Appendix B.1 poses the question outright and answers it by experiment: without weight decay, on the same modular addition, grokking remains, but "the transition is no longer sharp" — the test creeps from ~0 % to 100 % as the time grows from $10^{10^{2}}$ to $10^{10^{4}}$ ([p. 18, para. 4](#p18-4)), and the slowness is explained by the known convergence rate to the maximum margin, $\mathcal{O}(\frac{\log\log t}{\log t})$, for linear logistic regression ([p. 19, para. 1](#p19-1)). A second possible source of the late bias is a reduction of sharpness (label-noise SGD, 1-SAM): by the result of Li et al. 2022 the transition there is sharp only on a logarithmic time scale, "less sharp than our main results" ([p. 19, para. 6](#p19-6)). A detail of substance for the corpus that the authors leave undiscussed: in [fig. 4](#fig-4) the generalisation without weight decay comes at a *growing* parameter norm, whereas in every run with weight decay it coincides with the norm's collapse. What the work does not contain: a claim that a large initialisation and a small weight decay are necessary — on the contrary, the conclusion says that "these may not be the only source of the dichotomy of the implicit biases" ([p. 9, para. 9](#p9-9)).

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the work turns the scale into a control parameter with a formula: the transition time grows as $\log\alpha$, and the experiment shows the sharp transition being postponed as $\alpha$ grows from $1$ to $100$ ([p. 3, para. 7](#p3-7)). A caveat about the construction of the experiment matters too: at a large initialisation the model outputs a very large random number on every example, which gives a training instability, and to remove that confound the second layer's weights are zeroed — the same requirement stands in the theory as assumption 3.2 about a zero output at initialisation. What the work does not contain: a claim that the *standard* scale is already large — $\alpha$ here multiplies the He initialisation, and the baseline run is at $\alpha=1$; nor is there an analysis of the initialisation distribution (the symmetrised initialisation of Chizat et al. and the "difference trick" of Hu et al. are merely named as equivalent ways of zeroing the output).

**[The Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — the work rejects the explanation through a Goldilocks zone and builds its own in its place. The argument is homogeneity: in MLPs and CNNs with ReLU the output is homogeneous in the parameters, $f(c{\boldsymbol{\theta}};{\boldsymbol{x}})=c^{L}f({\boldsymbol{\theta}};{\boldsymbol{x}})$, "as a result, it is impossible to explain the effect of norm just from the expressive power, since all classifiers that can be represented with a certain norm can also be represented by all other norms" ([p. 4, para. 2](#p4-2)). It is separately noted that Fort and Scherlis 2019 and Liu et al. 2023 "did not provide much explanation on why such a narrow Goldilocks zone could exist in the first place". What the work does not contain: a refutation of the observation about the zone itself — the recipe "a large initialisation plus a small non-zero weight decay" is taken from Liu et al. 2023 entire and serves as the starting point; what is rejected is only the explanation through the norm.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work gives the corpus the first *quantitative* definition of sharpness: changing the multiplier from $(1-c)$ to $(1+c)$ at the same $\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ suffices for the implicit bias to change entirely, and this is declared the second of the two contributions against the predecessors' blurred transitions ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)). An essential limitation: the sharpness is a property of the limit $\alpha\to+\infty$ at a coupled weight decay $\lambda(\alpha)=\Theta(\alpha^{-p})$, and there are no bounds at particular finite $\alpha$ and $\lambda$ in the work. What the work does not contain: the language of phase transitions as a physical theory — no order parameter, no critical exponents and no thermodynamic limit; the phrase "phase transition" occurs in the authors twice and both times in a retelling of others — the slingshot of Thilak et al. and the HMM analysis of Hu et al. — while the authors' own usage is confined to "early phase" and "late phase".

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the work takes modular addition at $p=97$ as a motivating experiment and deliberately simplifies the setting "without involving too many confounding factors": a two-layer ReLU network of width $h=1024$ on a concatenation of one-hot representations, full-batch GD, 40 % of the data in training, a step of $0.002$ and a decay of $10^{-4}$ ([p. 3, para. 4](#p3-4)). The reproduction succeeds: the training accuracy reaches 100 % within $10^{6}$ steps, and the test accuracy jumps an order of magnitude later. What the work does not contain: no theory whatever for this task — it is proved neither that the max-margin solution for modular addition generalises nor that the NTK solution does not; the proofs concern only the two constructed examples, and modular addition serves as an illustration of the levers $\alpha$ and $\lambda$.

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — the work derives the sample-complexity gap from which grokking follows in the first example: on $k$-sparse linear classification the maximum-$L^{1}$-margin classifier needs $\mathcal{O}(k^{2}\log d)$ examples and the maximum-$L^{2}$-margin one $\mathcal{O}(kd)$ ([p. 7, para. 7](#p7-7)); both estimates are obtained through Rademacher complexity (theorems E.4 and E.5). What the work does not contain: a lower bound for the maximum-$L^{2}$-margin classifier — both estimates are upper bounds on the error, so what is strictly shown is not "the kernel solution does not generalise" but "its guarantee is weaker". On the regression side, by contrast, the non-generalisation of the kernel solution is shown explicitly and with no bounds at all: in the kernel regime all the unobserved entries of the matrix are filled with zeros ([p. 9, para. 2](#p9-2)).

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives the plateau an exact content: it is not "nothing is happening" but a full fit to the training set by a *kernel* predictor whose form is written out in closed form (the maximum $L^{2}$ margin on the NTK features in classification, the minimum-norm kernel solution in regression). The length of the plateau is meanwhile set by the formula $\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ rather than measured. What the work does not contain: the notion of "memorisation" as a separate mechanism — the word "memorization" is used descriptively, in a retelling of Power et al. and in the name of the transition "from memorization to generalization", and no measure of memorisation is introduced.

**[Ungrokking](../../concepts/ungrokking.md)** — the work introduces a related but differently constructed phenomenon, which it calls *misgrokking*: by inverting the condition a dataset is constructed that is separable with a large $L^{2}$ margin ($\gamma=25$, $n=32$), on which the network first attains 100 % accuracy on both training and test and, after long training, sees the test fall almost to 50 % ([p. 7, para. 8](#p7-8), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). The difference from the ungrokking of Varma et al. matters: there the regression is caused by shrinking the training set after the grokking, here by the late bias itself not matching the data, at an unchanged sample. Its value for the corpus is an argument against the reading "grokking is a transition to a simpler solution": the direction of the transition is set not by simplicity but by the agreement of the late bias with the data. What the work does not contain: a comparison of misgrokking with ungrokking and semi-grokking — both terms of Varma et al. are merely named in the survey of appendix A ([p. 16, para. 1](#p16-1)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work only mentions progress measures, and mentions them critically: a whole subsection of the survey is given over to them, and the conclusion is one — "why progress measures themselves can make progress is still not well understood and requires new insights into the training dynamics, which is the focus of our work" ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)). The work introduces no progress measure and computes none; in the concept card its place is under "Citations".

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)**, **[the slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)**, **[double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** and **[effective theory](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — all four are touched only by the survey of appendix A, and the complaint against all of them is one and the same: "none of these works provides rigorous justification their claims with mathematical analysis for neural net training, while our work is grounded by theoretical analyses of implicit biases in the kernel and rich regimes" ([p. 16, para. 1](#p16-1)). Varma et al. are retold through the parameter norm, Thilak et al. through the cyclic behaviour of the last layer's norm, and Davies et al. through two patterns with different learning speeds and generalisation quality; the effective theory of Liu et al. 2022 is analysed separately, in the subsection on dynamics ([p. 16, para. 3](#p16-3)), and reduced to a slow learning rate of the input embeddings. None of these readings is checked or refuted by experiment in the work.

**[Behavioural versus true grokking](../../concepts/behavioral-vs-true-grokking.md)** — the work sets out a two-pattern frame in which one pattern is learnt faster and generalises worse and the other more slowly and better ([`p16-1`](#p16-1)). The frame describes a distinction but itself requires a definition of "pattern", failing which it predicts nothing.
## Points we could not reconcile

**The $\mathcal{C}^{2}$-smoothness assumption excludes the ReLU on which the whole motivating experiment is run.** Assumption 3.1 requires $L$-homogeneity and $\mathcal{C}^{2}$-smoothness simultaneously, and the authors write themselves: "we also need to assume the $\mathcal{C}^{2}$-smoothness to ease the definition and analysis of gradient flow, which excludes the use of any non-smooth activation" ([p. 4, para. 6](#p4-6)). Yet both section 2 and the whole rhetoric of homogeneity ("when talking about MLPs and CNNs with ReLU activation, which are commonly used in practice", [p. 4, para. 2](#p4-2)) are conducted precisely on ReLU networks. A workaround is named — the Clarke subdifferential — but not carried out: "we choose to make this assumption for simplicity". The practical consequence: theorems 3.4, 3.5, 3.9 and 3.10 are formally inapplicable to the network of formula (1), on which figures 2 and 4 are obtained.

**The theory's two levers are coupled, while in the experiment they are turned separately.** The asymptotics is everywhere conducted at $\alpha\to+\infty$ with the decay tied to the scale as $\lambda(\alpha)=\Theta(\alpha^{-p})$ ([p. 4, para. 7](#p4-7)): $\alpha$ grows and $\lambda$ falls simultaneously. In the experiments of section 2, on the contrary, exactly one lever is varied with the other fixed — figure 2 (b) varies $\alpha$ at $\lambda=10^{-4}$, and figure 2 (c) varies $\lambda$ at a He initialisation. It is therefore impossible to separate within the theory which of the two creates the sharpness, and the prediction $t^{ * }=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ is checked experimentally in a setting to which the limit does not correspond.

**A reference to an equation in the proof of theorem E.4 points elsewhere.** The proof ends with the words "Combining this with (41) completes the proof", whereas the inequality derived two lines above and needed here is (39); (41) belongs to the proof of the following theorem E.5, where the same sentence stands correctly. An editing slip, but it derails a reader checking the computation.

**"For any all constants".** The statement of theorem 3.4 opens with a doubled quantifier — "For any all constants $c\in(0,1)$" ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1)); the parallel theorem 3.9 has "For all constants $c\in(0,1)$". The sense is unambiguous, but this is worth knowing when quoting the statement verbatim.

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

The work falls into layers of differing solidity, and the price of a quotation depends on the layer.

**Proved.** The early side of both pairs of theorems (3.4 for classification, 3.9 for regression) — finished statements: at the time $\frac{1-c}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ the predictor converges to the kernel solution as $\alpha\to+\infty$. The kernel regime under weight decay as such is proved too — a result declared to stand on its own ([p. 2, para. 5](#p2-5)). Both Rademacher upper bounds (E.4, E.5) are proved, from which the gap $\mathcal{O}(k^{2}\log d)$ against $\mathcal{O}(kd)$ is taken. And corollary 3.11 is proved: in the kernel regime matrix completion fills every unobserved entry with zeros.

**Proved at a price that must be named.** The late side (theorems 3.5 and 3.10) yields a KKT point rather than a global optimum, and this is admitted outright: "the KKT condition is a first-order necessary condition for global optimality, but it is not sufficient in general since $f_{i}({\boldsymbol{\theta}})$ can be highly non-convex" ([p. 6, para. 8](#p6-8)). The form of the statement is weaker than it reads too: not "for all sufficiently large $t$" but "there exists a sequence of times $t_{k}$ in the window $[\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha_{k},\frac{1+c}{\lambda}\log\alpha_{k}]$, every limit point of which lies along a KKT point". All of it lives in the limit $\alpha\to+\infty$ at $\lambda=\Theta(\alpha^{-p})$; the work gives no inequalities at particular finite $\alpha,\lambda$, so the "sharpness" is a property of the limit rather than a measured quantity. What is analysed is a gradient flow rather than the GD, SGD or Adam with which the experiments themselves are run.

**Predicted before the experiment and confirmed.** From $t^{ * }=\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$ it follows that increasing $\alpha$ postpones the transition and increasing $\lambda$ brings it forward — both sides are checked ([fig. 2](#fig-2)). From the sample-complexity gap it follows that there is grokking on $k$-sparse classification — checked at $n=256$, $d=10^{5}$, $k=3$ ([fig. 1](#fig-1) a). The strongest thing here is misgrokking: the phenomenon is constructed by inverting the theory's condition and then observed ([fig. 1](#fig-1) b); this is a prediction that could not have been obtained by starting from the data.

**Declared an argument or evidence.** The global optimality of the KKT point in matrix completion is not proved; theorem 3.12 is called "positive evidence" for it and speaks of the limit $t\to\infty$ ("if the time is not limited around $\frac{1}{\lambda}\log\alpha$"), that is, outside the window about which the rest of the theory speaks ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4)). For diagonal networks globality is left as "we believe that one may be able to obtain the global optimality" ([p. 7, para. 6](#p7-6)).

**Shown by experiment and by experiment only.** That modular addition obeys this picture at all ([fig. 2](#fig-2)); that matrix completion of a multiplication table separates the fitting of the training loss from the jump of the test loss by five orders of magnitude in the number of steps ([fig. 3](#fig-3)); that above a small threshold the decay destroys trainability; and that without weight decay grokking remains but ceases to be sharp ([fig. 4](#fig-4)). Not one of these experiments is accompanied by a number of seeds, a spread or a confidence band: the settings are given in the text, but there is no separate reproducibility section.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. For each one it is said what exactly gets distorted, with links to the authors' paragraphs where the qualification that goes missing still stands. The "Common misreadings and overstatements" sections on the citing side have not been started yet, so there is nothing detailed to address; the citing works are listed as plain links to their cards.

**"A large initialisation and a small weight decay are necessary for grokking" (erroneous).** A citing work retells the paper thus: `"They established that large initialization and small weight decay are essential conditions for the occurrence of the grokking phenomenon."` The phrase "essential conditions" is not in the paper and could not be: the authors formulate a sufficient condition, and the conclusion states the opposite outright — "our work only studies the training dynamics with large initialization and small weight decay, but these may not be the only source of the dichotomy of the implicit biases" ([p. 9, para. 9](#p9-9)) — and a whole appendix B is given over to two other sources of the late bias ([p. 18, para. 1](#p18-1)), with grokking observed at $\lambda=0$ in B.1 ([p. 18, para. 4](#p18-4)). Work concerned: Zhang and Wang 2024, "Exploring Grokking: Experimental and Mechanistic Investigations" (no card as yet).

**"Lyu et al. observed grokking without regularisation at an MSE loss" and "$\alpha$ multiplies the logits" (erroneous).** Prieto et al. 2025 assign the paper to works that showed that "regularization may not be necessary for grokking, at least on shallow networks with Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss (Kumar et al., 2024; Lyu et al., 2024; Gromov, 2023)", and repeat this twice: `"This explains why Barak et al. (2022), Kumar et al. (2024), and Lyu et al. (2024) observed grokking with MSE loss without regularization."` The distribution across settings in the authors is exactly the reverse: the only experiment without weight decay is run on cross-entropy ([p. 18, para. 4](#p18-4)), and the only experiment with a squared loss — the matrix completion of a multiplication table — is run with a decay of $10^{-4}$ ([p. 9, para. 7](#p9-7)). A second distortion in the same work: `"Liu et al. (2023a), Kumar et al. (2024) and Lyu et al. (2024) showed that an $\alpha$ parameter multiplying the logits can increase or reduce the delay in generalization."` In Lyu et al. $\alpha$ multiplies not the logits but the initialisation: the training starts from $\alpha\bar{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}}_{\mathrm{init}}$ ([p. 4, para. 7](#p4-7)), while the output at initialisation is, on the contrary, identically zero by assumption 3.2. Work concerned: [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md).

**"Grokking proved as a lazy→rich transition" (widening, partly refuted).** The corpus's commonest pattern: the work is cited as a proof that grokking is a transition from the lazy regime to the rich one. Liang et al. 2025 — `"Lyu et al. (2023) formalize a transition from \"lazy\" (kernel-like) to \"rich\" feature-learning"`; the authors of "The Norm-Separation Delay Law" — `"prove that homogeneous neural networks trained with large initialisation and small weight decay undergo a sharp transition from a kernel (memorisation) regime to a rich (margin-maximisation) regime, establishing provable grokking in this setting"`; the authors of "Grokking as a Falsifiable Finite-Size Transition" — `"prove that early- and late-phase implicit biases can induce grokking"`. More precisely: for $L$-homogeneous $\mathcal{C}^{2}$-smooth models under a gradient flow with weight decay in the limit $\alpha\to\infty$, a sharp transition from a kernel predictor to a KKT point of the maximum-margin problem is proved; that this transition yields generalisation is shown only on two constructed examples, and on modular addition only by experiment. The authors do not apply the word "lazy" to their own early regime: their terms are taken from Moroshko et al. and read as *kernel regime* and *rich regime*, while the relation to the lazy→rich of Kumar et al. is described as an agreement with a concurrent work ([p. 16, para. 4](#p16-4)). The widened thesis "a large initialisation and a non-zero decay give grokking" has been checked and partly refuted: Notsawo et al. 2025 show that in the overparameterised regime it does not hold and — importantly — point to the cause in the paper's own terms, a violation of assumption 3.2 about a zero output at initialisation: `"Contrary to prior studies (Lyu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a), we find that in the over-parameterized regime ($N<n$), large-scale initialization and non-zero weight decay do not necessarily lead to grokking"`. An exemplary formulation for comparison is in the authors of "To Grok Grokking": `"their technique only guarantees convergence to a KKT (Karush-Kuhn-Tucker) point, which is, in general, not sufficient for arguing global optimality, and their result does not provably imply grokking"`; Miller 2024 is equally careful: `"demonstrating that under idealised conditions, grokking could be provably induced through a sharp transition from an early kernel regime to a rich regime"`. Works concerned: [Liang et al. 2025](../2512.03437.grokked-models-are-better-unlearners/2512.03437.grokked-models-are-better-unlearners.card.md), ["The Norm-Separation Delay Law of Grokking"](../2603.13331.the-norm-separation-delay-law-of-grokking-a-first-principles-theory-of-delayed-generalization/2603.13331.the-norm-separation-delay-law-of-grokking-a-first-principles-theory-of-delayed-generalization.card.md), ["Grokking as a Falsifiable Finite-Size Transition"](../2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition/2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition.card.md); the refuting one — [Notsawo et al. 2025](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.card.md); the exemplary ones — ["To Grok Grokking"](../2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression/2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression.card.md), [Miller 2024](../2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity/2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity.card.md).

**"It is shown that the standard initialisation scale is large and therefore delays generalisation" (widening).** Kim et al. 2025 combine four works: `"A series of works (Liu et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2024; Lyu et al., 2024; Mohamadi et al., 2024) found that the default initialization scale is relatively large and causes generalization delay."` In Lyu et al. there is no claim about a "standard" scale: the baseline run is at $\alpha=1$, that is, at an ordinary He initialisation, and the delay is demonstrated by a deliberate multiplication by $\alpha$ up to $100$ ([p. 3, para. 7](#p3-7)). Work concerned: [Kim et al. 2025](../2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model/2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model.card.md).

Milder echoes not warranting separate analysis: the assignment of the work to "explanations through the NTK" on a par with Kumar et al. ([Miller 2024](../2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity/2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity.card.md)) and to "converging" explanations through a collapse of representations into low-dimensional structured manifolds ([Hwang et al. 2026](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md)) — there is no analysis of representations in the paper at all.
