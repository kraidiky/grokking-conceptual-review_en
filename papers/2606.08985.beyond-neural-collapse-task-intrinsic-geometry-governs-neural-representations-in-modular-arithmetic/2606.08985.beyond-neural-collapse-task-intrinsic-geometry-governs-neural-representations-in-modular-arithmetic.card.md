# Tan, Gai & Zhang 2026 — Beyond Neural Collapse: Task-Intrinsic Geometry Governs Neural Representations in Modular Arithmetic

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Hu Tan**, **Shihua Zhang** (Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, CAS; University of CAS), **Kuo Gai** (SIMIS, Shanghai).

arXiv [2606.08985](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08985), June 2026, 22 pages, 1 figure. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 1, in the corpus — 1. The second work by the same authors in the corpus (a continuation of the "two clocks" of 2606.05863): modular addition is declared not a refutation of neural collapse but a different, "task-structured low-rank" class of problems. Three moves: (1) a layer-wise account of "the separating layer first" — as soon as the classifier collapses into a rank-2 plane, all the task updates of the embeddings are locked into that same plane (Prop 5.4), while the orthogonal parts are damped by weight decay; (2) the in-plane dynamics is read as a transport with an entropic penalty on $S^{1}$, whose minima are the single-frequency characters of $\mathbb{Z}/P\mathbb{Z}$ (Thm 5.6, through the DFT and Parseval); (3) the trade: a simplex ETF wins only $O(1)$ in the cross-entropy, while a cyclic rank-2 code wins $\Theta(K)$ in the complexity penalty, whence the threshold $\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}=\Theta(1/K)$, which at $P=97$ is $\approx 2.9\cdot 10^{-3}$.

## Concepts covered

**[Neural collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — the geometric prediction NC2 (a simplex ETF in $K-1$ dimensions) is contested as the standard for modular addition: the right yardstick is declared to be "the best separation compatible with the symmetry of the task under the regularisation induced by the training" ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)); quantitatively, the ETF wins only $O(1)=\log I_{0}(1/\tilde{\tau})$ in the cross-entropy and loses $\Theta(K)$ in the Schatten/nuclear penalty ([`p12-6`](#p12-6)). The analysis: exactly two pre-chosen configurations with unit columns are compared; the corpus's works on NC in grokking (2509.20829, 2509.17738) are not cited; and the Frobenius penalty of the construction of §5 does not distinguish the two geometries at all — the gain requires a factorisation that is absent from the setting.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — redescribed as a "hierarchical event": first the separating layer finds the right low-rank code, then the embeddings line up under it, and only after that does generalisation become possible ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)). The analysis: about the length of the delay the theory is silent (the whole analysis concerns the terminal phase), there is no experimental section in the work at all — no optimiser, no data fraction, not a single training curve — and the main premise "the classifier before the embeddings" is taken from other people's works with a caveat about the lack of synchrony.

**[Fourier features](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the circular Fourier code here is not found by reverse engineering but **derived** from the training objective: after the locking and the homogeneity of the radii the problem becomes a phase problem, the DFT diagonalises the convolution, exactly one Fourier line interacts with an equiangular code, and Parseval forces all the spectral mass onto it — the minima are the characters $\theta_{i}=\theta_{0}+\frac{4\pi m}{P}i$ ([`p9-8`](#p9-8)). The analysis: the conditions of the theorem (equal radii, a common $\beta$) are visibly violated in the single figure, and panel B is built after a renumbering by the very automorphism that the theory predicts.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — a double role: in the locking of the subspace the orthogonal part of the embeddings is damped by a gradient of exactly $2\lambda\mathbf{e}_{i}^{\perp}$ ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)), while in the selection of the geometry a factorised weight decay is equivalent to the nuclear norm and gives the threshold $\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}\approx 2.86\times 10^{-3}$ at $K=97$ — the most checkable place in the work, directly comparable with the weight-decay values in common use across the corpus ([`p12-2`](#p12-2)). The analysis: the construction of §5 and the trade of §6 lean on different penalties.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the principle "geometry as algorithm": the network realises algorithms by embodying an algebraic structure in a geometry rather than by memorising labels ([`p13-4`](#p13-4)); and the chain "rank-2 at the separating layer → the two-dimensionality of the motion of the embeddings → the phase alignment → a character of the group" is a short geometric explanation of why the embeddings move within the plane of the separating layer ([`p7-6`](#p7-6)).

**[Selective weight decay](../../concepts/selective-weight-decay.md)** — the layer-wise form of the penalty acquires an exact meaning: a Frobenius decay on the factors $U,V$, after the factorisation is eliminated, equals the nuclear norm of the product ([`p11-7`](#p11-7)). Hence the selection between the geometries too — the cyclic one beats the simplex one in that norm.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — a coherent derivational chain and a cheap threshold.** The locking of the subspace (5.3)–(5.4) is a short exact explanation of the two-dimensionality of the motion of the embeddings; the character is derived from the Fourier concentration rather than reverse-engineered; the estimate of the $P$-periodic remainder through Bessel functions is written out carefully; and the threshold $\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}\approx 2.9\cdot 10^{-3}$ at $P=97$ is a cheap refutable prediction.

**There is no experimental section.** The only experimental object is figure 1; the optimiser, the learning rate, the weight decay, the data fraction, the steps, the seeds and the curves are not given; and there is no code — under the words "in our experiments". An "effective rank of 2" is declared empirical with no definition and no measured value. Panel B is built after a renumbering of the labels by an automorphism — the very transformation $i\mapsto mi$ that the theory predicts; the $m$ that was found and the picture before the renumbering are not shown, so the panel does not work as an independent check.

**The comparison is of two pre-chosen configurations.** There is no optimisation over all admissible geometries; "the cyclic code is globally preferable" means "preferable relative to the simplex ETF". Both configurations are taken with unit columns (the scale fixed by hand), whereas under a free norm both differences and the threshold depend on the scale. A conflict of penalties: the construction of §5 uses the Frobenius $\lambda\|W\|_{F}^{2}$, which at unit columns does not distinguish the two geometries ($\|W\|_{F}^{2}=K$ for both); the whole $\Theta(K)$ gain requires $q<2$, that is, a factorisation $W=UV^{\top}$, which the two-layer ReLU setting of §4 plainly does not have.

**Gaps in the proofs and in the premises.** The "sandwich" inequality of step 1 in A.2 is written out two-sidedly with no condition on the sign of the difference — it cannot be summed over $k$ in the general case, and the carry-over of the optimality from the pairwise energy to the logarithmic potential remains unproved. The additive model of the feature is "a description of the terminal class" with a remainder discarded without an estimate. The premise of separated time scales is taken from the authors' own work 2606.05863 — there is no independent support.

**Missing neighbours.** Not cited: the corpus's NC works on grokking (2509.20829, 2509.17738), the two nearest rivals by type of result — 2509.21519 (a Fourier solution from the dynamics) and 2511.01938 (the circle from a constrained norm minimisation) — as well as 2505.18266 (approximate cosets) and 2603.01968 (a three-era dynamics measured in time).

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that modular addition prefers the circle to neural collapse") — more precisely: under an assumed terminal alignment and fixed unit norms, two configurations are compared under a surrogate penalty and it is shown that at $\lambda>\Theta(1/K)$ the cyclic of the two is the more advantageous; not a single training run is carried out in the work.

###### p3-1

In this paper, we show that modular addition belongs to a distinct *task-structured low-rank regime*. The correct theoretical benchmark is not *maximal separation at any dimension*, but rather *best separation compatible with the task symmetry under the regularization actually induced during training*. Our main claim is that the network first organizes the last-layer weights into a rank-2 equiangular code, then pulls upstream embeddings into the same plane through backpropagated gradients, and finally phase-locks those embeddings into a cyclic character orbit. This perspective also clarifies how transport and geodesic language should enter the story: we do *not* need to claim that the full parameter trajectory is a Wasserstein geodesic; rather, after projection to the terminal classifier plane, the induced angular dynamics are naturally described by an entropy-regularized transport problem on the circle.

We make four contributions. **(1) Layerwise heterogeneity and classifier-first geometry.** We formalize a *classifier first, embeddings follow* mechanism: once the classifier collapses to a rank-2 plane, every task gradient that reaches the embeddings lies in that same plane, while orthogonal embedding components are damped by weight decay or implicit regularization. **(2) Geometric optimality of the circular codebook.** Within the two-dimensional terminal regime, we prove that the cross-entropy objective is uniquely minimized by an equiangular configuration of classifier directions. **(3) From alignment to characters.** Combining the additive feature model, an angular optimal-transport reformulation, and Fourier concentration, we show that embeddings aligned with the classifier must arrange as equal-angle points on a circle, i.e., as characters of the cyclic group. **(4) Why this is not NC.** We derive an explicit trade-off showing that the simplex ETF gains only an $O(1)$ advantage in cross-entropy, whereas the cyclic rank-2 solution gains $\Theta(K)$ in regularization cost, yielding a sharp threshold $\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}=\Theta(1/K)$ above which the cyclic solution is globally preferred.

###### p3-3

Together, these results refine the status of NC. Modular addition is not a counterexample in which optimization somehow fails to reach the *right* high-dimensional configuration; it is a structured regime in which the optimal representation is genuinely low-rank because the task itself has a low-dimensional algebraic generator. The sudden emergence of the circle is therefore best understood as a hierarchical grokking event: first the classifier discovers the correct low-rank code, then the embeddings align to it, and only then does perfect generalization become possible.

###### p7-4

*Consequently, once the classifier matrix $W$ has effective rank $2$, all task-relevant embedding updates are confined to the same two-dimensional subspace $A^{\top}\operatorname{span}(W)$.*

###### p7-5

*Proof idea.* The gradient formula in (5.3) is standard for softmax cross-entropy, and (5.4) follows by the chain rule through the local additive map $\mathbf{h}_{ij}\approx A(\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j})$. Appendix A.4 also shows how explicit weight decay removes embedding components orthogonal to this task-supported plane.

###### p7-6

This is the sense in which modular-addition grokking is *hierarchical*. The classifier and embeddings remain coupled throughout training, but they are not driven with equal strength. Dense gradients on the last layer can organize the classifier early, whereas the embeddings receive weaker, token-dependent signals filtered through the current classifier. Once the classifier plane forms, the embedding dynamics no longer explore the ambient space freely; they relax inside a plane that has already been selected downstream.

###### p9-8

*Proof idea.* When the correction term in (5.6) vanishes, the dominant loss becomes a phase-matching objective in the half-angles $\theta_{i}/2$. Writing $z_{i}=e^{i\theta_{i}/2}$ turns the total energy into a convolutional inner product against the target code $u_{t}=e^{i\varphi_{t}}$. The discrete Fourier transform diagonalizes this convolution; only one Fourier line can interact with the equiangular codebook, and Parseval forces the optimum to place all spectral mass on that line. Appendix B.3 spells out the reduction and the resulting character form.

###### p11-7

*Assume the effective classifier matrix is realized as $W=UV^{\top}$ and training uses layerwise Frobenius decay $\tfrac{\lambda}{2}(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{F}^{2})$. Then eliminating the factorization induces the variational penalty*

$$
\inf_{W=UV^{\top}}\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{F}^{2}+\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{F}^{2}\bigr)=\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{*}.
$$

*Therefore, explicit factorized weight decay corresponds to the nuclear norm, i.e. the case $q=1$. For the two candidate geometries,*

$$
\left\lVert W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\right\rVert_{*}=\sqrt{2K},\qquad\left\lVert W_{\mathrm{ETF}}\right\rVert_{*}=\sqrt{K(K-1)},
$$

*and hence the cyclic code again enjoys a $\Theta(K)$ regularization advantage.*

*Proof idea.* The variational characterization of the nuclear norm is obtained by taking an SVD of $W$ and balancing the square roots of the singular values across the two factors. Appending the exact singular-value formulae from Theorem 6.4 then gives the stated expressions. Appendix C.3 shows the details.

###### p12-2

*Take the experimentally relevant setting $K=97$, $q=1$, and $\tilde{\tau}=1$. Since $q=1$ coincides with the nuclear-norm surrogate induced by factorized weight decay, Proposition 6.5 gives*

$$
\Delta_{\mathrm{CE}} \approx\log I_{0}(1)\approx\log(1.2661)\approx 0.236, \\
\left\lVert W_{\mathrm{cyc}}\right\rVert_{*} =\sqrt{2K}=\sqrt{194}\approx 13.93, \\
\left\lVert W_{\mathrm{ETF}}\right\rVert_{*} =\sqrt{K(K-1)}=\sqrt{97\cdot 96}\approx 96.50, \\
\left\lvert\Delta_{1}\right\rvert \approx 82.57,\qquad\lambda_{\mathrm{crit}}\approx\frac{0.236}{82.57}\approx 2.86\times 10^{-3}.
$$

###### p12-3

*Thus, for $K=97$, a factorized weight-decay coefficient on the order of $10^{-3}$ is already sufficient for the regularization gain of the cyclic rank-$2$ code to dominate its bounded cross-entropy disadvantage. The calculation also makes the scaling transparent: the cross-entropy term is essentially constant in $K$, whereas the nuclear-norm saving grows linearly with the number of classes.*

###### p12-6

- **Cross-entropy cost:** replacing the ETF by the cyclic code incurs only a bounded $O(1)$ penalty, quantified by $\log I_{0}(1/\tilde{\tau})$.
- **Complexity gain:** the same replacement saves $\Theta(K)$ in either Schatten or factorized-weight-decay regularization.

The structured solution is therefore favored as soon as the effective regularization exceeds an order-$1/K$ threshold. This is exactly the regime in which recent low-rank-bias theories predict departures from deep NC [23; 29; 4]. What our analysis adds is the *identity* of the winning low-rank solution: modular addition does not merely prefer *some* compressed representation, but the specific cyclic code compatible with the group law.

Viewed this way, the two-dimensional circle is neither an optimization accident nor a nonsignificant variant of NC. It is the structured optimum of a task-aware trade-off between separation, symmetry, and complexity. That is why the classifier can settle into a rank-$2$ code before the embeddings fully reorganize, and also why the embeddings that eventually align with it are arranged on a regular polygon rather than on a simplex.

###### p13-4

- **Structure over separation.** When the gain from higher-dimensional separation is bounded but the complexity saving of a low-rank code grows with $K$, structured low-rank solutions can dominate the training objective.
- **Hierarchical collapse across layers.** In grokking-style algorithmic tasks, geometric organization need not arise uniformly across layers. Downstream classifiers can set the relevant low-dimensional subspace first, with upstream embeddings subsequently relaxing into it.
- **Geometry as algorithm.** The circular representation of $\mathbb{Z}/P\mathbb{Z}$ indicates that networks implement algorithms by physicalizing algebraic structure into geometry, rather than by memorizing isolated labels.

**Limitations and outlook.** Our theory still relies on balanced classes, near-terminal feature–weight alignment, and prime moduli. Extending the analysis to composite moduli, product groups, or non-Abelian settings may reveal tori or other structured manifolds in place of the circle. A second limitation is interpretive: we provide a geometric account of the induced angular dynamics after projection onto the classifier plane, but we do not claim that the full parameter trajectory is globally geodesic in parameter space. Finally, while we quantify the preference for the structured optimum, a sharper dynamical theory of when optimizers and schedules enter the classifier-first regime remains an important problem for future work.

In summary, deep neural networks trained on modular arithmetic do not simply expand dimensionality to separate classes. Guided by the trade-off between cross-entropy and regularization, they first identify a low-rank classifier code and then align the embedding layer with the underlying cyclic symmetry. This viewpoint not only explains the striking 2D circles observed in grokking but also offers a more general lesson: the geometry learned by a network is jointly determined by class separation, inter-layer dynamics, and the latent algebraic structure of the data.
