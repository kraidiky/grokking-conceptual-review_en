# Notsawo, Dumas, Rabusseau 2026 — Grokking Finite-Dimensional Algebra

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Pascal Jr Tikeng Notsawo** (Université de Montréal; Mila), **Guillaume Dumas** (Université de Montréal; Mila; CHU Sainte-Justine), **Guillaume Rabusseau** (Université de Montréal; Mila; CIFAR AI Chair).

arXiv [2602.19533](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19533), February 2026 (v2), 37 pages, 14 figures (24 files). Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The first systematic carrying of grokking beyond group operations — onto finite-dimensional algebras (FDA): any tensor $\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{C}}}\in\mathbb{F}^{n\times n\times n}$ defines an $n$-dimensional algebra, the corpus's group tasks become a particular case through the group algebra $\mathbb{F}[G]$ (the structure tensor = the Cayley table in a one-hot encoding), and associativity, commutativity and unitality turn into tensor identities that can be switched on and off one at a time. The main findings at $(n,p)=(2,7)$: the associative non-unital algebras grok earliest of all (unitality narrows the set of admissible representations), a perturbation of a single cell of the multiplication table sharply shifts the delay at unchanged data and model, and the quality of the representation $\mathcal{A}_{\text{rep}}$ (a bilinear reconstruction of the features) rises sharply exactly during the grokking era in an MLP, a Transformer and an LSTM alike.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the paper's cleanest lever: a single change of one product in the multiplication table (for example, $\mathbf{i}^{2}=-1$ into $\mathbf{i}^{2}=1$ in the complex numbers over $\mathbb{F}_ {7}$) sharply shifts the delay $\Delta t_{\text{gen}}=t_{4}-t_{2}$ at unchanged data, model and training budget — shown by a histogram over all single perturbations ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)); the grokking is reproduced on an MLP, a Transformer and an LSTM. The analysis: all the experiments are over finite fields only; the real-valued part (matrix factorisation, an implicit low rank, coherence) has remained purely theoretical although the abstract presents it as an equal; and there are few repeats (10 tensors per category, 2 runs per point), while the spread in the key figure 6 is large.

**[The canonical grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — a generalisation of the testbed of Power et al. from groups to algebras: the setting "predict $z=x\circ y$ from a random split of the table" is taken over word for word (together with the fraction $r$ and the $99$% threshold), but the set of symbols "with no internal structure" is replaced by an algebra which does have structure and which changes one property at a time; at $(n,p)=(2,7)$ all $7^{8}=5764801$ tensors are enumerated and stratified into eight categories $\lbrace a,\bar a\rbrace\times\lbrace c,\bar c\rbrace\times\lbrace u,\bar u\rbrace$ — three unital categories are empty, and $97.958$% falls to $\bar a\bar c\bar u$ ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)). The analysis: the ordering of the difficulty ($ac\bar u$ being the easiest, then $a\bar c\bar u$) rests on five non-empty categories, and the comparison "unital against non-unital" on the single non-empty unital category $acu$; and the results on groups (Nanda, Chughtai, Stander, Gromov) are declared a particular case but are not rechecked within the tensor frame.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the line "grokking is the formation of a representation" is carried over to algebras with a measure of its own: the quality of the representation $\mathcal{A}_ {\text{rep}}$ (the cosine proximity of the bilinear reconstruction of the features $\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})\approx\mathbf{\boldsymbol{\mathcal{W}}}\times_{1}\mathbf{f}^{(\ell)}(\mathbf{u})\times_{2}\mathbf{f}^{(r)}(\mathbf{v})$, a probe after the representation condition of proposition 2.3) stays low during the memorisation and rises sharply exactly during the grokking era — consistently in all three architectures ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)); the explanation of the ordering of the difficulty of the categories goes through the number of admissible representations (a non-unital associative algebra has the zero and the left regular representation, while unitality adds equations without degrees of freedom). The analysis: the probe is fitted by ridge regression over all the pairs with no held-out sample — this is a descriptive measure of consistency rather than a check of the probe's generalisation, and there is no intervention; and there is an admitted divergence from the corpus: in the Transformer $\mathcal{A}_ {\text{rep}}^{(0)}$ at the embedding layer does not change at grokking — against the emergence of structure precisely in the embeddings in Power and Liu, and the explanation (separate embedding and unembedding) is left as a conjecture.

**[Data fraction / critical sample size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the dependence on $r\in\lbrace0.2,\dots,0.9\rbrace$ reproduces the canonical picture on a new class of tasks: a small $r$ gives memorisation, a middling one a delayed generalisation, and the delay decays as $r\to 1$ ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)); on top of this there are two structural factors of difficulty — the test loss and the time to generalisation grow with the rank of the unfolding $r^{(3)}$ of the structure tensor (which is basis-invariant) and with the number of non-zero elements $s$. The analysis: at $n=2$ the rank takes only two values, so that "the higher the rank, the longer the grokking" is a binary comparison rather than a dependence; and the "sparsity level $s$" is nowhere defined and by the scale of the figure ($1\dots 8$ at $n^{3}=8$) is the number of non-zero elements — under a literal reading of the word "sparsity" §5.4 and the conclusion contradict one another.

**[The internal symmetry of the task](../../concepts/intrinsic-task-symmetry.md)** — the symmetry is decomposed into independent properties and enumerated: associativity, commutativity and unitality give eight classes of algebras ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)). The question "which symmetry drives generalisation" is thereby posed separately for each property.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — the frame and the lever.** A tensor notation in which all the corpus's group tasks are a particular case ($\mathbb{F}[G]$, the structure tensor = the Cayley table) and in which the properties of the algebra are switched on one at a time; a perturbation of a single cell of the multiplication table as a lever of the delay with everything else unchanged (fig. 1b) — the paper's cleanest argument; the consistent rise of $\mathcal{A}_{\text{rep}}$ during the grokking era in all three architectures; and honest caveats (the rank is basis-invariant, the sparsity is not; the conventionality of the canonical basis; the admitted limits of $n=2$).

**The explanations are arguments, not derivations.** The ordering of the difficulty of the categories is explained by the number of admissible representations ("labels" in the space of representations for the non-unital ones; half as many equations and two presentations of each product for the commutative ones) — this is a plausible argument made after the experiment, with no direct check; and the real-valued theory (a low-rank bias, coherence, sample complexity) is not accompanied by a single real-valued experiment.

**Internal inconsistencies.** "We selected 10 tensors in each category" with three categories empty (five appear on the plots); the caption of figure 12 defines $t_{2}$ twice (the second should be $t_{4}$); "$V=q+2$, where $1$ is for the service tokens"; a softmax "$\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^{V}$"; a "2-layer MLP" in the caption against a "three-layer" one in the appendix; the bias $\mathbf{b}^{(1)}$ declared to be from $\mathbb{R}^{2d}$ instead of $\mathbb{R}^{m^{2}}$; the reference "the privileged basis (Olsson et al. 2022)" pointing to a work on induction heads in which the privileged basis is not discussed; and random projections described in detail while only PCA is used in the reported experiments.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that the algebraic properties of the task govern grokking") — more precisely: at $n=2$ over $\mathbb{F}_{7}$, on five non-empty categories out of eight, with 10 tensors and 2 runs per point, differences in the grokking time and the test loss are measured; the explanation through the number of admissible representations remains an argument, and the real-valued case was not checked at all.
