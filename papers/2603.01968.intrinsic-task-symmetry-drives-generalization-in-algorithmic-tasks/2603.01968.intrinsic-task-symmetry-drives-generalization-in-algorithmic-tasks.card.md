# Hwang & Park 2026 — Intrinsic Task Symmetry Drives Generalization in Algorithmic Tasks

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Hyeonbin Hwang** (KAIST), **Yeachan Park** (Sejong University).

arXiv [2603.01968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01968), March 2026 (the sole version v1), 40 pages, 55 figures, 2 tables. There is no citation count for it in the corpus table. The work moves the question from "why do the representations compress" to "why do they take exactly this shape" and answers: the shape is set by the **intrinsic symmetry of the task** — the identities the correct solution is obliged to satisfy. Grokking is redescribed in three eras: memorisation, the acquisition of the symmetry, and the arrangement of the geometry. The proving ground is extended from modular arithmetic to graph-metric completion (six geometries) and to comparison by binary relations. The source is the LaTeX submission from arXiv.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the subject of the work, but redescribed: not two states (memorisation and generalisation) but **three eras** — memorisation, the acquisition of the symmetry, and the arrangement of the geometry ([`p1-6`](#p1-6), [`p5-1`](#p5-1), [`fig-6`](#fig-6)). The claim is that the generalisation arises precisely in the middle era, and that the visible geometry forms afterwards ([`p5-3`](#p5-3), [`p5-4`](#p5-4)).

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the initial observation: after the generalisation the embeddings invariably lay themselves out into a low-dimensional structure — a circle and a helix for modular arithmetic, the shape of the graph for metric completion, a lattice for comparison ([`p3-7`](#p3-7), [`p3-8`](#p3-8), [`fig-1`](#fig-1), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)). The work asks not "what does it look like" but "why exactly so" ([`p3-10`](#p3-10)).

**[The compression of representations](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the explanation offered: the symmetry imposes algebraic constraints on the representations and thereby reduces the number of degrees of freedom, while a weak bias of the optimisation already within that constrained space selects the low-norm solutions ([`p6-3`](#p6-3), [`p6-4`](#p6-4)). The effective dimension is computed as the number of principal components with a variance share above 1 % ([`p22-1`](#p22-1)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the first of the three proving grounds, and it is taken more broadly than usual: six operations from $x+y$ to $x+xy+y$ at $p=113$ and a training-data fraction of 0.3 ([`p2-7`](#p2-7), [`p36-4`](#p36-4)). For the non-commutative operations **surrogate identities** are written out, playing the part of commutativity and associativity ([`tab-1`](#tab-1)).

**[Knowledge graphs and reasoning](../../concepts/reasoning-knowledge-graphs.md)** — the third proving ground: comparison, in which the supervision is given only by binary pairwise relations while the global structure (a 2D or 3D lattice of features) must be inferred by the model itself ([`p3-3`](#p3-3), [`p3-4`](#p3-4), [`p36-6`](#p36-6)). A second, intermediate proving ground is also introduced — **graph-metric completion**: to predict the geodesic distance from two vertices, over six geometries from a path to a three-dimensional lattice ([`p3-1`](#p3-1), [`p3-2`](#p3-2), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the main working result: the **symmetry violation**, measured by a symmetric Kullback–Leibler divergence between the predictions on symmetry-related inputs ([`p5-3`](#p5-3), [`p12-2`](#p12-2)). For the graph the yardstick is the triangle equality through a convolution of the predicted distributions ([`p16-1`](#p16-1)), for comparison the transitivity through the additivity of the log odds ratio ([`p19-1`](#p19-1)). A **soft threshold** is claimed: once the violation falls below it, a run reliably reaches a perfect accuracy ([`p5-8`](#p5-8), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)).

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — redefined: memorisation is an era out of which one can move without ceasing to train, since the training loss is already near zero while the symmetry violation is still falling ([`p4-8`](#p4-8), [`p5-2`](#p5-2)). The argument is simple: if the dataset contains commutative pairs and associative triples, the network is confronted with the algebraic symmetry itself rather than only with individual examples ([`p4-9`](#p4-9)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — demoted deliberately: an experiment without weight decay reaches a perfect validation accuracy all the same ([`p6-1`](#p6-1), [`fig-8`](#fig-8)). Hence the conclusion that weight decay is not necessary but merely stabilises and simplifies the representations in the third era ([`p5-9`](#p5-9), [`p5-4`](#p5-4)).

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — an intervention: either **symmetry-hinting** terms are added to the loss (a penalty on the violation of commutativity, associativity, the triangle equality, transitivity) or **geometry-encouraging** biases are — the nuclear norm, the entropy, a Lipschitz smoothness ([`p7-12`](#p7-12), [`p8-3`](#p8-3)).

**[Low-pass filtering of the gradient](../../concepts/gradient-low-pass-filtering.md)** — a rival in the comparison: GrokFast speeds the generalisation up in some settings and delays the convergence in others, whereas symmetry hinting shortens the time to generalisation invariably ([`p8-6`](#p8-6), [`fig-9`](#fig-9)).

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — one of the stock explanations from which the work demarcates itself: in the authors' view they describe a *bias* towards compression but not the *shape* of the resulting geometry ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)).

**[The lazy-to-rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** and **[initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — analysed as competing explanations and declared complementary: the output scaling and the initialisation norm govern the *form* and the *timing* of the feature learning, while the symmetry sets the *direction* ([`p8-9`](#p8-9), [`p35-1`](#p35-1), [`p35-2`](#p35-2)).

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — a connection the work leans on only by reference: theoretical works relating the learning of an invariance to generalisation bounds ([`p2-5`](#p2-5)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon and the proving ground; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the decomposition into memorisation, circuit formation and cleanup, argued with outright here; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the weight norm and the initialisation scale; [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — the lazy-to-rich transition and the output scaling; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — circuit efficiency; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — the rearrangement of the geometry of the representations as a mark of generalisation; [Lee et al. 2024](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md) — GrokFast, compared against here; [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md) — generalisation without weight decay; [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — the solvable model on which the related-work section leans.

**[The intrinsic symmetry of the task](../../concepts/intrinsic-task-symmetry.md)** — the work introduces the notion as a cause rather than a companion: the intrinsic symmetry of the task is declared the principal cause of the generalisation ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)), and the training is divided into memorisation, the acquisition of the symmetry and the arrangement of the geometry ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The symmetry violation becomes measurable with a threshold ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)), and the causality is checked by an intervention ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)).
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is large: 9 pages of main text and 31 pages of appendices, 55 figures, 2 tables.

**The proving ground is extended, and this is the main thing the work contains.** Modular arithmetic is taken as a particular case of a walk on the cyclic graph $C_p$, and from it a step is made to arbitrary graphs with an explicit geometry, and then to comparison, in which there is no metric at all and only binary relations ([`p3-1`](#p3-1), [`p3-3`](#p3-3)). Three domains with different symmetries — commutativity and associativity, the triangle equality, transitivity — and one and the same three-era description for all three. Works going so far beyond modular addition are few in the corpus.

**The symmetry yardstick is defined and computed rather than declared.** For each domain it is written out what exactly is measured: a Kullback–Leibler divergence between the predictions on algebraically equivalent paths ([`p12-2`](#p12-2)), a convolution of distance distributions against a direct prediction ([`p16-1`](#p16-1)), the additivity of the log odds ratio ([`p19-1`](#p19-1)). Twenty seeds per task, and scatter plots of "the yardstick against the accuracy" ([`p5-7`](#p5-7), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)).

**A negative control on weight decay is carried out and reported.** Without weight decay the model reaches a perfect accuracy all the same ([`p6-1`](#p6-1), [`fig-8`](#fig-8)) — that is, the claim is not "symmetry instead of weight decay" but "weight decay is not necessary, symmetry is". This is more honest than pulling the blanket to one's own side.

**The intervention is set up as a check of causality.** If symmetry governs the onset of generalisation, then encouraging it ought to shift the timing; the encouragement is introduced directly into the loss, and the timing shifts ([`p7-11`](#p7-11), [`p8-5`](#p8-5)). The comparison with GrokFast is doubly useful: it does not accelerate everywhere, whereas symmetry encouragement does ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)).

**The "threshold", however, is called soft, and it is soft for a reason.** The text says outright that the transition is not entirely sharp ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)); not a single number — neither the threshold itself, nor the rate of correctly predicted runs, nor an error — is given. A yardstick proposed as a predictor of generalisation is not assessed by any quantity by which it could be compared with rivals from the corpus.

**The causality is shown in one direction only.** Encouraging the symmetry accelerates the generalisation — that is an experiment. There is no converse experiment: the symmetry was not suppressed to see whether the generalisation would be delayed. Without that, the "causal role" ([`p8-7`](#p8-7)) remains a claim about consistency rather than about a cause.

**The theory proves something other than what is claimed.** Propositions 7.1–7.3 say: *if* an embedding already preserves the symmetry and lives in a low-dimensional space, *then* its image has that very shape ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`p7-5`](#p7-5), [`p7-8`](#p7-8)). These are derivations from symmetry to geometry — and they are correct. But that the network acquires that symmetry the theory does not show: the proofs concern continuous embeddings and topological groups rather than the course of the training. The gap between the theory and the experiment is not stated in the work.

**The word "symmetry" carries two different senses.** In modular arithmetic it is a genuine algebraic symmetry of the operation; in the graph it is the triangle equality, that is, a property of the metric itself; in comparison it is transitivity, that is, a property of an order relation. What they have in common is that all three are identities the correct solution is obliged to satisfy. Calling them by one word is possible, but the claim "the intrinsic symmetry of the task" already sounds like symmetry in the group-theoretic sense, and in two cases out of three that is not what it is.

**Three misprints and one broken sentence.** "Moodular Arithmetics" in a subsection heading, "sepcifically", "Comparsion" in the caption of figure 9; and the sentence about the geometry-encouraging biases breaks off with a full stop in the middle of the argument ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)). The caption of figure A.37 and of the three that follow repeats "[p. 22]" where the figures stand on pages 27–29 — a trace of the captions floating separately from the text in the source.

**The settings are not levelled across the domains.** A training-data fraction of 0.3 for modular arithmetic, 0.8 for the graph, 0.2 for comparison; weight decays of 1, 3 and 1 ([`p36-4`](#p36-4), [`p36-5`](#p36-5), [`p36-6`](#p36-6)). The differences are explicable but are nowhere explained, and the three-era description is compared across domains as though the settings were identical.

**What is valuable for the corpus is the yardstick and the proving ground.** The symmetry violation as a computable quantity preceding the generalisation takes its place alongside the earlier progress measures and, unlike many of them, is defined for three different domains. And the set of tasks — six graph geometries and comparison by binary relations — is by itself fit to serve as a proving ground for other people's work, for which modular addition has long been exhausted.

Hyeonbin Hwang (KAIST), Yeachan Park (Sejong University)

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2501-04697
**Prieto et al. (2501.04697) — instability as an explanation, and a dropped condition of the intervention.** (1) `"Prevailing explanations range from … or numerical instability (Prieto et al., 2025)"` — numerical instability is placed among the explanations of the delayed generalisation: in Prieto it explains [the stalling of the training without regularisation](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p4-4), while [the delay is explained by NLM](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p7-1). (2) `"the model reliably converges to perfect test accuracy … even in the absence of explicit regularization, as observed by previous works. (Kumar et al., 2024; Prieto et al., 2025)"` — "as observed" drops a condition: in Prieto grokking without regularisation is [achieved by an intervention — StableMax or a gradient projection](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p5-10); a vanilla cross-entropy without regularisation does not generalise in their runs.

###### mc-2506-05718
**Notsawo et al. (2506.05718) — "the compression of representations" and a truncation of the generality.** (1) `"prior findings that low-rank structure facilitates representation compression (Walker et al., 2025; DeMoss et al., 2025; Junior et al., 2025)"` — the work says nothing about representations or their compression: [a low rank there is a regularisable property of the solution of a convex problem](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/original/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.md#p3-1). (2) `"optimization-centric views that cite … low-rank biases (Junior et al., 2025)"` — a general law for an arbitrary regularisable property P is narrowed to "low-rank biases"; [weight decay, the lasso and a low rank are equal particular cases in the authors' account](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/original/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.md#p3-6). In a neighbouring place the same paper phrases it more correctly ("linking low-rank structure to grokking dynamics"); in both cases the first author is mangled — "Junior et al.".

###### mc-2412-09810
**DeMoss et al. (2412.09810) — the measure carried over into representation space.** `"prior findings … entropy reduction to representation compression (DeMoss et al., 2025)"` and `"low-rank structure facilitates representation compression (Walker et al., 2025; DeMoss et al., 2025; …)"`: in DeMoss [the measure lives in parameter space: the spectral entropy of the singular values of the weight matrices and the compression of the coarsened weights](../2412.09810.the-complexity-dynamics-of-grokking/original/2412.09810.the-complexity-dynamics-of-grokking.md#p10-2), and the work says nothing about the compression of representations. The extension is refuted within the corpus: [2604.13123](../2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation/2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation.card.md) records explicitly that DeMoss operate in parameter space and that the two spaces "are not interchangeable".
