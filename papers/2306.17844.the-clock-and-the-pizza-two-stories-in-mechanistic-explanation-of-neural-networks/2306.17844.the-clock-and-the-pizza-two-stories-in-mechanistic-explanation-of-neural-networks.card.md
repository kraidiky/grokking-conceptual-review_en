## Concepts covered

**[The Clock and Pizza algorithms](../../concepts/clock-vs-pizza.md)** — the work introduces the distinction itself and both halves of its name. *Clock* is taken here as a ready-made description given by [Nanda et al.](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) for their model: the inputs are placed on a circle and the angles are added by trigonometric identities ([§2.1](#p3-2)). *Pizza* is described for the first time: the embeddings are averaged **within** the circle rather than added by angles on it, and the network determines which of the $2p$ mirrored "slices" the midpoint has fallen into ([§3.1](#p4-2), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). The distinction is formulated mechanically: the clock needs a multiplication of the inputs, the pizza only an absolute value, "which is easily implementable by ReLU layers" ([§3.1](#p4-3)); whence an argument from the form of the logit — in the clock $\nabla_{\mathbf{E}_{a}}Q_{abc}\neq\nabla_{\mathbf{E}_{b}}Q_{abc}$, so a symmetry of the gradient **excludes** the clock ([§2.2](#p3-5)). What the work does not contain: the word "memorisation" applied to the pizza — both solutions give a perfect accuracy and differ only in their internal computation ([§5](#p9-4)); the pizza is just as circular (a circularity of about $99.8\%$, [appendix B](#p14-7)) and just as assembled from trigonometry ([appendix A](#p13-2)) as the clock; nor is there a claim that one of the measures matters more in both directions — the authors write outright that the decisive measure for the pizza is the distance irrelevance, while the gradient symmetricity serves only to exclude the clock ([§4.1.3](#p7-11)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work calls into question an implicit support of the whole area: that a well-understood task has one solution, which the network rediscovers. The question is carried into the abstract's first sentence — whether networks reliably rediscover known algorithms for tasks that are themselves well understood ([abstract](#p1-2)) — and the answer is negative. Whence the declared shift of aim: to describe not a unique solution but an **algorithmic phase space**, "a characterisation of algorithmic phase spaces rather than only of individual algorithmic solutions" ([§1](#p2-1)); the conclusion adds two open questions to this — how to find and interpret unfamiliar algorithms systematically, and how to disentangle several parallel implementations ([§6](#p10-2)). The work also gives the corpus techniques: circle isolation, a visual analysis of the correct logits, gradient measures of symmetry, and an analysis of the linear pizza down to individual weights ([appendix L](#p26-5)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of the non-circular solutions that make up the majority of runs in deep networks — it is postponed entirely ([§4.2](#p9-1), [appendix B](#p14-4)); nor is there a direct dispute with the universality hypothesis — the work of [Chughtai, Chan and Nanda](../2302.03025.a-toy-model-of-universality-reverse-engineering-how-networks-learn-group-operations/2302.03025.a-toy-model-of-universality-reverse-engineering-how-networks-learn-group-operations.card.md) stands in Zhong et al. only in a general list of works on mechanistic interpretability, and "universality" as a term is not discussed in the text.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the task is taken as "prototypical" and is the only one: $a+b=c\ ({\rm mod}\ p)$ at $p=59$ throughout ([§2](#p2-3)). The work sets the corpus a setting in which **different** internal solutions are compared on one and the same task, and introduces two reproducible measures for that comparison. What the work does not contain: any dependence of anything on $p$ — the modulus is single; there are neither other operations nor non-commutative tasks. The limitation is acknowledged by the authors: "We have focused on a single learning task: modular addition" ([Limitations](#p10-3)).

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the circular embeddings $\mathbf{E}_{a}\approx[{\rm cos}(w_{k}a),{\rm sin}(w_{k}a)]$ are taken as a known fact with a reference to [Liu et al.](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) ([§2.1](#p2-4)), and the work's contribution is that **on one and the same Fourier geometry** two different computations stand. Both schemes are derived from the identities $\cos x+\cos y=\cos((x-y)/2)(2\cos((x+y)/2))$ and $\sin x+\sin y=\cos((x-y)/2)(2\sin((x+y)/2))$ ([appendix A](#p13-2)), and the difference between them is a factor $\lvert\cos(w_{k}(a-b)/2)\rvert$ in the pizza's logit ([§3.2](#p4-4)). A measure of **circularity** by the Fourier content of the first four principal components is introduced, models with $c\geq 99.5\%$ counting as circular ([appendix B](#p14-7)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of how the set of frequencies is chosen and how many are needed — the frequencies are taken from a ready-made visualisation; and there is no claim that circular solutions predominate: in single-layer transformers $34.31\%$ are circular ([fig. 10](#fig-10)), and in two-, three- and four-layer ones $9.95\%$, $11.55\%$ and $6.08\%$ ([fig. 11](#fig-11)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work gives the corpus a transition of a particular kind and names it itself: "the phase transition studied in this paper is *representational*: both the clock and the pizza give a perfect accuracy but arrive at their answers through different internal computations" ([§5](#p9-4)). The transition is sharp and two-dimensional: in the attention rate $\alpha$ at a fixed width and in the pair "attention rate — layer width", in which the boundary is nearly linear and the transition point grows with the width ([§4.2](#p8-5), [fig. 7](#fig-7)). The explanation is the authors' own and is marked as such — "Our explanation is": at a high attention rate multiplication is available and a clock arises, at a low one the linear layers predominate and a pizza arises ([§4.2](#p8-7)). What the work does not contain: **the axis of the transition here is a hyperparameter, not training time**; every point in figure 7 is a separate completed run rather than a moment of one run. There is no order parameter, no theory of the transition, and the lines in figure 7 are drawn by a logistic regression on binary features ($s_{g}>98\%$ and $q<0.6$), that is, they are a classifier's boundary rather than a measured transition point.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the work introduces an **attention rate** $\alpha$: the post-softmax attention matrix is replaced by $M^{\prime}=M\alpha+J(1-\alpha)$, that is, by an interpolation between ordinary attention ($\alpha=1$) and constant attention ($\alpha=0$) ([§4.2](#p8-2)). This gives the corpus a continuous knob between a "transformer" and an "MLP" — Model A at $\alpha=0$ the authors equate outright with a ReLU MLP ([§2](#p2-3)) — and makes the role of attention measurable: it is precisely attention that, by the authors' analysis, supplies the network with the multiplication of the inputs without which a clock is unrealisable ([§2.1](#p3-3)). What the work does not contain: the rule "strong attention means a clock" as an unconditional one; appendix H gives a pizza at $\alpha=1$ and a width of $1024$ ($q=0.156$, $s_{g}=0.995$), but "after several attempts", and the fraction of such runs is not named ([appendix H](#p21-3)).

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the work gives the corpus a technique of **circle isolation**: the embedding matrix is replaced by a series of rank-2 approximations (the first and second principal components, then the third and fourth, and so on), after which the model goes on behaving meaningfully, and from the residual behaviour one can see which algorithm is embedded in it ([§3.3](#p5-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). By the same technique an observation about **error correction by ensembling** is obtained: one circle in Model A gives $32.8\%$ overall accuracy and three circles $91.4\%$, whereas Model B on the first six components gives $100\%$. What the work does not contain: an intervention during training — the circles are removed only from a trained network; and the conclusion about ensembling itself is drawn from the fall of accuracy on removal rather than from switching an individual circle on or off during training. The closest thing to dynamics is a snapshot at epoch 600, at which removing the accompanying circle drops the accuracy from $99.7\%$ to $97.9\%$ ([appendix K](#p23-1)).

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the fraction of variance explained is applied here as a means of distinguishing two formulas on one and the same data: over **all** $59\times 59\times 59$ logits of Model A the pizza formula explains $99.18$–$99.28\%$ of the variance against $75.38$–$75.62\%$ for the clock formula, both sides having first been normalised to mean $0$ and variance $1$ ([§3.3](#p5-2)). The accompanying pizzas are measured the same way — $97.23$–$97.69\%$ ([appendix D](#p15-4)). The work leans on principal component analysis too: the circles are sought pairwise in a PCA of the embeddings, and in appendix L a PCA is applied to the embedding and unembedding matrices at once ([appendix L](#p26-5)). What the work does not contain: the spectrum of the weights, a distribution of singular values, and any relation of FVE to the course of the training — every number is taken from trained models.

**[The sparse subnetwork / the lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the work merely borrows the language of lottery tickets, and does so in an expressly declared conjecture: "We conjecture that the training dynamics is as follows: (1) at initialisation the pizzas #1/#2/#3 correspond to three different “lottery tickets”… (3) as the training proceeds (in the presence of weight decay) the network is pruned" ([§3.4](#p6-2)). Not a single measurement is attached to this conjecture: no tracking of the circles from initialisation and no restarts with masks. What the work does not contain: any contribution of its own to the notion — the lottery ticket here is a metaphor for explaining why the accompanying pizzas are visible in embedding space yet take almost no part in the prediction.

**[Weight decay / L2 regularisation](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work sets an unusually strong weight decay ($\beta=2$ under AdamW at a learning rate $\gamma=0.001$) as part of the setting without discussing it ([appendix F.1](#p20-2)) and gives it a substantive role once — in the conjecture about the pruning of the accompanying pizzas ([§3.4](#p6-2)). What the work does not contain: a sweep of $\beta$, a comparison with runs without regularisation and any measurement whatever of weight decay's influence on the choice between clock and pizza — the phase diagrams are built over attention rate, width and depth.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting is exactly the one in which the corpus observes delayed generalisation: a small prime modulus $p=59$, the whole table of $p^{2}=3481$ pairs, a random $80\%$ in training and $20\%$ in validation, full batch with no minibatches, AdamW with a strong weight decay, and $20\,000$ epochs from a random initialisation ([appendix F.1](#p20-1), [F.1](#p20-2)). The work inherits the proving ground and reuses it for a different question — which algorithm results, rather than when the generalisation sets in. What the work does not contain: training and validation curves; moreover, the runs that did not reach $100\%$ validation accuracy are **discarded** from the analysis ([appendix F.1](#p20-2)), so the generalisation delay on this proving ground is in principle unobservable here. The cost of the setting is named: $226$ V100 GPU-days ([appendix F.3](#p20-6)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the word "grokking" does not occur once in the body of the paper: it appears only in four bibliography entries (Power et al., Nanda et al., Liu et al., Omnigrok). The work does not measure delayed generalisation and gives no curves of it; every transition it speaks of runs **over hyperparameters** (attention rate, width, depth) and separates not moments of one run but different trained models ([§4.2](#p8-2), [§5](#p9-4)). The work's indirect relation to grokking is twofold. First, the setting is the same as the corpus's ([appendix F.1](#p20-1)). Second, two appendices show that the intermediate algorithm does not match the final one: in a run with attention a pizza-like pattern is visible early and gradually "dissolves", and the outcome is a clock ([appendix J](#p21-8), [fig. 22](#fig-22)); in a run without attention an accompanying pizza arises early, and at epoch 600 removing it drops the accuracy from $99.7\%$ to $97.9\%$, whereas in the trained Model A it costs almost nothing ([appendix K](#p23-1), [fig. 23](#fig-23)). What the work does not contain: repetitions of these two runs, loss curves, or any tying of the snapshots to the moment of generalisation — figure 22 is labelled by steps (90, 210, 300, 510, 840), and how they relate to the attainment of $100\%$ validation accuracy is not said. The authors cannot be credited with the claim "the clock↔pizza transition is the grokking transition".

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work introduces two measurable quantities, **gradient symmetricity** $s_{g}\in[-1,1]$ — the mean cosine similarity of the logit's gradients with respect to the two input embeddings ([§4.1.1](#p7-3)) — and **distance irrelevance** $q\in[0,1]$ — the ratio of the $d$-averaged standard deviation of the correct logits along the diagonals $L_{i,i+d}$ to the overall standard deviation of the matrix ([§4.1.2](#p7-7)) — but these are measures **not of progress but of membership in an algorithm**: they are computed on a trained model and serve to sort the runs automatically. Neither is measured over the course of the training or presented as a precursor of anything. The term "progress measures" is not used in the paper, although the work of Nanda et al., whence it comes, is cited as the source of the clock algorithm. The threshold values are assigned by hand and without justification: $98\%$ for $s_{g}$, $0.6$ for $q$, $99.5\%$ for circularity, and the "typical" ranges of distance irrelevance ($0$–$0.4$ for the pizza, $0.4$–$1$ for the clock) are given simply by observation ([§4.1.2](#p7-10)).

**[The order parameter](../../concepts/order-parameter.md)** — the two-dimensional diagram "attention rate — width" with a sharp boundary between two phases, and the two scalar quantities by which the phases are distinguished, are constructed just as a phase diagram is in physics; and the authors do say that within-model transitions are "closer to the corresponding phenomena in physical systems" ([§5](#p9-4)). But the term "order parameter" does not appear in the paper, neither of the two measures is so called or tested for that role: their joint distribution is shown as a cloud of points over the set of runs ([fig. 6](#fig-6)) rather than as a dependence on a control parameter with a singularity at the transition point. The language of order parameters cannot be credited to the work.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the work gives the corpus an important negative observation: a circular representation **underdetermines** the algorithm. One and the same geometry — points on a circle — serves two different computations, and beyond that there exist wholly non-circular representations: lines and three-dimensional Lissajous-like curves ([§4.2](#p9-1), [appendix B](#p14-4)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of how the circle arises and any dynamics of representations whatever — the embeddings are examined only in trained networks; the non-circular solutions are not analysed and the authors have no features distinguishing them from one another.

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the training-data fraction is fixed at $80\%$ and declared a means of speeding up the training: "This choice (a small $p$ and a high training-data fraction) helps to speed up the training" ([appendix F.1](#p20-1)). The work only mentions the quantity as a setting. What the work does not contain: a sweep of the data fraction, a critical dataset size, and any dependence whatever of the choice of algorithm on the volume of training data.

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — AdamW at a learning rate $\gamma=0.001$, full batch, $20\,000$ epochs ([appendix F.1](#p20-2)). The work merely sets the optimiser as part of the setting: there is no comparison of optimisers, the role of adaptivity is not discussed, and none of the results is tied to the choice of AdamW.

**[Emergence / emergent abilities](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — mentioned only in the survey of related work: an ability is called "emergent" if performance on a subtask rises suddenly with the size of the model, "although such claims depend on the choice of measure", and a hypothesis is adduced that the emergence of an ability corresponds to the arising of a modular circuit ([§5](#p10-1)). The work has no contribution of its own to the notion: it does not vary the model scale (the width is swept in the narrow range $[32,512]$ and serves as an axis of the phase diagram rather than as a measure of scale), it observes no sudden rise of performance, and it does not apply the word "emergence" to its own transitions.

**[Group representations and cosets](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** — the work only mentions the adjacent: the learning of group structures is named in a list of mathematical tasks to which machine learning has been applied ([§5](#p9-3)), and the work of Chughtai, Chan and Nanda on networks rediscovering group operations stands in a general list of works on mechanistic interpretability. There are no group representations and no decomposition into irreducible characters in the paper: the whole task is abelian and the whole geometry a circle.

**[The universality hypothesis](../../concepts/universality-hypothesis.md)** — the strongest refutation of its literal form: the training set is fixed, the difference is introduced only by the settings and the initialisation, and the algorithms come out qualitatively different ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The claim, though, is not that there is no universality at all but that at the level of the algorithm there is none.

**[The approximate Chinese remainder theorem](../../concepts/approximate-chinese-remainder-theorem.md)** — it is precisely the pair of competing schemes found here that became the subject of a later unification: the aCRT declares the Clock/Pizza distinction low-level rather than algorithmic ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The work proposes no such reading and insists on the difference.

**[The phase diagram](../../concepts/phase-diagram.md)** — here the regions are marked out by the algorithm learnt rather than by the fact of generalisation, and the boundary between them turns out to be nearly linear in the attention rate and the layer width ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)). This distinguishes the diagram from the customary map of "groks / does not grok": the transition occurs between two solutions, both of which generalise.
## Points we could not reconcile

**Model A and Model B are transposed in the first sentence of section 4.** It says: "In section 3 we showed a typical *Clock* (Model A) and *Pizza* (Model B)" ([§4](#p7-1)), whereas everywhere in the paper Model A is the pizza (constant attention, symmetric gradients, logits depending on $a-b$) and Model B the clock. The error is the authors' rather than a consequence of the conversion: checked against the PDF, page 7. This sentence cannot be cited as a definition of the models.

**One and the same triple of circles gives two different accuracy figures.** In [§3.3](#p5-1) the circle isolation of Model A over the first six principal components (three circles) gives "an overall accuracy of $91.4\%$", while a footnote to [§3.4](#p6-2) says: "The accompanied pizzas #1/#2/#3 can reach an accuracy of 99.7%". The numbers reconcile only under different readings: $91.4\%$ is the model **cut down** to three circles, and $99.7\%$ the full model from which the three accompanying circles have been **removed** (as it is put in [appendix K](#p23-1): "removing the accompanying pizzas in the trained Model A drops the accuracy only to 99.7%"). The footnote itself does not stipulate this difference, and citing the figure $99.7\%$ as "the accuracy of the three pizzas" produces a claim that does not exist in the work.

## What is shown, what is derived from the form of the algorithm and what is conjectured

The work marks its own conjectures, and those marks are the first thing lost in citation.

**Shown by measurement.** The gradient symmetricity of Model A and Model B — $99.37\%$ and $33.36\%$ ([§4.1.1](#p7-6)); the dependence of Model A's correct logits on $a-b$ and its absence in Model B ([§2.3](#p3-7)); the behaviour of both models after circle isolation ([§3.3](#p5-1)); the fractions of variance explained — $99.18$–$99.28\%$ for the pizza formula against $75.38$–$75.62\%$ for the clock formula ([§3.3](#p5-2)) and $97.23$–$97.69\%$ for the accompanying pizzas ([appendix D](#p15-4)); the sharp two-dimensional phase boundary ([§4.2](#p8-5), [fig. 7](#fig-7)); the fractions of circular solutions by depth ([fig. 10](#fig-10), [fig. 11](#fig-11)); the robustness of the picture to replacing ReLU by GeLU, to separate embeddings of the two tokens and to adding an "=" sign ([appendix I](#p21-4)); and the inertness of the second ReLU in the linear pizza — after its removal the accuracy stays at $100\%$ and the loss even falls, from $6.20\times 10^{-7}$ to $5.89\times 10^{-7}$ ([appendix L.3](#p26-2)).

**Derived from the form of the algorithm rather than measured.** That the clock is excluded by the symmetry of the gradient is a consequence of expression (1) rather than an observation: since the clock's logit contains a product of the embeddings, the gradients with respect to the two inputs are in general different, and hence a model with symmetric gradients implements something else ([§2.2](#p3-5)). What is checked by measurement is already the consequence.

**Conjectured, and the authors say so.** The dynamics of the arising and pruning of the accompanying pizzas — "We conjecture that the training dynamics is as follows" ([§3.4](#p6-2)). The unexpected dependence of the clock's logits on $a+b$ — "Our guess is that steps 1 and 2 are performed (almost) without noise… while step 3 after circle isolation is imperfect" ([§3.3](#p5-4)). The possibility of hybrid algorithms — "The continuous change of phase suggests the existence of networks lying between the *Clock* and *Pizza* algorithms" ([§4.2](#p8-8)); no such model is separately analysed. And the explanation of the phase boundary itself — "Our explanation is" ([§4.2](#p8-7)).

**Assigned by hand.** The thresholds of $98\%$ for gradient symmetricity, $0.6$ for distance irrelevance and $99.5\%$ for circularity, together with the "typical" ranges of distance irrelevance ($0$–$0.4$ and $0.4$–$1$), are given without justification ([§4.1.2](#p7-10), [appendix B](#p14-7)); and the phase-transition lines are the boundary of a logistic regression on those binary features rather than a measured transition point ([fig. 7](#fig-7)).

**Selection of runs.** Runs that did not reach $100\%$ validation accuracy are discarded from the analysis ("most runs reached $100\%$", [appendix F.1](#p20-2)); and the pizza at full attention and a width of $1024$ was obtained "after several attempts", with the fraction of such runs not named ([appendix H](#p21-3)).

**Limitations acknowledged by the authors.** One task, one $p$, and "Considerable further work is required to carry these techniques over to far more complex models" ([Limitations](#p10-3)); the analysis of the non-circular solutions is postponed to the future ([§4.2](#p9-1), [appendix B](#p14-4)), although in deep networks it is precisely those that make up the majority of runs.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns noticed in the citing works of the corpus; each entry says what exactly is distorted and leads to the authors' paragraphs where the lost caveat stands.

**"The pizza is a piecewise-memorising solution that delays generalisation" (erroneous).** Citing works credit the paper with an opposition of "a structured Fourier solution against memorisation": in Yildirim (2603.05228) the pizza is `"a fragmented “Pizza” algorithm relying on piecewise memorization"`, and the very possibility of such a solution is declared the cause of the delay — `"memorization-heavy solution pathways that delay the emergence of invariant representations"`. Nothing of the kind exists in Zhong et al. The pizza is just as much a circular Fourier solution as the clock: its embeddings lie on a circle, Model A's circularity is about $99.8\%$ ([appendix B](#p14-7)), and its logit is derived from the same trigonometric identities as the clock's ([appendix A](#p13-2)), differing from it by one factor ([§3.2](#p4-4)). Both phases reach a perfect accuracy, and the authors call the transition between them representational rather than qualitative outright: "both the clock and the pizza give a perfect accuracy but arrive at their answers through different internal computations" ([§5](#p9-4)). The word "memorization" does not appear in the paper, and the runs that did not reach $100\%$ validation accuracy are discarded from the analysis ([appendix F.1](#p20-2)), so a "delay" here is not only unshown but unobservable. For comparison, a formulation preserving both halves: 2606.12966 has "Zhong et al. 2023 show that small hyperparameter changes induce qualitatively different procedures (a “clock” and a “pizza” algorithm), both Fourier-based", with the essential "both Fourier-based" in place. Details are in the card of the citing work: [Yildirim, 2603.05228](../2603.05228.the-geometric-inductive-bias-of-grokking-bypassing-phase-transitions-via-architectural-topology/2603.05228.the-geometric-inductive-bias-of-grokking-bypassing-phase-transitions-via-architectural-topology.card.md).

**"The clock and the pizza arise at the grokking transition" (erroneous).** In Saheb Pasand et al. (2510.04930) we read: `"Notably, Zhong et al. (2023) identify complementary algorithmic mechanisms (“clock” and “pizza”) and circular embeddings that emerge at the grokking transition"`; in Xu (2602.16746) the work stands in a list of lenses on grokking as `"circuit formation (Zhong et al., 2024)"`. The word "grokking" does not occur once in the body of Zhong et al.'s paper — it appears only in four bibliography entries. The axis of the transition here is a hyperparameter: the attention rate $\alpha$, the layer width, the depth ([§4.2](#p8-2), [§4.2](#p8-5)), and every point of the phase diagram is a separate completed run rather than a moment of one run; the authors separate their transition from the performance transitions customary in the literature precisely by its being representational ([§5](#p9-4)). What Zhong et al. call "complementary" is not the clock and the pizza but the accompanied and accompanying pizzas — two circles within one model, of which the second works best on the antipodal pairs that ruin the first ([§3.4](#p6-1), [§3.4](#p6-2)). Details are in the cards of the citing works: [Saheb Pasand et al., 2510.04930](../2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking/2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking.card.md), [Xu, 2602.16746](../2602.16746.low-dimensional-and-transversely-curved-optimization-dynamics-in-grokking/2602.16746.low-dimensional-and-transversely-curved-optimization-dynamics-in-grokking.card.md).

**"The pizza is what results without attention, and it manages without trigonometric identities" (erroneous).** A footnote in Furuta et al. (2402.16726) says: `"Zhong et al., 2023 revealed the existence of algorithms independent of trigonometric identities if the model does not have attention. Since we employ a Transformer with attention, this paper assumes the algorithms based on trigonometric identities."` Both halves diverge from the text of Zhong et al. The pizza is derived **from** trigonometric identities: appendix A begins with $\cos x+\cos y=\cos((x-y)/2)(2\cos((x+y)/2))$ and arrives at $Q^{\prime}_{abc}\approx\lvert\cos(w_{k}(a-b)/2)\rvert\cos(w_{k}(a+b-c))$ ([appendix A](#p13-2)), and lemma A.1 itself shows that the factor $\cos((x-y)/2)$ appears in **any** symmetric linear combination of sines and cosines. Nor is the pizza tied to the absence of attention: appendix H exhibits a pizza at full attention $\alpha=1$ and a width of $1024$ ([appendix H](#p21-3)), and the phase boundary in figure 7 shifts upwards as the width grows, so that in a wide model a pizza is possible under strong attention too. The absence of attention in Zhong et al. is not a condition of the pizza but one end of a continuous scale of $\alpha$. Details are in the card of the citing work: [Furuta et al., 2402.16726](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md).

Milder echoes not warranting separate analysis: retellings of the kind "different architectures converge on different solutions" (2604.17673, 2606.17399) are correct in substance, but "architecture" in Zhong et al. means a position within one family of single-layer transformers in attention rate and width rather than different architectures in the ordinary sense; and in several works ([Xu, 2602.16746](../2602.16746.low-dimensional-and-transversely-curved-optimization-dynamics-in-grokking/2602.16746.low-dimensional-and-transversely-curved-optimization-dynamics-in-grokking.card.md), [2511.01938](../2511.01938.the-geometry-of-grokking-norm-minimization-on-the-zero-loss-manifold/2511.01938.the-geometry-of-grokking-norm-minimization-on-the-zero-loss-manifold.card.md), 2604.06256, 2602.18523) the work is dated 2024, whereas the preprint appeared in June 2023 and v2 in November 2023.
