# Hidajat, Stoll, An 2026 — Grokking as Structural Inference: Transformers Need Bayesian Lottery Tickets

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Kai Hidajat**, **Solden Stoll**, **Joseph An** (Department of Computer Science, University of Washington, Seattle).

arXiv [2605.15787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15787), May 2026 (v1), 25 pages, 11 figures (19 files), 2 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The claim: all three families of explanations of grokking (the norm and the landscape; lazy→rich; circuits and lucky tickets) miss the distribution of the attention as an independent condition of generalisation. The attention is interpreted as a Bayesian posterior over the dependency graph of the task (a view imported from Singh & Buckley); a separation theorem: a zero test error requires simultaneously the structural condition $\mathcal{B}_ {\gamma}$ (an attention mass of at least $\gamma$ on every informative position) and a "Goldilocks" condition $\mathcal{N}$ on the norm of the MLP. The delay of grokking is a delay of the structural inference: a memorising MLP drives the loss to zero and starves the attention of gradient (an "explaining-away" effect); an added term $\beta D_{\text{KL}}(\alpha^{ * }\Vert\alpha)$ bypasses the plateau with a delay scaling of $\mathcal{O}(1/\tilde{\beta})$, and the "lucky ticket" is decomposed into a routing ticket and a representation ticket.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — four eras of the dynamics: a fast memorisation; an "explaining-away" plateau (the loss near zero, the structural gradient on the attention dying out as $\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_ {\text{CE}}}$ by lemma 5.1, and weight decay contracting the norm of the MLP geometrically); a revival of the signal (the norm falls below the memorisation capacity, the loss rises slightly, the gradient comes back to life); and the grokking — together with a derivation of the delay law $\Delta t_{\text{plateau}}\geq(2/\lambda)\log(\Vert\theta_{\text{mem}}\Vert_{F}/r_{\max})$ as a structural waiting time ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). The analysis: equation (39) gives an *upper* bound on the norm — from it follows the time by which the fall is guaranteed, not a lower bound on the length of the plateau as (5) asserts: the inequality reads the other way round; in §5.1 the contraction is written for the norms while in (39) it is written for the squares of the norms with the same exponent (the rates differ by a factor of two); the $1/\lambda$ law is declared justified, yet no experiment varies $\lambda$; the predicted rise of the training loss in the revival era is shown nowhere; and the era boundaries in fig. 1 are placed with no rule described.

**[Attention and routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the central thesis: if the attention has discarded an informative token, no bounded downstream computation can bring it back; the necessity of $\mathcal{B}_ {\gamma}$ is proved by a representational bottleneck ($\Vert h(s)-h(s^{\prime})\Vert\leq(\alpha_{j_{0}}(s)+\alpha_{j_{0}}(s^{\prime}))\Delta_{\text{emb}}$ against the Lipschitz margin of the MLP), and the measurable indicator $D_{\text{KL}}(\alpha^{ * }\Vert\alpha)$ collapses *before* the rise of the test accuracy ([`p7-11`](#p7-11)). The analysis: appendix C proves only a pairwise condition on the *sum* of two masses, whereas $\mathcal{B}_ {\gamma}$ requires a threshold for each — the authors themselves call it a "convenient strengthening", so that the "if and only if" leans on an unproved side; the sufficiency rests entirely on assumption C.1 about a separated maximal margin (admitted twice); and the threshold $\gamma$ is expressed through the unmeasurable $c$, $L_{\max}$, $\Delta_{\text{emb}}$ — the $D_{\text{KL}}$ itself is measured, but the threshold it is supposed to cross is not.

**[Sparse subnetworks and the lucky ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — a reinterpretation of the observation of Minegishi et al.: a transferable winning ticket is not monolithic but decomposes into a *routing ticket* (a configuration of the attention satisfying $\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}$) and a *representation ticket* (the MLP weights satisfying $\mathcal{N}$); a "Bayesian ticket" — a fresh MLP initialisation plus a KL prior on the attention — matches or surpasses the transfer of a full lucky ticket ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). The analysis: the comparison is unequal — the Bayesian ticket makes use of oracle knowledge of the informative positions $\mathcal{I}^{ * }$, whereas the ticket of Minegishi is extracted from a trained model with no external knowledge, and the conclusion in the abstract does not state this; there is no comparison with any of the corpus's grokking accelerators (GrokFast, NeuralGrok, Muon); a mitigation — a teacher attention transfers almost the same speed-up, so an exact oracle is not obligatory.

**[The Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — the "Goldilocks" norm of the MLP enters the theorem in full as the condition $\mathcal{N}$ ($\Vert\theta_{\text{MLP}}\Vert_{F}\in[r_{\min},r_{\max}]$, bounding the Lipschitz constant), but is declared necessary and *insufficient*: a factorial experiment shows that under a norm fit (a projection onto the hypersphere $r_{\max}$) with an adversarial attention the accuracy stays at chance, while an oracle attention with no control of the norm gives only a partial generalisation — full generalisation appears only under both conditions at once ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)). The analysis: the necessity of $\mathcal{N}$ is derived by the phrase "standard generalisation bounds prescribe", with a reference to Liu and Notsawo — this is a citation, not a derivation (an upper bound on the norm does not follow from a zero test error); the $\lambda$ of the theory is an effective per-step decay rather than the AdamW hyperparameter $=1.0$ of table G, and the relation between them is nowhere written out; and appendix E declares the theory to be derived for a linear ReLU attention and "extends" it to softmax by a hypothesis, whereas the derivations D.1 and D.4.1 lean on the softmax Jacobian precisely.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — a measurable indicator and a near-causal check.** Five predictions are confirmed experimentally: a fall of the task gradient on the attention by orders of magnitude after the memorisation (the starvation of lemma 5.1); the collapse of $D_{\text{KL}}(\alpha^{ * }\Vert\alpha)$ before the rise of the accuracy; both sides of the separation taken apart in a factorial experiment; the linearity of the grokking time in $1/\beta$ down to a floor; and a measured gradient everywhere below the theoretical bound. The experiment with a frozen oracle attention (the network generalises far earlier) is the link closest to a causal check; and the self-correcting gradient of the KL penalty $\partial D_{\text{KL}}/\partial\ell_{j}=\alpha_{j}-\alpha^{ * }_ {j}$ is simple and right.

**An "if and only if" theorem with two unproved halves.** The necessity is proved only in the pairwise form (the sum of two masses), and the per-input strengthening $\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}$ is adopted for convenience; the sufficiency is assumption C.1; the necessity of $\mathcal{N}$ is a citation, not a derivation; the derivation of the KL speed-up runs the flow over the logits as though they were free variables, hiding the parameters inside $\tilde{\beta}$, and leans on a local Polyak–Łojasiewicz inequality with a full support, whereas the oracle is sparse; "the plateau is removed entirely" holds only in words: even at the strongest $\beta$ the time runs into a floor around 1500 epochs; and "a speed-up growing linearly with the strength of the penalty" describes an inverse, not a linear, dependence of the delay.

**What is not stated.** The "Bayesian ticket" uses an oracle while its rival does not; there are no comparisons with grokking accelerators; "the code is in the repository" comes without a link; and hypothesis E.1 is called "observationally complete" — a substitution of agreement for a proof — while its mechanism for entering era 3 (a spreading of the softmax towards the uniform) argues with the mechanism of §5.1 (a rise of the loss).

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that for grokking the attention must land on the informative tokens") — more precisely: in a single-layer single-head transformer with a fixed query on a $k$-sparse task the pairwise necessity is proved (the sum of the masses over a pair of sequences), while the sufficiency is adopted as an assumption.

###### p6-6

- **Phase 1: Fast Memorization ($t<t_{\text{mem}}$):** The MLP rapidly fits the data using dense, unaligned attention representations. $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ plummets to near zero.
- **Phase 2: The Explaining-Away Plateau ($t_{\text{mem}}\leq t<t_{c}$):** By Lemma 5.1, structural learning halts because $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}\approx 0$. Because the task gradient is negligible, the optimizer acts purely via weight decay, contracting the MLP norm geometrically: $\|\theta_{\text{MLP}}^{(t)}\|_{F}\leq\|\theta_{\text{mem}}\|_{F}(1-\lambda)^{t-t_{\text{mem}}}$.
- **Phase 3: Signal Resurgence ($t_{c}\leq t<t_{\text{grok}}$):** Weight decay finally erodes the MLP’s capacity below the $r_{\max}$ threshold required to sustain rote memorization. The training loss slightly rises, which reactivates the structural gradient (Eq. 4). The attention distribution $\alpha$ is pushed monotonically toward the oracle $\alpha^{*}$.
- **Phase 4: Grokking ($t\geq t_{\text{grok}}$):** The attention distribution crosses the necessary threshold ($\mathcal{B}_{\gamma}$) while the MLP norm remains optimal ($\mathcal{N}$). Test accuracy sharply transitions to $100\%$.

The overall grokking delay $\Delta t_{\text{grok}}$ is heavily dominated by the wait time in Phase 2. Solving the geometric weight decay contraction from Phase 2 for the time required to erode the parameters from their memorization norm down to the Goldilocks capacity shell ($r_{\max}$) yields a strict lower bound on the plateau duration:

$$
\Delta t_{\text{plateau}}\geq\frac{2}{\lambda}\log\left(\frac{\|\theta_{\text{mem}}\|_{F}}{r_{\max}}\right) \tag{5}
$$

###### p6-8

This mathematically grounds the inverse-weight-decay ($1/\lambda$) delay laws empirically observed in standard grokking literature [Liu et al., 2023, Lyu et al., 2024]. Structural alignment (Phase 3) cannot reliably begin until this wait is over.

###### p7-11

Concurrently, Figure 1 (Left) tracks test accuracy alongside the structural divergence $D_{\text{KL}}(\alpha^{*}\|\alpha)$. The attention posterior condenses before accuracy rises; test performance remains near chance until the structural divergence is small. The transition is therefore not caused by training loss alone. It occurs when the attention posterior has become sufficiently close to the oracle dependency graph.

###### p8-2

Figure 2 displays the ablations. Under Norm-Matching, the MLP is kept in the intended capacity shell, but test accuracy remains at chance when attention is forced onto distractors. Conversely, satisfying the structural condition with Oracle attention is not enough when the MLP norm is left unconstrained: the model improves, but does not reach the fully generalizing solution. Perfect generalization appears only when capacity control and structural routing are present together.

###### p9-1

Figure 4 demonstrates that the Bayesian Ticket matches or outperforms the full Lottery Ticket transfer across random initializations. Resolving the structural attention bottleneck directly bypasses the need for weight pruning entirely.
