## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the corpus **a measure that distinguishes training from test points** and one observation about its behaviour under grokking: a continuing improvement of the test error after a perfect training performance has been reached is accompanied by a divergence of $p^{train}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ and $p^{test}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$, whence the authors conclude that "grokking may reflect a *transition into* a measurably benign overfitting regime during training" ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). The hedge "may" is the authors' own and carries the whole conclusion: the observation is taken on two runs and speaks of a coincidence in time rather than of a cause. Its strength lies elsewhere — in being robust to a shift of the control quantities in both directions: the later the generalisation sets in, the later the measures diverge (at $\epsilon\in\{0.05,0.2,0.5\}$ and at $\alpha\in\{5,6,7\}$, [p. 27, para. 1](#p27-1), [fig. 11](#fig-11), [fig. 12](#fig-12)), and where there is no grokking there is no divergence either ([fig. 13](#fig-13)). Grokking here is placed in one row with epoch-wise double descent and read as a variety of benign overfitting rather than as a distinct phenomenon with a mechanism of its own. What the work does not contain: causality — there are no interventions forcibly bringing $p^{train}$ and $p^{test}$ together or apart, there is no forward prediction, and the measure is taken from the same runs; nor is there modular arithmetic — both settings are taken from [KBGP24] and [LMT22]; nor is there a mechanism — what happens inside the network on the plateau the work does not analyse.

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — here the work does more than in the case of grokking: it carries over to neural networks an explanation of double descent that the authors had earlier obtained for linear regression and trees. On CIFAR-10, as the width grows, $p^{train}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ increases monotonically while $p^{test}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ **decreases** beyond the interpolation threshold, whence it follows that larger networks do not necessarily learn a more complex prediction function for unseen examples, and "the apparent contradiction between deep double descent and the classical U-shaped curve" is removed ([p. 5, para. 5](#p5-5), [fig. 3](#fig-3)). It matters that $p^{test}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ is computable **without access to the test labels** — in this the measure differs from the corpus's other complexity measures. The same is repeated on MNIST and MNIST-1D ([p. 26, para. 2](#p26-2), [fig. 9](#fig-9), [fig. 10](#fig-10)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of epoch-wise double descent by experiments of its own — it is merely named as grokking's neighbour ([p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2)); and a carry-over to deep modern networks — all the experiments run on single-layer networks at $8\times8$ inputs.

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the work offers the corpus a way of using the tangent kernel **without giving up the variability of the features**: the linearisation is applied not once around $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}$, as in the lazy approximation, but anew at every step, and the updates are telescoped along the whole trajectory ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1), [fig. 1](#fig-1), equation 5). Whence its chief empirical value: the error of such an approximation is orders of magnitude smaller than that of an expansion around the initialisation, and the accuracy matches that of the actual network ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). Explicit forms of the kernel $K^{T}_{t}$ for momentum, weight decay, adaptive steps and an output sigmoid are derived here too ([p. 10, para. 1](#p10-1), [p. 10, para. 3](#p10-3), [p. 10, para. 4](#p10-4)), and an inter-temporal tangent kernel $K^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{t,k}$ is introduced. What the work does not contain: theory — no theorems about convergence and no conditions under which the approximation holds beyond a requirement that $||\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}||$ be small; the authors stipulate outright that this is a tool of (empirical) analysis rather than a way of training networks, and that its computation requires gradients for every training and test example at every step, which is "likely prohibitive" for large networks ([p. 3, para. 4](#p3-4)).

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the work embeds itself in this line through [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md), whose polynomial-regression experiment is reproduced exactly ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1)), and accepts their argument that neither weight decay nor adaptive optimisers can be the sole cause of grokking ([p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2)). Its own contribution to the notion is different: the telescoping model is built precisely as a step **beyond** the laziness assumption ("beyond the laziness assumption", [p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)), and by its own means it is shown that a passage into the lazy regime is sufficient for linear mode connectivity ([p. 8, para. 4](#p8-4)). That is, laziness here is not an explanation of grokking but a working assumption to be departed from. What the work does not contain: a measurement of the transition from lazy to rich dynamics itself — neither the change of the kernel over time as a quantity nor an alignment of the kernel with the task; the word "rich" does not appear in the work at all, and "feature learning" occurs only in retellings of others' explanations.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the initialisation scale serves here as the chief lever switching the observed phenomenon on and off, and the work adds to it **a measurable consequence**. A large initialisation $\alpha\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}$ (in the main text $\alpha=6$, in the appendix $\alpha=5$ and $7$) gives very large initial predictions $|f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}}(\mathbf{x})|\!\gg\!1$, and, since no sigmoid is applied, "the model has to learn that all $y_{i}\!\in\![0,1]$" — a large $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}$ is called "a very poor inductive bias for this task" ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1)). The guess that the better the inductive bias, the less complex the component learnt from the data is stated **before** the experiment and confirmed: at a standard initialisation the generalising solution is found within $10^{2}$ steps instead of $10^{5}$, and substantially fewer effective parameters are used, fewest of all with the sigmoid added ([fig. 13](#fig-13), [p. 27, para. 2](#p27-2)). What the work does not contain: a sweep of intermediate scales, a separation of the contributions of "a large norm" and "the absence of a sigmoid", and any claim about the cause of the delay beyond this particular explanation through a mismatch of ranges.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work takes the dispute about the direction in which the weight norm moves as an **occasion**: it places side by side the explanation of [LMT22], in which $||\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}||$ *contracts* late in the training, and the experiment of [KBGP24], in which the norm *grows*, and reproduces both ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)). Its contribution to the notion is negative with respect to the norm and positive with respect to the measure proposed: the divergence of $p^{train}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ and $p^{test}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ is observed in **both** differently constructed settings — that is, an axis of measurement is proposed that does not depend on which way the norm goes. What the work does not contain: the dispute about the norm is neither settled nor even discussed in substance — the weight norm is measured in no experiment, neither of the two readings is contested, and no relation between $p^{0}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ and $||\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}||$ is established.

**[Weight decay / L2 regularisation](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — a part of the work useful in its own right: for weight decay an explicit form of the linearised functional update is derived, from which it can be seen that it "effectively weakens that example's previous contributions", and a comparison of signs in equations 12 and 11 reveals that **momentum can balance the influence of weight decay** on the learnt updates in function space, whereupon weight decay acts chiefly through the term that weakens the initial weights $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}$ ([p. 10, para. 1](#p10-1)). This is a place, rare in the corpus, in which weight decay is analysed not as a device but as a correction to the kernel. In the experiments $\lambda=.1$ is taken from [LMT22] unchanged ([p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)). What the work does not contain: not a single experiment varying $\lambda$; the question of whether weight decay is necessary for grokking is not posed, and the conclusion of section 5 is nowhere related to the observations of section 4.1.

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work retells the argument of [KBGP24] that weight decay and adaptive optimisers "cannot be the sole cause of grokking" ([p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2)) and supports it with its own set of settings: in the polynomial-regression experiment grokking is obtained by full-batch gradient descent with no regularisation ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1)), whereas in the MNIST experiment weight decay is present ([p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)), and the divergence of the measures is visible in both. What the work does not contain: an intervention of its own — regularisation is neither switched on nor off, and the authors make no claim about the necessity or superfluity of weight decay.

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the work makes two different contributions to the notion. The first is general: adaptive and parameter-dependent steps are built into the kernel, and $\boldsymbol{\phi}_{t}$ receives a reading as "a rescaling of the relative influence of the features" on the tangent kernel ([p. 10, para. 3](#p10-3)). The second is a methodological caveat important for reading every experiment: the quality of the approximation depends on the optimiser, and "Adam(W) naturally makes larger updates because of the rescaling and therefore requires a smaller $\gamma$ to secure the quality of the approximation than SGD does" ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). It is precisely this that forced a change to the experiment of [LMT22], which runs under AdamW. What the work does not contain: a comparison of optimisers by their influence on the phenomenon itself — Adam and SGD are nowhere set against each other as causes or removers of grokking, and the explanation of [TLZ+22] through adaptive optimisers is merely mentioned in the survey.

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — here the learning rate acts as a **condition of the tool's applicability** rather than as a lever of the phenomenon: $\gamma$ "controls the quality of the approximation, since it determines $||\Delta\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}||$" ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1)). Whence a forced change to the Omnigrok experiment: instead of $10^{-3}$, $\gamma=10^{-4}$ is taken and a linear warm-up over the first 100 batches is added, "to ensure that the weight updates are small enough for the quality of the telescoping approximation" ([p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)). The authors name the side consequence themselves: "the grokking phenomenon appears visually less sharp, since perfect training performance is reached later in the training" ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)). What the work does not contain: a dependence of the delay on $\gamma$ — the step is lowered for the sake of the approximation rather than studied as an intervention.

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — all the work's experiments run on vision datasets and on tabular data, but in a heavily reduced form, and this limits the weight of the conclusions: CIFAR-10 (cats versus dogs) and MNIST (3 versus 5) are converted to greyscale, reduced to $d=8\times 8$ and cut down to $n=1000$ training examples ([p. 22, para. 5](#p22-5), [p. 22, para. 6](#p22-6), [p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)). Of the larger settings only a ResNet-20 on CIFAR-10 in the linear-mode-connectivity experiment remains ([p. 24, para. 6](#p24-6)) — but there no telescoping model is built and only changes of the gradients are measured. What the work does not contain: grokking on full-size images, on linguistic data or anywhere outside binary classification with a squared loss — the definition of the measure requires a single output ($k=1$) and a squared loss ([p. 4, para. 5](#p4-5)).

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — label noise here is a **condition for the visibility of double descent** rather than a device inducing delayed generalisation. On MNIST without noise there is no worsening of the test error at any hidden size, and only with $20\%$ of labels flipped does the double-descent shape arise ([p. 22, para. 6](#p22-6), [p. 26, para. 2](#p26-2), [fig. 9](#fig-9)); on MNIST-1D $15\%$ noise is taken ([fig. 10](#fig-10)). It matters that the divergence of the effective parameters on training and on test is observed in those same runs, that is, the measure behaves the same under fitting to noise and under a clean fit. What the work does not contain: an analysis of how exactly the noise changes $p^{0}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$, and experiments with noise in the grokking settings.

**[The loss landscape / basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the third case study is entirely about loss barriers between solutions: it is shown that a passage into a regime of constant gradients is **sufficient** for linear mode connectivity under a mild assumption that an ensemble of two networks performs no worse than the individual models ([p. 8, para. 4](#p8-4), section B.2). The empirical half is a reproduction of the setting of [FDRC20] on a ResNet-20: the disappearance of the loss barrier coincides with the time at which the gradients become more stable across all the layers, most markedly at the linear output layer ([p. 9, para. 1](#p9-1), [fig. 6](#fig-6)). In the same place the work notes the limit of its own explanation: changes in the other gradients continue, and in a pretrained model the BatchNorm parameters change **more**, so the relation "exists but cannot explain it fully" ([p. 9, para. 2](#p9-2)). What the work does not contain: any relation of this case to grokking — the three case studies are united only by the telescoping formulation itself.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the claim in the abstract ("measures that help to **predict** and understand a priori unexpected performance of neural networks", [p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2), repeated on [p. 2, para. 1](#p2-1)) reads as a promise of a progress measure, and it is sometimes retold as such. The work does not bear it out: $p^{0}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ is in none of the three cases computed **before** the phenomenon is observed and is not used for a forecast; in every experiment the measure is plotted on the same graph as the error curves and compared with them after the fact. The term "progress measure" does not appear in the work, and Nanda et al. are mentioned only in a line of the survey. The nearest thing to a prediction is the second case study, in which the kernel norm $||\mathbf{k}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{t}(\mathbf{x})||_{2}$ "can predict differences in performance caused by irregularities in the dataset" ([p. 7, para. 3](#p7-3), [fig. 5](#fig-5)), but this too is tracking rather than a forecast ahead.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work **does not use** the canonical proving ground: there is no modular arithmetic, no binary operation table and no fraction of training data in it at all. [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) are cited only as the source of the phenomenon and as an occasion ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6), [p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2)). In place of the canon two non-algorithmic settings are taken: the polynomial regression of [KBGP24] at $d=100$, $n_{train}=550$ and a binary classification of 3 versus 5 on MNIST at $n=1000$ and $8\times8$ inputs ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1), [p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)). The reason for the choice is instrumental rather than substantive: the measure is defined only for a single output and a squared loss, and the approximation requires small steps. It matters for the corpus that the second setting reproduces [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) **inexactly** (see below), whereas the first — [Kumar et al.](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — is reproduced, by the authors' statement, exactly.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The promised NeurIPS paper checklist is absent from the paper.** The introduction to the appendices promises: "The NeurIPS paper checklist is included after the appendices" ([p. 17, para. 1](#p17-1)). The PDF has 29 pages, the last of them occupied by figure 14, and there is no checklist after the appendices. The discrepancy is present in the published PDF, so it is not an artefact of the conversion.

**One and the same column of figure 4 is called by two different names.** In the main text and in the figure caption it is "column (3)" ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)), while in appendix C.1 it is "Panel (C)" ([p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)). The reference is to the same experiment (MNIST at $\alpha=1$, with and without a sigmoid), but in citing it one must choose a name.

**"Full-batch gradient descent" with a batch smaller than the training set.** For the experiment of [KBGP24] it is written that "we train by full-batch gradient descent with $\gamma=B=500$" at $n_{train}=550$ ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1)): the full batch at such a set ought to equal 550 rather than 500. The same expression simultaneously sets the learning rate at $\gamma=500$; the setting of [KBGP24] does indeed require large learning rates, but which of the two numbers refers to what cannot be checked from the text.

**In section B.1 one and the same symbol is defined twice with different dimensions.** After equation 19 stands "with $\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{S}_{0}=\mathbf{0}^{p\times n}$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{S}_{0}=\mathbf{0}^{p\times 1}$": by the sense the second initial condition pertains to $\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{C}_{0}$ rather than to $\tilde{\mathbf{U}}^{S}_{0}$. In the same place and in equation 21 the update vector introduced in equation 16 as $\mathbf{U}^{C}_{t}$ is called $\mathbf{C}^{S}_{t}$. Both slips are preserved in the translation as they stand; in reproducing the computations one should rely on equation 16. These inconsistencies are absent from the paper's claims file — they were found while checking the card.

**In section B.2 the notation diverges from the main text.** The ensemble there is defined as $\bar{f}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})=\alpha f_{\alpha\boldsymbol{\theta}^{t^{\prime}}_{1T}}(\mathbf{x})+(1-\alpha)f_{\alpha\boldsymbol{\theta}^{t^{\prime}}_{2T}}(\mathbf{x})$ — with a superfluous $\alpha$ in the subscripts — whereas in the main text the same ensemble is written as $\textstyle\bar{f}^{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{:=}\alpha f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{t^{\prime}}_{1T}}(\mathbf{x})+(1-\alpha)f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{t^{\prime}}_{2T}}(\mathbf{x})$ ([p. 8, para. 4](#p8-4)). In equation 22 the gradient symbol has vanished from the second term ($f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t^{*}}}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}$ instead of $\nabla_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}f_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t^{*}}}(\mathbf{x})^{\top}$), and the models, numbered $j\in\{1,2\}$ everywhere, are numbered in the same place "for each $j\in\{0,1\}$". These are slips, but they stand in the paper's only formal derivation.

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

The work is made up of three layers of differing solidity, and when citing it matters which layer a claim comes from.

**Derived.** The telescoping formulation (equation 5) is an identity plus a first-order approximation at every step; from it are obtained by algebra the form of the smoother $\tilde{f}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{s}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}}(\mathbf{x})\mathbf{y}+c^{0}_{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}}(\mathbf{x})$ ([p. 4, para. 5](#p4-5), section B.1), the explicit kernels for momentum, weight decay, adaptive steps and the sigmoid ([p. 10, para. 1](#p10-1), [p. 10, para. 3](#p10-3), [p. 10, para. 4](#p10-4)), and the sufficiency of a constant-gradient regime for linear mode connectivity under the ensemble assumption ([p. 8, para. 4](#p8-4), section B.2). This is the work's only formal part, and it is not about grokking.

**Measured.** The quality of the approximation: the error of the telescoping model is orders of magnitude smaller than that of a linearisation around $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}$, and its accuracy matches that of the actual network ([fig. 2](#fig-2), [fig. 7](#fig-7), [fig. 8](#fig-8)) — at a small $\gamma$ and at 4 seeds per setting. The decrease of $p^{test}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ beyond the interpolation threshold on CIFAR-10, on MNIST with label noise and on MNIST-1D ([fig. 3](#fig-3), [fig. 9](#fig-9), [fig. 10](#fig-10)). The coincidence of the divergence of the measures with the grokking in two settings and its shift following the control quantities ([fig. 4](#fig-4), [fig. 11](#fig-11), [fig. 12](#fig-12)), and its absence where there is no grokking ([fig. 13](#fig-13)). The coincidence of the disappearance of the loss barrier with the stabilisation of the gradients ([fig. 6](#fig-6)). And the tracking of the performance gap between the neural network and the GBT by the kernel norm as the fraction of irregular examples grows ([fig. 5](#fig-5)).

**Declared a conjecture in so many words.** The explanation of the differences on tabular data: "This leads us to hypothesise that this difference may turn out to be able to explain the observation of [MKV+23]…" ([p. 7, para. 3](#p7-3)). The guess about the inductive bias: "One might expect that the better the inductive bias… To check whether this notion is measurable…" ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1)) — this one, unlike the first, is stated before the experiment and confirmed. The conclusion about grokking is hedged in its very formulation: "grokking **may** reflect a transition into a measurably benign overfitting regime" ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)).

**Acknowledged by the authors as the limit of the explanation.** The stabilisation of the gradients "cannot explain [linear mode connectivity] fully"; in a pretrained model the BatchNorm parameters change more rather than less ([p. 9, para. 2](#p9-2)). The tool is declared unsuitable for training networks and probably prohibitively expensive for large models ([p. 3, para. 4](#p3-4)). The conclusion expressly limits the scope: the authors "deliberately restricted ourselves to particular, notable empirical examples" ([p. 10, para. 6](#p10-6)).

**Changed relative to the source being reproduced.** The experiment of [LMT22] is reproduced **inexactly**, and the caveat about this is carried into a footnote: the task is remade into a binary "3 versus 5" on $8\times8$ images at $n=1000$, the learning rate is lowered tenfold, and a warm-up over 100 batches is added — with the consequence named by the authors themselves: "the grokking phenomenon appears visually less sharp" ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6), [p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)). The experiment of [KBGP24] is reproduced, by the authors' statement, exactly ([p. 23, para. 1](#p23-1)).

**Not measured at all.** Causality: there are no interventions bringing $p^{train}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ and $p^{test}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ apart or together; the forward prediction promised by the word "predict" in the abstract appears in none of the three cases. The comparability of the values of $p^{0}_{\hat{\mathbf{s}}}$ across different settings is not discussed — only the shapes of the curves are compared. The number of seeds is small: 4 or 5 per setting.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

Editorial notes on the places where this work conveys others' results with an error or without the authors' caveats. The "details" links from the "Erroneous retellings and citations of this paper" sections of the cited cards lead here.

###### mc-2210-01117

**Liu et al. (2210.01117) — the LU mechanism reduced to weight decay.** In the main text: `"While [LMT22] attribute this to weight decay causing $||\boldsymbol{\theta}_{t}||$ to shrink late in training – which they demonstrate on an MNIST example using unusually large $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}$"`. In the survey of appendix A the same thought is given more briefly and already without the condition: `"[LMT22] attribute grokking to the effects of weight decay setting in later in training"` ([p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2)).

The explanation of Omnigrok does not consist in that. There grokking is derived from a **mismatch of the reduced training and test losses as functions of the weight norm** — the "L" and "U" mechanism — and weight decay in it is merely the force that moves the weights from the region of large norm into the Goldilocks zone. In the formulation of Jeffares et al. the mechanism disappears and its consequence ("the norm falls") takes the place of the cause: this is exactly the pattern the [card on Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) warns about ("a decrease of the weight norm" as a universal mechanism of grokking). It mitigates matters that in the main text the condition of a large initialisation is preserved — and that it is precisely that setting the authors then reproduce — whereas in appendix A the caveat is lost entirely. The other references to the grokking literature in the same paragraph ([p. 18, para. 2](#p18-2)) are accurate, including the argument of [KBGP24] that weight decay and adaptive optimisers cannot be the sole cause.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. For each it is said what exactly is distorted, with references to the authors' paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. There are as yet no `mc-2411-00247` anchors in the citing cards, so the detailed analyses are not yet addressed and the citing works are listed by simple links.

**"Jeffares et al. explain grokking by phase transitions in local complexity („delayed robustness“)" (erroneous).** AlQuabeh et al. 2025 write in a survey of explanations of grokking: `"jeffares2024deep attribute it to phase transitions in local complexity (“delayed robustness”), while others link it to circuit efficiency"`. Neither "local complexity", nor "delayed robustness", nor phase transitions appear in this work: all three belong to [Humayun et al. 2024](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md), who introduce local complexity as the density of linear regions and "delayed robustness" as a distinct form of grokking. In Jeffares et al. a different quantity is measured — the effective parameters of the smoother, distinguishing training from test points — and the conclusion is hedged: grokking "**may** reflect a transition into a measurably benign overfitting regime" ([p. 5, para. 6](#p5-6)), and the work nowhere speaks of robustness to adversarial examples. Work concerned: AlQuabeh et al. 2025 (`2505.15624`, no card as yet).

**"Lowering the initialisation scale is a method from this work" (widening).** Nam et al. 2025, enumerating ways of taking a model out of the lazy regime, write: `"Alternatively, we can decrease $\Sigma_{0}$ by downscaling initialization (Jeffares et al. 2024)"`. The observed relation is indeed present in Jeffares et al.: at a standard initialisation ($\alpha=1$) there is no grokking, the generalisation sets in within $10^{2}$ steps instead of $10^{5}$, and the learnt complexity is markedly lower ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1), [fig. 13](#fig-13)). But the initialisation scale as a lever was not introduced here: the setting with an inflated $\alpha\boldsymbol{\theta}_{0}$ is taken from [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md), and in Jeffares et al. the standard initialisation serves as a **comparison column** for checking the guess about the inductive bias rather than as a proposed way of removing grokking.
