## Concepts covered

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work carries this line of the corpus through to an exact statement. Previously the weight norm served as an explanation ("weight decay pushes towards a smaller norm"); here something **much stronger** is proved: after the memorisation the training proceeds not merely in *some* direction of decreasing norm but along the direction that lowers the norm **the most** while staying on the manifold of zero loss ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). In other words, training after memorisation is gradient descent on the weight norm **constrained** to the zero-loss set.

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the main technical result is geometric: **theorem 4.14** asserts that near the zero-loss set $\mathcal{Z}$ the gradient of the loss becomes orthogonal to every tangent direction ([`p6-10`](#p6-10), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). Hence a direct consequence: near $\mathcal{Z}$ the gradient of the loss **causes no motion at all** — the loss merely holds the model at the manifold, while weight decay is free to lead it towards the minimum of the norm along the tangents ([`p6-14`](#p6-14)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the role of this factor is named precisely: it does not speed the generalisation up or slow it down as a side effect but **is** the driving force of the whole post-memorisation phase. Moreover, the smaller $\lambda$ is, the closer the trajectory keeps to zero loss — and the longer it takes to reach generalisation ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)). The limit $\lambda\to 0$ is taken not for convenience but as an approximation to practice ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)).

**[The naive minimisation of the loss](../../concepts/nlm.md)** — theorem 4.8 closes the question of whether the model stays on the manifold: for every trajectory beginning at a zero-loss solution and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a $\lambda_{\varepsilon}$ at which the trajectory never departs further than $\varepsilon$ ([`p5-13`](#p5-13)). The proof is simple and therefore convincing: the gradient flow never increases $\mathcal{L}(\theta)+\lambda\|\theta\|^{2}$, and both terms are non-negative ([`p5-14`](#p5-14)).

**[Singular points and regularity](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — the second support of the argument: to speak of tangents, the zero-loss set must be a manifold rather than something with singularities. **Theorem 4.10**: for *almost every* dataset there will be no singular points in $\mathcal{Z}$ ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). The proof leans on the **Morse–Sard theorem**: the image of the critical points has Lebesgue measure zero ([`p6-5`](#p6-5)). This is a rare case in the corpus in which "almost never" is a measure rather than a turn of phrase.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the final check is set up on modular addition, and the result agrees with the picture of Nanda et al. on three independent counts: the norms of the real and the imaginary parts of the Fourier features **equalise** (circles of the same size), those parts become **orthogonal** (circles in orthogonal planes), and the absolute values diverge across frequencies (each circle engaging its own subset of hidden units) ([`p10-11`](#p10-11), [`fig-6`](#fig-6)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the setting is deliberately small: a two-layer network, $p=37$, a hidden dimension of 512, ReLU, 70 % of the data for training ([`p10-7`](#p10-7)). It matters that the authors **do not train** the network: they simulate the evolution of the embedding matrix by the derived formula and see whether it reproduces grokking ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)).

**[Representation learning in separate parts of a network](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the work's second contribution is separate and transferable: an approximation that **decouples** the dynamics of some of the parameters from the rest of the network. The slow part $\theta_{1}$ is treated as leading and the fast part $\theta_{2}$ as instantaneously adjusting to it ([`p7-8`](#p7-8)); the dynamics of $\theta_{1}$ is then the gradient flow of some cost function ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)). For a two-layer network this yields a **closed-form expression** for the dynamics of the embedding layer ([`p9-1`](#p9-1), [`p9-2`](#p9-2)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the most vivid demonstration in the corpus: **a linear model with two weights groks addition from a single example** $1+1=2$. The training loss falls within a dozen steps and the test loss within hundreds, while the weights travel smoothly from $(0,2)$ to $(1,1)$ ([`p2-9`](#p2-9), [`p3-3`](#p3-3), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). The delay here requires neither non-linearity nor a high dimension — only two phases.

**[The lazy-to-rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the picture is two-phase, but the phases differ from those of that line: first a minimisation of the loss down to $\mathcal{Z}$, then a motion **along** $\mathcal{Z}$ towards the minimum of the norm ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)). The concurrent work of Boursier et al. (2026) obtains the same separation through a Riemannian gradient flow on the manifold of critical points ([`p2-7`](#p2-7)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no cross-entropy loss — the whole theory is for the mean squared error ([`p11-1`](#p11-1)). Nor are there networks deeper than two layers in the decoupled-dynamics part. And there is no explanation of **why** the minimum-norm solution turns out to be the generalising one: it is shown where the training moves, but not why the destination of that motion is good.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is mathematical: three theorems, one approximation and one check by simulation.

**The main theorem is stated precisely and proved.** The orthogonality of the loss gradient to the tangent directions near $\mathcal{Z}$ ([`p6-10`](#p6-10)) is exactly the statement needed for "the minimisation of the norm on the manifold" to cease being a metaphor. The proof runs through a Taylor expansion and leans on the equality of the tangent space with the kernel of the Hessian ([`p6-12`](#p6-12), [`p6-13`](#p6-13)).

**The regularity is justified by a measure rather than by convenience.** Singular points could bring the whole argument down; instead of forbidding them, the authors show that they arise only on a set of target outputs of Lebesgue measure zero ([`p6-3`](#p6-3), [`p6-5`](#p6-5)). This is the right way to handle degenerate cases.

**The caveat about ReLU is made honestly but not carried through.** Real networks are non-smooth, and the authors admit it: ReLU breaks the loss into smooth regions with non-smooth boundaries, and the non-smooth points also form a set of measure zero ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). Assumption 4.2, however, requires a realisation function that is **$d-km+1$ times continuously differentiable** ([`p5-6`](#p5-6)), which ReLU does not satisfy — and all the empirical checks are meanwhile carried out with ReLU.

**The toy examples make the work a rarity for the corpus.** A two-parameter linear model grokking addition from a single example ([`p2-9`](#p2-9)) and a three-parameter one with a plane of zero loss ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)) are the most transparent illustrations of the phenomenon in the wiki. They are also a warning: if grokking is reproduced by two weights, then explanations requiring subnetworks or circuits are not necessary for it.

**The decoupled-dynamics approximation is a strong move with an unjustified assumption.** That $\theta_{2}$ can be parametrised by a function of $\theta_{1}$ ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)) rests on the absence of self-intersections of the trajectory, and that is justified by a reference to **Brownian motion** and Gaussian random fields ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)). A gradient flow is not a random process, and carrying results about multiple points over here is an analogy rather than an argument.

**The approximation's second assumption is stronger still.** "$\theta_{2}$ is optimal for the current $\theta_{1}$" ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)) means that the second layer adjusts to the first instantaneously. This is the very content of the approximation, and in the experiment it is **imposed by force**: $W$ is recomputed by the formula at every step, whereupon the training loss is identically zero ([`p10-6`](#p10-6)).

**The final check is not a check of training.** The authors write outright that their aim is not to train a network but to see whether the approximate dynamics reproduces the observed phenomena ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)). It does: a delay of about 500–2000 steps at zero loss and the Fourier structure of the embeddings ([`p10-8`](#p10-8), [`p10-11`](#p10-11)). But this is a simulation of a derived formula rather than a comparison with a real training run — the similarity to a real trajectory is measured separately, in section 4.6, and by a different quantity.

**The empirical check of the theory is careful but narrow.** The cosine proximity of the parameter update to the norm-minimising direction is measured on **five** networks with $n=11$ and $h=128$ ([`p18-3`](#p18-3)), the smallness of the networks being chosen so that the Jacobian is computable. The result is substantive and pleasing: the proximity is **greatest exactly during the grokking** ([`fig-5`](#fig-5)).

**The concurrent work is named outright.** Boursier et al. (2026) obtain the same answer to the first question through a Riemannian gradient flow ([`p2-7`](#p2-7)). The authors neither conceal the coincidence nor dispute priority — for the corpus this means the result is independently confirmed.

**Its place in the corpus.** The work turns "weight decay leads to generalisation" from an observation into a theorem about constrained optimisation: after the memorisation the training is a projected gradient descent on the weight norm. The second contribution is separate and perhaps more useful: a device for decoupling the dynamics of one part of a network, which for the two-layer case yields a closed-form formula. The price: a mean-squared loss, smoothness, two layers and the assumption that the second layer adjusts instantaneously.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2210-01117
**Omnigrok (2210.01117) — the caveat about inducement and the elevation of weight decay into the principal cause.** Two inaccuracies. (1) `"was later shown to also happen in real-world datasets (Liu et al., 2022b; Humayun et al., 2024)"`: "happen" presents grokking as something met with, whereas in the cited work it is induced — ["in a slightly non-standard setting where it is relatively weak"](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#p1-9). (2) `"weight decay is mainly responsible for driving the delayed generalization (Liu et al., 2022b)"` — and in the same place, in assumption 4.5, `"a small $\lambda$ is essential for the delayed-generalization phenomona"`: in the original authors' account weight decay is one knob among others alongside the initialisation scale, the data volume and the norm constraint, and they themselves limited the generality of the mechanism — [the "U" shape is better regarded as empirically widespread than as provably universal](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#sec-6). The universal reading is refuted: [2310.06110](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md), section 3 — grokking at a growing weight norm; [2506.05718](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.card.md) — a large initialisation with a non-zero weight decay does not necessarily give grokking.

###### mc-2301-02679
**Gromov (2301.02679) — "embeddings on a circle" ascribed to him.** `"interpretability research has revealed that neural networks achieve generalization by placing the embedding vectors on a circle (Gromov, 2023; Zhong et al., 2024)"`: in Gromov's setting [there is no learnable encoder at all — and grokking is present all the same](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md#p9-9); this is his own counterexample to explanations through representation learning. The periodic structure in his account lives [in the weights of the first layer](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md#p4-9); circular embeddings are the setting of Liu et al. and Zhong et al., and the grouping of the references carries it over onto a work in which there are no embeddings.
