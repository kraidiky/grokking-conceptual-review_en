## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the corpus the first geometric accompaniment of grokking: it neither explains it nor measures it anew, but shows which phases of the behaviour of the partition of the input space memorisation and generalisation fall on. Grokking here is induced by the technique of Liu et al. 2022 — multiplying the initial weights of a fully connected network by $8$ when training on MNIST ([p8-2](#p8-2)) — and the chief claim is this: the memorisation falls on the phase of the rise of the local complexity, and the generalisation on its second descent ([fig. 9](#fig-9)). From the same place come two observations found nowhere else in the corpus: under induced grokking the first descent is practically absent, the rise proceeding from the very start of the training ([p8-2](#p8-2)), and immediately before the onset of the delayed generalisation a **second rise** of the local complexity is visible ([p9-1](#p9-1), [fig. 8](#fig-8)). What the work does not contain: causality — a coincidence in time is shown, and there is no intervention that would change the local complexity independently and check the consequences for grokking; nor are there algorithmic tasks, although grokking is defined after Power et al. 2022 precisely on them; nor is there a number of seeds, a number of runs or an analysis of how the second rise looks beyond the three curves shown — the authors themselves call their reading a "possible indication" ([p9-1](#p9-1)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work introduces **local complexity** (LC): a quantity depending only on the architecture and the weights of the network and requiring neither a dataset, nor labels, nor a loss function ([p2-3](#p2-3)). It is computed in a single forward pass: around a point the convex hull of $2P$ vertices $conv(\lbrace x\pm{\boldsymbol{v}}_{p}:p=1,2...,P\rbrace)=conv({\boldsymbol{V}})$ is taken over orthonormal directions, and the number of units of a layer whose pre-activations over the vertices are not all of one sign is counted, summed over the layers ([p4-3](#p4-3), [p4-1](#p4-1)). It is precisely this measure — rather than the observations about grokking — that turned out to be the work's chief legacy: in this form it was inherited by [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md), and through it entered the corpus. What the work does not contain: the term "progress measure" itself — neither Barak et al. 2022 nor Nanda et al. 2023 are cited here, and the authors claim no connection with that notion; nor is there an estimate of the approximation error — for the deeper layers the measure counts intersections of hyperplanes with the hull of the **embedded** vertices rather than with a hull in the input space, and the magnitude of that substitution is nowhere bounded ([p4-4](#p4-4)).

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — the work carries the term over to a new carrier: what is called "double descent" is the shape of the local-complexity curve over training time — a descent after the initialisation, a rise, a second descent ([p1-2](#p1-2), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). The shape is checked against an exact count of the regions via SplineCAM: both curves give the same double descent ([p4-6](#p4-6), [fig. 12](#fig-12)). Classical double descent is meanwhile mentioned in the abstract as an example of a phenomenon introduced through the loss function ([p1-2](#p1-2)) — that is, the authors know of it and take the word deliberately. What the work does not contain: a caveat that the sense is a different one. A non-monotonicity of the risk in capacity or in time is nowhere measured and is not compared with the local-complexity curve, and the coincidence of names is not justified in any way.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives the plateau a geometric signature on the input-space side: the phase of the rise of the local complexity is a crowding of nonlinearities around the training points, and under induced grokking it falls exactly on the memorisation ([p8-2](#p8-2), [fig. 9](#fig-9)). The argument for relating the rise to overfitting is assembled from two sweeps: an increase in the number of honestly labelled samples accelerates the generalisation and gradually removes the rise phase ([p9-3](#p9-3), [fig. 10](#fig-10)), while an increase in the number of randomly labelled ones, on the contrary, lengthens it and raises the peak ([p9-4](#p9-4), [fig. 11](#fig-11)). What the work does not contain: a measure of what has been memorised as such — the "need for memorisation" is set only by the volume of randomly labelled data; there is no separation into memorising and generalising components, and from a coincidence in time a conclusion about a relation to overfitting is drawn ([p9-3](#p9-3)) with no intervention that would check it.

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the whole work stands on real data: MNIST with fully connected networks (1K training samples, 10k validation and 10k random points as measurement sites) and CIFAR10 with convolutional networks (2K) and ResNet18 (1K) ([p5-2](#p5-2)). There are no algorithmic tasks at all. This is what makes the work the predecessor of the whole line of "grokking beyond algorithmic data" in its geometric rendering: the proving ground on which local complexity was later measured is set here. What the work does not contain: any setting larger than CIFAR10, any linguistic setting, any sweep over the number of runs; the band in every figure is "a confidence interval corresponding to $25$% of the standard deviation of LC" ([p5-2](#p5-2)), that is, the spread over measurement points rather than over runs.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the technique of Liu et al. 2022 is used as the sole way of obtaining grokking: the parameters of a fully connected network are multiplied by $8$ at initialisation ([p8-2](#p8-2)). The work adds to the technique an observation of its own: under such a launch the first descent of the local complexity is practically absent, and the rise proceeds from the very start of the training ([p8-2](#p8-2)) — that is, an increased norm of the initial weights changes not only the performance curves but the geometry of the partition. What the work does not contain: any sweep of the multiplier or any dependence on it; nor is there a setting in which grokking would be observed without this technique — that will appear only in [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — weight decay here is not a condition of the phenomenon but one of the two levers that damp the migration of regions: in training a CNN of depth 5 and width 32, an increase of the weight decay delays or removes the second descent, shortens the rise phase and lowers its peak ([p7-3](#p7-3), [fig. 7](#fig-7)). The mechanism is named in a single sentence: weight decay reduces the norm of the weights, and with it the norm of the slope per region of the partition ([p7-3](#p7-3)). In all the other experiments the weight decay is fixed at $0.01$ ([p5-3](#p5-3)). What the work does not contain: the values in the sweep, an analysis of how the reduction of the norm relates to the migration of the regions, and an experiment with no weight decay at all — the zero point of the sweep is not shown.

**[Varieties of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — the work's most substantive negative result: batch normalisation removes the second descent in every setting shown and at the same time **raises** the overall level of the local complexity — on the training, validation and random points at once ([p7-2](#p7-2), [fig. 4](#fig-4), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). It is thereby directly opposed to weight decay, which also removes the second descent but lowers the local complexity ([p7-3](#p7-3)). The explanation is taken ready-made from Balestriero & Baraniuk 2022: normalisation moves the boundaries of the partition layer by layer right up against the training data, whereupon the departure of the regions towards the decision boundary is suppressed ([p7-2](#p7-2)). What the work does not contain: a derivation of this claim of its own — it is given as already proved in another work; nor is there an analysis of why the overall level of the measure rises at the same time.

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — random labels are used as a knob separating two directions in the volume of data: with honest labels a growth of the set removes the rise phase, with random ones it lengthens it and raises the peak of the local complexity ([p9-4](#p9-4), [fig. 11](#fig-11)). This is the work's most substantive sweep: both branches are obtained on one setup and differ only in the nature of the labels. What the work does not contain: mixed noise fractions — the labels are either all genuine or all random; there is no dependence on the fraction of corrupted labels, nor any check that with random labels the network actually reaches interpolation.

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the size of the training set serves as a lever that changes the phase picture: the more honestly labelled samples, the stronger and earlier the generalisation and the weaker the peak of the rise ([p9-2](#p9-2), [p9-3](#p9-3), [fig. 10](#fig-10)). What the work does not contain: no fraction of a table or dataset as a control quantity in the sense of Power et al., no critical size, no threshold and no estimate of one; absolute numbers of samples are swept, and those numbers themselves are not named in the text — they are visible only in the figures.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — a mention, and no more: the introduction says that most studies of training dynamics find phase transitions at some moment of the training (with a reference to Papyan et al. 2020), and that some show how those dynamics may be stretched — for instance, making generalisation arise long after the convergence of the training loss (Power et al. 2022) ([p2-2](#p2-2)). The language of transition is not applied to the authors' own three phases of local complexity: there is no order parameter, no transition point and no use of the word "transition" in the description of their own curves — the phases are called phases and no more. In [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md) the same curves will be declared a phase transition; this formulation cannot be credited to the present work.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work takes from the proving ground of Power et al. only the definition of the phenomenon: grokking is described as a marked generalisation after overfitting on small algorithmic tasks such as modular addition ([p8-2](#p8-2)). Beyond that the proving ground is reproduced in no part: neither the table of operations, nor the split fraction, nor the transformer — in their place stand MNIST, a fully connected network and the technique of Liu et al. 2022, while from the settings there a mean-squared error over one-hot vectors is inherited, taken for comparability with the non-grokking experiments ([p5-3](#p5-3)). It matters for the corpus that a work cited as the geometry of grokking never once computed local complexity on the canonical task.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The analysis of the claims (the file `.claims.txt` alongside) adds up to this picture: a strong descriptive part, a weak explanatory one, and one downright destitute in numbers.

**Shown.** Three phases of local complexity and their dependence on architecture: depth in the MNIST-MLP reduces both the sharpness of the rise and the gap between the training and validation LC ([p5-4](#p5-4)); on the CNN-CIFAR10 the picture is the reverse — depth increases the peak of the rise, and the authors note the opposition themselves ([p6-1](#p6-1)); width shifts the onset of the rise earlier and raises the peak, but at a width of $2000$ the peak falls again ([p6-3](#p6-3), [fig. 6](#fig-6)). Batch normalisation removes the second descent and raises the level of the measure ([p7-2](#p7-2)), weight decay removes the same descent but lowers the measure ([p7-3](#p7-3)) — two interventions acting in opposite directions. The separation by the nature of the labels ([p9-3](#p9-3), [p9-4](#p9-4)) is obtained on one setup and holds. Checking the measure against an exact count of the regions via SplineCAM gives the same curve shape ([p4-6](#p4-6), [fig. 12](#fig-12)).

**Shown qualitatively, by pictures.** The migration of regions itself — the work's central observation — is presented only by exact SplineCAM visualisations on 2D slices passing through three training samples ([fig. 1](#fig-1)). There are no numerical densities of regions near the decision boundary against densities far from it here; those will appear only in [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md).

**A coincidence in time presented as a relation.** From the fact that under induced grokking the rise comes early, and that under an early generalisation there is no rise, "a relation of the rise phase to overfitting" is inferred ([p9-3](#p9-3)). There is no intervention in the work that would change the local complexity independently and check the consequences for grokking; the same applies to the chief claim about grokking ([p8-2](#p8-2), [fig. 9](#fig-9)).

**Declared to be an argument.** The gap between the training and validation LC in the rise and second-descent phases is called an indication of vulnerability to membership inference, with a reference to Tan et al. 2023 — there is no check ([p5-5](#p5-5)); the fall of the peak at a width of $2000$ is read as an implicit regularisation by overparameterisation ([p6-3](#p6-3)); and the second rise before the grokking is read as a growth of local complexity in both memorisation and generalisation, with the authors' hedge "possible indication" ([p9-1](#p9-1)).

**Neither shown nor caveated.** The conclusion asserts that the finding "refuted a number of earlier works resting on the incorrect assumption that the partition is static" ([p9-5](#p9-5)) — not one such work is named, either there or in the related-work section. The claim of a computational advantage over SplineCAM is given with not a single number: neither time nor memory, only "significantly longer" ([p4-6](#p4-6)). The optimiser is nowhere named: the experimental setting gives only a learning rate of $10^{-3}$ and a weight decay of $0.01$ ([p5-3](#p5-3)); the number of seeds and the number of runs are nowhere stated. The work proposes no mechanism of the double descent and does not pose the question of why the network passes from accumulating nonlinearities near the training points to repelling them. The code is promised "upon completion of the review" ([p2-6](#p2-6)); the arXiv version has remained the only one.

**An observation that violates the work's own rule.** A layerwise sweep of ResNet18 without batch normalisation shows that the second descent alternates by layer: the first ReLU of the shallow pre-act blocks has none, the second does ([fig. 14](#fig-14)). The universality of the three phases is thereby violated within a single network, and the observation is not built into the general conclusion.

**A divergence from the work's sequel, useful when citing.** Here an increase of depth under grokking **delays** the generalisation ([p8-2](#p8-2), [fig. 8](#fig-8)); in the summary table 1 of [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md) an increase of depth from 2 to 6 on the same MNIST-MLP pair **accelerates** the grokking. The settings differ (there the time of adversarial grokking, here the time of generalisation on the validation set), but both are presented as claims about the influence of depth on the time of grokking, and neither of the two works cites the other at this point — indeed, [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md) does not cite the present work at all.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The neighbourhood is described by two different bodies.** In the [implementation](#sec-3-2) the neighbourhood is set as the convex hull $conv(\lbrace x\pm{\boldsymbol{v}}_{p}:p=1,2...,P\rbrace)=conv({\boldsymbol{V}})$ over $P$ orthonormal directions ([p4-3](#p4-3)), that is, a body with $2P$ vertices. In the check against SplineCAM and in the experimental setting the same neighbourhood is called a "$25$-hypercube" with a diagonal length of $2r$ and "hypercubes" of dimension $p=25$ ([p4-6](#p4-6), [p5-3](#p5-3)). These are different bodies with different numbers of vertices, and which of them was computed determines both the cost of the measurement and the meaning of the parameter $r$.

**The parameter $r$ is introduced by a reference to a section that does not contain it.** The experimental setting says: the size of the neighbourhood is governed by the length of the diagonal "through the parameter $r$ mentioned in section 3.2" ([p5-3](#p5-3)) — no $r$ is introduced in [section 3.2](#sec-3-2); it first appears a page earlier, in the check against SplineCAM ([p4-6](#p4-6)), and its value is not named there either.

**The abstract and the conclusion describe different sequences of phases.** The abstract: a descent after the initialisation, then a rise, then a final descent — three phases ([p1-2](#p1-2)). The conclusion: the number of regions "first grows around the training points, then falls before the generalisation begins, then grows again and finally drops" — four phases, beginning with a growth ([p9-5](#p9-5)). Only the ending coincides.

**The measure has three names and two different definitions.** The section heading calls it partition density, the text everywhere local complexity, the caption to [fig. 3](#fig-3) "local nonlinearity/local complexity". Worse, along with the names the object counted diverges too: the abstract describes the measure as a concentration of **linear regions** in a neighbourhood ([p1-2](#p1-2)), whereas what is counted is **intersections of hyperplanes** with the neighbourhood ([p4-1](#p4-1), [p4-3](#p4-3)), and the caption to [fig. 12](#fig-12) directly opposes "the number of linear regions computed exactly by SplineCAM" to "the number of hyperplane cuts" of their own method. This inconsistency is absent from the analysis in `.claims.txt`; it was found while checking the card.

**The caption of figure 3 diverges from the file names of its panels.** In the caption the upper row corresponds to a radius $r=0.005$ ([fig. 3](#fig-3)), whereas the plot files of that row in the source are named `mlp_d*_r0.05`. Which value corresponds to what is shown cannot be established from the paper.

**Traces of an unfinished edit.** A dangling reference "In Appendix, we present layerwise visualization…" with no appendix number ([p6-2](#p6-2)); [appendix A](#sec-a) is an empty stub with the heading "Appendix A Appendix" and not a single line; a missing verb in the experimental setting ("For the Resnet experiments we $p=20$", [p5-3](#p5-3)); "as pose the following question" in the introduction ([p2-2](#p2-2)); the caption to [fig. 13](#fig-13) speaks of a "depth 6 and weight 200 MLP" — a network of that depth is not swept in the main text, and "weight" stands in place of "width". The same dangling appendix reference was then carried over into [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md).

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus; each entry says what exactly is distorted and gives references to the authors' paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. The citing works' own formulations are analysed in their cards; separate anchors to those analyses have not yet been created in the other folders.

**"At a threshold fraction of inferred facts a generalising circuit arises in the model" (erroneous).** In the introductory part of [Abramov et al. 2025](../2504.20752.grokking-in-the-wild-data-augmentation-for-real-world-multi-hop-reasoning-with-transformers/2504.20752.grokking-in-the-wild-data-augmentation-for-real-world-multi-hop-reasoning-with-transformers.card.md) the present work stands in a list of sources for the claim that in toy settings the number of inferred facts can be raised to a threshold ratio $\phi$ at which a "generalising circuit" arises in the model; in that work's bibliography the reference is precisely to "Training dynamics of deep network linear regions" rather than to the neighbouring paper by the same authors. Not one of the three components exists in the work: the word "circuit" does not occur in it once, there is no data-fraction threshold — absolute numbers of samples are swept ([p9-2](#p9-2), [p9-3](#p9-3)) — and there are no algorithmic tasks at all, the only way of obtaining grokking being the multiplication of the initial weights by $8$ on MNIST ([p8-2](#p8-2)). All the work says about the generalisation fraction is that an increase in the number of honestly labelled samples removes the rise phase of the local complexity.

**An example of accurate citation.** [Lee et al. 2024](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md): "Recent studies associated grokking with a double descent phenomenon in the DNN mapping geometry training dynamics [Humayun et al., 2023]" — exactly what is shown: a relation between grokking and a double descent in the geometry of the mapping, called a relation rather than a mechanism.

**Separately — the risk of confusion with the same authors' sequel.** "Humayun et al., 2023" is the present work; "Humayun et al., 2024" is [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md), in which the same measure received the name *region migration*, a numerical estimate of the densities near the decision boundary and a relation to delayed robustness. The confusion is aggravated by the sequel's not citing its predecessor: the bibliography of [2402.15555](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md) contains only SplineCAM (twice, as "2023a" and "2023b") and other works of Humayun, and the present paper is absent. The corpus therefore, when referring to local complexity, almost always points to the year 2024 (2504.03162, 2504.12700, 2512.03437, 2505.15624), although the measure, the three phases, the migration of regions and the sweeps over depth, width, batch normalisation, weight decay and volume of data with honest and random labels were all introduced here, in 2023.
