## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work offers an explanation in full, and offers it in words rather than in computation: already [in the abstract](#p1-2) the explanation is promised by "modelling grokking by stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (Brownian motion) and applying ideas of thermodynamics", grokking is declared "an analogue of an entropy-driven chemical reaction", and the passage to generalisation proceeds [by the second law of thermodynamics](#p1-4). The setting of Power et al. is not reproduced here — it is [retold](#p4-2) together with four observations ([1](#p4-3)–[4](#p4-6)), and all the work's numbers ("$10^{3}$ and $10^{6}$ steps") are taken from there too. The explanation proper occupies a page and a half: [memorisation](#p4-12) is a fall into a ravine at a non-zero gradient (a path $\sim t$), [grokking](#p4-13) is a random walk over the zero-risk manifold (a path $\sim\sqrt{t}$), and [the delay](#p4-14) comes out as the square of the length of the memorisation, "which is what is observed in the simulations". What is valuable for the corpus is the shortest formulation of the entropic hypothesis: [the generalising solution lies in a region of high entropy](#p4-11), and it is there that the second law drives the system. What the work does not contain: not a single experiment, no numerical example with two wells, no simulation of SGLD in a model ravine landscape; weight decay is not mentioned once — even though the [remark on the optimiser](#p5-4) names AdamW, whose decay of the weights is exactly the letter W. Nor does the work say that the energy on the zero-risk manifold is constant and that the minimisation of the free energy therefore reduces there exactly to the maximisation of the entropy — the one place where an appeal to the second law would be legitimate is left unspoken by the author.

**[Effective theory and statistical mechanics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — the frame of the whole work and its sole content. The chain is standard and set out without abbreviation: gradient flow → a numerical iteration → [SGLD with a Gaussian term](#p2-1) → the continuous limit (SDE (1)) → the Fokker–Planck equation (2) → [convergence to the Gibbs distribution](#p2-8) $e^{-\beta f}$ at $\beta=1/\theta$. Hence the slogan of section 2.1: [gradient descent is a minimisation of energy, stochastic descent a minimisation of free energy](#p3-6), with $F=E-\theta S$ ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)) at constant energy, that is, the entropy of a region is the logarithm of its volume. The work gives the corpus a vocabulary rather than a quantity: [entropy effects "ought to be discussed in learning theory"](#p4-1) is an appeal, not a measurement. What the work does not contain: entropy is nowhere defined operationally (no measure, no coarse-graining, no weight scale); the zero-risk manifold has Lebesgue measure zero in $\mathbb{R}^{d}$, so "the volume of its region" is not defined at all without passing to a tubular neighbourhood — and it is precisely the width of the ravine that carries the whole driving force of the construction. There is one rigorous part of the text — [the box on thermalisation](#p3-1) with the conjugation $A=e^{-\beta f/2}\nabla e^{\beta f/2}$, set out after Cycon, Froese, Kirsch and Simon and bearing no direct relation to grokking.

**[Weight norm minimisation and the zero-loss manifold](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work arrives at the same manifold as the corpus's norm-minimisation line and traverses it in a fundamentally different way. [For overparameterised models the local minima merge into a manifold of zero empirical risk](#p4-8) (a reference to Liu, Zhu and Belkin), and [every new condition $\mathcal{L}(z,x)=0$ contracts it](#p4-9). The second phase of the training proceeds **along** that manifold — but what drives it is not a fall of the norm under weight decay but [a Brownian walk in the direction of high entropy](#p4-11). This is a direct rival to the explanation through norm minimisation: the same delay, the same carrier, a different driving force. What the work does not contain: the weight norm — it is not mentioned once, neither as a quantity nor as a regulariser; nor is there an analysis of why a uniform walk along a ravine would drift the system towards wide valleys (the transverse diffusion term, which alone gives rise to such a drift, is not written out in the text).

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the support of the construction. The [flat-minima approach](#p3-11) is taken up: narrow (sharp) minima are associated with overfitting and wide (flat) ones with generalisation, whence [the prediction that the outcome of SGLD training is captured by wide potential wells "at the expense of the entropic part of the free energy"](#p3-13). The work adds to the corpus a language rare here: [the zero-risk manifold is described as ravine-like — narrows (low entropy) and wide valleys (high)](#p4-10), with a reference to the ravine method of Gelfand and Tsetlin, and the training is decomposed into a fall into the ravine and a motion along it. What the work does not contain: the thesis that "the generalising region is wider than the overfitting one" is postulated rather than shown, although the whole construction rests on it; the common count suggests rather the opposite — memorising solutions are many, algorithmic ones few.

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — noise here is not an incidental circumstance but the mechanism itself: without the Gaussian term $w_{k}$ there is neither a Gibbs distribution, nor an entropic term, nor a second phase. [The temperature](#p2-3) $\theta$ is introduced as a scalar constant in the SDE. What the work does not contain: any relation of $\theta$ to the learning rate or to the batch size — the work introduces no effective temperature; SGD noise is anisotropic and point-dependent, whereas the stationarity of $e^{-\beta f}$ requires precisely isotropy, and there is no caveat about this. The carry-over from SGLD to actual optimisers is declared to be the author's belief: ["We believe that the same effect will work for other forms of stochastic gradient descent (such as the mini-batch procedure)"](#p3-13).

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — defined geometrically and briefly: [memorisation is the reaching of the zero-risk manifold by stochastic gradient descent at a non-zero gradient](#p4-12), and the path in hypothesis space is proportional to the number of steps. The plateau after that is not "empty": the system moves along the manifold, only slowly. What the work does not contain: a measurable quantity distinguishing the two phases — neither a progress measure nor a diagnostic; the boundary of the phases is set by the condition $f(\lbrace z\rbrace,x)=0$ rather than by an observation.

**[The data fraction and the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — it is here that the work aims its single quantitative conclusion. [The threshold behaviour is explained thus](#p5-1): the entropy $S_{1}$ of the generalising region does not depend on the sample, the entropy $S_{0}$ of the overfitting region falls as the sample grows, and as soon as $S_{0}<S_{1}$ the second law drives the system towards generalisation. The exponential dependence is obtained by an additional [assumption of an equal percentage of volume being cut away](#p5-2): then $S_{0}$ falls linearly, $F_{0}$ [grows linearly, $F_{1}$ is constant, and by Eyring the grokking time falls exponentially](#p5-3). What the work does not contain: a check — the agreement with observation 3 of Power et al. is achieved by a fitted assumption, the critical dataset size is neither computed nor named, and the spread of grokking times across tasks and data fractions the author does not discuss.

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the subject of a separate [remark](#p5-4): in [9] AdamW was used, but "on the zero-risk manifold the gradient vanishes, and AdamW turns into a random walk with no acceleration". The author needs the argument in order to apply the Brownian picture to someone else's experiment, which was not run on SGLD. What the work does not contain: an analysis of the two circumstances that remove this argument — under weight decay the gradient of the full objective does not vanish on the zero-risk manifold (a drift $\lambda x$ remains), and Adam divides the step by the root of the mean square of the gradient, whereupon at vanishing gradients the step is not damped but normalised.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work does not reproduce the proving ground but [retells](#p4-2) it: an overparameterised network on modular arithmetic, four observations ([the times $10^{3}$ and](#p4-3) $10^{6}$, [the threshold in sample size](#p4-4), [the exponential growth of the time as the sample shrinks](#p4-5), [the circle of embeddings](#p4-6)) — and this is the whole of the paper's empirical material. The literature on grokking is confined to four works of 2022–2023; over three years and four revisions the list has not gained a single new one.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — [mentioned in a single line](#p4-2): addition, multiplication and other operations modulo 97. Neither the task, nor the modulus, nor the fraction of training data takes any further part in the analysis — the construction is not tied to arithmetic and would apply equally to any overparameterised setting.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — [observation 4](#p4-6) is retold closely to the text of Power et al. and Nanda et al.: the embeddings of the residues lie approximately on a circle, adding a residue is a shift along it, and the network's modular addition therefore "looks like a ”structure” (an algorithm given by trigonometric polynomials)". The work places this "structure" in [a region of high entropy of the manifold](#p4-11) — and stops there. What the work does not contain: an explanation of why the structured solution lies in a wide valley — no volume estimate, no argument from dimension; the relation between "structure" and entropy is postulated.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work gives the corpus a picture of the transition in the language of chemical kinetics rather than of phase transitions: [the Eyring formula](#p3-2) for a reaction rate, [the free energy of the transition state](#p3-3), and, in the Conclusions, ["the passage across an entropy barrier in the Eyring formula"](#p5-5). The nearest predecessor in this frame, Rubin et al. with a first-order transition and an entropic barrier, is not cited, and the work takes no part in the dispute about the entropic barrier, although the dispute turns on exactly its thesis. What the work does not contain: the words "phase transition" — they do not appear in the text at all; there is no order parameter, no free-energy landscape as a computed object, and no temperature axis along which the transition might be placed.

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — [mentioned as a prop](#p3-11): the observation about flat minima is "related to algorithmic stability, that is, to the stability of the solution of the learning problem under perturbations of the training sample", with references to Bousquet & Elisseeff, Kutin & Niyogi and Poggio et al. Stability is not used thereafter: not a single bound is written out in the work.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The optimiser is named differently in observation 1 and in the remark.** In the [retelling of observation 1](#p4-3) it is said that the model in [9] was trained by "stochastic gradient descent", and in the [box of the same section](#p5-4) that it was AdamW there. The second is correct; the first matters, because the whole estimate "the grokking time is the square of the memorisation time" is written out for pure stochastic descent.

**$F_{1}$ is defined in section 2.1 and applied in section 3 to different quantities.** In the [definition of the Eyring formula](#p3-3) $F_{1}$ is the free energy of the **transition state** — the saddle between two wells — and $F_{0}$ the free energy of the initial well. In the [conclusion about the exponential dependence](#p5-3) $F_{0}$ and $F_{1}$ are called "the free energies of the overfitting region and of the generalising region", that is, of the initial and the **final** states. The formula is applied to quantities other than those for which it is written out; the consequence is substantive, since the Eyring reaction rate does not depend on the free energy of the final state at all — this is kinetics, not the thermodynamics of the transition — and at $F_{1}<F_{0}$ the exponent is positive and the "rate" grows without bound.

**The manifold contracts entire, while the entropy of the generalising region is declared independent of the sample.** It is [said](#p4-9) that as the sample grows the zero-risk manifold contracts, since additional conditions $\mathcal{L}(z,x)=0$ are imposed; it is [also said](#p5-1) that the entropy $S_{1}$ of the generalising region "does not depend on the training sample (by the generalisation property)". The conditions cut the manifold entire, including its generalising part, and the generalisation property means a small risk on new data rather than an independence of a region's volume from the number of conditions imposed. The [assumption of an equal percentage of volume being cut away](#p5-2) is meanwhile applied to the overfitting region alone; applied to the whole manifold — and no other grounds for singling out a part are given — it gives a linear fall of $S_{1}$ too, the difference $S_{1}-S_{0}$ stays constant, and no threshold arises at all.

**The delay is explained twice and differently.** In section 3 it is derived from Brownian slowness — [a path $\sim\sqrt{t}$ against $\sim t$ for descent](#p4-13) — and there is no barrier between the regions there at all. In the [Conclusions](#p5-5) the same delay is declared "the result of the passage across an entropy barrier in the Eyring formula". Two mechanisms for one delay, and which of them is the principal one is not said.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

There is no experimental part in the work: no training of networks, no simulation of SGLD in a model ravine landscape, no numerical example with two wells. One part is set out rigorously — [the box on thermalisation](#p3-1) — and it is taken from someone else's book on statistical mechanics; it bears no direct relation to grokking. Everything else is an argument by analogy, and its load-bearing places rest on two assumptions declared as assumptions by the author himself.

The first: ["If we assume that memorisation and grokking give similar path lengths"](#p4-14) — from this and this alone comes the square ($10^{3}$ and $10^{6}$). The equality of the path lengths is supported by nothing, and the estimate involves neither the dimension of the hypothesis space, nor the temperature, nor the step size, although the length of a Brownian path depends on them directly. The second: ["Let us assume that the imposition of each additional condition … removes an equal percentage of the volume of the overfitting part"](#p5-2) — from this and this alone comes the linear fall of $S_{0}$, and from it the exponential dependence of the grokking time on the sample size. Both assumptions are introduced after the answer is known and are fitted to observations 1 and 3 of Power et al.

It is worth noting separately that the means of checking exist in the corpus and are not used by the work: the Boltzmann entropy on the grokking landscape is measured by sampling the density of states in 2505.11411, and the conclusion there — that there is no entropic barrier in the memorisation → generalisation transition — diverges directly from [the concluding sentence](#p5-5) of this note; the advantage in high entropy found there for generalising solutions, on the contrary, supports [the premise](#p4-11).

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus; each entry says what exactly is distorted and gives references to the author's paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. Neither citing work has a card as yet, so there are no "details" links here.

**"It was shown that the grokking time decreases exponentially with the growth of the training sample" (widening).** Lopatin, Kozyrev & Pechen (2509.10562) write: `"In the works [7, 8] it was shown that the grokking time decreases exponentially with the growth of the training sample."` In the author himself this is not shown but obtained from an assumption introduced on the spot, about [an equal percentage of volume being cut away](#p5-2), and the result is stated more cautiously: the Eyring formula ["gives an approximation of the dependence"](#p5-3), and the conclusion ["agrees with the observations in [9]"](#p5-3). The exponential behaviour itself is not a result of this work but [a retold observation 3 of Power et al.](#p4-5). The citation, meanwhile, traces its lineage to a genuine observation, only someone else's: the measured dependence belongs to Power et al., and here an explanation has been fitted to it after the fact.

**"The transition to generalisation is irreversible by the second law of thermodynamics" (widening).** The same work: `"the neighborhood of the optimal solution has increased entropy, which entails the irreversibility of the transition to generalization during grokking due to the second law of thermodynamics [7, 8]."` The word "irreversibility" does not appear in the note under analysis: it is said only that [a system performing a Brownian motion will move into regions of high entropy](#p4-11), and that [for sufficiently large samples $S_{0}$ becomes smaller than](#p5-1) $S_{1}$. The irreversibility is a strengthening added by the citing authors; it does not follow from the argument given, since a walk over the manifold is in itself reversible, and a difference of entropies sets only a preferred direction.

An example of accurate citation is given by Manning-Coe et al. (2502.01739): `"several mechanisms have been identified as its possible origin (Thilak et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2023a; Kumar et al. 2024; Tan and Huang 2024; Imtiaz Humayun et al. 2024; Kozyrev 2024)"` — the work is placed in a row of conjectures about the origin, with the hedge "possible", and nothing beyond what it says is credited to it.

# Quoted fragments

Only the fragments the corpus cards refer to are
reproduced here (right of quotation); the paper's licence does not permit
republishing the whole text. Anchor numbering matches the original.

###### p1-2

Explanation of grokking (delayed generalization) in learning is given by modeling grokking by the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (Brownian motion) and applying the ideas of thermodynamics.

Keywords: grokking, overfitting, generalization

###### p1-4

Stochastic gradient descent, which is a basic model in machine learning, when taken in the form of the stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics, is a direct analogue of a random walk along the reaction coordinates, which is a principal model in chemical kinetics. It is natural to use in learning the ideas of kinetics and thermodynamics, in particular, if the gradient descent is an analogue of the energy driven chemical reaction, then the stochastic gradient descent should include also the discussion of entropy, i.e. of the free energy minimization. We show that the transition to generalization in grokking is an analogue of the entropy driven chemical reaction and is related to the second law of thermodynamics (see the discussion below).

###### p2-1

The stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (or SGLD) is given by iteration of

$$
x_{k+1}=x_{k}+w_{k}-\alpha_{k}f^{\prime}(x_{k}),
$$

where $w_{k}$ are independent Gaussian random vectors.

###### p2-3

Continuous limit of SGLD is described by the stochastic differential equation (SDE) for Brownian motion in a potential

$$
d\xi^{i}(t)=\sqrt{2\theta}dw^{i}(t)-{\partial f(\xi(t))\over\partial x^{i}}dt, \tag{1}
$$

where $dw^{i}(t)$ is the stochastic differential of Wiener process which satisfies

$$
dw^{i}(t)dw^{j}(t)=\delta_{ij}dt.
$$

This equation was discussed in relation to learning in [1], [2].

The Fokker–Planck equation for SGLD (1) reduces to the diffusion equation in the potential

$$
{\partial u\over\partial t}=\theta\Delta u+\nabla u\cdot\nabla f+u\Delta f, \tag{2}
$$

where $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $u=u(x,t)$ is the distribution function, $f=f(x)$ is the potential, $f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, $\theta>0$ is the temperature. Equivalently,

$$
{\partial u\over\partial t}=\theta\,{\rm\bf div}\,\left[e^{-\beta f}\,{\rm\bf grad}\,\left[ue^{\beta f}\right]\right],
$$

###### p2-8

therefore the Gibbs distribution $e^{-\beta f}$, $\beta=1/\theta$ is a stationary solution of the equation, and the solution converges to the Gibbs distribution (under some conditions on $f$).

**Remark**. Let us discuss a short introduction to statistical mechanics for mathematicians, based on arguments from [3]. Let us put (2) as

$$
{\partial u\over\partial t}=-\theta e^{-\beta f/2}A^{*}Ae^{\beta f/2}u,\quad A=e^{-\beta f/2}\nabla e^{\beta f/2}.
$$

The operator $A^{*}A$ is positive, moreover

$$
e^{-t\theta e^{-\beta f/2}A^{*}Ae^{\beta f/2}}=e^{-\beta f/2}e^{-t\theta A^{*}A}e^{\beta f/2},
$$

$e^{-t\theta A^{*}A}$ is a contraction semigroup in $L_{2}$. The condition $e^{\beta f/2}u\in L_{2}$ is

$$
\int e^{\beta f(x)}u^{2}(x)dx<\infty. \tag{3}
$$

For the stationary solution $e^{-\beta f}$ eq. (3) is the integrability of the Gibbs distribution

$$
\int e^{-\beta f(x)}dx<\infty, \tag{4}
$$

zero is the eigenvalue of $A^{*}A$

$$
\left[e^{-\beta f/2}A^{*}Ae^{\beta f/2}\right]e^{-\beta f}=0.
$$

**[p. 3]**

###### p3-1

Then if the initial condition $u_{0}$ satisfies (3) and Gibbs distribution is integrable (4), the dynamics $e^{-t\theta A^{*}A}e^{\beta f/2}u_{0}$ belongs to $L_{2}$ and converges to $e^{-\beta f/2}$, and the solution $e^{-\beta f/2}e^{-t\theta A^{*}A}e^{\beta f/2}u_{0}$ of (2) converges to Gibbs distribution $e^{-\beta f}$, i.e. we get thermalization.

###### p3-2

**The Eyring formula of kinetic theory** describes the reaction rate (for the transition between two potential wells due to diffusion of the type (2)): the reaction rate is proportional to

$$
e^{-\beta(F_{1}-F_{0})}, \tag{5}
$$

###### p3-3

where $F_{1}$ is the free energy of the transition state (the saddle between two potential wells) and $F_{0}$ is the free energy of the initial state of the reaction (the potential well from which the transition occurs).

The free energy of a domain $U$ (for Gibbs distribution) is

$$
e^{-\beta F(U)}=\int_{U}e^{-\beta E(x)}dx,
$$

###### p3-5

if we will take constant energy $E$, we get $F=E-\theta S$, where $E$ is the energy and $S$ is the entropy of the state $U$.

###### p3-6

Gradient descent in the potential $f$ is the minimization of energy, and stochastic gradient descent is the minimization of free energy.

###### p3-11

We will use the known approach of flat minima: narrow (sharp) minima of empirical risk (in the hypothesis space) are associated with overfitting, and wide (flat) minima correspond to solutions of the learning problem with generalization [4]. This known observation is related to algorithmic stability, i.e. stability of the solution of a learning problem to perturbations of the training sample [5], [6], [7].

Learning by stochastic gradient descent for the learning problem (6) we will describe by the SGLD (1) in the hypothesis space, i.e. for distribution we get the diffusion equation in the potential (2). Therefore, the distribution function $u(x)$ converges to the Gibbs distribution concentrated in potential wells with low free energy.

###### p3-13

This predicts the capture of the SGLD learning result by wide potential wells (due to the entropic part of free energy), i.e., the reduction of the overfitting effect in the flat minima approach. We believe the same effect will work for other forms of stochastic gradient descent (such as mini-batch procedure). This (and other) approaches to control overfitting are discussed in [8].

**[p. 4]**

###### p4-1

Therefore, we consider the stochastic gradient optimization as a minimization of free energy in statistical mechanics, or an analogue of chemical reaction. Since entropic contribution to the free energy is important, effects of entropy should be discussed in learning theory.

###### p4-2

Grokking (delayed generalization) phenomenon in learning was discovered in [9], see also [10], [11], [12]. Overparameterized network was trained to perform modular arithmetics (in particular, addition, multiplication and other operations modulo 97). The phenomenon of delayed generalization was found, and the following observations were made:

###### p4-3

1) The model was trained in $10^{3}$ steps (by the stochastic gradient descent), memorization of training sample was achieved with almost $100\%$ error on the validation set (i.e. total overfitting). If the training was continued, then in about $10^{6}$ steps total generalization was obtained (no overfitting).

###### p4-4

2) Threshold behavior was found: grokking works for sufficiently large learning samples.

###### p4-5

3) Exponential growth of grokking time with decreasing of training sample was observed.

###### p4-6

4) Some kind of principal component analysis for embeddings of residues in the obtained model of modular arithmetics was performed. It was observed that embeddings of residues lie approximately on a circle, adding a residue is a shift along such a circle. Therefore, modular addition, performed by the neural network, looks like ”structure” (an algorithm, given by trigonometric polynomials [12]).

We propose the following explanation of grokking.

###### p4-8

For overparameterized models local minima merge into a manifold of zero empirical risk in the hypothesis space [13], given by the condition (since the loss function is non-negative)

$$
f(\{z\},x)=0,\quad{\rm i.e.}\quad\mathcal{L}(z,x)=0,\,\forall z\in\{z\}.
$$

###### p4-9

As the training sample $\{z\}$ grows, the zero-risk manifold $f(\{z\},x)=0$ shrinks since additional conditions $\mathcal{L}(z,x)=0$ for $z\in\{z\}$ are imposed.

###### p4-10

The zero-risk manifold contains narrows, or ravines (regions with lower entropy) and wide valleys (with higher entropy). Ravine landscapes were discussed by Gelfand and Tsetlin, and the ravine method [14] was developed to improve optimization for such a landscape.

###### p4-11

For ravine, or river–valleys landscape, learning works in two steps: the system falls in a ravine, and then performs motion along the ravine. The generalizing solution for learning problem (”structure”, for example algorithm of modular addition) lies in the high-entropy region of the zero-risk manifold. By the second law of thermodynamics the system performing Brownian motion will travel to regions of high entropy at the zero risk manifold (which is the grokking transition).

###### p4-12

Memorization of the training sample is achieving the zero risk manifold $f(\{z\},x)=0$ by the stochastic gradient descent with non-zero gradient, in this case the path traveled in the hypothesis space is proportional to the number of descent steps $\sim t$.

###### p4-13

Grokking is a random walk in the zero risk manifold $f(\{z\},x)=0$ (or Brownian motion). For Brownian motion the path traveled is proportional to the root of the number of steps $\sim\sqrt{t}$.

###### p4-14

If we assume that the memorization and grokking result in similar path lengths, then the duration of grokking (in the SGD steps) will be the square of the duration of memorization, which is observed in simulation ($10^{3}$ and $10^{6}$).

**[p. 5]**

###### p5-1

Other properties of grokking can be discussed as follows. The zero-risk manifold contains regions without generalization (i.e. with overfitting) and some region with generalization. Grokking is the transition from the overfitting region to the generalizing (grokking) region of the zero-risk manifold. Entropy $S_{1}$ of the region with generalization does not depend on the training sample (by the generalization property), and entropy $S_{0}$ of overfitting region reduces with the increase of the training sample. This explains the threshold behavior of grokking: for sufficiently large samples the entropy $S_{0}$ of the overfitting region becomes less than the entropy $S_{1}$ of the generalizing region, and second law of thermodynamics predicts transition to generalization by increasing of entropy.

###### p5-2

Let us suppose that the imposition of each additional condition $\mathcal{L}(z,x)=0$, $z\in\{z\}$ removes an equal percentage of the volume of the overfitting part of the zero-risk manifold. Then the entropy $S_{0}$ of the overfitting region decreases linearly as the sample size increases.

###### p5-3

The Eyring’s formula $e^{-\beta(F_{1}-F_{0})}$ gives an approximation of the dependence of the reciprocal of the grokking time on the training sample size. Here $F_{0}$ and $F_{1}$ are free energies of the overfitting region and of the generalizing region. The entropy of the overfitting region decreases linearly with the training sample size (hence the free energy $F_{0}=E_{0}-\theta S_{0}$ increases linearly). The free energy $F_{1}$ of the generalizing region is constant. Consequently, the grokking time decreases exponentially when the training sample increases (this agrees with observations in [9]).

###### p5-4

**Remark**. In [9], the AdamW accelerated gradient descent algorithm was used for optimization. At the same time, in a zero-risk manifold, the gradient vanishes, and AdamW turns into a random walk without acceleration. When hitting the walls of ravines, the acceleration of the gradient can affect the dynamics, but this does not affect the progress along the ravine, which is well described by Brownian motion, as can be seen from the comparison of the properties of grokking and Brownian motion described above.

###### p5-5

Simple ideas of thermodynamics and kinetic theory allow us to explain the grokking (delayed generalization) phenomenon and some properties of grokking observed in [9] as a manifestation of thermodynamic and kinetic phenomena — transition to generalization in grokking proceeds by the second law of thermodynamics, of by entropic mechanism of chemical reaction, properties of grokking are explained by Brownian motion and Eyring’s formula. Delay of generalization in grokking is a result of transition through the barrier of entropy in Eyring’s formula.
