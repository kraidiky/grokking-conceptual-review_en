# Muckatira, Shivagunde, Deshpande, Rumshisky 2026 — A Pre-Training Analogue of Grokking in Language Models: Tracing Delayed Grammatical Generalization

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Sherin Muckatira**, **Namrata Shivagunde**, **Vijeta Deshpande**, **Anna Rumshisky** (University of Massachusetts Lowell).

arXiv [2606.00230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00230), May 2026 (v1), 18 pages, 10 figures, 9 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The subject is a transfer of the classical grokking setting to the pre-training of language models through a proxy split on exposure: for every minimal pair of BLiMP a "critical phrase" is extracted, and the examples whose phrase occurred verbatim in the C4 stream within the pre-training window form the proxy-train, the rest the proxy-validation. On five grammatical phenomena and LLaMA-type models of 35M/60M/130M the proxy-train crosses the 80% threshold 100–900 steps earlier than the proxy-validation, and a permutation test on matched random splits shows that the delay does not reduce to the sizes of the parts. The internals: the grammatical concept vectors become more discriminative (AUROC) and occupy a subspace of larger effective rank after the generalisation; and the gain of attention from the critical token to the context token is concentrated in single heads, different for each phenomenon.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the classical setting is declared untransferable to pre-training (there is neither supervision, nor a split of the task's examples, nor multiple passes) and is replaced by a split on exposure: the proxy-train is the BLiMP examples whose critical phrase occurred verbatim in C4, the proxy-validation is the rest; on five phenomena the first part reaches 80% 100–900 steps earlier than the second, and matched random splits do not reproduce that delay ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). Analysis: there is no memorisation of a training sample here at all, which is where classical grokking begins — the model was trained on neither part of BLiMP, and both curves rise smoothly with no "100% against chance" gap; the exposure is determined over the whole 2000-step window, so an example whose phrase first occurred at step 1900 counts as "exposed" already at step 100; and the fifth selection filter keeps the sets with a significant delay — and then that same significance is presented as a result.

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — the delay is tied to the phenomenon rather than general: short for Regular Subject–Verb Agreement 1, longest for Irregular Past Participle Verbs and Existential There Subject Raising; the ordering "exposed before unexposed" reproduces at 35M and 130M, and the smaller model reaches the threshold earlier than the larger one, which the authors explain by capacity at a fixed step budget — and present precisely as a guess ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)). Analysis: the "LLM pre-training" of the title means models of 35–130M weights and about 200M tokens of C4; the scale of the delays is whole checkpoints of 100 steps, so every observed lag is 1–9 readings; and the sets in which the proxy-validation never reached 80% are discarded by the fourth filter before the measurement.

**[Linear probing](../../concepts/linear-sparse-probing.md)** — a grammatical concept vector as the difference of the hidden states of an acceptable and an unacceptable sentence: the mean vector, computed on the proxy-train, separates grammaticality on the proxy-validation by an inner product (AUROC), and the effective rank of the centred matrix of concept vectors is higher after the transition than before in all five sets — against the line "after generalisation the representations compress" ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)). Analysis: the AUROC rises distinctly only for three phenomena out of five (for the irregular participles the separability is high from the start, and for the second subject–verb agreement it stays at chance); the dispute about compression is conducted on incommensurable measures — the rank here is computed on a matrix of pairwise differences, whereas the works cited measured the geometry of the hidden states themselves; and the comparison of the points $t_{\text{before}}=100$ and $t_{\text{after}}$ (500–1400 steps) is confounded with the general course of early training, with no equidistant control pair.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the gain of attention from the critical token to the context token is not general but concentrated in single heads, different for each phenomenon (L2H7, L6H2, L5H0, L6H2, L5H3), with magnitudes up to $+0.81$ at a maximum weight of 1.0; the attention entropy of the critical token more often falls or comes to a shelf after the transition, and the heat maps show a shift *sketch*→*this*, *references*→*Paula* ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)). Analysis: the full layer×head grid is published (Table 9, 64 heads for five sets) — a rarity for work of this kind; but by that same grid, for four phenomena out of five the majority of the heads *lower* their attention to the context, the mean over the 64 heads is negative for three sets, and the conclusion's formulation about "increased attention" conflicts with this; and the numbers of the best heads in Table 8 and Table 9 do not agree ($+0.81$ against $+0.77$, $+0.63$ against $+0.62$).

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**What is valuable — a reproducible frame and honest statistics around it.** The proxy split on exposure is the first direct transfer of the "training/validation part" pair onto evaluation examples during pre-training, rather than a definition of grokking through the training loss alone; the permutation test on 1000 matched splits with per-seed $p$-values and the full layer×head attention grid are published in full; and the limitations (verbatim matching as a proxy for exposure, grammar only, a delay at the resolution of the checkpoints) are acknowledged by the authors themselves.

**The split is not a training set, the selection is closed on the outcome, and there is no frequency control.** The model was not trained on BLiMP, so there is no "memorisation" in the classical sense and the name proxy-train misleads; the exposure is determined over the whole window while the points compared lie inside it; the fifth filter requires a significant delay — and the significance is then presented as a test; the permutation test holds only the sizes of the parts and does not answer the objection that "verbatim exposed phrases are frequent n-grams, that is, easy examples"; and the arithmetic of the filters does not add up (Table 3 contains 16 rows instead of 15, and the third filter "excludes 4" with six rows in the lower group), while existential there sums to 935 examples out of 1000 with no explanation.

**How the work will be cited** ("grokking is shown in the pre-training of language models") — more precisely: on five selected BLiMP sets and models of 35–130M, the examples whose critical phrase occurred verbatim in C4 cross the 80% threshold 1–9 checkpoints earlier than the rest, where the sets themselves are selected by the significance of that very delay, and there is no comparison with a random split equalised for phrase frequency.
