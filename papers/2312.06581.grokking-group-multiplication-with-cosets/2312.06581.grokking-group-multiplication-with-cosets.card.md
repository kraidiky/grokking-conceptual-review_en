## Concepts covered

**[Group representations and cosets](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** — the work introduces the coset side of this notion into the corpus, and introduces it as a rival rather than as a complement. The mechanism is named outright: the network performs full group multiplication by "selecting the common cosets of conjugate subgroups" ([p5-1](#p5-1)); two conjugate $H_{i},H_{j}$ have a common coset $H_{i}\tau_{ij}=\tau_{ij}H_{j}$, and a neuron recognises the product's falling into it by the sum of the pre-activations vanishing ([p5-1](#p5-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). A measurable quantity is introduced too — the coset concentration of a function $C_{H}(f)$, the fraction of variance remaining after restriction to the cosets of $H$ ([p5-8](#p5-8), [p5-9](#p5-9)); an exhaustive sweep of all 156 subgroups of $S_{5}$ and 1455 subgroups of $S_{6}$ gives a minimising subgroup for every neuron, and over $99.2\%$ of the linear layer's neurons have $\min_{H}C_{H}<1.0$ ([p6-3](#p6-3)). The concentration of the pre-activations of the overwhelming majority of neurons on cosets is named as behaviour that GCR does not predict at all ([p9-1](#p9-1)). The work also gives a mathematical analysis of why a coset function **appears** concentrated on a few irreducible representations (lemma G.2, [p20-7](#p20-7)) and why a correlation of the logits with characters arises from a mere count of common cosets, with no matrix multiplication of representations ([p8-7](#p8-7), [p22-12](#p22-12), [p22-14](#p22-14)). What the work does not contain: approximate cosets and a carry-over to cyclic groups — those appeared later, in McCracken et al. 2025, and do not belong to these authors; nor is there a claim that the relation of a circuit to an irreducible representation is fully explained — on the contrary, it is said that the authors have no explanation of why coset circuits **always** concentrate on exactly one representation ([p23-5](#p23-5)).

**[Group composition / non-commutativity](../../concepts/group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md)** — the work deepens the proving ground without widening it: exactly two groups are analysed, $S_{5}$ and $S_{6}$, but with 128 and 100 runs respectively ([p14-11](#p14-11)). Non-commutativity here is not an ornament of the setting but a condition of the mechanism itself: it is precisely because $S_{n}$ is non-abelian that it has many non-normal subgroups, and "conjugate subgroups are guaranteed to exist in non-abelian groups" ([p16-12](#p16-12)) — on a normal subgroup such a circuit cannot be built, and the authors note separately that the construction works for every subgroup of $S_{n}$ **except** $A_{n}$ ([p5-7](#p5-7)). Whence the corpus also gains a division of roles between the left and right embeddings: they encode different things — membership in right and in left cosets respectively — and are not interchangeable ([p7-3](#p7-3)). What the work does not contain: a sweep of groups — the cyclic, dihedral and $A_{5}$ cases analysed in Chughtai et al. 2023 are absent here entirely, and any carry-over of the coset description to the abelian case does not belong to this work.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work gives the corpus a rare object: an analysis of two incompatible analyses of one and the same model. The setting is reproduced word for word after Chughtai et al. 2023: "our experimental setting is identical to that of Chughtai et al. 2023, but our analysis led us to a different conclusion" ([§7](#sec-7), [p8-3](#p8-3)); the other work's four pieces of evidence are retold and analysed one by one ([p8-4](#p8-4)), and understanding the reasons for the divergence required a separate labour ([p1-5](#p1-5)). The methodological conclusion is declared a second, equally weighted purpose of the work and is formulated as a rule: "we must treat proposed neural mechanisms as theories until they are checked thoroughly" ([p9-7](#p9-7)); evidence for a circuit's role in a task is by nature observational and correlational, and the nodes of a computational graph are causally related, but the relation of their actions to the task is only **observed** to be correlated ([p9-8](#p9-8)). It matters that the authors date their own conviction: it arose not from observations but "only after the causal experiments of section 6.3 had been carried out" ([p9-9](#p9-9)). What the work does not contain: neither automated circuit-finding techniques nor a carry-over to real models — the opposite is said, that the analysis enjoyed advantages unavailable when working with real models and that "interpreting real models will be harder still" ([p9-10](#p9-10)).

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the work gives the corpus a set of interventions with pre-declared predicted outcomes and one striking result. The design is declared before the experiments: observing a circuit's behaviour over the whole distribution is not enough, the circuit must be "broken" deliberately and checked to behave as predicted ([p7-2](#p7-2)). Swapping the left and right embeddings destroys the performance ([p7-3](#p7-3)); flipping the sign of both embeddings at once changes nothing, while flipping the sign of one gives zero accuracy — only the **relative** sign matters ([p7-4](#p7-4)); a noise shift of $\mathcal{N}(1,1)$ harms markedly more than $\mathcal{N}(-1,1)$, which points to zero as the threshold value ([p8-1](#p8-1)). The most unexpected outcome is that replacing ReLU by the absolute value $x\mapsto|x|$ gives a hundred per cent accuracy and a **smaller** loss ([p7-5](#p7-5)), and the authors call this outright a property that GCR does not predict ([p9-4](#p9-4)). The ablations are arranged differently from the interventions: neurons whose concentration exceeds a threshold are removed, and in 126 of the 128 $S_{5}$ models removing all the neurons with $\min_{H}C_{H}(N_{i})\geq 1$ does not change the accuracy at all ([p6-6](#p6-6), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). What the work does not contain: interventions over the course of the training — all of them are taken from trained models; and the claim that "coset neurons are not only sufficient but necessary" ([p6-5](#p6-5)) is supported by the numbers in the text on one side only.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — grokking here is a criterion for selecting models rather than a subject of explanation, and this is said outright: the analysis was conducted "on fully grokked models with a hundred per cent test accuracy", since such models often form clean generalising circuits that are easier to analyse ([p2-2](#p2-2)). Its own contribution to the notion is single and observational: the formation of coset circuits coincides in time with the fall of the validation loss — as the loss goes to a small value, the median coset concentration passes abruptly from about $1$ to a negligible value ([p6-1](#p6-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). This is shown on one model, with the caveat that "different runs form coset circuits at different times, but the effect is representative" ([fig. 4](#fig-4)). What the work does not contain: no explanation of grokking, no analysis of the role of weight decay (it is merely set to $1.0$, [p14-11](#p14-11)), no division of the training into eras, and no joint distribution of the transition times over the 128 runs.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work gives a quantity built like a progress measure but does not use the term and does not claim it in that role. The model-median concentration $\min_{H\in\operatorname{sub}(G)}C_{H}$ is computed from the network's internal state, changes smoothly and falls together with the validation loss ([p6-1](#p6-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)); it is shown separately that the germ of a circuit is visible in a neuron long before its final form — in neuron 115 (seed 11) at 100 thousand steps the variance is $5.23$ at $C_{F_{20}}=2.96$, and by 130 thousand the variance grows to $9.06$ while $C_{F_{20}}$ falls below $10^{-5}$ ([p5-9](#p5-9), [fig. 3](#fig-3)). The term "progress measures" (Nanda et al. 2023) stands in the bibliography but is never used in the text of the work, and the authors cannot be credited with a framing about progress measures: they do not check any causal relation of the quantity to the jump, and the quantity itself is introduced in order to identify a neuron's subgroup rather than to predict the transition.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the work gives the corpus a case in which the learnt structure is checked against a complete finite list, but the list is made up of **subgroups** rather than representations: $S_{5}$ and $S_{6}$ have only 156 and 1455 subgroups, so an exhaustive sweep computing $\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{H\in\operatorname{sub}(G)}C_{H}(f)$ is feasible ([p6-2](#p6-2), [p6-3](#p6-3)). The structure found turns out to be varied: every model has at least a few $S_{n-1}$ neurons and at least two sign ones ([p4-7](#p4-7)), many models mix subgroups, and there is often a "long tail" of subgroups represented by one or two neurons ([fig. 6](#fig-6)). The unembedding side matters too: the correlation of the unembeddings of neurons of the **same coset** averages $81.4\%$, and of neurons of one conjugacy class of subgroups $-0.2\%$ ([p9-3](#p9-3)), that is, the model holds cosets together and keeps representations and conjugacy classes apart. What the work does not contain: a reconstructed working replacement for the model reproducing its outputs — the claim of a complete reverse engineering ([p1-2](#p1-2)) rests on the subgroup sweep, the ablations and the causal interventions.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the work inverts the order of explanation: it is not the Fourier concentration that explains the mechanism but the mechanism that explains the Fourier concentration. The group Fourier transform is called not central to the exposition but an important instrument of the analysis ([p3-10](#p3-10)), and it is acknowledged that for the first half of the investigation it was the authors' principal one ([p19-13](#p19-13)). Thereafter the relation is analysed formally: if $f$ is constant on the cosets of $H$, then its Fourier transform vanishes on every irreducible $\rho$ whose restriction to $H$ does not contain the trivial representation (lemma G.2, [p20-7](#p20-7)); and if the permutation representation of $G$ on $G/H$ has exactly two irreducible summands — which holds for $S_{n}$ and $S_{n-1}$ (lemma G.6, [p22-14](#p22-14)) — then the membership-counting function is expressible through $\operatorname{trace}(A\cdot\rho(\sigma^{-1}))$ (corollary G.5, [p22-12](#p22-12)). Whence a counting argument for the non-equivalence of the frames: subgroups are asymptotically far more numerous than irreducible representations, and for $S_{5}$ there are already $156$ against $7$ ([p23-1](#p23-1)). What the work does not contain: a claim that the Fourier description is false — it is put more mildly, that the authors "do not think there is any relation between what the circuit does and an irreducible representation", and it is admitted in the same breath that they have no explanation of the stable concentration on exactly one representation ([p23-5](#p23-5)).

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work inherits the proving ground through Chughtai et al. 2023 and inherits it deliberately word for word, for the sake of comparability of conclusions: the same architecture ([fig. 1](#fig-1)), $40\%$ of the multiplication table in training, the full training set at every step, Adam at a constant rate of $0.001$, a weight decay of $1.0$, $\beta_{1}=0.9$, $\beta_{2}=0.98$ ([p14-11](#p14-11)). What is its own in the setting is the scale: 128 runs on $S_{5}$ of 250 000 epochs each and 100 runs on $S_{6}$ of 50 000, given that one $S_{5}$ model takes about 8 hours to train on an RTX 2080 and one $S_{6}$ model about 100 ([p14-11](#p14-11)). What the work does not contain: a sweep of the data fraction, a comparison of optimisers and variants of regularisation — all these quantities are fixed after the other work and are not varied.

**[Margin maximisation](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — a mention only, and in the form of an open guess at that: the authors have no explanation of why coset circuits always concentrate on exactly one irreducible representation, and they "suspect that it may have something to do with the margin maximisation effect discussed in [Morwani et al. 2024]" ([p23-5](#p23-5)). No experiment in that direction is set up in the work.

**[Weight decay / L2 regularisation](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — a mention only, in the form of a fixed setting: weight decay is set to $1.0$ under Adam ([p14-11](#p14-11)). The work analyses neither the role of weight decay in the formation of coset circuits nor an experiment without it, and no claims about the necessity of regularisation belong to it.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — a mention only, and a mathematical rather than an experimental one at that: the discrete Fourier transform is read as a function on the cyclic group $C_{n}$, isomorphic to addition modulo $n$, and it is precisely this reading that is generalised to non-commutative groups ([p3-13](#p3-13), [p3-14](#p3-14)). Not a single model on modular arithmetic is trained here, and the carry-over of the coset description to the cyclic case does not belong to this work.

**[The universality hypothesis](../../concepts/universality-hypothesis.md)** — the work refutes its strong form on permutations: instead of a composition of representations the networks converge on subgroup-based schemes ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The refutation is addressed not to the idea of universality itself but to a particular claim about one mechanism for all group tasks.

**[The approximate Chinese remainder theorem](../../concepts/approximate-chinese-remainder-theorem.md)** — the coset schemes found here later entered it as a particular case: approximate cosets encompass ordinary ones ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The work proposes no such generalisation itself — it was made on top of its result.

**[The generalising circuit](../../concepts/generalization-circuit.md)** — the circuit is identified by the model's behaviour before it is analysed in the weights: first memorisation, much later a perfect generalisation on held-out data ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). The subgroup analysis comes after and fills the behavioural definition with a mechanism.
## Points we could not reconcile

**The text of section 6.3 diverges from table 1 in two numbers out of ten.** [Of the embedding swap](#p7-3) it says that "a significant fall of accuracy to 0" was observed, whereas the "Embedding Swap" row of table 1 gives $1\%$. [Of replacing ReLU by the absolute value](#p7-5) it says that "an even smaller loss than half the original" is observed, whereas the table gives $3.69\times10^{-13}$ against $1.97\times10^{-6}$ for the original model — a difference of seven orders of magnitude rather than a factor of two. Both discrepancies work in favour of the authors' conclusion, but when citing the numbers one should take them from the table.

**The caption of table 1 promises runs of different sizes, while table 2 gives one size for $S_{5}$.** The caption states that the interventions are averaged over 128 runs on $S_{5}$ "of different sizes", whereas [table 2](#p14-11) gives for $S_{5}$ a single linear-layer size ($128$) and a single embedding size ($256$). The sentence in the text is itself broken off mid-way — "128 $S_{5}$ models of different and recorded their average loss and accuracy" ([p7-2](#p7-2)) — so what difference exactly was meant cannot be recovered from the paper.

**Broken and incorrect cross-references.** [At the start of G.3](#p23-1) stands "For the direct comparison of $S_{n}$ refer to" with no table named (table 4 is meant). In [§7.2](#p9-2) the reader is sent to "Appendix 7.2" for the asymptotic analysis, whereas that analysis is in appendix G.3; in the [introduction](#p1-5), "see Appendix 7" instead of section 7. In the proof of lemma G.2 it is said "where in 9 we decompose $\rho|_{H}$", whereas the computation is numbered (7) ([p21-1](#p21-1)).

**Typos in the mathematical part, not affecting the conclusions but breaking the examples.** In the right coset $H_{4}\tau$ as written the second element is $(1\;4\;3\;1)$ — not a permutation, with 1 repeated twice ([p3-5](#p3-5)). In example E.9 the permutation representation is written as $S_{n}\rightarrow GL(\mathbb{C}^{3})$ where $S_{3}$ is meant ([p17-15](#p17-15)). In the matrices of the $(2,1)$-representation of $S_{3}$ the elements $(3\;1\;2)$ and $(2\;3\;1)$ are assigned one and the same matrix, which is impossible for a faithful representation ([p18-4](#p18-4)) — and the reader is invited in the same place to check by exercise an identity that will not hold for those matrices.

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

The work's parts rest on different things, and it is useful to distinguish them when citing.

**Proved.** That a function constant on the cosets of $H$ has a zero Fourier transform on every irreducible $\rho$ whose restriction to $H$ does not contain the trivial representation (lemma G.2, [p20-7](#p20-7)). That the Fourier transform of the indicator $1_{H}$ is non-zero only on the irreducible summands of the permutation representation of $G$ on $G/H$ (lemma G.4, [p21-9](#p21-9)), and that for $G=S_{n}$, $H=S_{n-1}$ there are exactly two such summands (lemma G.6, [p22-14](#p22-14)), whence the membership-counting function is given by formula (11) (corollary G.5, [p22-12](#p22-12)). These are statements about mathematics: they say that relating the logits to characters **does not require** a matrix multiplication of representations, not that the network does not perform one.

**Shown by measurement.** That over $99.2\%$ of the linear layer's neurons in 128 $S_{5}$ models and 100 $S_{6}$ models have $\min_{H\in\operatorname{sub}(G)}C_{H}(f)<1.0$, and the overwhelming majority below $10^{-6}$ ([p6-3](#p6-3)). That in 126 of the 128 $S_{5}$ models removing the neurons with $\min_{H}C_{H}(N_{i})\geq 1$ does not change the accuracy, and in the two remaining it falls to $99\%$ and $98\%$ ([p6-6](#p6-6)). That the correlation of the unembeddings within a coset is $0.814$ against $-0.002$ within a conjugacy class of subgroups and $-0.003$ at the baseline ([p9-3](#p9-3), table 3). That the interventions give exactly what was predicted: the relative sign matters, the sign itself does not; replacing ReLU by the absolute value does not spoil the performance; and shifting the noise upwards harms more than downwards ([p7-4](#p7-4), [p7-5](#p7-5), [p8-1](#p8-1), [§6.3](#sec-6-3)).

**Negative results of a direct attempt at reproduction.** That no evidence of the embeddings and the unembedding acting as a "lookup table" of representation matrices could be found for any representation other than the one-dimensional sign one ([p8-6](#p8-6)). That no evidence of a matrix multiplication in the linear layer could be found, again apart from a scalar multiplication for the sign representation ([p8-8](#p8-8)). This is said of the authors' own search on the reproduced setting rather than of what is not there.

**Conjectured and expressly marked unclosed.** That there is no relation whatever between what the circuit does and an irreducible representation — even though it is admitted in the same breath that coset circuits **always** concentrate on exactly one representation, and that the authors have no explanation of this ([p23-5](#p23-5)). The guess about a role for margin maximisation (Morwani et al. 2024) is left unchecked in the same place.

**The caution of the conclusion matters.** The dispute with Chughtai et al. 2023 is closed not by refutation but by underdetermination: GCR "could indeed solve the group multiplication problem", the coset circuit is consistent with all the evidence they adduce and besides explains what GCR does not ([p9-5](#p9-5)). It is claimed that more than one theory may be consistent with observational data ([p9-9](#p9-9)), not that the other description is false.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus; each entry says what exactly is distorted and leads to the authors' own paragraphs. There are no "details" links here: anchors `mc-2312-06581` have not yet been created in the citing cards; the candidates are listed in the import working materials.

**"The work refuted GCR" (widening).** The commonest formulation is stronger than the authors': [Sharma et al. 2025](../2505.18266.uncovering-a-universal-abstract-algorithm-for-modular-addition-in-neural-networks/2505.18266.uncovering-a-universal-abstract-algorithm-for-modular-addition-in-neural-networks.card.md) write that the work `"reverse-engineered models trained on $S_{n}$ under identical conditions and found that networks instead learn *coset*-based circuits, refuting the GCR universality claim"`, and repeat this as established — `"that the GCR algorithm [8] is not universal and instead coset circuits are learned"`. The authors say something different in strength: the GCR set out by Chughtai et al. 2023 "could indeed solve the group multiplication problem", and the coset circuit "is also consistent with all the evidence they adduce and besides is consistent with evidence that GCR does not explain" ([p9-5](#p9-5)). Their negative part is hedged too and pertains to their own search: "we were unable to find any evidence that the linear layer performs a matrix multiplication" ([p8-8](#p8-8), [p8-6](#p8-6)). An exemplary formulation for contrast is in [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md): `"although some of these findings were disputed in Stander et al. (2024) which propose that grokked networks learn a coset based algorithm for these same tasks"` — both "disputed" and "propose" preserved.

**"The work showed that networks recover the representation of the group" (erroneous).** The pattern of the opposite sense — to include the work in a list of evidence for a representation-theoretic solution: `"On finite groups like $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ and $S_{n}$, it has been shown that models like Transformers grok by recovering the representation of the group itself (Gromov 2023; Nanda et al. 2023; Chughtai et al. 2023; Stander et al. 2024)"` (2602.19533); `"careful analysis of models trained to perform modular arithmetic Nanda et al. (2023a) or composition of permutations Chughtai et al. (2023); Stander et al. (2023); Wu et al. (2024) have revealed sophisticated algorithms that depend on the representation theory of the corresponding groups"` ([Kvinge et al. 2026](../../externals/2601.21150.can-neural-networks-learn-small-algebraic-worlds/2601.21150.can-neural-networks-learn-small-algebraic-worlds.card.md)). The work asserts exactly the opposite on this point: a concentration of the weights and activations on irreducible representations in the Fourier basis is indeed observed, but "this happens because of the concentration on the cosets of particular subgroups rather than because matrix representations are implemented anywhere in the weights" ([p8-6](#p8-6)), and the representation frame and the coset frame "cannot be *equivalent*" ([p9-2](#p9-2), [p23-1](#p23-1)). The same work 2601.21150 cites accurately elsewhere — `"although they sometimes differ in the specific algorithms they reverse-engineer"` — so the warning here concerns the formulation rather than the work.

**"Coset structures are incompatible with continuous geometry and are not low-norm" (widening).** [Yildirim 2026](../2603.05228.the-geometric-inductive-bias-of-grokking-bypassing-phase-transitions-via-architectural-topology/2603.05228.the-geometric-inductive-bias-of-grokking-bypassing-phase-transitions-via-architectural-topology.card.md) explains the failure of the spherical constraint on $S_{5}$ by a reference to this work: `"successful $S_{5}$ solutions rely on discrete coset structures (Stander et al., 2024) rather than the continuous Fourier features that align with spherical geometry"`, and further, `"For tasks whose generalizing representations are not low-norm relative to memorizing ones—as with the discrete coset structures required for S5 permutation composition Stander et al. (2024)"`. The authors say not a word about the norms of the solutions or about compatibility with spherical geometry; nor is there the word "required" — what is shown is that models **converge** on coset circuits, with the construction serving every subgroup except $A_{n}$ ([p5-7](#p5-7)), and which subgroups a model takes varies from run to run ([fig. 6](#fig-6)). In the same place the model analysed is called a transformer — `"Prior interpretability analyses of transformers trained on this task"` — whereas what is trained here is a single-layer fully connected network with separate left and right embeddings and ReLU ([fig. 1](#fig-1), [p4-1](#p4-1)), and there are no transformers in the work at all. Finally, both frames are placed there side by side as compatible variants — `"whether through higher-dimensional irreducible representations Chughtai et al. (2023) or subgroup coset circuits Stander et al. (2024)"` — whereas here it is said outright that they cannot be equivalent ([p9-2](#p9-2)).

**Corruption of the reference itself (erroneous).** In the bibliography of 2604.06256 (no card in the corpus) the work is listed as `"D. Stander, Q. Yu, H. Fan, and E. Vonosek. Grokking group multiplication with cosets"` — the fourth author replaced by a non-existent person (in fact S. Biderman). The dating has diverged across the corpus separately too: some works cite the preprint as 2023 and others the ICML paper as 2024, sometimes within a single card; the discrepancy is harmless, but the reference should be checked against the identifier 2312.06581.

Milder echoes not warranting separate analysis — enumerations of the kind "group operations were also studied by such-and-such": 2606.17399 (no card in the corpus; `"finding coset-based circuits"` — accurate), [2604.00316](../2604.00316.breaking-data-symmetry-is-needed-for-generalization-in-feature-learning-kernels/2604.00316.breaking-data-symmetry-is-needed-for-generalization-in-feature-learning-kernels.card.md), 2604.06256 (no card in the corpus), [2402.16726](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md), [2310.19470](../2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks/2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks.card.md), [2506.05718](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.card.md), [2402.09469](../2402.09469.fourier-circuits-in-neural-networks-and-transformers-a-case-study-of-modular-arithmetic-with-multiple-inputs/2402.09469.fourier-circuits-in-neural-networks-and-transformers-a-case-study-of-modular-arithmetic-with-multiple-inputs.card.md). They are correct as to fact but say nothing about what the work actually showed.
