## Concepts covered

**[Grokking / delayed generalisation](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the notion its first rigorous proof in a neural-network setting, taking the definition from [Power et al.](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) unchanged: [the model first attains a perfect training accuracy at a complete absence of generalisation (that is, no better than a random predictor), and on further training passes to a nearly perfect generalisation](#p1-4). The claim is formulated literally: [no rigorous proof of grokking in a neural-network setting has been established in any preceding work](#p2-1). What is proved is exactly this ([theorem 3.1](#p6-4)): at step $t=1$ the training accuracy is 100 % while the test error is pinned near $\tfrac{1}{2}$, and at steps from $Cn^{0.01}$ to $\sqrt{n}$ the training accuracy is the same while the test error is nearly zero; the authors say outright that the network [exhibits benign overfitting, and attains it by way of grokking](#p6-6). What the work does not contain: the length of the plateau is not bounded below — the catastrophic stage is proved at exactly one point $t=1$; the sharpness of the transition is not shown; the behaviour as $t\to\infty$ is [left for the future](#p6-7). The work introduces no phase transitions, no progress measures and no circuits, touches no algorithmic data, and the scale of the delay here is a dozen steps rather than $10^{5}$ epochs.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work identifies the "memorising" stage as a concrete object rather than as a black box: after a single step [the neural-network classifier behaves similarly to the linear classifier](#p10-1) $\operatorname{sgn}(\langle\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}x_{i},\cdot\rangle)$, which fits any labels so long as the training data is nearly orthogonal, but on the XOR-cluster distribution gives exactly 50 % error. The ability to fit is thereby ascribed to the [high dimensionality of the feature space](#p8-1) rather than to the structure of the network. The work notes separately that the preceding analyses of benign overfitting [did not show a separation in time between catastrophic overfitting and generalisation](#p2-3), and that the taxonomy "catastrophic / tempered / benign" is taken from [Mal+22](#p3-1). What the work does not contain: no loss plateau is observed here — the training loss stays at about $\log 2$ throughout the window analysed (see the section "What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured"); and neither competing subnetworks nor a "memorisation table" appears in the analysis.

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — the work makes label noise a condition of the result rather than a nuisance: a fraction $\eta$ of the labels is flipped [in definition 2.1](#p4-3), and it is precisely the perfect fit to the flipped labels at a nearly zero clean test error that constitutes the "benignity". The requirement on the fraction is weak — (A3): [the noise fraction satisfies](#p5-9) $\eta\leq 1/C$. What the work does not contain: it studies no dependence of the delay or of the performance on the magnitude of $\eta$, and the 50 % stage at step 1 rests on the nonlinear separability of XOR and does not depend on $\eta$ at all.

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work gives a direct objection to the necessity of a regulariser: [we observe grokking with no explicit forms of regularisation whatever, which reveals the significance of the implicit regularisation of GD](#p11-5). The mechanism of the delay here is wholly geometric — a linear classifier at step 1 against neurons aligned with the cluster means later — and there is no explicit penalty in the setting. What the work does not contain: none of the corpus's grokking settings (Adam, mini-batches, algorithmic data) appears in the analysis, and the authors make no claim that "regularisation is not needed at all" — it is said only that in this setting it is not required.

**[Weight decay / L2 regularisation](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work opposes itself to explanations through weight decay, crediting its predecessors with the claim that [the use of weight decay is essential for grokking](#p11-5); in the survey the same attribution is answered by a more careful formulation, that [LMT23 identified a large initialisation scale together with weight decay as a mechanism of grokking](#p3-2). The training here is full-batch GD on the logistic loss with no norm penalty whatever. What the work does not contain: there are no experiments with weight decay in it, so it cannot be used to assert that weight decay hinders or helps grokking; that one of the two works named advanced no such thesis is in the section "Erroneous citations" below.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the work makes the ratio of the step size to the initialisation scale the control parameter of the delay. Assumption (A5) — [the initialisation variance satisfies](#p5-9) $\omega_{\text{init }}nm^{3/2}p\leq\alpha\Vert\mu\Vert^{2}$ — is read by the authors outright: it [ensures that the step size is large relative to the initialisation scale, so that the network's behaviour after a single GD step differs substantially from its behaviour at a random initialisation](#p6-2). An experiment with a reduced step at the same $\omega_{\text{init}}=10^{-15}$ stretches the same phenomenon to about $10^{3}$ steps ([fig. 1](#fig-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). What the work does not contain: this experiment lies outside the theorem's domain (see below), the authors name no "lazy" or "rich" regimes, and there is no comparison with the concurrent work of [Kumar et al.](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md).

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — the step size $\alpha$ is here one of the two levers of the delay: [at a smaller step the very same grokking phenomenon arises, but with both the overfitting and the generalisation postponed](#p2-2). The step is bounded above by (A4) and below by (A5) through the initialisation scale; the experiments take $\alpha=10^{-12}$ and $\alpha=10^{-16}$ [with everything else equal](#p53-8). What the work does not contain: there is no quantitative law of the delay in $\alpha$ — there are two points and a proved window $[1,\sqrt{n}]$ for the larger step.

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the work gives a mechanism of generalisation in the form of feature learning by individual neurons: [the neurons gradually align with the key features $\pm\mu_{1}$ and $\pm\mu_{2}$, which suffices for generalisation](#p2-4), with the positive neurons having to align with $\pm\mu_{1}$ and the negative ones [with](#p9-5) $\pm\mu_{2}$. The direction a neuron will learn is set by a random imbalance at the initialisation: [the points of cluster $\nu$ that activate the $j$-th neuron are at least $n^{1/2-\kappa}$ more numerous than those of cluster](#p8-10) $-\nu$. The empirical confirmation is [histograms of the projections](#fig-3) and [the geometry of the decision boundary and of the weights](#fig-2). What the work does not contain: the features here are set by the structure of the data (the means of Gaussian clusters) rather than learnt as structure; the work offers no Fourier features, no decompositions of representations and no measures of feature complexity.

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the work gives a picture akin to this notion but does not name it: after a single step the network [approximately realises a linear classifier](#p2-4), that is, it behaves as a fixed-feature predictor, and the generalisation comes later, when the neurons rearrange. The condition (A5) — "the step size is large relative to the initialisation scale" — coincides in sense with the lever by which this transition is governed in the corpus. What the work does not contain: the authors do not use the words lazy, rich or NTK regime of their own dynamics (the NTK is mentioned only [in the survey, in connection with Wei+19](#p3-3)); the work measures neither a kernel nor its change, and it cannot be credited with an explanation of grokking through a change of regime.

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — the work gives explicit test-error bounds in both directions: [a nearly chance error at $t=1$, pinned between $\tfrac{1}{2}(1-n^{-\Omega(1)})$ and $\tfrac{1}{2}(1+n^{-\Omega(1)})$, and $\exp(-\Omega(n^{0.99}\Vert\mu\Vert^{4}/p))=\exp(-\Omega(n^{2.01}))$ for](#p6-5) $Cn^{0.01}\leq t\leq\sqrt{n}$, with an appendix in the form of [theorem 4.8](#p11-3). All of them are stated at a probability of at least $1-n^{-\Omega(1)}-O(1/\sqrt{m})$ ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)) over the data and the initialisation. What the work does not contain: bounds depending on the weight norm, the margin or a complexity; the estimate is obtained by a trajectory analysis rather than through the capacity of a class.

**[Margin maximisation](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — the work places its own result in a row with analyses of the implicit bias of GD: [the implicit regularisation of GD](#p11-5) is named as the reason why generalisation arises without a penalty, and the lower bound is derived through the normalised margin ${\mathbb{E}}_ {x\sim N(\nu,I_{p})}[f(x;W)]/\Vert W\Vert_{F}$ (lemma A.9, [theorem 4.8](#p11-3)). The survey names separately the works that showed benign overfitting [through the stationary points of margin-maximisation problems](#p3-1). What the work does not contain: the work does not consider the limiting direction as $t\to\infty$ at all, so it affords no judgement about convergence to a margin-maximising solution.

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — the setting shows that optimiser noise is not needed for delayed generalisation: the training is [full-batch gradient descent at a constant step size](#p5-3) on the logistic loss, there is no stochasticity, and the whole spread between runs comes from the randomness of the data and the initialisation. What the work does not contain: there is no comparison with SGD or mini-batches, so the work does not yield the conclusion "gradient noise is not needed for grokking at all" — it gives a setting in which there is none and grokking occurs all the same.

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — the work only mentions the adjacent family: the XOR-cluster distribution is placed beside [the sparse parity problem as its own variant](#p3-3), and analyses of the sample complexity of learning sparse parities on the hypercube by SGD are named ([Barak et al.](../2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit/2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit.card.md), Tel23). There are no experiments on parities of the work's own; XOR here is a mixture of four Gaussians in $\mathbb{R}^{p}$ rather than a Boolean function on the hypercube.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work takes from the canonical proving ground only the definition of the phenomenon and replaces the ground itself: [the task is a binary classification of XOR-cluster data](#p4-3), the architecture a two-layer ReLU network with a [frozen second layer](#p5-3), the optimiser full-batch GD, the observation window a dozen steps. What the work does not contain: no modular arithmetic, no transformers and no fraction of training data as a control parameter; reproducibility on algorithmic data is neither claimed nor checked.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)**, **[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)**, **[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)**, **[The sparse subnetwork / the lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — all four notions the work merely mentions, in a single sentence each, [in its survey of grokking](#p3-2): [Bar+22, Nan+23] are named as having proposed progress metrics, [Var+23] as having used circuit efficiency and discovered ungrokking and semi-grokking, [Thi+22] as having ascribed grokking to the slingshot mechanism, and [MTS23] as having explained grokking by a competition of dense and sparse subnetworks. None of them is used in the work's own analysis and none is checked experimentally; that its retelling of [Thi+22] is stronger than the authors' own is in the section "Erroneous citations".

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — mentioned once and only historically: [neural networks in the mean-field regime, in which networks are able to learn features, have better sample-complexity guarantees in this setting than networks in the neural tangent kernel (NTK) regime](#p3-3). The work's own dynamics is not analysed in kernel terms, no kernel is computed, and the authors classify the network's regime in no way.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The definition of a neuron's alignment is written in the paper in three different ways.** The second condition of $(\nu,\kappa)$-alignment in the main text (4.2) is imposed on a maximum: $\max\lbrace d_{-\nu,j}^{(0)},d_{\nu,j}^{(0)}\rbrace<\min\lbrace c_{\nu},c_{-\nu}\rbrace-2(n_{+\nu}+n_{-\nu})-\sqrt{n}$ ([the explanation of it](#p8-10)). In the appendix the same set $\mathcal{J}_ {\nu}^{\kappa}$ is [defined only through](#p19-11) $d_{-\nu,j}^{(0)}$, and in the rewriting of $\mathcal{J}_ {1}$ and $\mathcal{J}_ {2}$ [in section A.5.2](#p39-5) only through $d_{\nu,j}^{(0)}$. The three writings define three different sets of neurons. The proof, meanwhile, establishes the estimate for both indices at once ((A.14): $\mu\in\lbrace\pm\nu\rbrace$), that is, it supports the variant of the main text; what diverge are the definitions, not the proof. In citing "the fraction of aligned neurons" one should take the formulation (4.2).

**(D4) and the (D'4) declared equivalent to it are different statements.** In lemma 4.4 the condition (D4) sums $(c_{\nu}-n_{\nu}-d_{-\nu,j}^{(0)})$ ([`p8-12`](#p8-12)), while its "reformulation" in the appendix sums $(c_{\nu}-d_{-\nu,j}^{(0)})$ ([`p21-5`](#p21-5)) — the term $n_{\nu}$ has vanished, even though the text says outright: "Unfolding the definitions, note that (D'1)-(D'4) are equivalent to (D1)-(D4)". The proof ((A.25) and the computation preceding it) is conducted for the variant with $-n_{+\mu_{1}}$, that is, for (D4); (D'4) has an omission.

**The priority claim and the work's own survey differ in scope.** The introduction asserts that [no rigorous proof of grokking in a neural-network setting has been established in any preceding work](#p2-1), while the survey two pages later reports that [Žunkovič and Ilievski](../2210.15435.grokking-phase-transitions-in-learning-local-rules-with-gradient-descent/2210.15435.grokking-phase-transitions-in-learning-local-rules-with-gradient-descent.card.md) [showed a separation in time between the attainment of zero training error and of zero test error in a binary classification task on a linearly separable distribution](#p3-2). The caveat that removes the contradiction — the nonlinearity of the distribution — does not stand in the claim itself and appears only in the neighbouring paragraph about a [genuinely nonlinear setting](#p2-3).

**Minor notational discrepancies not affecting the conclusions.** In (A.26) the expression for the expectation is typeset as $c_{+\mu_{1}}-n_{+\mu_{1}}(c_{-\mu_{1}}-n_{-\mu_{1}})/2$ — the minus sign before the bracket is missing (the PDF is the same). In [(A.46)](#p33-6) the minimum's bracket is closed differently from (4.2) and (A.13): $\min\lbrace c_{\nu},c_{-\nu}-2n_{\pm\nu}-\sqrt{n}\rbrace$ instead of $\min\lbrace c_{\nu},c_{-\nu}\rbrace-2n_{\pm\nu}-\sqrt{n}$. In the induction step of [lemma A.3](#p26-8) the statement $P(t)$ is written with $\frac{2\alpha p}{n}(t+2)$ but applied in the form $\frac{2\alpha p}{\sqrt{n}}(\tau+2)$.

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

**Proved.** The three parts of [theorem 3.1](#p6-4) under assumptions (A1)-(A6) and at a probability of at least $1-n^{-\Omega(1)}-O(1/\sqrt{m})$: a fit to all the training points for $1\leq t\leq\sqrt{n}$; a test error exactly at the level of chance at $t=1$; and a nearly zero error for $Cn^{0.01}\leq t\leq\sqrt{n}$. The gap in time between the second and third parts is grokking in this setting; this is how it is summed up [in the discussion](#p11-4).

**What the proof does not give, although the abstract speaks more widely.** The abstract calls the catastrophic stage [a period of classical, harmful overfitting](#p1-2), but it is proved at exactly one point $t=1$: the theorem contains no lower bound on the length of the plateau. The ratio of the timings is $Cn^{0.01}$ at an unspecified universal constant $C$, so no quantitative prediction of the delay comparable with those observed in the corpus can be extracted from the theorem. The upper bound $t\leq\sqrt{n}$ is essential and is [left for the future](#p6-7); [the near-orthogonality of the data, that is, *p* large relative to *n*, the authors acknowledge as a second bottleneck](#p11-6).

**A "perfect fit" here means signs rather than a small loss.** By [lemma A.3](#p26-8), throughout the window analysed $\max_{i}|h_{i}^{(t)}|\leq 2/n^{3/2}$, whence [$g_{i}^{(t)}\in[1/4,1]$](#p28-7), that is, $y_{i}f(x_{i};W^{(t)})\approx 0$ and the training loss stays at about $\log 2$ the whole time; [remark A.2](#p26-7) says outright that throughout the window the logistic loss is replaceable by its first-order term. In the corpus's setting of grokking, memorisation is a loss that has gone to zero; here only the accuracy curves coincide, not the loss curves.

**"Forgetting" is relative.** Section 4.2.2 says that the positive neurons [forget both](#p10-4) $\pm\mu_{2}$, but [lemma 4.7](#p11-1) gives a growth of both projections — what separates them is a ratio of rates of order $\sqrt{n/\log n}$. There is no absolute decrease of the projection onto the "foreign" mean in the work.

**Shown by experiment but not proved.** The right-hand panel of [figure 1](#fig-1) and [figure 4](#fig-4) are made at $\alpha=10^{-16}$ at the same $\omega_{\text{init}}=10^{-15}$ [(section A.7)](#p53-8), that is, the step is not increased but decreased — directly against what (A5) requires. The stretching of the delay to about $10^{3}$ steps is therefore shown outside the theorem's domain. The constants taken do not satisfy the assumptions literally at all: at $n=200$, $p=40000$, $\Vert\mu\Vert^{2}=1250$ the condition (A2) would require $p\geq 5\cdot 10^{7}C$. The experiments are declared to be an illustration and are not a check of the theorem's numerical bounds.

**Two phenomena in one setting — but not from one source.** The 50 % at step 1 comes from the nonlinear separability of XOR: [any linear classifier gives exactly half the error on this distribution](#p10-1), and this does not depend on $\eta$ — at $\eta=0$ assumption (A3) holds and the stage remains. The "benignity", by contrast, is substantive only at $\eta>0$. The work shows that the two phenomena coexist but does not separate their sources.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2205-10343

**Liu et al. (2205.10343) — weight decay declared essential for grokking.** Section 5 opposes its own result to its predecessors with the formulation `"In contrast to prior works on grokking which found the usage of weight decay to be crucial for grokking [Liu+22, LMT23]"`. For [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) (LMT23) the attribution is correct: there, at a large initialisation and without regularisation there is no generalisation, at a small one it comes quickly, and weight decay is the control parameter. But [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) advanced no such thesis: in them weight decay is one of the two axes of a phase diagram, grokking occupies a band between comprehension and memorisation, and an increase of weight decay leads out of grokking into comprehension, that is, into fast rather than delayed generalisation. Of grokking itself they say `"grokking is sandwiched between comprehension and memorization, which seems to imply that it is an undesirable phase that stems from improperly tuned hyperparameters"`. The pedigree of the error is visible: this is a feature of Omnigrok carried over to the predecessor work of the same group — the very conflation pattern already recorded in the card of [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md). In the [survey](#p3-2) that same paper is cited accurately — "proposed an effective theory of representation learning for understanding grokking"; the warning concerns the formulation of section 5 rather than the work as a whole.

###### mc-2206-04817

**Thilak et al. (2206.04817) — a coincidence in time retold as an explanation.** The survey has `"[Thi+22] attributed grokking to the slingshot mechanism"`. In the authors themselves slingshots are an empirical observation with two caveats: `"It is empirically observed that grokking as reported in [17] almost exclusively happens at the onset of Slingshots, and is absent without it"` — a coincidence in time, with the hedge "almost exclusively" and with no theory explaining it. The verb "attributed" turns an observed coincidence into a causal explanation; the pattern "the Slingshot causes grokking" is already recorded on the side of [Thilak et al.](../2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md) as a widening one. The second half of the same sentence — "which is measurable through cyclic phase transitions between stable and unstable training regimes" — matches the authors' description.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns of erroneous or widened citation of this work observed in the citing papers of the corpus. Each entry describes what exactly is distorted in the retelling and leads to the authors' own paragraphs, where the lost caveat stands. The work is young and is cited in the corpus mostly by passing mentions, which are accurate; the pattern below concerns the minority.

**"Xu et al. divide the training into memorisation, circuit formation and cleanup" (erroneous).** In a survey enumeration this work is placed in a list of those in whom `"the training process could be a phase transition divided into memorization, circuit formation, and cleanup phase"`. None of this appears in the paper: the work considers no circuits, calls the process no phases, and proves a separation in time between two points — [step $t=1$, at which the test accuracy is at chance](#p6-5), and steps from $Cn^{0.01}$, at which it is nearly perfect — with [the sharpness of the transition and the behaviour as $t\to\infty$ left outside the scope outright](#p6-7). The three-phase division belongs to [Nanda et al.](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md), named first in that same enumeration: their feature has been carried over to their neighbours in the list. Observed in [Furuta et al.](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md).

**What is proved presented as observed (mild).** Retellings of the kind `"the grokking phenomenon observed on XOR cluster data in Xu et al. (2023), where high-dimensional linear classifiers were found to first fit the training data"` are correct in substance but transfer a theorem into the category of observations and credit the fitting to "linear classifiers", whereas in the authors what fits is a network that [merely approximates a linear classifier after a single step](#p10-1). Mild echoes: [Zhou, Zhang, Xu](../2405.17479.a-rationale-from-frequency-perspective-for-grokking-in-training-neural-network/2405.17479.a-rationale-from-frequency-perspective-for-grokking-in-training-neural-network.card.md).

Examples of careful citation: [Notsawo et al.](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.card.md) — `"provide a theoretical example of grokking in ReLU networks by analyzing the feature learning process under GD, and show that test generalization can emerge well after perfect (and initially non-generalizing) memorization of noisy XOR-cluster training data"`, preserving both the setting and the order of events; Liu and Lu (2605.18022, no card as yet) — the only retelling that keeps [the freezing of the second layer](#p5-3) as a limitation of the setting (`"under the assumption of fixed second-layer weights, which deviates from standard end-to-end training dynamics"`); [Xu et al. 2025](../2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model/2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model.card.md) — an honest `"this configuration approximates one of the distributions explored in Xu et al. (2024), where grokking was observed"` where the data is on the hypercube rather than [the Gaussian mixture of definition 2.1](#p4-3).
