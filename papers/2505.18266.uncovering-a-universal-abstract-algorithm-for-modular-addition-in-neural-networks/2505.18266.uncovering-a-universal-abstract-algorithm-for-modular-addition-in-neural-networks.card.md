# McCracken et al. 2025 — Uncovering a Universal Abstract Algorithm for Modular Addition in Neural Networks

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Gavin McCracken**, **Gabriela Moisescu-Pareja** (equal contribution; McGill University and Mila), **Vincent Létourneau** (Université de Montréal), **Doina Precup** (McGill University, Mila and Google DeepMind), **Jonathan Love** (Leiden University).

arXiv [2505.18266](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18266), May 2025 (the sole version v1). There is no citation count for it in the corpus table. The work is large: 53 pages, 46 figures, 6 tables, six appendices; the main part is ten pages. It restores the universality hypothesis by showing that the "clock", the "pizza" and Gromov's analytical solution are realisations of one abstract operation — an **approximate Chinese remainder theorem** — and that $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ frequencies suffice for deep networks. The source is the native arXiv HTML of version v1.

## Concepts covered

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the subject and the proving ground. The task is the same as everyone's: $c=(a+b)\bmod n$ over all $n^{2}$ pairs. What is new is that not one modulus is taken but a whole spread — primes, composites, highly composite numbers and powers of two, from 3 to 4999 ([`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`tab-4`](#tab-4)) — and that both MLPs and transformers of depth 1 to 4 are trained ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work aims at its sorest point: the universality hypothesis looked refuted. On modular arithmetic alone, single-layer MLPs found $\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor$ frequencies, single-layer transformers markedly fewer, and a change of settings gave different circuits ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)). The work's answer: the dispute was about the level of abstraction rather than about the substance, and "circuit" was used to mean anything from a small cluster of neurons to nearly the whole network ([`p14-2`](#p14-2)).

**[Group representations and cosets](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** — the central innovation. A coset requires a strict modular equivalence; an **approximate coset** requires only closeness on the Cayley graph generated by a step $d$ ([`p4-5`](#p4-5), [`p4-6`](#p4-6)). Theorem 4.4 proves that all the ReLU neurons of the first layer fire on an approximate coset containing the correct answer, and that all the neurons of the later layers fire on linear combinations of such cosets ([`p4-7`](#p4-7), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**[The clock against the pizza](../../concepts/clock-vs-pizza.md)** — a contradiction reconciled outright. Both circuits embed the inputs on a circle, but the clock adds the angles after the attention while the pizza takes a vector mean of the embeddings ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). After a re-basing (bringing the frequency to 1) a neuron from an MLP, from a pizza and from a clock look qualitatively identical ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)), and both circuits turn out to be realisations of one abstract operation ([`p49-1`](#p49-1), [`fig-44`](#fig-44), [`fig-46`](#fig-46)).

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the model of a simple neuron: a neuron adapts to a sinusoid of frequency $f$ ([`p3-7`](#p3-7)). This is a generalisation of the sinusoidal model of Gromov and Morwani et al. to architectures with learnable embeddings. It is precisely the presence of learnable embeddings that explains why Nanda et al. and Zhong et al. observed handfuls of frequencies (3–7) instead of $\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor$ ([`p6-6`](#p6-6), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)).

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the main quantitative result: $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ frequencies suffice for deep networks. Theorem 4.7 gives that at $m$ learned frequencies the incorrect logits are suppressed exponentially, and corollary 4.8 that $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ frequencies give a margin of $\Omega(\log n)$ and a probability mass of $n^{-\Omega(1)}$ on the incorrect classes ([`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`p6-3`](#p6-3), [`p6-4`](#p6-4)). Experimentally this is checked at moduli differing by several orders of magnitude ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)).

**[Margin maximisation](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — theorem 4.7 recovers the result of Morwani et al. as a particular case: the maximum-margin solution requires learning **all** $\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor$ frequencies, but beyond that the work gives the size of the margin at smaller numbers of them too ([`p6-3`](#p6-3)). Single-frequency solutions are possible but have poor margins and are therefore learned rarely — only at the edge of the settings grid ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)).

**[The sparse subnetwork](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — pruning experiments: removing all the neurons of the refinement cluster barely affects the accuracy, whereas the clusters of simple neurons are essential ([`fig-38`](#fig-38)). Multiplicative noise with a deviation of up to 0.225 is tolerated by the network ([`fig-37`](#fig-37)).

**[Network depth](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — depth changes the solution twice over: it lowers the number of learned frequencies from $\mathcal{O}(n)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ ([`p6-6`](#p6-6), [`fig-4`](#fig-4), [`fig-41`](#fig-41)) and at the same time improves the loss and the margins ([`fig-42`](#fig-42)). The authors' explanation: the layers after the first preserve the fraction of first-order sinusoids, repeating the same computation on cosets — and this is read as a **noise-resistant code** ([`p47-3`](#p47-3)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — it is not the subject of the work: grokking is named as the phenomenon for the sake of understanding which this branch of interpretability began ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)), and it is related in the survey to representations, margin maximisation, label corruption and non-neural architectures ([`p15-3`](#p15-3)). The work itself explains **what** is learned rather than **when** it happens.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the connection with the work of Nanda et al. is twofold: their "Fourier multiplication operation" is analysed as a particular case, and the previously unexplained appearance of second-order sines under ReLU activation receives an explanation — firing on a coset $c$ requires understanding the membership of $a$ and $b$ ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no answer to **why** the transition from $\mathcal{O}(n)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ frequencies occurs: the authors themselves call it an open question and a sign that new interpretability instruments are needed ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)). Nor is there an estimate of the number of neurons per frequency — only a trivial lower bound $\Omega(1)$ is given ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)). And there are no groups between the two extremes: cyclic groups (the least structure) are checked and, through somebody else's work, permutations (the most), while everything in between is left to further work ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the "clock", whose operation is analysed here as a particular realisation; [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — the analytical solution with $\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor$ frequencies, generalised by the simple-neuron model; [Doshi et al. 2024](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md) — the circuits in grokked transformers on modular polynomials; [Mohamadi et al. 2024](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.card.md) — margin maximisation as an explanation of the grokking of modular addition; [Saxena et al. 2024](../2410.03569.making-hard-problems-easier-with-custom-data-distributions-and-loss-regularization/2410.03569.making-hard-problems-easier-with-custom-data-distributions-and-loss-regularization.card.md) — modular addition with many summands, where the limit turned out to be a limit of the data; [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon with which this branch began. The works of Zhong et al. ("the clock and the pizza"), Morwani et al. (margin maximisation) and Chughtai et al. (GCR), on which this paper leans most heavily, are absent from the corpus.

**[The approximate Chinese remainder theorem](../../concepts/approximate-chinese-remainder-theorem.md)** — the notion is introduced by this work as an abstract description of the computation ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The key to it is a weakened notion of a coset: closeness on the Cayley graph instead of an exact division of the modulus ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)), whence both the checkable statement about the neurons ([`p4-7`](#p4-7)) and the count of the frequencies needed ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[The universality hypothesis](../../concepts/universality-hypothesis.md)** — the work restores it after two refutations by moving the claim from the neurons onto an abstract operation ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The earlier coset-based schemes thereby become a particular case ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)), and the history of the dispute itself is set out here as a justification for changing the level of description ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)).

**[Architectural inductive bias](../../concepts/architectural-inductive-bias.md)** — hence a limitation on its role: if multi-layer perceptrons and transformers realise one and the same abstract operation ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)), then the architecture governs the timing and the reachability but not the content of the learned solution.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is large: 53 pages, 46 figures, 6 tables, six appendices; the main part is ten pages.

**The definition of an approximate coset is a genuine find.** It is general enough to cover both the case $\gcd(f,n)>1$ (exact cosets) and the case of coprime $f$ and $n$ (a decay with distance on the Cayley graph), and yet narrow enough for theorem 4.4 to be proved in a page and a half ([`p4-5`](#p4-5), [`p4-7`](#p4-7)). The authors admit as much: the simplicity of the proof is a consequence of a well-chosen definition ([`p19-3`](#p19-3)).

**The re-basing (bringing the frequency to 1) makes different neurons comparable.** It is a simple device — a change of variable through the modular inverse — but it is precisely what makes it possible to set a neuron from an MLP, from a pizza and from a clock side by side and see that they are the same ([`p4-1`](#p4-1), [`p4-3`](#p4-3), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)).

**The ablation is carried out in earnest.** The neurons are replaced by the sinusoids approximating them and the model's accuracy is rechecked; a green tick is given only if **all ten** seeds retained 100 % ([`p25-1`](#p25-1)). This is the strictest check seen in the corpus that an abstraction loses nothing essential.

**The coverage of settings and seeds is enormous.** A grid of 10 learning rates by 20 weight decays with 10 seeds in each cell, 5000 models for figure 9, 100 thousand random seeds for the frequency histograms ([`p25-1`](#p25-1), [`p26-1`](#p26-1), [`p37-1`](#p37-1)). The earlier works analysed single exemplary networks.

**The $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ result is confirmed over several orders of magnitude of the modulus.** Primes, composites, highly composite numbers and powers of two — from 3 to 4999 ([`fig-3`](#fig-3), [`tab-4`](#tab-4)); the logarithmic fits give a very high $R^{2}$.

**Open questions of Zhong et al. are closed along the way.** Non-circular embeddings and Lissajous curves are explained by the two principal components coming from **different** frequency clusters; when they come from one, the embedding is circular ([`p27-4`](#p27-4), [`p27-6`](#p27-6), [`fig-14`](#fig-14)–[`fig-22`](#fig-22)).

**Universality across groups, however, is obtained not by the authors' own experiment but by a splice with somebody else's.** The claim of "universality between cyclic groups and permutation groups" rests on Stander et al. having found coset circuits in networks for $S_{n}$, and on approximate cosets generalising cosets here ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)). The authors trained no network on $S_{n}$ and checked no groups between the two extremes ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)).

**The universality hypothesis is reformulated so as to be hard to refute.** An "approximate coset" with distances $k_{1},k_{2}$ up to $n$ ([`p4-6`](#p4-6)) covers the whole group at sufficiently large $k$. The work gives no threshold beyond which the abstraction ceases to be substantive — and without one, any activation falls within some approximate coset.

**Theorem 4.7 is proved not rigorously but in the spirit of an estimate.** A Taylor expansion is applied where the authors themselves admit it is "quite bad" ([`p21-3`](#p21-3)); the probability comes out "approximately equal" to one minus a volume estimate ([`p21-5`](#p21-5)); the frequencies are assumed independent and uniform, and the contributions of all the clusters equal in magnitude ([`p21-1`](#p21-1)). This is a plausible argument rather than a proof, and the authors themselves call it a rule of thumb.

**The main experimental result remains unexplained.** The transition from $\mathcal{O}(n)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ frequencies when depth or learnable embeddings are added is the work's most unexpected observation, and the authors write outright that they do not know why it happens ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)).

**The number of neurons per frequency is not estimated at all.** A trivial lower bound $\Omega(1)$ is given; why the phases sometimes fall into a correct grid and sometimes do not is likewise unknown ([`p33-4`](#p33-4)).

**The text is unproofread.** Twice in appendix D there stands an unresolved reference `Fig. LABEL:fig:embedding-weights-histogram` — that is, a figure referred to twice is simply not in the paper ([`p22-10`](#p22-10), [`p23-2`](#p23-2)); section F.1 consists of the sentence "this section will be removed in future versions"; a sentence in appendix G.3 breaks off mid-word ([`p33-1`](#p33-1)); and the claim about a noise-resistant code is labelled "experimental results" although there is one result there — a conjecture about $\mathcal{O}(n^{-\Omega(L)})$ ([`p47-3`](#p47-3)).

**Its place in the corpus.** The work is not about grokking but about **what** exactly is learned in the task on which grokking is most often studied. Four things are valuable: the definition of an approximate coset, reconciling the "clock", the "pizza" and Gromov's analytical solution under one pattern; the proof that all the ReLU activations fall on such cosets; the quantitative estimate of $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ for the number of frequencies needed, with an experimental check over several orders of magnitude; and the recognition that depth and learnable embeddings change the number of learned frequencies by an order of magnitude. The price: universality across groups is obtained by a splice with somebody else's work, theorem 4.7 is proved in the spirit of an estimate, the most unexpected result is left unexplained, and the text is plainly unfinished.
