# Beck, Bar-Sinai & Levi 2026 — The Implicit Bias of Logit Regularization

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Alon Beck**, **Yohai Bar-Sinai** (Tel Aviv University; the Hebrew University of Jerusalem), **Noam Levi** (EPFL).

arXiv [2602.12039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12039), February 2026 (v2), 28 pages, 20 figures. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 1, in the corpus — 1. A convex penalty placed directly in the space of the logits (label smoothing being a particular case) changes the implicit bias of a linear classification from margin maximisation to a **clustering of the logits** around finite per-target values; for Gaussian data or the quadratic loss it is proved that the clustering aligns the weights exactly along the Fisher discriminant $\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mu}$. In the "signal plus noise" model at zero signal noise the penalty shifts the interpolation threshold from $\lambda_{c}=d/N=1/2$ to $\lambda_{c}=1$, and in the band $1/2<\lambda<1$ under a weak penalty grokking arises with a delay that diverges as $\alpha\to 0$; the limiting accuracy is strictly independent of the scale of the orthogonal noise. A direct continuation of Beck et al. 2025 (grokking at the edge of linear separability).

## Concepts covered

**[Margin maximisation](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — the central move of the work: without a penalty the per-target cross-entropy is monotone, and gradient descent drives $\Vert\boldsymbol{S}\Vert\to\infty$ by the implicit bias of Soudry et al.; any convex logit penalty creates a finite minimum $z^{ * }$ of the per-target loss, and the problem turns into a geometric trade-off — to squeeze all the logits towards $z^{ * }$ ([`p2-11`](#p2-11)). The proved chain: the clustering is equivalent to a minimisation of the coefficient of variation $\sigma/\mu$ (Proposition 3.1), and its minimum is the Fisher discriminant $\hat{\boldsymbol{S}}\propto\Sigma^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mu}$ (Corollary 3.3) — the direction depends only on the geometry of the data, which is why the accuracy is insensitive to the form and the strength of the penalty ([`p4-7`](#p4-7), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — arises in the band $1/2<\lambda<1$ between the old and the new thresholds: the data are already separable, the "cheap" overfitting solution is available at once, and the generalising one exists only thanks to the penalty; the model first overfits in the direction of the unregularised bias and then converges to the clustering, and the delay diverges as $\alpha\to 0$ ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)). A rare mechanism: the controlling parameter of grokking is the **strength of the regularisation** rather than the sample size or the weight norm. The analysis notes: the whole analytics is about the minimum of the loss, there is no time scale of the delay, and the single binary experiment on grokking is set up at a signal ten times weaker ($\mu_{f}=0.1$) than the rest of the text.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the contrast is drawn by a check rather than by words (appendix E.4, [`p24-2`](#p24-2), [`fig-18`](#fig-18)): the $L_{2}$ penalty does not shift the interpolation threshold and leaves the accuracy dependent on the orthogonal noise — both features belong precisely to the per-target decomposition of the loss and not to a norm constraint in general. It is honestly stated that for a linear model the quadratic logit penalty equals a generalised Tikhonov regularisation $\alpha\boldsymbol{S}^{\top}\hat{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{S}$ over the uncentred covariance of the data ([`p25-2`](#p25-2)).

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — label smoothing is formally reduced to a logit penalty: in the binary case $f_{\mathrm{LS}}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\log(2+2\cosh t)$ at $\alpha=2\varepsilon$ (appendix B), in the multi-class one equation (14) ([`p13-1`](#p13-1)); all the results are claimed for a whole class of convex logit penalties rather than for LS alone. In all the numerical experiments, meanwhile, $f(z)=z^{2}$.

**[Softmax collapse](../../concepts/softmax-collapse.md)** — the claimed connection with Prieto et al. 2025: "logit regularization in this context was briefly discussed… we find an analogous region of delayed generalization with a shifted threshold" ([`p12-5`](#p12-5)). The analysis notes that the mechanisms are incompatible and that the paper does not say so: in Prieto et al. the penalty merely keeps the logits from diverging until finite arithmetic zeroes the gradient, whereas here the arithmetic is exact and the delay is created by the penalty itself; nothing is said in the paper about the precision of the computations, so it is indistinguishable whether the $\alpha=0$ baseline in fig. 5 stalls for reasons intrinsic to the problem or because of float32 arithmetic.

**[Neural collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — in the multi-class case the targets $\boldsymbol{z}^{ * }_{(c)}$ form the vertices of a symmetric simplex, and the clustering goes towards them ([`p8-1`](#p8-1), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)); the resemblance to neural collapse is claimed by the word "reminiscent", and not a single NC measure is computed. A multi-class repetition of grokking: the logits first diverge towards the overfitting solution and then fall into the vertices of the simplex ([`fig-8`](#fig-8)).

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong side — what is proved and checked.** The chain "penalty → clustering → coefficient of variation → Fisher discriminant" is proved for Gaussian data and for the quadratic loss (Cauchy–Schwarz, C.1); the shift of the threshold (Statement 5.1) and the invariance to $\sigma_{n}$ (Statement 6.1) are exact results with transparent proofs. Four predictions are checked by separate experiments: the insensitivity of the direction to $\alpha$ (fig. 2, Student noise), the linearity of $1/\cos^{2}\theta_{1}$ in $1/\sigma_{n}^{2}$ (fig. 19), the difference of the noise scales on the features of a ResNet-18 (fig. 3), and the non-reproduction of both effects under weight decay (fig. 18).

**Grokking is the least measured part.** The delay is explained by a change of the bias over time, but the time scale is not derived — there is neither a formula nor a fit of $t_{\mathrm{grok}}(\alpha)$; the evidence in the binary case is one fig. 5 (four curves over $\alpha$, with no averaging over seeds), set up at $\mu_{f}=0.1$, whereas the rest of the text lives at $\mu_{f}=1$, and this is nowhere stated. The whole analytics is about the minimum of the loss; Statements 5.1 and 6.1 say where the optimum is and are silent about how long it takes to reach it. The reference to the divergence of the time in Levi et al. 2024 is an analogy: there the controlling parameter is the geometry of the problem, here it is the strength of the penalty.

**The check on real data is qualitative and silently weakened.** A linear probe on top of a frozen ResNet-18 checks two of the four claims; no grokking is shown there at all, the clustering towards the Fisher discriminant is not measured, and the strict independence of $\sigma_{n}$ is weakened to "more robust".

**Minor mismatches.** The lower bound in §3.2 is written as $\mathcal{L}\geq z^{ * }$ instead of $\ell(z^{ * })$; the letter $f$ in C.3 denotes the whole per-target loss while in §3.1 it denotes the penalty alone; in E.6 the caption "Figure 1 (Zero Feature Noise)" refers to figure 4, and "Fig. 11, Right" in E.6.1 to the right panel of figure 9; formulas (23)–(24) are typeset with LaTeX errors from the source (visible in the PDF too), which do not affect the conclusion.

**How the work will be cited** ("label smoothing causes grokking") — more precisely: in a linear classifier on synthetic data with strictly zero noise along the signal, in the band $1/2<\lambda<1$ and at a small $\alpha$, a delayed generalisation with a non-monotone test loss is observed; this is shown in no non-linear model and on no real data.
