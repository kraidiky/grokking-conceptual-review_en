## Concepts covered

**[Low-pass filtering of the gradient](../../concepts/gradient-low-pass-filtering.md)** — the work starts from Grokfast and generalises it: instead of a **fixed** low-pass filter, the gradient is transformed by a **learnable** module ([`p1-3`](#p1-3), [`p1-4`](#p1-4)). The claim is stated broadly: the technique speeds generalisation up on tasks ranging from simple operations to composite arithmetical ones ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). The comparison is direct: NeuralGrok speeds generalisation up by as much as $2.95\times$ against ordinary training and $2.08\times$ against GrokFast-MA, and on the hardest task by $4.67\times$, whereas GrokFast-EMA and ordinary training fail to solve it at all within $10^{6}$ steps ([`p4-5`](#p4-5), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)).

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the technique is built as a bilevel optimisation: in the inner loop the gradients of the main model are transformed by a *neural amplifier* and applied to its update, and in the outer loop the amplifier itself is trained to lower the loss on a separate small subset of the training data (a ratio of $49:1$) ([`p2-4`](#p2-4), [`p4-2`](#p4-2)). The transformation is given explicitly: a softmax distribution over the elements of the gradient rotates it without changing its length, while a multiplier $c$ sets the magnitude ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work's most interesting objection to the corpus. It is shown that weight decay is not only unnecessary but harmful: under ordinary training with it the test accuracy after the generalisation oscillates between $\sim 100$% and $<5$%, and at a tenfold larger value the model ceases to learn at all ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)). On the hard task the use of weight decay alone gives a ruinous collapse ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — a working definition is taken and stated in the abstract: an intricate phenomenon in which generalisation is attained after a long era of overfitting ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The practical conclusion for the corpus: **plain gradient normalisation** turns out to be a better regularisation than weight decay. At $c=0.5,1.0,2.0$ the training is balanced with no spikes, and the hard task ($a\times c$+$b\times d$−e) mod 7, which was intractable at an unchanged gradient magnitude, becomes solvable ([`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — a new quantity is proposed: the **absolute gradient entropy** (AGE), $H(\mathcal{G})=-\sum|g_{i}|\ln|g_{i}|$, after the model of Golechha's absolute weight entropy ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`p7-2`](#p7-2)). The observation: in the memorisation phase the AGE grows and in the generalisation phase it falls, and this correspondence holds in all three methods compared ([`p7-4`](#p7-4), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)).

**[Kolmogorov complexity](../../concepts/kolmogorov-complexity.md)** — the work joins the line "generalisation is compression": NeuralGrok speeds generalisation up by lowering the complexity of the model as measured by the entropy of the weights and the gradients ([`p7-5`](#p7-5), [`fig-6`](#fig-6)). It is noted separately that the weight norm, by recent works, explains the phase transition poorly — which is why entropy is taken instead ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — five tasks of varying difficulty: four two-argument ones ((a+b), (a−b), ($a\times b$), ($a\times a$−b) mod 97) and one five-argument one, ($a\times c$+$b\times d$−e) mod 7 ([`p4-4`](#p4-4)). A 50/50 split, a two-layer transformer (four-layer for the hard task), a three-layer MLP as the amplifier ([`p3-7`](#p3-7), [`p4-4`](#p4-4)). All the tasks are modular with a prime $p$, and the size of the dataset at $n$ variables is roughly $p^{n}$ ([`p11-3`](#p11-3)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the transition windows are marked on the plots and set against the course of the AGE; the claim is that the AGE serves as an **indicator** of phase transitions rather than as a mere companion ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)).

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — a reading of the two phases through the AGE: during the memorisation the model fits an intricate feature space, and during the generalisation it gradually compresses the memorised features into a generalisable pattern ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — a side observation, but a valuable one: subtraction turns out to be **harder** for a transformer than addition and multiplication, contrary to human intuition about difficulty ([`p5-2`](#p5-2)). The authors draw from this outright the need for a mechanistic interpretation based on the model rather than on human judgements.

**[Composition in a non-commutative group](../../concepts/group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md)** — the composite five-argument tasks play the role here of a difficulty test, similar in conception to the non-commutative settings in the corpus ([`p4-4`](#p4-4)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no transferability: the learned gradient transformation hardly transfers even between $+$ and $-$, which is admitted and given a section of its own ([`p8-2`](#p8-2), [`p8-3`](#p8-3)). Nor is there a check beyond artificial arithmetical tasks ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)). And there is no analysis of the cost: the bilevel optimisation requires a copy of the model and additional passes, but a reckoning of that cost is not given.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the tasks and the phenomenon; [Grokfast](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md) — the nearest rival and the source of the idea; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — the Goldilocks zone, with which the work argues; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the weight norm as an explanation; [DeMoss et al. 2024](../2412.09810.the-complexity-dynamics-of-grokking/2412.09810.the-complexity-dynamics-of-grokking.card.md) — the dynamics of complexity, on which the choice of an entropy measure leans; [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — the lazy-to-rich transition, named in the survey.

**[Accelerating grokking](../../concepts/accelerated-grokking.md)** — the gradient transformation here is not specified by hand but learned by an auxiliary module jointly with the model ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). This distinguishes the technique from filtering with fixed coefficients: the form of the transformation becomes a result of training rather than a guess.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is an applied one: one technique, five tasks, one new measure.

**The main practical result is not NeuralGrok itself but the gradient normalisation.** Simply bringing the gradient to a constant length balances the training and makes solvable a task that is otherwise not solvable at all ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)). This is an order of magnitude cheaper than the bilevel optimisation and requires no auxiliary network. The work shows this honestly, though it presents it as a side experiment.

**The speed-up is measured, but there is only one comparison baseline.** All the numbers ($2.95\times$, $2.08\times$, $4.67\times$) are obtained at one value of weight decay, $1e^{-3}$, chosen as "stable for the baseline methods" ([`p4-1`](#p4-1)). The appendix shows that at $1e^{-2}$ the baseline methods do not learn at all — so the choice is justified, but the hyperparameter space of the baselines is surveyed at two points.

**The number of runs is stated nowhere.** Neither the main text nor the figure captions says how many seeds were averaged; there are no spread bands on the curves. For a work whose main claim is quantitative (how many times faster), this is a substantial gap.

**The AGE measure is proposed but not checked as a predictor.** A correspondence is shown: the AGE grows during the memorisation and falls during the generalisation ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)). It is not shown that the AGE can be used to **predict** the moment of the transition in advance or to tell a run that will generalise from one that will not. The measure remains descriptive.

**The claim "NeuralGrok speeds things up by lowering the complexity" is not checked causally.** All that is observed is that NeuralGrok's AWE and AGE values are lower than those of the methods compared ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)). There is no intervention that would lower the AGE directly and check the consequence.

**The negative result on transferability is given honestly and in detail.** An amplifier trained on addition does not transfer to subtraction ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)); the explanation is the narrowness of the outer objective $\mathcal{D}_{outer}$ and an adaptation to the local geometry of the task ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)). This limits the practical value of the technique: the amplifier has to be trained anew for each task.

**The objection about weight decay is stronger than it sounds.** The statement "weight decay is not always good" is supported by two different observations: oscillations of the accuracy after the generalisation under ordinary training ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)) and a complete collapse on the hard task under weight decay alone ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)). For a corpus in which weight decay is almost everywhere held to be a condition of grokking, this is a notable correction.

**The observation about the difficulty of subtraction deserves separate attention.** Subtraction requires more steps than addition and multiplication in **all** the methods compared ([`p5-2`](#p5-2)). This is a reproducible anomaly which the work does not explain but honestly singles out as an argument for a mechanistic analysis.

**The cost of the technique is not counted.** The bilevel optimisation requires a copy of the model and an additional pass at every outer step. The speed-up is measured in optimisation steps rather than in computation or wall time — and under such a reckoning a technique that makes each step dearer looks more advantageous than it is.

**Its place in the corpus.** The work continues the line of accelerating grokking by intervening in the gradient and takes a step from a fixed filter to a learnable transformation. The most valuable things in it are two side conclusions: gradient normalisation as a more effective and far cheaper replacement for weight decay, and the absolute gradient entropy as a quantity whose behaviour is aligned with the phases. The price: no spread bands and no number of seeds, a descriptive (rather than predictive) role for the AGE, an amplifier that does not transfer between tasks, and unaccounted computational costs.
