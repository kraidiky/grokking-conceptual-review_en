# Ersoy & Wiesner 2026 — Noise-Driven Escape from Metastable Phases explains Grokking in Deep Neural Networks

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Ibrahim Talha Ersoy**, **Karoline Wiesner** (Complexity Science Group, Institute of Physics and Astronomy, University of Potsdam).

arXiv [2606.17120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17120), June 2026 (the sole version v1), 13 pages, 4 figures. Accepted at HiLD 2026 — the 4th Workshop on High-dimensional Learning Dynamics. There is no citation count for it in the corpus table. The work explains grokking by **hysteresis**: the transitions in the strength of the L2 regularisation are first-order transitions, the metastable phases are separated by energy barriers, and the delay of the generalisation is the waiting time until the SGD noise carries the model across the barrier. The escape time obeys the Kramers–Arrhenius law with an effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}}\propto\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}/B$. The source is the native arXiv HTML of version v1.

## Concepts covered

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the foundation of the whole work, and it is taken ready-made from the same group's earlier works: as the strength of the L2 regularisation changes, a deep network undergoes **first-order** transitions, one per non-zero singular value of the data covariance ([`p1-2`](#p1-2), [`p2-1`](#p2-1)). Hence the main line of thought: if the transition is first-order, then there are coexisting phases, a barrier between them and **hysteresis** ([`p3-1`](#p3-1)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — explained not by the transition itself but by an **escape from a metastable state** in which the model is locked by a barrier ([`p1-3`](#p1-3)). The delay is the waiting time until the SGD noise carries the model across the barrier; the abruptness is a property of the escape itself ([`p3-5`](#p3-5), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)).

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the object is measured directly: a slice of the landscape along the minimum-loss path, a saddle point $\lambda^{\mathrm{sad}}\approx 0.41$, a barrier $\Delta E_{\mathrm{min}}\approx 0.003$ ([`p4-1`](#p4-1), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)). A bifurcation diagram shows how the stable minimum and the saddle merge and annihilate in a saddle-node bifurcation ([`fig-4`](#fig-4)).

**[Effective theory and statistical physics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — the frame is borrowed wholesale: SGD is mapped onto a Langevin process with an effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}}\propto\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}/B$, and the escape time obeys the Kramers–Arrhenius law ([`p3-2`](#p3-2), [`p3-3`](#p3-3)). Hence a refutable prediction: $\ln\tau$ is linear in $B/\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}$ ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)).

**[Optimisation methods](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the learning rate and the batch size enter the explanation not as settings but as the **single** quantity $\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}/B$ that sets the temperature. The check: five values of the learning rate, 50 seeds per point, a linear fit with $R^{2}=0.991$ ([`p4-2`](#p4-2), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — here it is not the cause of the generalisation but a **control lever**: the strength of the regularisation is used deliberately to set a trap, so as to reproduce grokking ([`p3-5`](#p3-5), [`p10-1`](#p10-1)).

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — countable: the number of metastable states equals the number of features to be learned, one per singular value of $\Sigma_{yx}$ ([`p1-2`](#p1-2), [`p3-1`](#p3-1)). Hence a prediction of **stepwise** grokking in $d$ steps ([`p5-3`](#p5-3)).

**[Data fraction](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — needed in order to obtain the textbook curve: with dense data there is no gap between the training and the validation at all, and it appears only under a sparse subsample — 25 examples out of 5000 ([`p3-5`](#p3-5), [`p6-2`](#p6-2)).

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — a direct statement: the network is linear, the task is random, memorising the sample is impossible, and grokking is reproduced all the same ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)). Hence the conclusion that memorisation is not a necessary part of the phenomenon, and that "memorisation" and "generalisation" are descriptions of a partial and a complete progress through a succession of rank transitions.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the regularisation as the central cause, argued with here; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — interpretable representations at the transition point; [Rubin et al. 2024](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.card.md) — the comparison with a first-order phase transition through an entropic barrier, to which this work is closest and from which it demarcates itself; [Tian 2025](../2509.21519.provable-scaling-laws-of-feature-emergence-from-learning-dynamics-of-grokking/2509.21519.provable-scaling-laws-of-feature-emergence-from-learning-dynamics-of-grokking.card.md) — the scaling laws of the emergence of features; [Levi et al. 2024](../2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity/2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity.card.md) — solvable models that also grok.

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — the only work in the corpus in which SGD noise is placed at the foundation of the mechanism: grokking is an escape from a metastable state, and the escape time obeys an Arrhenius law with an effective temperature $T_{\mathrm{eff}}\propto\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}/B$ ([`p1-2`](#p1-2), [`p3-3`](#p3-3)). The check is made in a deliberately noisy setting (SGD, a deliberate trapping); the question of the carry-over to the full-batch runs of the chain in the concept card remains open.

**[The solution manifold](../../concepts/execution-manifold.md)** — the exit from the trap is described as hysteresis at a first-order phase transition in the strength of the $L_2$ regularisation ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). This gives the trap a physical mechanism of exit, which a geometric description does not itself contain.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is short: 5 pages of main text and 7 appendices, 4 figures.

**The mechanism is named precisely and checkably.** Not "grokking resembles a phase transition" but "grokking is a wait for an escape from a metastable state, and the waiting time obeys an Arrhenius law with a temperature $\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}/B$" ([`p3-3`](#p3-3), [`p3-4`](#p3-4)). Such a claim either breaks at the first measurement or holds — and it was checked: five values of the learning rate, 50 seeds per point, $R^{2}=0.991$ ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)).

**The negative result about memorisation comes free and is therefore convincing.** A linear network on a random task cannot memorise the sample — its training error runs into the Bayes limit — and grokking is reproduced all the same ([`p6-2`](#p6-2)). This is the strongest place in the work: it cuts off a whole class of explanations without requiring a single additional experiment.

**The discrepancy between the computed and the measured barrier is not hidden but explained.** $\Delta E_{\mathrm{min}}\approx 0.003$ against $\Delta E_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.15\pm 0.05$ — a factor of about fifty, ascribed to the entropic corrections of the Kramers–Langer formula in $D=170$ dimensions ([`p5-1`](#p5-1), [`p11-1`](#p11-1)). The explanation is plausible but remains qualitative: the Hessian is not computed, and the number 50 is not derived from the formula.

**Grokking here, however, is not observed but manufactured.** All three trapping protocols start from a **checkpoint trained at a larger regularisation**, that is, the model is placed into the trap by hand ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)). This is a sound check that *if* the model is trapped, *then* it behaves as in grokking. That under ordinary training a network falls into such a trap of its own accord the work does not show.

**There is not a single experiment on a real grokking task.** The modular arithmetic on which the phenomenon was discovered is mentioned only in a reference. Three predictions — the stepwise behaviour, the dependence on depth, and control through $B/\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}$ — are declared immediately checkable on the existing benchmarks ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)) and are checked on none of them.

**The carry-over to non-linear networks rests on references.** Appendix G says that deep non-linear networks exhibit the same first-order transitions — with a reference to the same authors' earlier works ([`p13-1`](#p13-1)); their own "preliminary experiments" are mentioned in a single line with not a number and not a figure ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)).

**The setting is extremely small.** $d=2$ features, a depth of 3, a width of 10, $D=170$ values, $N=512$ examples ([`p9-1`](#p9-1), [`p9-2`](#p9-2)). For an analytically solvable model this is justified, but the claim that "the same mechanism most likely operates in general non-linear DNNs too" does not hold on such a foundation.

**Three numbers in the paper do not agree.** The caption of figure 1 says $\beta=0.03$, whereas both the text and appendix A say $\beta=0.32$ ([`fig-1`](#fig-1), [`p3-5`](#p3-5), [`p10-1`](#p10-1)). Appendix D derives the ordering $\beta_{c}^{(1)}>\cdots>\beta_{c}^{(d)}$ ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)), while appendix B reports $\beta_{c}^{(1)}=0.36$ and $\beta_{c}^{(2)}=0.42$ ([`p10-2`](#p10-2)) — the reverse ordering. Appendix A describes the initialisation from a checkpoint as training at $\beta_{\mathrm{init}}=0$ up to a full-rank solution ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)), whereas in section 3.1 the checkpoints are trained at $\beta>\beta_{c}$ ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)); these are different settings under one name.

**The "two orders of magnitude" cannot be reconstructed.** The claimed range of escape times is two orders of magnitude ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)), while the numbers given are $\tau\approx 10$, $\tau\approx 5500$ and $\tau>10\,000$ epochs ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)), that is, three orders from the first to the last and less than one between the two trapped protocols. What exactly is being compared with what is not said.

**What is valuable for the corpus is a quantity one can control.** $\ln\tau\propto B/\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}$ is a prediction checkable on any dataset on which grokking is already observed: it suffices to vary the batch size and the learning rate and see whether the logarithm of the delay falls on a straight line. No other work in the corpus offers so cheap a check — and it is the more regrettable that the authors did not carry it out themselves.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2310-03789
**Rubin et al. (2310.03789) — an "entropy barrier" ascribed to them.** `"Rubin et al. [11] proposed a first order phase transition analogy involving an entropy barrier. This was then challenged by Zhang et al. [23], who found no entropy barrier"`. There is no barrier in Rubin: [the saddles exist separately and remain degenerate in the mixed phase](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/original/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.md#p6-6), and the kinetics of the transition is not considered — the construction of an "entropy barrier" belongs to the operationalisation of Zhang et al. and is projected back onto Rubin, which makes Zhang's "challenge" look like a refutation of something Rubin never claimed.
