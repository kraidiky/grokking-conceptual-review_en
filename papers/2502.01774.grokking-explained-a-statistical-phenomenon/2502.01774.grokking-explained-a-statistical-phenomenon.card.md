## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives the corpus two things: a formal definition and a statistical framing. The authors state outright that the literature has no definition of grokking, "only descriptions and examples of the phenomenon", and reduce the descriptions to Definition 3.1 ([p4-1](#p4-1)): grokking has occurred if, after the training curve converges at a moment $\alpha_{0}$, the test performance at a moment $\alpha_{1}$ falls into an interval $\Delta_{T}$ and stays there for an interval $S\gg(\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{0})$; together with the definition, the quantities $\Delta_{S}$ ("how much grokking occurred"), $\Delta_{T}$ ("room for improvement") and a reference interval $S$ are introduced ([fig-2](#fig-2)). The substantive thesis is that grokking is a statistical phenomenon arising from a distribution shift between the test and training data, while a high regularisation and parameterisation "amplify but do not define" it ([p4-3](#p4-3)). What the work does not contain: not a single measure of the shift — the distance between the distributions is nowhere computed, and the two-sample tests for whose sake Rabanser et al. 2019 are cited ([p3-3](#p3-3)) are not applied; $\Delta_{S}$ is introduced as a measure of intensity and is never computed as a number. The definition rests on the moment $\alpha_{0}$ at which the training curve converges — and in the results plots the training accuracy is drawn as a horizontal line at one from the very first step shown ([fig-3](#fig-3), [fig-4](#fig-4)): they are captioned "Average test set curves after the training loss has converged", that is, they begin where the convergence has already happened, and $\alpha_{0}$ cannot be located from them. The thresholds for "$\ll$" and "$\gg$" are not named, the points $\alpha_{0}$ and $\alpha_{1}$ are determined by eye, and so the decision "grokking has occurred" remains a visual one.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work leaves the canonical proving ground deliberately and offers one of its own instead. In place of a modular-arithmetic table with a random split, two synthetic Gaussian datasets with a class–subclass hierarchy are constructed: the classes are encoded by binary indices of $p=9$ bits (512 classes, a point $x\in\mathbb{R}^{117}$), the distance between classes is the number of differing bits, and the configuration required is cut out as a clique in the subgraph of edges of a given weight ([p10-6](#p10-6), [p10-8](#p10-8)). Dataset 1 is 4 classes of 2 equidistant subclasses each, and dataset 2 is 4 classes of 10 subclasses each, in which one's own are nearer than others' ([p5-4](#p5-4), [p5-5](#p5-5), [fig-1](#fig-1)). The declared aim is to induce grokking "with minimal tuning of model parameters", with no sweep of the hyperparameter space ([p4-6](#p4-6)). What the work does not contain: a reproduction of the canonical setting. The list of contributions has "We replicate previous experiments" ([p2-3](#p2-3)), but neither modular arithmetic nor MNIST with 1,000 examples is run in the paper; the relation to Power et al. remains verbal ([p2-6](#p2-6)). Nor is the chief consequence of leaving the proving ground analysed: in the canonical setup both parts are drawn from one finite table in the same way, that is, there is no distribution shift by construction — and the carry-over of the explanation there is not discussed and is not carried into the limitations.

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the control quantity here is not the fraction of the training set but the fraction $f\in[0,1]$ of examples retained in *selected subclasses*: formula (1) distributes the budget $\gamma_{D}$ between the thinned subclasses and the rest ([p5-2](#p5-2), [p5-3](#p5-3)). There are three observations. Under balanced thinning the text asserts agreement with the literature — the smaller the sample, the stronger the grokking, and at large sizes it disappears ([p6-2](#p6-2)); the caption of panel 3(a) itself, meanwhile, says the opposite — "grokking does not depend on the data size, it arises in every case" — with only the intensity $\Delta_{s}$ growing as the set shrinks ([fig-3](#fig-3)), and that is what the plot shows. Under unbalanced thinning of dataset 1 a complete removal of a subclass ($f=0$) prevents grokking — the models get stuck on a suboptimal plateau — and returning 20 % of the examples switches it on, everything else being equal ([p6-3](#p6-3)); in the same series it is shown that at $f=0.01$ and $f=0.05$ grokking is observed at 4000 and 5000 samples too ([p6-4](#p6-4)), which the authors expressly oppose to the notion that grokking occurs only on small datasets. What the work does not contain: a critical size as a quantity — no threshold is estimated or predicted, no dependence of the time to generalisation on the sample volume is built, and the monotonicity itself is violated by the work's own data (the maximal skew $f=0$ kills grokking, a smaller $f=0.2$ switches it on), which is not articulated in the paper.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the work comes at this from the data side rather than the network side: the structure in question is built into the dataset rather than learnt. The thesis of the introduction is that a weak training signal in data organised into classes and subclasses is able to compensate for sparsity "by leveraging relationships among subclasses" ([p1-4](#p1-4)), and that from this too the successes of geometric and graph methods and of the neurosymbolic approach may be explained. The key observation: in dataset 2 grokking is found even at a complete absence of a subclass's examples ($f=0$) — in contrast to dataset 1 — and this is explained by proximity: the subclasses of one class are nearer to each other than to those of others and therefore carry information about one another ([p6-6](#p6-6), [fig-4](#fig-4)); the figure caption calls it "the proximity in representation space". What the work does not contain: measurements of representations. The network's latent space is never analysed, and the "proximity" pertains to the constructed centroids of the Gaussians ([p10-8](#p10-8)) rather than to learnt features; "information leakage among subclasses" ([p6-5](#p6-5)) is named but not measured. An analysis of the weights and entropic measures of the latent space are relegated to future work ([p7-1](#p7-1)).

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the paper takes the side of "not necessary, only amplifying": the hypothesis is formulated so that the phenomenon is "amplified, not defined" by a high regularisation and by the choice of parameterisation and initialisation ([p4-3](#p4-3)), and in the analysis of the mechanism regularisation is called a means "influencing the occurrence of grokking but not defining it" ([p4-4](#p4-4)). What the work does not contain: a check of this thesis. The weight decay is fixed at $10^{-4}$ in every experiment ([p6-1](#p6-1)), there is no unregularised run and no ablation of the regularisation. Moreover, its own explanation of the delay in section 5.2 is a regularisational one — the influence of a small fraction of examples shows itself only "after the training has converged and the weight decay has brought the network to a sparser internal representation" ([p6-3](#p6-3)) — that is, in the one place where the paper explains the *delay* itself, the work is done by weight decay.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — here it is not a subject of study but a fixed setting: $10^{-4}$ for both architectures, not varied ([p6-1](#p6-1)). Its only substantive mention is the explanation of the delay already named, through a sparsification of the internal representation ([p6-3](#p6-3)); that explanation is given in words, with no measurement of sparsity or of the norm of the weights. What the work does not contain: no analysis of the role of weight decay, no switching it off and no justification whatever for the value chosen.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the work takes the recipe of Liu et al. 2022 entire: an initialisation scale factor of 8, "we multiply all the model's weights by this number" ([p6-1](#p6-1)). It matters for reading the results that this is exactly a "large initialisation" — one of the three factors the paper declares in its introduction to be secondary — and that it is switched on in every experiment and is never varied. What the work does not contain: an ablation of the initialisation scale; the claim of grokking "with minimal hyper-parameter tuning" ([p1-2](#p1-2)) was not checked, since a lever known to switch grokking on has been left in the on position.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives the phase a name within its own definition: $\alpha_{0}$ is called the "end of memorization phase", $\alpha_{1}$ the "start of generalization phase", and the interval between them the transition phase ([p3-4](#p3-4), [fig-2](#fig-2)). A stable plateau with no transition is separately observed: under a complete removal of a subclass the models "get stuck on a suboptimal plateau" and show no late generalisation ([p6-3](#p6-3)) — that is, in this setting a plateau does not always precede a break. What the work does not contain: measurements within the plateau. No quantity changing during the plateau is tracked; the beginning of the training is absent from the plots altogether — the training accuracy there already equals one — and about what happens to the weights on the passage from $\alpha_{0}$ to $\alpha_{1}$ the authors speak as of future work ([p7-1](#p7-1)).

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the work promises MNIST and does not deliver it. The introduction describes the setting in detail: inducing a shift by clustering the digits' representations in the latent space of a trained ResNet classifier, so that the training set contains the same digits as the test set but with a different distribution of representations ([p1-6](#p1-6)); the same experiment stands third in the list of contributions ([p2-3](#p2-3)). These experiments are absent from the text of the paper — every result is taken on the two synthetic Gaussian datasets. MNIST is in fact present in the card only as someone else's material: a retelling of Liu et al.'s experiment with an MLP on MNIST at a training set of 1,000 examples ([p2-7](#p2-7)) and a mention of Kumar et al.'s attempts to govern grokking on MNIST ([p3-1](#p3-1)). This work cannot be cited as a confirmation of a distribution shift on real data.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — a mention only, and with a demarcation at that. The setting of Žunkovič & Ilievski 2022, in which grokking is analysed as a phase transition in rule learning, is set out and immediately opposed: "in our work grokking is formulated in purely statistical terms, with no need for rule learning" ([p3-1](#p3-1)). The work introduces no language of phase transitions of its own: there is no order parameter, no critical point, and the authors' word "phase" pertains to the phases of training (memorisation, transition, generalisation) rather than to a transition in the physical sense. The only use of the term of its own is in the plans: to relate the work to the literature on "how a model's weights change during phase transitions" ([p7-1](#p7-1)).

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — a mention only. Kumar et al. 2024 are retold in the survey: grokking as a transition from lazy to rich learning, with the caveat that those same authors assert that weight decay cannot explain grokking ([p3-1](#p3-1)). The contrast is drawn at the level of description — "Kumar et al. emphasise the training of a particular model, whereas we show that grokking on the whole does not depend on the model" — but not a single quantity from the lazy→rich theory (the output scale, the initial NTK alignment) is measured or contested in the paper.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — a mention only, in the survey of Nanda et al. 2023: the authors "believe that by defining progress measures for each component of a network one will be able to track and understand the training dynamics better" ([p2-8](#p2-8)). The work introduces no progress measures of its own: the single quantity proposed, $\Delta_{S}$, is measured from the test curve after the fact and has no predictive power by construction.

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — a mention only, in two places and in a descriptive sense. In the survey of Nanda et al., "emergent behaviour" is ascribed to what the discrete Fourier transforms do in a single-layer transformer ([p2-8](#p2-8)); in the conclusion, the authors' own observation is called "observations of emergent behaviour when training on very limited examples of a pattern" ([p6-7](#p6-7)). Schaeffer et al. 2023 are cited, but only in the list of future connections ([p7-1](#p7-1)); there is no analysis of emergent abilities and no dispute about their dependence on the choice of metric in the paper.

**[Distribution shift](../../concepts/distribution-shift.md)** — the claim is causal: data sparsity causes grokking by producing a distribution shift over the course of the training ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)). It is checked by inducing a shift where it is known by construction — by clustering the MNIST digits in a learnt feature space ([`p1-6`](#p1-6)).
## Points we could not reconcile

**The MNIST experiments are promised in the introduction and in the list of contributions, but they are not in the paper.** The [introduction](#p1-6) describes the setting in detail — a clustering of the digits by their representations in the latent space of a trained ResNet, a training set with the same digits but a different distribution — and concludes that "these experiments confirm the role of distribution shifts in grokking and extend our findings beyond synthetic data". The same experiment stands third in the [list of contributions](#p2-3). There is not one such result in section 5, and they are not mentioned in the conclusion. A reader citing the work would do well to remember that its whole experimental material is synthetic.

**The transformer results are promised in Appendix A, and Appendix A is entirely about the construction of the datasets.** [Section 5.1](#p5-10) says that the MLP results are given chiefly because both architectures produced "quite similar patterns" and that the results of a two-layer transformer "can be found in Appendix A". [Appendix A](#p10-1) describes the procedure for constructing the datasets and nothing more; there is not a single plot and not a single number for the transformer in the paper. The contribution "late generalization is largely architecture-independent" ([the list of contributions](#p2-3)) is thereby supported by nothing that can be read.

**The abstract credits the second dataset with a study of transfer learning.** The [abstract](#p1-2) says that one dataset studies the influence of a limited sample "while the other investigates the role of transfer learning in grokking". Transfer learning is nowhere else mentioned in the paper: [dataset 2](#p5-5) is described as a dataset with equivariant subclasses, and the experiments on it ([p6-6](#p6-6)) are about a transfer of information between subclasses within one task rather than between tasks.

**The work's own explanation of the delay contradicts its own hypothesis.** The [hypothesis](#p4-3) declares regularisation to be amplifying but not defining; in the one place where the paper explains why the effect of a small fraction of examples shows itself *late*, the explanation is regularisational: their influence "showed itself only after the training had converged and the weight decay had brought the network to a sparser internal representation" ([p6-3](#p6-3)). The distribution shift explains where the gap between the curves comes from; the delay in that paragraph is produced by weight decay.

**The text of section 5.2 and the caption of the figure it refers to assert opposite things.** The [paragraph on sparsity](#p6-2) says: "we find that grokking weakens as the number of samples grows and exists only at markedly small sample sizes, as depicted in figure 3(a). This agrees with the literature". The caption of panel 3(a) itself ([fig-3](#fig-3)) states the opposite: "Grokking does not depend on the data size. It arises in every case" — and only then adds that what grows as the set shrinks is the *intensity* $\Delta_{s}$. In the panel all seven sample sizes (from 100 to 800) do indeed give the same late rise of the test curve, differing only in the depth of the dip. The statements "exists only at small sizes" and "arises in every case" are incompatible, and they stand in two adjacent elements of one page.

**The references to figures in section 5.3 have diverged from the figures.** [Paragraph 5.3](#p6-5) says: "First, we analyse the balanced sampling of classes, as shown in figure 4" — whereas the whole of [figure 4](#fig-4) is about unbalanced sampling, and the balanced case does not appear in it. In the same place, "the influence of unbalanced sampling, depicted in figure 4(b)" — even though both panels are unbalanced. In the [next paragraph](#p6-6) the removal of samples "entirely from certain subclasses" is assigned to figure 4(b), whereas $f=0$ is panel 4(a) and 4(b) is labelled $f=0.2$. In the same place the reference "as described in section 4(a)" points to no section: in the source it is a reference to a subfigure label.

**In Appendix A the example of the distance between classes is computed for classes other than those named.** The [paragraph](#p10-5) declares: "the distance between $C_{1}$ and $C_{4}$ is computed thus" — after which it takes $\mathbf{B_{4}}(1)$ and $\mathbf{B_{4}}(5)$ and obtains $\mathbf{D}(C_{1},C_{5})=1$. The computation is correct for the pair $C_{1},C_{5}$; the pair named is $C_{1},C_{4}$.

**The number of distributions at $p=4$ diverges from the work's own construction.** The [paragraph](#p10-3) asserts: "at $p=4$ we can create 15 distributions". The set is defined as $\{\mathcal{D}_{0},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{k}\}$ with $k=2^{p}-1$, that is, it contains $2^{p}=16$ elements, and that is how the number is computed in the [working configuration](#p10-6): at $p=9$ one obtains 512 classes rather than 511.

**A replication of earlier experiments is declared that does not exist in the paper.** The second item of the [list of contributions](#p2-3) begins with the words "We replicate previous experiments and conduct new analyses". Not a single third-party setting — neither the modular arithmetic of Power et al. nor the MNIST with 1,000 examples of Liu et al. — is run in the paper; every experiment is taken on the two synthetic datasets introduced here.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

What is experimentally shown and reproducible in the paper's own terms is exactly this: on two synthetic Gaussian datasets with a class–subclass hierarchy a skew of the subclass sampling switches late generalisation on and off — at $f=0$ in dataset 1 the models stay on a plateau, and on returning 20 % of the examples the curve goes up late ([p6-3](#p6-3), [fig-3](#fig-3)); late generalisation persists at 4000 and 5000 samples ([p6-4](#p6-4)); and in dataset 2 it is observed even at $f=0$ ([p6-6](#p6-6), [fig-4](#fig-4)). Each of these observations is averaged over 10 runs, and the spread over runs is drawn as a band around the mean curve.

It is worth looking at the vertical axis, though. In the [equidistant dataset](#fig-3) the test accuracy in panel (a) starts at 0.85–0.93 and arrives at 1.0, and in the [equivariant one](#fig-4) it starts at 0.945–0.99; the "region of low performance" $\sigma_{S}$ of definition 3.1, in which $\sigma_{S}\ll\sigma_{E}$ is required, stands within a few per cent of the high region in these experiments, and the curve itself runs not plateau-then-jump but dip-then-rise. The exception is the upper row of panel 3(b), in which at $f=0$ the test accuracy holds at about 0.60 at a training accuracy of 1.0: this is the only case shown with a large $\Delta_{S}$, and it is precisely the case in which there is no grokking. The label of the ordinate on all four results plots is `train_accuracy`, although both curves are drawn and the subject of the analysis is the test one.

Not shown but interpreted: that what governs this is precisely a *distribution shift*. The shift in this setting is introduced by construction — the training set is skewed and the test set left as it was — and is therefore a property of the setup rather than a result of measurement. Not one measure of shift is computed, although the means for it are cited ([p3-3](#p3-3)). Moreover, the magnitude of the shift and the presence of grokking are related non-monotonically: the maximal skew in dataset 1 kills grokking and a smaller one switches it on, while the divergence between datasets 1 and 2 the authors explain not by the shift but by the proximity of the subclasses ([p6-6](#p6-6)), that is, by the relational structure of the data. Separately unjustified is the identification of the sampling noise of a small sample with a distribution shift, on which the original thesis "data sparsity induces grokking by causing a distribution shift" rests ([p1-4](#p1-4)).

Conjectured and relegated to future work: the mechanism of the delay. The authors admit outright in the [limitations](#p6-9) that "we were unable to determine how long the training process might need to be continued before late generalisation sets in", and an analysis of the changes in the weight distribution on the passage from $\alpha_{0}$ to $\alpha_{1}$ and entropic measures of the latent space are named as plans ([p7-1](#p7-1)).

Separately on the operationality of Definition 3.1 ([p4-1](#p4-1)): the thresholds for "$\ll$" and "$\gg$" are not set, the inflection points $\alpha_{0}$ and $\alpha_{1}$ are introduced descriptively, the training curve $T_{R}$ entering the definition through $\Delta_{T}$ is given in no figure, and $\Delta_{S}$, declared a measure of intensity, is never measured as a number. The strict-inequality sign in the text of the definition is typeset as "¿" — the typo is present in both the PDF and the HTML version, and it is preserved in the card as it stands. The definition is therefore serviceable as a language of description but not as a criterion applicable to others' curves.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

Editorial notes on the places where this work conveys others' results erroneously or without the authors' caveats. The "details" links from the "Erroneous retellings and citations of this paper" sections of other cards lead here.

###### mc-2201-02177

**Power et al. (2201.02177) — credited with grokking in models having no internal latent space.** In the analysis of the mechanism it is said: [`"Research by (Power et al. 2022) demonstrated grokking in models that do not rely on an internal latent space to represent data"`](original/2502.01774.grokking-explained-a-statistical-phenomenon.md#p4-4). In Power et al. a decoder-only transformer with learnt symbol embeddings is trained, and a whole section is given over to an analysis of the structure of those embeddings: [`"we visualized the matrix of the output layer for the case of modular addition and $S_{5}$"`](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-3-4). The class is **erroneous**: the formulation contradicts the work cited. Curiously, two pages earlier this same paper cites Power et al. accurately and on the same score — "as the authors show, comparing the model's latent space before and after the grokking occurs" ([p2-6](#p2-6)); the warning concerns the formulation rather than the work. It appears that in the paragraph [p4-4](#p4-4) the subject shifted: a correct observation that grokking is observed in models whose performance depends weakly on the quality of the representation (in Liu et al. and in OmniGrok this is MNIST against the algorithmic tasks) has been carried over to Power et al., whose experiments are precisely algorithmic.

###### mc-2205-10343

**Liu et al. (2205.10343) — declared not to study the characteristics of the training data.** The survey says: [`"(Liu et al. 2022) do not study what characteristics of the training data might contribute to or avoid grokking"`](original/2502.01774.grokking-explained-a-statistical-phenomenon.md#p2-7). The central quantitative result of Liu et al. consists in exactly that: the training-set fraction determines whether the training set determines the structure of the representation uniquely, and from this a critical fraction is derived — [`"our effective theory predicts that the probability that the training set implies a unique linear structure (which would result in perfect generalization) depends on the training [data fraction]"`](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/original/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.md#p6-1). The class is **erroneous**. In other respects Liu et al. are cited accurately: both the experiment with an MLP on MNIST at a training set of 1,000 examples and the recipe of multiplying the initial weights by a constant are taken from their [Appendix J](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/original/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.md#sec-j), in which both are described.

###### mc-2310-16441

**Levi et al. (2310.16441) — credited with support for the distribution-shift hypothesis.** The survey says: [`"The paper supports our hypothesis that grokking is caused by distribution shifts in the specific case of liner models."`](original/2502.01774.grokking-explained-a-statistical-phenomenon.md#p3-1). Levi et al. obtain grokking in a teacher–student setting with Gaussian inputs, in which the training and validation data are drawn from one distribution and the delay is derived from the dynamics' dependence on the input and output dimensionalities, the training set size, the regularisation and the initialisation: [`"We present exact predictions on how the grokking time depends on input and output dimensionality, train sample size, regularization, and network initialization."`](../2310.16441.grokking-in-linear-estimators-a-solvable-model-that-groks-without-understanding/original/2310.16441.grokking-in-linear-estimators-a-solvable-model-that-groks-without-understanding.md#p1-2). There is no distribution shift between training and validation in their model by construction. The class is **erroneous**. The weaker formulation in the same paragraph — that Levi et al. gave a consideration that served as a motivation, "grokking may arise from the distribution of the data rather than from the complexity of the model" ([p3-1](#p3-1)) — does not contradict the work cited: what is at issue there is the finite-sample properties of one distribution rather than a divergence between two.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. Each entry says what exactly is distorted and leads to the authors' paragraphs in this paper. The work appeared in February 2025 and is rarely cited in the corpus, so there is so far one pattern.

**"Grokking is explained as a phase transition measurable through complexity or information-theoretic quantities" (erroneous).** Citing works place this paper in a row with explanations that measure grokking by complexity or information-theoretic quantities and read it as a phase transition. The formulation observed is in the related-work survey of [Topological Signatures of Grokking](../2605.06352.topological-signatures-of-grokking/2605.06352.topological-signatures-of-grokking.card.md): `"Existing explanations largely frame grokking as a phase transition measurable through complexity or information-theoretic quantities"`, with the reference to this paper standing inside the list. The authors have neither the one nor the other. The work deliberately does not take up the language of phase transitions: the setting of Žunkovič & Ilievski, in which grokking is analysed precisely as a phase transition, it sets out and immediately demarcates itself from — ["in our work grokking is formulated in purely statistical terms, with no need for rule learning"](#p3-1). Not a single complexity or information-theoretic quantity is introduced or measured in the paper: the only quantity proposed, $\Delta_{S}$, is taken from the test curve ([fig-2](#fig-2)), and entropic measures of the latent space are expressly relegated to [future work](#p7-1). The authors' own formulation is that grokking is a [statistical phenomenon arising from a distribution shift between the test and the training data](#p4-3).

Three further caveats are lost when this work is cited, and they are worth keeping beside any retelling of it: it contains no experimental material on real data, although [MNIST experiments are promised](#p1-6); it contains no transformer results, although [they are promised in Appendix A](#p5-10); and the distribution shift itself is never measured in it — [the means for it are cited](#p3-3) but not applied.
