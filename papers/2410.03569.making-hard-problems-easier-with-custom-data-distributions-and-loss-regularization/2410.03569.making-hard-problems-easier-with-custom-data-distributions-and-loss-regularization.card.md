# Saxena, Alfarano, Charton, Allen-Zhu, Wenger, Lauter 2024 — Making Hard Problems Easier with Custom Data Distributions and Loss Regularization: A Case Study in Modular Arithmetic

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Eshika Saxena**, **Alberto Alfarano**, **François Charton**, **Zeyuan Allen-Zhu** (FAIR at Meta), **Emily Wenger**, **Kristin Lauter** (Duke University and FAIR at Meta).

arXiv [2410.03569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03569), October 2024; version v2 (June 2025) is analysed. There is no citation count for it in the corpus table. This is a reworked and renamed form of the paper "Teaching Transformers Modular Arithmetic at Scale". The work is applied: modular arithmetic is taken as the bottleneck of ML attacks on LWE, and two techniques — a custom training-data distribution and an addition to the loss — raise the limit from $N\leq 6$, $q\leq 1000$ to $N=128$, $q\leq 974269$. The source is the native arXiv HTML of version v2.

## Concepts covered

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the subject of the work, and it pushes the frontier of the possible at a stroke: the models add up to $N=128$ summands modulo $q\leq 974269$ ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)), whereas the best earlier results were $N\leq 6$ at $q\leq 1000$ ([`p2-6`](#p2-6), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). This is not an improvement of a few per cent but a different order of problem.

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the main innovation: a custom training-data distribution. The number of non-zero components of an example is drawn from a density $f$, and $f_{\mathrm{inv\_sqrt}}(z)\propto 1/\sqrt{N-z+1}$ is skewed towards hard examples with few zeros while still admitting simple sparse ones, whereas the ordinary $f_{\mathrm{default}}$ almost never generates examples with many zeros and the model has to learn from hard ones alone ([`p3-6`](#p3-6), [`p4-1`](#p4-1), [`p4-2`](#p4-2), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). The difference is striking: under the ordinary $f_{\mathrm{default}}$ the very same architecture gives 1.2 % accuracy, under $f_{\mathrm{inv\_sqrt}}$ 99.8 % ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`tab-3`](#tab-3)). The technique is explicitly set against curriculum learning: the distribution does not change over time and therefore requires neither a difficulty labelling nor a choice of switching moment, whereas a curriculum at large $N$ turns out to be noisy (85.1 % ± 10.3 % against 95.8 % ± 0.5 % at $N=128$) ([`p7-3`](#p7-3), [`p7-4`](#p7-4), [`tab-4`](#tab-4)). It is separately measured how many **distinct** examples are needed: at a fixed budget of 100 million presentations, 1 million distinct ones suffice, that is, about a hundred repetitions of each ([`p8-2`](#p8-2), [`tab-6`](#tab-6)).

**[The Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — the notion is carried over from the parameters to the data distribution: too small a Kullback–Leibler divergence between the training and the test sets and too large a one (too many simple examples) are equally bad, and a "golden mean" is needed ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work is especially curious for the corpus in that it **does not observe** grokking and explains why: it takes a negligible fraction of the example space ($2.76\cdot 10^{-31}$ at $N=16$, $q=257$), and so the models learn gradually with an ordinary loss behaviour ([`p5-7`](#p5-7)). Modular arithmetic thereby ceases to be a task in which grokking is inevitable.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — instead of an exact match, an accuracy with a tolerance $\tau$ is taken: the fraction of predictions within $0.005q$ or $0.01q$ of the correct answer ([`p5-5`](#p5-5)). This is a measure not of "learned or not" but of "how close", and it makes a partial mastery of the operation visible.

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — the second innovation: an addition to the loss penalising a prediction of the origin. Without it the model degenerates on a hard task: the predictions of the whole batch contract towards zero, since under a uniform $b$ the smallest mean MSE loss is attained exactly at the origin ([`p10-4`](#p10-4), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)).

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the angular embedding of Stevens et al.: an integer $t$ is encoded by the point $(\cos\phi,\sin\phi)$ at $\phi=2\pi t/q$ on the unit circle, so that the adjacency of residues is preserved ([`p4-3`](#p4-3)). This is the same consideration about the circular geometry of the residue field that stands behind the Fourier circuits, but applied to the embedding rather than to an analysis of the weights.

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — the technique is checked on it too: under $f_{\mathrm{default}}$ the model predicts at chance (50.3 % at length 32), under $f_{\mathrm{inv\_sqrt}}$ it gives 100 % at length 32 and 99.4 % at length 256 ([`p12-2`](#p12-2), [`tab-12`](#tab-12)).

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the same techniques are checked on copying, associative recall and selective copying, that is, on tasks usually associated with induction heads ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)); associative recall under $f_{\mathrm{default}}$ falls to 1.8 % at length 64, and with their sampling holds 100 %.

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — the setting: an encoder-only transformer, a hidden dimension of 256, 4 attention heads, 4 layers, a batch of 250, Adam at a rate of $3\cdot 10^{-5}$, a warm-up of 1000 steps, a cosine schedule, a single V100 accelerator, about 3 hours per run ([`p5-3`](#p5-3)).

It is worth saying that the work is **applied** by design: it is about breaking LWE, and modular arithmetic is merely the bottleneck of that attack ([`p1-5`](#p1-5)). Grokking is mentioned in the survey as a common frame for studying modular arithmetic ([`p2-7`](#p2-7)) but is not the subject.

Cards of corpus papers: [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) and [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — works on modular addition whose limits ($N=2$, $q\leq 401$) are pushed here; [Doshi et al. 2024](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md) — modular polynomials, the best earlier result in the number of summands ($N=6$ at $q\leq 23$) and the conjecture about the class of learnable operations, refuted here; [Mohamadi et al. 2024](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.card.md) — the theoretical analysis of the grokking of modular addition and the requirement of a constant fraction of all possible examples, contested here outright; [Miller et al. 2024](../2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity/2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity.card.md) — the control of grokking through the amount of data and the initialisation.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work comes from FAIR Meta and Duke University; it is a reworked and renamed form of the paper "Teaching Transformers Modular Arithmetic at Scale" (October 2024).

**The gain is not contestable but striking.** $N\leq 6$, $q\leq 1000$ in the predecessors against $N=128$, $q=974269$ here ([`tab-1`](#tab-1), [`tab-2`](#tab-2)). And the architecture of the model did not change — only the data and the loss did.

**The ablation is set up cleanly.** The very same architecture under $f_{\mathrm{default}}$ gives 1.2–1.3 % accuracy at every $N$, under $f_{\mathrm{inv\_sqrt}}$ from 96.1 to 99.8 % ([`tab-3`](#tab-3)). The difference is ascribed entirely to the data distribution, and this is supported by a direct measurement.

**The explanation through the Kullback–Leibler divergence is checkable and elegant.** It predicts that both a distribution too similar to the test one and one too dissimilar are bad — and the results fall out exactly so: $f_{\mathrm{default}}$ gives a divergence of 0 and a zero accuracy, $f_{\mathrm{uni}}$ the largest divergence and a lower accuracy than the $f_{\mathrm{inv\_sqrt}}$ in between ([`p7-2`](#p7-2), [`tab-3`](#tab-3)).

**The technique does not depend on the chosen sparsity element.** Replacing zero with an arbitrary but constant integer $K$ gives the same 97.9–100 % ([`p7-5`](#p7-5), [`tab-5`](#tab-5)). This cuts off the explanation "the model has simply learned zero" and confirms that what matters is the shift of the distribution.

**The comparison with curriculum learning is honest and in favour of simplicity.** At $N=128$, $q=257$ the curriculum gives 85.1 % ± 10.3 % and their technique 95.8 % ± 0.5 % over 8 runs ([`tab-4`](#tab-4)), and other curriculum designs, switching thresholds, learning rates, weight decays, difficulty labellings and data mixtures were swept as well ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)).

**The design is confirmed by direct observation rather than only by the final accuracy.** A model trained at $N=64$, $q=257$ first adds examples with a single non-zero summand correctly and only later those with many; the more non-zeros, the later the accuracy arrives ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)). This is exactly what the sparse examples are mixed in for, and it is the most convincing part of the work.

**The analysis of the degeneration under the MSE loss is carried through to a derivation.** It is shown that under a uniform $b$ the mean loss is smallest exactly at the origin, which is why a model without the addition slides there ([`p10-4`](#p10-4)); this is confirmed by pictures of the outputs too ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)).

**The main result, however, is applied, and it gives little understanding.** The work shows **what** helps but not **why** modular arithmetic is hard: there is no analysis of the learned representation, no connection with the Fourier circuits, and no explanation of what exactly the model takes from the sparse examples.

**The observation about the disappearance of grokking passes in a flash.** One sentence to the effect that there is no grokking because the fraction of the data seen is negligible ([`p5-7`](#p5-7)) — but that is precisely what interests the corpus, and it is not checked: there is neither a run with a larger data fraction nor any training curves.

**The conjecture of Doshi et al. is refuted by a direct construction.** They held that two-layer fully connected networks can learn only operations of the form $h(g_{1}(a_{1}),\ldots,g_{N}(a_{N}))$; here a class of asymmetric operations outside that set is introduced, $h_{j,k}=(\sum_{i}a_{i}^{j})^{2}+a_{1}^{k}$, and over 95 % is obtained on them ([`p11-2`](#p11-2), [`p11-3`](#p11-3), [`tab-9`](#tab-9)).

**Some of the results on the side tasks are weak.** Modular multiplication at $q=3329$ gives 3 % at every $N$, and the dot product at $N=8$ and $q=257$ gives 2 % ([`p11-4`](#p11-4), [`tab-10`](#tab-10), [`tab-11`](#tab-11)); in the text this is called "a drop in quality at larger $q$", although in substance the technique does not work there at all, sinking to the level of the ablation.

**The claim of "twice as hard secret vectors" rests on 20 runs with a success rate of 10–20 %.** The recovery succeeds in 15 % of cases at a Hamming weight of 6 against 0 % under a pure MSE loss ([`tab-8`](#tab-8)); and the error vector $\boldsymbol{e}$ is discarded altogether ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)), that is, what is solved is not LWE but "learning without errors".

**The text is unproofread in places.** Appendix 8 says "in table 13 and table 15… and in table 15" ([`p16-1`](#p16-1)), and a paragraph below "in tables 18, 18 and 18" ([`p16-2`](#p16-2)); in the section on polynomials both tasks refer to one and the same table.

**Its place in the corpus.** The work is applied and, for the corpus, a side branch: grokking here is not the subject but a circumstance. Three things are valuable: it is shown that the limit "$N\leq 6$, $q\leq 1000$" was a limit of the training data rather than of the architectures; a checkable explanation is given through the Kullback–Leibler divergence with a "golden mean"; and a demonstration is provided that on a hard task the MSE loss over an angular embedding degenerates towards the origin unless a penalty is added. Separately valuable is the observation that at a negligible fraction of the example space no grokking arises at all. The price: the work gives no understanding of why the task is hard, some of the side results are weak, and the LWE claim is obtained with the noise discarded.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2407-12332
**Mohamadi et al. (2407.12332) — a lower bound carried over to models in general.** `"Mohamadi et al. (2024) … found that models need to be trained on a constant fraction of all possible modular arithmetic behaviors for a given N and q to generalize"`: in Mohamadi this is [a lower bound for permutation-equivariant kernel methods only](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/original/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.md#p3-4); the work's main result is opposite in spirit — [in the rich feature-learning regime a fraction of order 1/p suffices](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/original/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.md#p3-5). The paraphrase turns half a theorem into a conclusion about all models. Alongside it, "observations about data diversity" — a thesis Mohamadi does not have.
