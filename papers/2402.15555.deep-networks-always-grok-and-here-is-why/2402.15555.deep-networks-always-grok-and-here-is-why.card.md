## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work widens the subject of the notion rather than explaining it within its former bounds. Its contribution is **delayed robustness**: the observation that a network "groks" adversarial examples long after it generalises on the validation set ([observation](#p2-3), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). The device by which this is obtained is worth keeping in mind when citing: the validation set is replaced by adversarial examples generated anew after every training step by an $\ell_{\infty}$-PGD attack on the validation points ([p2-2](#p2-2)) — that is, what is measured is not a delay of generalisation but a delay of robustness. It matters that the phenomenon is obtained at an *ordinary* initialisation, with and without weight decay ([p2-3](#p2-3)), across six families of settings ([p2-4](#p2-4)). What the work does not contain: a definition of grokking that would distinguish it from any late rise of any performance measure — the notion is widened to "a late rise of anything", and part of the ubiquity claimed is obtained by that widening rather than by new experiments; there is no analysis of what an adversarial example in the token-embedding space of a language model means ([fig. 9](#fig-9)), and no statement of the number of seeds; and there is no setting in which the phenomenon was predicted in advance — all the observations are post hoc.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work offers the corpus a measure of a new kind: **local complexity** (LC) — the density of the nonlinearities of the spline partition in the neighbourhood of a point, approximated by the number of neuron hyperplanes intersecting a cross-polytope of radius $r$ around it ([p5-3](#p5-3)). Its difference from all the corpus's earlier measures is stated outright: `"Our novel measure does not rely on the dataset, labels, or loss function that is used during training"` ([p2-6](#p2-6)) — the measure can be computed around random points drawn from the data domain too, which the work makes use of. The measure is declared a progress measure after Barak et al. 2022, with the authors themselves quoting the definition from there — "scalar quantities *causally* related to the state of the training" ([p3-5](#p3-5)). What the work does not contain: causality. A coincidence in time is shown — the second descent of LC precedes the delayed generalisation or robustness ([p7-5](#p7-5)); there is no intervention changing LC independently and checking the consequences for grokking, and the turn "These results undoubtedly show" refers to the relation of LC to the geometry of the partition rather than to a causal relation with grokking. Nor is there an estimate of the approximation error: the replacement of $\Phi$ by $\widehat{\Phi}$ is bounded by nothing and is only indirectly assessed by the distortion of the neighbourhood ([p5-4](#p5-4), [fig. 5](#fig-5)).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work carries the language of transition over from the performance curves to the geometry: it declares `"the first evidence that, for classification problems, the linear regions undergo a phase transition during training"` ([abstract](#p1-2)). Substantively, the transition is a change in the sign of the motion of the nonlinearities: before it they accumulate at the training points, after it they depart from them towards the decision boundary ([p7-4](#p7-4)). The word is used descriptively: there is no order parameter, no finite-size scaling, the transition point is nowhere defined numerically, and the authors themselves speak of a "phase change" in the LC dynamics on a par with a "phase transition". The cause of the transition is declared outright to be not understood ([p7-5](#p7-5)).

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — the term is applied to a new carrier: double descent here is the shape of the LC curve over training time (a descent, a rise, a second descent, [p7-1](#p7-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)) rather than a non-monotonicity of the error in capacity or in epochs. The shape is confirmed by a check against an exact count of the regions via SplineCam ([fig. 35](#fig-35)). What the work does not contain: a relation to the double descent of the error — neither epoch-wise nor model-wise double descent is measured or compared with LC; a theoretical justification of the shape of the curve itself the authors call absent ([p9-6](#p9-6)).

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the work gave the corpus the widest set of non-algorithmic settings of its time: an MLP on MNIST, a CNN on CIFAR10 and CIFAR100, a ResNet18 on CIFAR10 and Imagenette, and a GPT (12 heads, 12 layers) on the text of Shakespeare ([p2-4](#p2-4), [fig. 6](#fig-6)), with the common measurement settings in the [experimental setup](#p6-1). An essential caveat lost when citing: the only genuinely large setting — a ResNet18 with batch normalisation on full ImageNet — stands in the appendix, and in it the local complexity grows without bound and there is no region migration at all ([p9-3](#p9-3), [fig. 22](#fig-22)). What the work does not contain: not a single run on full ImageNet without batch normalisation, that is, in the setting in which all the other results are obtained.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the work takes its start from the technique of Liu et al. 2022: its chief claim is that delayed robustness is observed *without* an increase of the initial weight norm, at an ordinary initialisation ([p2-3](#p2-3), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). The technique itself is meanwhile used as a separate setting — the parameters are multiplied by eight ([fig. 7](#fig-7)) — and a separate observation is made in it: at a scaled initialisation the first descent of LC disappears ([p7-2](#p7-2)), and the robustness grows not monotonically but in bursts coinciding with a sharpening of the decision boundary. What the work does not contain: a sweep of the scaling multiplier and any dependence on it.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives the plateau a geometric signature: the rise phase of LC proceeds until interpolation of the training set is reached, and within it the nonlinearities gather around the training points more densely than around the validation ones ([p7-3](#p7-3)). Hence its explanation of the delay: the more memorisation is required, the longer the rise phase and the later the region migration ([fig. 13](#fig-13), [p9-5](#p9-5)). What the work does not contain: a measurement of memorisation as such — the "need for memorisation" is set only by the volume of randomly labelled data, there is no separate measure of what has been memorised, and there is no separation into memorising and generalising components.

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — random labels are used as a knob separating two directions in the volume of data: a growth in the volume of honest data accelerates the grokking, a growth in the volume of randomly labelled data delays it ([table 1](#tab-1), [fig. 13](#fig-13)). This is one of the work's two interventions acting in opposite directions under an outwardly identical change, and therefore its most substantive line about data. What the work does not contain: mixed noise fractions — the labels are either all genuine or all random; there is no dependence on the fraction of corrupted labels.

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the size of the training set is swept from 1K to 60K MNIST samples, and a growth in volume accelerates both the grokking and the region migration ([p9-5](#p9-5), [table 1](#tab-1)). What the work does not contain: a critical dataset size — no threshold, no estimate of one and no fraction of training data as a control quantity in the sense of Power et al.; the sweep runs over absolute numbers of samples and serves only to separate generalisation from memorisation.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work relieves weight decay of the role of a necessary condition: in every experiment it equals 0, except for MNIST-MLP, where it equals $0.01$ ([experimental setup](#p6-1)), and delayed robustness is observed without it too ([p2-3](#p2-3)). A separate sweep of weight decay is made, but its outcome is described in the paper in three non-matching ways (see [p9-2](#p9-2) and the section on inconsistencies). A mechanism is named: weight decay reduces the norm of the weights and hence the norm of the slope per region of the partition ([p9-2](#p9-2)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of how that reduction relates to the region migration — the relation is only named.

**[Varieties of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — the work's strongest negative result: batch normalisation *removes* the grokking of adversarial examples rather than shifting it in time — it also removes the first descent and raises the level of LC overall ([p9-3](#p9-3), [fig. 12](#fig-12)). This is a case, rare in the corpus, of an intervention that extinguishes the phenomenon. What the work does not contain: the justification promised. Appendix B ([sec-b](#sec-b)) carries the computation as far as formula (11) and breaks off; the substantive claim — that normalisation moves the partition boundaries right up against the training data — is borrowed entire from Balestriero & Baraniuk 2022 and is not derived here.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work offers a bridge from spline geometry to the language of circuits: to every region of the partition corresponds its own subgraph of active neurons, neighbouring regions differ by one node, and so LC can be read as the density of distinct circuits in a locality of the input space ([p8-1](#p8-1)). Whence the conclusion that by the end of the training the number of distinct circuits falls sharply, especially for the deeper layers — and a correlation with the cleanup phase of Nanda et al. 2023. What the work does not contain: circuits proper — not one circuit is isolated, ablated or matched with an algorithm; the correlation is qualitative, and the number of circuits is nowhere counted (the authors themselves note that in fact it would be exponential).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the work merely shows that its measure behaves the same on the canonical proving ground too: in a transformer with GeLU activation on modular addition the region migration occurs in the course of the grokking ([fig. 8](#fig-8)). What the work does not contain: no modulus, no fraction of training data, no number of layers and no seeds — the whole claim rests on a single figure with no numerical detail and no sweep; in the text it occupies half a sentence ([p2-4](#p2-4)).

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — Adam is set as part of the setting in every experiment ([experimental setup](#p6-1)), and the authors explicitly count this among the limitations: the evidence of region migration is obtained under Adam alone, while the dynamics of stochastic gradient descent and of sharpness-aware minimisation is named a subject for future work ([p9-6](#p9-6)). What the work does not contain: a comparison of optimisers and any check whatever that the phenomenon is not a property of Adam.

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — the work gives an observation and a reading of it: increasing the batch size from 64 to 512 accelerates the adversarial grokking ([table 1](#tab-1)), and this is read as `"reduced SGD noise allows region migration to occur earlier in training"` ([fig. 17](#fig-17)). What the work does not contain: a measurement of the noise itself — the reading is given in a figure caption and is checked by nothing; there is no estimate of the gradient variance and no full-batch experiments.

**[Neural collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — a mention, and no more: the conclusions say that relations between region migration and neural collapse (Papyan et al. 2020) are possible and are not investigated in the paper ([p9-6](#p9-6)). The work computes no measures of neural collapse.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The analysis of the work's claims (the file `.claims.txt` alongside) adds up to a distinct picture: a strong empirical half and a weak explanatory one.

**Shown, and holds.** Delayed robustness as a phenomenon: a late rise of adversarial accuracy at an ordinary initialisation, with and without weight decay, across six families of settings ([p2-3](#p2-3), [p2-4](#p2-4)). A check for gradient masking is made: the robustness rises to Auto-Attack too, not only to the PGD by which the measurement was conducted ([fig. 18](#fig-18)). The measure itself is checked three times — on a linear MLP with zero bias, where all the layerwise intersections are recovered even at $P=2$; on a random MLP, where the fall of LC with distance from the origin agrees with the prediction of Hanin & Rolnick 2019; and by a check against an exact count of the regions via SplineCam, which gives the same double-descent shape ([p12-1](#p12-1), [fig. 35](#fig-35)). Region migration is shown not only indirectly: at equal volume the density of regions near the decision boundary against the density far from it is 123156 and 88362 against 33273 and 32018 ([fig. 26](#fig-26)). The negative result with batch normalisation ([p9-3](#p9-3), [fig. 12](#fig-12)) is an intervention that extinguishes the phenomenon rather than shifting it.

**A coincidence in time shown, and presented as an explanation.** The central claim — that grokking occurs *because* a robust partition arises ([claim](#p3-1)) — rests on the second descent of LC preceding the rise of performance ([p7-5](#p7-5)). There is no intervention changing LC independently in the work, so a "progress measure" in the sense of Barak et al., where such quantities are defined as **causally** related to the state of the training ([p3-5](#p3-5)), is not justified here. The neighbouring turn "These results undoubtedly show" ([p7-5](#p7-5)) refers to the relation of LC to the geometry of the partition — a reader citing it as a proof of the cause of grokking would do well to reread the sentence entire: in the same paragraph the authors write that they do not understand why the network passes from accumulating nonlinearities to repelling them.

**Declared an argument.** The correspondence "a region of the partition — a circuit" and the conclusion about a sharp fall in the number of distinct circuits ([p8-1](#p8-1)); the reading of the acceleration of grokking by a growth of the batch size through a reduction of SGD noise ([fig. 17](#fig-17)); the remark about vulnerability to membership inference, inferred from the gap between the training and validation LC ([p17-6](#p17-6)); and the guess about an implicit regularisation by overparameterisation, inferred from the fall of the LC peak at a width of 2000 ([p17-9](#p17-9), [fig. 14](#fig-14)). None of these four claims is checked by experiment.

**Acknowledged by the authors.** There is no theoretical justification of the double descent; it is unclear why the migration happens so late; the experiments use Adam alone; and the relation to neural collapse is not analysed ([p9-6](#p9-6)). An exact count of the regions in an $N$-dimensional neighbourhood is combinatorially intractable, and this is named outright as the reason for the approximation ([p12-1](#p12-1)); the error of the approximation is nowhere bounded and is only indirectly assessed by the distortion of the neighbourhood ([p5-4](#p5-4)).

**Not acknowledged.** The claim of universality standing in the title and in the assertion that "grokking always occurs during region migration" ([p3-3](#p3-3)) is checked only on the settings shown; there is no search for counterexamples, and the single setting found without region migration is not built into the rule — see the next section. The $99$% confidence interval in every figure is taken over the sample of points rather than over runs ([experimental setup](#p6-1)); the number of seeds is nowhere stated.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The title against figure 22.** The title asserts "Deep Networks **Always** Grok", and among the contributions it is recorded that `"grokking always occurs during region migration"` ([p3-3](#p3-3)). Yet section 4 says: in training a ResNet18 with batch normalisation on full ImageNet the local complexity grows without bound, `"removing any signs of region migration"` ([p9-3](#p9-3)), and figure 22 confirms it — LC on the validation points rises monotonically to the end of the run, and Top-1 and Top-5 grow smoothly, with no late jump ([fig. 22](#fig-22)). The work's only genuinely large setting is the one in which the universal law declared does not hold. Formally there is no contradiction (no migration, no grokking), but the title and the contribution are formulated as a claim about all deep networks, and no caveat accompanies them; this case is not built into the rule "the second descent always precedes".

**Weight decay is described in three non-matching ways.** In section 4: the effect is non-monotone, weight decay `"both delays and hastens region migration"` ([p9-2](#p9-2)). In appendix C: an increase of weight decay `"delays or removes the second descent in training LC"` ([p18-1](#p18-1)). In table 1: simply "Delayed" ([table 1](#tab-1)). Three formulations about one and the same experiment and one and the same figure 30.

**The weight-decay experiment is described on a different model and a different dataset from its figure.** Section 4 says: `"We train a CNN with depth 5 and width 32 on CIFAR10 with varying weight decay"` ([p9-2](#p9-2)); appendix C repeats the same without CIFAR10 ([p18-1](#p18-1)). The caption of figure 30 reads `"Local complexity dynamics training an MLP on MNIST with weight decay"`, and the regularisation row of table 1 likewise stands with MNIST and an MLP ([table 1](#tab-1)). Besides, section 4 promises training, validation and random LC in figure 30, whereas appendix C promises only training and random. This inconsistency is absent from the analysis in `.claims.txt`; it was found while checking the card.

**Figure 12 is described as a sweep that it does not contain.** Section 4 and the caption describe a single run of a CNN with batch normalisation in exactly the setting of figure 6 on the left ([p9-3](#p9-3), [fig. 12](#fig-12)); appendix C describes the same figure as `"the local complexity during training for CNNs trained on CIFAR10 with varying depths with and without batch normalization"` ([p17-7](#p17-7)). The figure is a single one and contains no sweep over depth. Nearby there also remains a dangling reference `"In Appendix, we present layerwise visualization of the LC dynamics"` with no appendix number ([p17-8](#p17-8)) — a trace of an unfinished edit.

**The caption of figure 32 repeats the caption of figure 30.** Figure 32 is a sweep over the training set size, but its caption reads "Local complexity dynamics training an MLP on MNIST with weight decay", that is, it repeats the caption of figure 30 word for word. This too is absent from the analysis in `.claims.txt`.

**Appendix B breaks off at a formula.** Section 4 promises: `"In Appendix B, we show that at each layer $\ell$ of a DN, BN explicitly adapts the partition so that the partition boundaries are as close to the training data as possible"` ([p9-3](#p9-3)), and the paper's plan promises a "theoretical justification". Appendix B ([sec-b](#sec-b)) carries the computation as far as formula (11) and ends with a comma; there is no conclusion drawn from it, and the substantive claim itself is given in appendix C with a reference to Balestriero & Baraniuk 2022 as already proved there.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus; each entry says what exactly is distorted and gives references to the authors' paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. The citing works' own formulations are analysed in their cards; separate anchors to those analyses have not yet been created in the other folders.

**"Grokking has been observed in models other than neural networks too" (erroneous).** In the survey part of [Xu, Vardi, Safran 2026](../2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression/2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression.card.md) the work of Humayun et al. stands in a list of works that have met grokking "in models different from neural networks", beside Blanc et al. 2020 and Merrill et al. 2023. There are no such models in the work at all: every setting is a deep network (MLP, CNN, ResNet18, GPT, transformer), and the local-complexity measure is defined through the neuron hyperplanes of a piecewise-linear network ([p5-3](#p5-3)), that is, it is inapplicable to non-neural models by construction. The list of settings is given in [p2-4](#p2-4) and in the [experimental setup](#p6-1).

**"Grokking is explained by delayed robustness" (erroneous).** The same work writes that Humayun et al. "attributed the grokking of deep neural networks to their delayed robustness". The relation here is inverted: delayed robustness in the authors is a *variety* of grokking, an observation rather than an explanation ([observation](#p2-3)); what is declared the explanation is the arising of a robust partition and the region migration ([claim](#p3-1), [p7-4](#p7-4)), with the cause of the migration itself named outright as not understood ([p7-5](#p7-5)). A retelling that loses this distinction credits the work with an explanatory force where it carries a hedge.

**"Delayed robustness and local complexity are the result of Jeffares et al." (erroneous, a misattribution).** The survey of Ganesh et al. 2025 (2505.15624, no card in the corpus as yet) has: `"jeffares2024deep attribute it to phase transitions in local complexity (“delayed robustness”)"` — both terms are introduced here ([abstract](#p1-2), [observation](#p2-3)), and Jeffares et al. 2024 ("Deep learning through a telescoping lens") write nothing about local complexity; both works stand in the citing paper's bibliography, so the reference keys have been mixed up.

**"After grokking the representations become modular" (widening).** [Liang, Li 2025](../2512.03437.grokked-models-are-better-unlearners/2512.03437.grokked-models-are-better-unlearners.card.md) write that grokked models exhibit "more structured, modular representations (Humayun et al., 2024; Furuta et al., 2024)". The work measures modularity in no form whatever: what is shown is a lowering of LC around the training points and a gathering of nonlinearities at the decision boundary ([p7-4](#p7-4), [fig. 26](#fig-26)), and the relation to the mechanistic vocabulary is called a reading rather than a measurement ([p8-1](#p8-1)). The warning applies to this formulation alone: the LC measure itself the same work sets out accurately and with the right reference.

**Examples of accurate citation.** [Singh, Belilovsky, Aljundi 2025](../2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold/2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold.card.md): "(Humayun et al., 2024) describe delayed robustness, where models eventually grok adversarial examples long after interpolation" — exactly what is shown. [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md) preserve the hedge: "recent findings suggest that grokking may be a more pervasive phenomenon" — "suggest", not "show". [Gu et al. 2025](../2504.03162.beyond-progress-measures-theoretical-insights-into-the-mechanism-of-grokking/2504.03162.beyond-progress-measures-theoretical-insights-into-the-mechanism-of-grokking.card.md) name the source of the measure precisely and dispute its applicability rather than its content.

**Separately — the risk of confusion with the same authors' neighbouring work.** Part of the corpus cites "Humayun et al., 2023" — that is 2310.12977, "Training dynamics of deep network linear regions", the predecessor of the present paper, in which the double descent of the density of linear regions was introduced. So cite [Lee et al. 2024](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md) (correctly) and [Abramov et al. 2025](../2504.20752.grokking-in-the-wild-data-augmentation-for-real-world-multi-hop-reasoning-with-transformers/2504.20752.grokking-in-the-wild-data-augmentation-for-real-world-multi-hop-reasoning-with-transformers.card.md) (where the work is besides credited with the arising of a "generalising circuit" at a threshold fraction — which appears in neither of the two papers). When checking citations it is worth distinguishing the year: 2023 is linear regions, 2024 is delayed robustness.
