# Ahuja et al. 2024 — Learning Syntax Without Planting Trees: Understanding Hierarchical Generalization in Transformers

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Kabir Ahuja**, **Tianxing He**, **Noah A. Smith**, **Yulia Tsvetkov** (University of Washington), **Vidhisha Balachandran** (Carnegie Mellon University), **Madhur Panwar**, **Navin Goyal** (Microsoft Research).

arXiv [2404.16367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16367), April 2024 (v3), 30 pages, 20 figures. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 17, in the corpus — 0. A continuation of the *structural grokking* line of Murty et al.: the question is not "does delayed hierarchical generalisation occur" but "why does a transformer prefer a hierarchical rule at all, having no architectural inclination towards one". Three answers: (1) the source of the inclination is the **training objective**: of five objectives only language modelling stably yields hierarchical generalisation on all five synthetic tasks (and even a GRU groks under LM — against the architectural conclusion of McCoy et al.); (2) both competing generalisations — the hierarchical and the linear — live inside the trained network **as subnetworks**, extractable by pruning attention heads tens of thousands of steps before the behavioural turning point, and they coexist thanks to the ambiguity of the data; (3) the preference is explained by a Bayesian Occam's razor: where a hierarchical grammar receives a higher posterior than a regular one, the transformer generalises hierarchically, and where it does not, the transformer does not generalise. The cause of the *delay* itself, meanwhile, is not analysed — as is admitted in the conclusion.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — structural grokking is reproduced on all five tasks (100 % in-distribution long before the rise of the generalisation) and acquires a new controlling variable: the **training objective** — of five objectives only language modelling stably leads to hierarchical generalisation, and it is precisely the difference of objectives that explains the divergence of the predecessors (seq2seq in McCoy/Petty/Mueller against LM in Murty) ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)); a strong side check — a two-layer GRU under LM also generalises hierarchically, and under seq2seq does not ([`p6-7`](#p6-7)). The analysis: the main gap in the design is that the work explains *which* of the two generalisations wins and says nothing about *why* the victory is delayed (the Bayesian comparison is static; admitted in the conclusion); and none of the corpus's usual measures (the norm, the sparsity, the spectrum) is measured.

**[Sparse subnetworks and the lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the competition is not "memorisation against generalisation" but one between **two different generalisations**: pruning the attention heads (48 gates with an $L_{0}$ penalty, the weights frozen) at an intermediate checkpoint at which the full model generalises at 30 % finds both a subnetwork with 100 % generalisation (Gen-prune) and a "linear rule" subnetwork with 0 % generalisation at 100 % in-distribution ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)); the chronology: the linear subnetwork is extractable from about 6000 steps and the hierarchical one from about 9000, when the full model generalises at only 5 %, after which the two coexist to the end of the training ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)); an intervention on the data kills either subnetwork at will — 10k unambiguously hierarchical examples destroy the linear subnetwork (128 pruning hyperparameter settings fail to find it), and 10k linear ones destroy the hierarchical one ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)). The analysis: there is a single control group (a random initialisation); and with 1 head per layer no subnetwork is found at all, although the model generalises better than the four-headed one — an open question.

**[Kolmogorov complexity](../../concepts/kolmogorov-complexity.md)** — simplicity is operationalised through the Solomonoff–Occam razor: the posterior of a grammar $p(G\mid D)\propto p(D\mid G)\,p(G)$ balances the fit against the description length (a geometric prior penalising non-terminals and productions) ([`p11-1`](#p11-1)); on a diverse set (120 sentence types) a context-free grammar wins — and the transformer generalises hierarchically; on a poor one (12 types) a single-state grammar wins — and the transformer prefers nothing ([`p14-3`](#p14-3)); the sensitivity to the prior and the sub-optimality of hand-made grammars are checked. The analysis: the whole part rests on two points, in which the diversity of the data and the posterior advantage are not disentangled; the likelihood is computed over sentence types while the model is trained on 50k sentences; and the "agreement with the ideal learner" on the poor set is a stretch — the ideal learner chooses memorisation, whereas the transformer shows indifference.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — hierarchical generalisation as a test of whether phrase structure is learned without trees given at the input; a check against a substitute: a seq2seq model on tense reinflection attains a high generalisation accuracy by a *non-hierarchical* shortcut — the tree-ness of its representations (Murty et al.) does not grow, and a linear probe recovers the syntactic categories at 50 % against 99 % under LM ([`p24-1`](#p24-1)); the generalising mechanism exists in the network tens of thousands of steps before the behavioural turning point — the delay appears as a change in which of the coexisting subnetworks governs the output ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)). The analysis: the measures are single-token ones (the first word, the main verb), the generation as a whole is not checked — a weakness admitted and partly closed by a strict "over all verbs" measure.

**[Systematic generalisation](../../concepts/systematic-generalization.md)** — the preference for the hierarchical rule is induced by the training objective rather than by the architecture, and it carries over to recurrent networks ([`p6-7`](#p6-7)). This constrains the explanations that ascribe hierarchy to the architecture of the transformer.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — three measurements the corpus did not have.** The training objective as the source of the hierarchical inclination (with a direct explanation of the divergence of the predecessors and a GRU check against the architectural conclusion); the evidence that the generalising subnetwork exists tens of thousands of steps before the behavioural turning point — the delay as a change of the governing subnetwork rather than a late birth of the mechanism; and an intervention on the ambiguity of the data as a way of killing either subnetwork at will. The check against a substitute (tree-ness plus probes for seq2seq) and the control on random initialisations are carried out; and the Bayesian part is defended against two obvious loopholes (a sensitivity to the prior over 49 combinations; a local search over grammars by model merging).

**The claim of "only language modelling" does not hold everywhere.** A hyperparameter search gives a prefix LM with a causal mask 60 % on English question formation at 12 layers (3 seeds out of 5 being perfect), and on German question formation and on passivisation it is level with the LM; this caveat is not sounded in the abstract or the conclusions. The numbers are more modest than the slogan: for the LM the mean generalisation accuracy on two tasks is about 75 %, with a large seed-to-seed spread. Removing the identical pairs collapses the generalisation on question formation — part of the success is inherited from the construction of McCoy et al.'s datasets rather than from the objective alone.

**The delay is not explained, and the Bayesian part is static.** The Bayesian comparison sets two outcomes side by side and does not describe the course of the training; "it remains unclear why hierarchical generalisation is delayed" are the words of the conclusion. The inverted U in depth from Murty et al. is not checked. The discussion of the theories of grokking is a single survey paragraph: the paper's own explanation belongs to the "simplicity bias" family but is compared with none of the theories listed. Minor sloppiness: §A.3 says "a lower posterior" where the table gives a higher one; Mueller et al. 2022/2023 and Müller are conflated; and the simple-agreement experiments are referred to §3.1, where they are not.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is explained why transformers grok the hierarchy of language") — more precisely: on five artificial datasets it is shown that hierarchical generalisation appears practically only under a language-modelling objective; pruning the heads shows the coexistence of a hierarchical and a linear subnetwork; the Bayesian explanation is checked on one task and two datasets and, by the authors' own admission, remains a correspondence at the computational level; and the cause of the delay itself is not analysed.
