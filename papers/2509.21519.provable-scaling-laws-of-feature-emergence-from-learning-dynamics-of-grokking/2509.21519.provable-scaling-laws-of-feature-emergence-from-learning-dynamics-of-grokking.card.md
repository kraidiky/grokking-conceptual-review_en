## Concepts covered

**[Feature learning](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the work gives the corpus what it did not have: **an equation from which the features follow**. The dynamics of the hidden layer at the second stage is exactly a gradient ascent of an energy function $\mathcal{E}(\mathbf{w})=\frac{1}{2}\Vert\tilde{Y}^{\top}\sigma(X\mathbf{w})\Vert^{2}$ (theorem 1), and the features learned are its local maxima ([`p2-5`](#p2-5)). For group tasks these maxima are described completely (theorem 2): either a real irreducible representation or a pair of complex ones, and there are no others.

**[From lazy to rich](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the Li2 frame analyses the transition into three stages and explains **why the lazy phase is necessary**: until the top layer has fitted, the back-propagated gradient $G_{F}$ is pure noise, and there is nothing from which to learn features ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). The work explicitly goes beyond the NTK and mean field ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)): in its regime the hidden weights do depart substantially from their initialisation.

**[The memorisation phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — an important distinction: memorisation in grokking is an **overfitting on random features** (stage I), and that is not the same as falling into a memorising solution in the course of feature learning under scarce data (theorem 5). Hence a conclusion that inverts the customary formulation: grokking switches not from memorisation to generalisation but from **overfitting** to generalisation ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)).

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — here it is derived rather than tuned: the local maxima of $\mathcal{E}$ are preserved at $n\gtrsim d_{k}^{2}M\log(M/\delta)$ (theorem 4), whence the training-data fraction $p=n/M^{2}\sim M^{-1}\log M$ ([`p2-5`](#p2-5)). The prediction is checked on cyclic groups of prime and composite order, on direct products and on non-abelian groups ([`fig-5`](#fig-5)) — and it agrees with the observed boundary.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the boundary between generalisation and memorisation acquires a mechanism: it is not a threshold in the dynamics but a **change of the landscape** of $\mathcal{E}$ as the data distribution changes. The empirical curves agree with the prediction ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)), and the transition itself is observed as a jump of the test accuracy from 0 to 1.

**[Semi-grokking](../../concepts/semi-grokking.md)** and **[ungrokking](../../concepts/ungrokking.md)** — both phenomena of Varma et al. are derived from the same picture: near the boundary local maxima of both kinds coexist, and which one the model lands in is decided by the learning rate ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)); and a grokked model, fine-tuned on a smaller dataset, returns to memorisation because the landscape has changed.

**[Group representations](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** and **[groups](../../concepts/group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md)** — the task is taken not as "modular addition" but as **group arithmetic** in general, and the solution is expressed in terms of irreducible representations ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)). For abelian groups these are one-dimensional and give Fourier bases ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)), for non-abelian ones blocks of dimension $d_{k}>1$, and it is precisely $d_{k}$ that enters the scaling law. The non-abelian groups for the check were generated by GAP programs ([`p42-12`](#p42-12)).

**[Fourier features](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the Fourier solution of modular addition, which Nanda et al. found by reverse engineering, is **derived as a consequence** here: for an abelian group the local maxima of the energy are single-frequency ([`p6-9`](#p6-9)). This is perhaps the strongest confirmation of the Fourier picture in the corpus — not an observation but a derivation.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — receives an unexpected role: since $G_{F}(+\infty)\propto\eta\tilde{Y}\tilde{Y}^{\top}F$, the weight decay $\eta$ works as the **learning rate of the features** ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). Hence the laws of the form $t\sim 1/\eta$ from earlier works are explained. Weight decay is meanwhile declared sufficient but not necessary: all that is required is a non-zero $G_{F}$, and it can be maintained otherwise.

**[Numerical stability techniques](../../concepts/numerical-stability-fix.md)** — a direct incorporation of the result of Prieto et al. into this frame: a stable softmax slows the overfitting to the labels and therefore preserves a non-zero back-propagated gradient ([`p11-8`](#p11-8)). Two explanations found independently are joined here into a single causal chain.

**[Initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — a practical corollary of the theory: if the top layer is initialised at **zero**, then only the signal term remains in $G_{F}(t)$ from the outset, and the feature learning is accelerated ([`p4-9`](#p4-9)); in a multi-layer setting the gain reaches $10\times$.

**[Optimisers](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — it is explained from first principles why Muon is effective: it suppresses the part of the gradient that repeats the directions already occupied and therefore pushes the units towards unoccupied local maxima ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). Formally this is a coupon-collector problem, and Muon lowers the expected number of units from $T_{0}$ to about $L$ (theorem 8).

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — a conclusion contrary to the common one: near the boundary a **small** learning rate is more likely to lead to a generalising solution, because it keeps the trajectory within its basin, whereas a large one leads away to a non-generalising solution of higher energy ([`fig-7`](#fig-7)).

**[Effective theory](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** and **[circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — both lines are named in the survey ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)), and the second receives a grounding from below: "the generalising circuit is more efficient but slower" ceases to be a postulate and becomes a consequence of which local maxima the landscape preserves at a given amount of data ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. The analysis of the local maxima leans on the group structure of the input — the author names this as a limitation outright; for arbitrary data only $\mathcal{E}$ itself is derived, not the structure of its maxima. Nor are there times of the transitions between the stages: the frame says that the stages come in this order but not when each will end.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the task; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the Fourier solution, derived here; [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — the analytical solution for a quadratic activation; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — circuit efficiency, semi-grokking and ungrokking, which receive a grounding here; [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — the lazy-to-rich transition; [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md) — the stable softmax, built in here as a way of maintaining $G_{F}$; [Rubin et al. 2024](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.card.md) — the mean field, which the work deliberately goes beyond.

**[Scaling laws](../../concepts/scaling-laws.md)** — the laws here are not fitted but derived: they follow from how the landscape changes together with the data distribution ([`p2-5`](#p2-5)), and are checked by the agreement of the predicted memorisation-generalisation boundary with the measured one ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)).
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). This is the corpus's most theoretical work after Mohamadi et al., and it should be read with an eye to which assumptions make the proofs possible.

**The main achievement is a derivation rather than an observation.** That Fourier features are learned in modular addition the corpus has known since 2023 from reverse engineering. Here this is a **consequence**: the dynamics of the hidden layer is a gradient ascent of $\mathcal{E}$, and the local maxima of $\mathcal{E}$ for an abelian group are single-frequency ([`p6-9`](#p6-9)). The chain "gradient → energy → its maxima → the Fourier basis" is closed.

**The scaling law is checked broadly.** $p\sim M^{-1}\log M$ is derived from the stability of the local maxima under subsampling (theorem 4) and confirmed on four families of groups at once, the non-abelian ones included ([`fig-5`](#fig-5)). For the corpus this is a rare case in which a prediction is quantitative and agrees with experiment across different types of task.

**The key assumption is the quadratic activation.** The complete description of the local maxima (theorem 2) is proved for $\sigma(x)=x^{2}$ and extends to $ax+bx^{2}$. For ReLU and SiLU all that remains of this analysis is what concerns memorising solutions (theorem 5): there it is shown that power activations give a **concentrated** memorisation and the practical ones a **smeared** one. In other words, the most elegant part of the theory is tied to an activation that practical networks do not use.

**The second support is the group structure of the input.** The author names this a limitation himself: $\mathcal{E}$ is derived for any data, but its maxima are described only for group tasks. Outside algebraic tasks, then, the frame gives a language but not answers.

**The staging is grounded in different ways.** Stage I is analysed rigorously: appendix C gives an expansion of $G_{F}(t)$ at short times and an exponential convergence at $\eta=0$ and $\eta>0$. Stage II is the heart of the work, with a complete description of the maxima. Stage III rests on three results of differing strength: the repulsion of similar features (theorem 6) is derived carefully through a Schur complement; the top-down modulation (theorem 7) is proved for a quadratic activation; and the behaviour of Muon (theorem 8) is proved **for a model dynamics** with a particular initialisation distribution (an inverse-exponential one) rather than for real training.

**The practical corollaries are checked selectively.** A zero initialisation of the top layer is a direct consequence of the theory, and it really does accelerate the feature learning, up to $10\times$ in a multi-layer setting ([`p4-9`](#p4-9)). This is the corpus's best example of a theoretical frame yielding a working technique rather than only an explanation after the fact.

**The section "when grokking happens" is a systematisation of other people's observations.** Eight factors (the learning rate, the initialisation, the multiplier $\beta$, weight decay, the data size, the number of units, early stopping) are explained through one quantity — the strength of $G_{F}$. The explanations are consistent with the works of Gromov, Liu, Kumar, Prieto and Power, but each is an argument within the model rather than a separate check.

**A caveat about the use of LLMs.** The work states outright that the proofs were worked through with large language models, that many generated proofs were subtly wrong and required substantial correction, and that the author takes responsibility for their correctness. For the corpus this is the first such case; a reader of the card should know it when consulting the appendices.

**Its place in the corpus.** Before this work, explanations of grokking divided into measurement ones (what changes over the course of the training) and structural ones (how the final solution is built). Li2 ties them together: the final structure is derived from the dynamics, and the quantities others measured (the weight norm, circuit efficiency, the moment of the transition) turn out to be manifestations of one picture — of which local maxima of the energy a given data distribution preserves. The price is the narrowness of the setting: a two-layer network, a quadratic activation, group arithmetic.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2210-01117
**Omnigrok (2210.01117) — an inversion of the result about the initialisation.** `"(Liu et al., 2023) reports that grokking happens with small initialization, regardless of the weight decay"`. In the original authors' account it is exactly the reverse: [a small initialisation (α=0.5) always generalises fast, independently of weight decay](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#fig-2) — the delay is created by a large initialisation, and it is precisely by scaling the initial weights that grokking is induced in the cited work. Elsewhere the same paper cites Omnigrok correctly: `"for large weight initialization, regularization is needed for grokking to happen"` — so the inversion is a slip in one passage, but a published one and capable of misleading anyone choosing a setting.

###### mc-2310-06110
**Kumar et al. (2310.06110) — an inversion of the effect of the output scale.** `"(Kumar et al., 2024; Chizat et al., 2019) reports that scaling the output by a factor β>1 will make the grokking faster"`. In Kumar it is exactly the reverse: [a larger output scale makes the dynamics lazier and lengthens the delay](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/original/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.md#p4-2), and in the limit of an infinite scale the network linearises and does not generalise; what accelerates grokking is precisely a reduction of the scale. The paper's own derivation (a small initialisation plus an amplification of the output signal) describes a different regime — the attribution is wrong, not the mathematics. A neighbouring paraphrase of the same work is accurate: grokking without regularisation under full-batch gradient descent.

###### mc-2310-03789
**Rubin et al. (2310.03789) — the mean field characterised oppositely.** `"mean field regime (Rubin et al., 2024), in which the hidden weights do not update substantially from initializations"`: in Rubin it is exactly the reverse — [a mean-field formalism with feature learning: the kernel develops peaks in the direction of the target and the droplet neurons travel towards the teacher's solution](../2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks/original/2310.03789.grokking-as-a-first-order-phase-transition-in-two-layer-networks.md#p7-1). The characterisation is confused with the lazy (NTK) regime. Elsewhere the same paper correctly cites another work by the same group for kernel adaptation — an error of attachment rather than of understanding the literature.

###### mc-2405-15071
**Wang et al. (2405.15071) — called as a witness for the opposite.** (1) `"there exists a critical data size (Varma et al., 2023) or more precisely a critical data ratio (Wang et al., 2024a; Abramov et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2022), above which the grokking suddenly leads to generalization"`: the φ of Wang [governs the speed rather than the possibility of generalisation](../2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners/2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners.card.md#p4-1); his argument is directed against the threshold frame of a critical size. (2) `"more samples lead to better shaped local maxima with less noise and thus the feature learning is faster, which is consistent with (… Wang et al., 2024a …)"` — this directly contradicts Wang's main result: at a fixed φ the sample size does not affect the relative speed.
