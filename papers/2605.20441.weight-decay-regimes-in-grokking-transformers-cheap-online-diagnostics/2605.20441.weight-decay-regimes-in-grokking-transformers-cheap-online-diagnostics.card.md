## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work takes the phenomenon as given and turns it into an object of cartography: it does not explain the delay but measures at which values of a single hyperparameter it occurs at all. Its contribution to the notion is a map in $(\lambda,N)$ with three qualitatively different regions and **two** failure edges, measured on one grid: below $\lambda_{c}=0.0158$ the grokking rate is nearly zero, in the interval $\lambda\in[0.1,2.0]$ it is between 90 and 100 %, and at $\lambda=10$ the heads collapse to identity patterns ([§4.1](#p6-2), [fig. 1](#fig-1)). It follows that grokking here is neither a property of the task nor a property of the architecture but a region in the space of settings, and that "did not grok" splits into two different failures. What the work does not contain: a mechanism — no circuits, no Fourier analysis and no analysis of what exactly is learnt; the definition of grokking is operational (a test accuracy $\geq 0.99$ within the horizon), and the whole scope of the claims is narrowed by the author himself to modular arithmetic in attention models of up to 85 M parameters ([§5.1](#p16-3)).

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the paper inherits the proving ground of Power et al. 2022 word for word and does so deliberately: the canonical set $(p,L,H,d,\mathrm{lr},\mathrm{batch})=(97,4,8,128,10^{-3},512)$ is chosen for direct comparability with the wave of works of 2026 ([§3.1](#p5-1)). In return it gives the proving ground a number: the value of $\lambda_{c}$ with a confidence interval computed by bootstrap over 210 runs, and it answers directly the open question left in §4 of Power et al. about whether cheap measures correlate with the transition ([§5.2](#p17-2)). What the work does not contain: sweeps of $p$, of the fraction of training data and of the learning rate — all three are fixed in every experiment — and there is no composition of permutations $S_{5}$ at all, so the work says nothing about non-commutative tasks.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the proving ground is widened from addition alone to four operations ($\mathrm{mod}_{+}$, $\mathrm{mod}_{-}$, $\mathrm{mod}_{\times}$, $\mathrm{mod}_{\div}$) at $p=97$, with the pairs having $b=0$ discarded for division before the split ([§3.1](#p5-1)). A multi-task replication over 280 runs gives the corpus a rare thing — an ordering of the operations by grokking rate under an identical protocol: $\mathrm{mod}_{+}\,0.73$, $\mathrm{mod}_{\div}\,0.71$, $\mathrm{mod}_{\times}\,0.70$, $\mathrm{mod}_{-}\,0.49$ ([§4.7](#p13-2), [fig. 9](#fig-9)). The author caveats himself that the ordering across tasks at $n{=}5$ per cell is underpowered and that what carries the conclusion is the aggregate monotone picture rather than the ordering; the ordering cannot be cited as a result.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work's central notion: it is precisely weight decay that is declared a scalar **empirical control parameter** whose sweep carries the training through qualitatively different regimes ([abstract](#p1-2)). It matters that the sense of the word is stipulated narrowly and at once: no Hamiltonian, no free-energy functional and no equilibrium partition function is derived, SGD trajectories are not declared a thermal ensemble, and the contribution is named as the empirical identification of a two-dimensional diagram ([§5.1](#p16-4)). For the first time in the corpus weight decay receives not a "direction of influence" but a **numerical boundary with an interval** and a second, upper boundary of the regime. What the work does not contain: a comparison with other regularisers — dropout and label smoothing are declared outright to be outside the scope, and dropout is fixed at zero; nor is there an experiment in which weight decay varies over the course of the training.

**[The direction of weight decay's influence](../../concepts/weight-decay-direction.md)** — this is the corpus's first work to build a non-monotone curve entirely on one grid: a collapse below $\lambda_{c}$, a monotone acceleration $1090\to 83$ epochs as $\lambda$ grows from 0.1 to 2.0, and a collapse at $\lambda=10$ ([§4.1](#p6-2), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). Within the working interval the direction supports the side of Omnigrok and Kumar against the numbers of Nanda's appendix D.1. But the acceleration here is **slower than inverse proportionality**: a 20-fold rise of $\lambda$ accelerates the grokking 13.1-fold, whereas Omnigrok's law $t\propto\gamma^{-1}$ would predict 20. There is no comparison with that law in the paper: Liu et al. 2023 are cited once and only as an extension of grokking beyond algorithmic data. The exponent $\nu=0.757$ ([§4.4](#p7-1)) has nothing to do with this — it pertains to the distance from the threshold $(\lambda-\lambda_{c})$ rather than to $\lambda$ itself, and the two must not be confused. What the work does not contain: measurements between $\lambda=2.0$ and $\lambda=10$, so the optimum and the upper boundary are still not localised by measurement.

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the author answers the countervailing frame of Yıldırım 2026 with a direct refusal of the strong claim: weight decay is **not** declared necessary for generalisation, and it is asserted only that within a standard attention architecture it behaves as an empirical control parameter with a definite regime diagram ([§2](#p3-1)). Three cross-architecture trials strengthen this from another side: the weight-decay-governed transition is reproduced even where there is no attention at all — on an MLP, an LSTM and Mamba ([§4.9](#p14-1), [§4.9](#p14-3), [fig. 10](#fig-10)) — which means that the phenomenon is not a peculiarity of attention. What the work does not contain: a setting with $\lambda=0$ and a different mechanism of generalisation; "necessity" here is not checked but circumvented by a caveat.

**[The order parameter](../../concepts/order-parameter.md)** — the work introduces into the corpus two scalar order parameters computed **from the forward-pass attention weights alone**: the mean pairwise cosine similarity of the heads $\bar{s}(t)$ and the spread of the entropies across heads $\sigma_{H}(t)$ ([§3.2](#p5-2)). Their difference from the corpus's earlier order parameters lies in cost and location: $\mathcal{O}(LH^{2}BT^{2})$ and $\mathcal{O}(LHBT^{2})$ per evaluation, about 3 % overhead when measured every 10 steps, with no access to checkpoints and no chain sampling ([§3.2](#p5-3)). An essential caveat of the author's own: the pre-registered predictiveness threshold (AUC $\geq 0.85$) is **not met** — a random forest on held-out runs gives $0.799$, and the diagnostics are declared correlational rather than predictive ([§4.8](#p13-4)). What the work does not contain: a definition of these quantities for architectures without heads, and any relation to symmetry in Landau's sense — "order parameter" here is used in a lexical rather than a statistical-physics sense.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the language of a phase transition is taken from the authors and backed by a number: a logistic fit of $P(\mathrm{grok})$ in $\log\lambda$ gives $\lambda_{c}=0.0158$ with a 95 % interval $[0.0109,0.0200]$, and a power-law fit $t_{\text{grok}}\propto(\lambda-\lambda_{c})^{-\nu}$ gives an exponent $\nu=0.757$ ([§4.4](#p7-1), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). What matters for the corpus is not the value itself but the honesty of its status: the reference exponents $\nu=1/2$ and the three-dimensional Ising $\nu\approx 0.63$ are rejected, but with the caveat "at our grid of four bins"; an assignment to a universality class is expressly postponed; and a full finite-size data collapse is admitted to be unachievable on three sweep bins ([§3.4](#p6-1)). What the work does not contain: a thermodynamic derivation, Binder cumulants or a data collapse — that half the author leaves to Bi et al. 2026 and positions his own work as a supplier of phenomenology.

**[Anti-grokking](../../concepts/anti-grokking.md)** — the work gives the notion its first independent observation **under conditions opposite to the original ones**. In Prakash & Martin the late collapse sets in on the original setup **without** weight decay after about $10^{7}$ steps; here a qualitatively similar collapse is visible at $\lambda=1.0$ at 20 000 epochs, and across the persistence cohort the collapse **concentrates at larger** weight decay ([§4.2](#p6-3), [fig. 3](#fig-3)). The second contribution is negative and important: the cycle is declared a **seed-dependent fragility** rather than a universal rule (persistence rates $5/5$, $4/5$, $3/5$, $4/5$), and the most pronounced five-stage shape occurs in about a third of the seeds at the canonical WD. The third is a divergence from the mechanism: the heavy-tail exponent $\alpha$ falls $2.07\to 1.39$ already by epoch 500, that is, on **entry** into the grokking, and stays below 1.5 right up to the collapse, so it is unfit as an indicator of the time of the collapse; the author himself calls this a partial parallel rather than a reproduction ([§4.5](#p8-2), [fig. 7](#fig-7)). What the work does not contain: correlation traps, catastrophic forgetting or any mechanism of the collapse — only its course in time and its dependence on $\lambda$; figure 7 is moreover built on a single seed.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the training is decomposed into two successive phases, both described through the alignment of the heads: phase 1 is a convergence of the heads ($\bar{s}$ rising $0.93\to 0.995$) near the era of grokking, phase 2 their divergence ($\bar{s}\to 0.88$) at an already plateaued test accuracy ([§4.2](#p6-3), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). A separate contribution is the identification of the **per-head dimension** $d/H$ as the empirically dominant variable for the amplitude of the divergence: the peak of $\sigma_{H}$ grows with $d/H$ monotonically and saturatingly, plateauing at $d/H\geq 16$ ([§4.3](#p6-4), [fig. 4](#fig-4)), and the elementary rank bound $\mathrm{rank}(QK^{\top})\leq d_{h}$ gives this a consistent but unproved explanation ([appendix C](#p17-3)). What the work does not contain: an analysis of what the diverged heads compute — the author writes outright that his diagnostics mark **when** the divergence happens and how large it is, not **what** the heads do individually, and leaves the identification of the algorithm to subsequent work ([§5.2](#p18-2)).

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the paper's strongest argument and its best device: a paired experiment with three groups and pre-registered hypotheses, in which re-initialising 2 heads out of 8 at the era of the $\sigma_{H}$ peak lowers that peak ($B-A=-0.038$, paired $t$ with $p_{t}=9.3\times 10^{-4}$), whereas a **matched** clipping of the weight norm to the same median at the same era has no effect ([§4.6](#p11-2), [§4.6](#p11-4), [fig. 8](#fig-8)). Its value for the corpus lies precisely in the control: it separates the structure of the head patterns from the magnitude of the weights, that is, it disentangles two explanations usually conflated in the corpus. The author meanwhile narrows the conclusion himself: the intervention does not prevent generalisation (all three groups grok at a rate of one hundred per cent), the grokking epoch does not shift significantly, and a carry-over to other architectures, tasks and values of $\lambda$ outside $\{0.015,0.05\}$ is expressly not claimed ([§4.6](#p12-1)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the paper proposes its order parameters for the role of a cheap online measure and **itself rejects** that role by a pre-declared criterion: on held-out runs at unseen values of $\lambda$ a random forest gives an AUC of $0.799$ against a threshold of $0.85$, a logistic regression $0.678$, and the verdict is "correlational only" ([§4.8](#p13-4)). A detail of substance for the corpus: in the feature importances weight decay itself stands first ($0.247$), the weight norm second ($0.220$), and the two order parameters together give $0.275$ — comparable with the weight norm alone. A comparison with the refined local learning coefficient (rLLC) gives a Pearson correlation $r=0.46$ at a Fisher interval $[-0.20,0.82]$ which, by the author's own words, is consistent with anything from zero correlation to a strong one ([§2](#p4-1)). What the work does not contain: a comparison with the corpus's progress measures — neither Nanda's restricted and excluded loss nor Barak's hidden progress is mentioned or computed.

**[Heavy-tailed self-regularisation](../../concepts/heavy-tailed-self-regularization-htsr.md)** — the work applies Weightwatcher to its canonical trajectory and obtains a result **diverging in time** from the one for whose sake the measure was brought in: $\alpha$ falls from $2.07$ at initialisation to $1.39$ by epoch 500 and stays in the heavy-tailed regime ($\alpha<2$) right up to phase 5 ([§4.5](#p8-2), [fig. 7](#fig-7)). The heavy tail therefore forms on entry into the grokking rather than at the late collapse, and the "third phase" feature is unfit as a direct indicator of the onset of the collapse. This is the corpus's only direct comparison of the course of $\alpha$ with an independent diagnostic on one and the same trajectory. What the work does not contain: an analysis of the correlation traps themselves, a layerwise cut (a median over layers is taken) and a cross-seed coverage — figure 7 is built on a single seed, and the author declares a cross-seed computation postponed outright.

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the work's second spectral quantity lives not in the weights but in the **covariance of the head patterns**: the affinely normalised participation ratio $\mathrm{PR}_{\mathrm{norm}}$ of its spectrum, running $0.86$ (initialisation) $\to 0.71$ (phase 1) $\to$ oscillations of $0.18$–$0.55$ (phases 2–4) $\to 0.13$ (phase 5) ([§4.5](#p7-2), [§4.5](#p8-1), [fig. 6](#fig-6)). The quantity is declared a direct check of the breaking of the permutation symmetry $S_{H}$ and replaces the indirect measure $M_{\text{perm}}$ of the manuscript's previous revision; its algebraic relation to the coefficient of variation of the spectrum is proved and machine-checked. What the work does not contain: a causal threshold of the symmetry breaking — the author writes himself that the check certifies only an algebraic equivalence and does not carry the diagnostic over to architectures without attention.

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — AdamW here is not a background but part of the argument: an amplification factor $\kappa$ absorbing the adaptive rescaling of the step is calibrated against the trajectory of the Frobenius norm and gives $\kappa=18.6$ on the canonical trajectory of seed 42 ([appendix C.5](#p26-4), [fig. 11](#fig-11)). The corpus gains two things from this: the first empirical calibration of how far the decoupled weight decay of AdamW contracts the norm faster than the nominal $\eta\lambda$, and an honestly presented failure — across seeds $\kappa$ is bimodal ($\{18.4,19.2,17.9,8.1,8.6\}$ when fitted over the transition window), and the slow relaxation of seeds 31 and 123 the model does not explain ([appendix C.5](#p26-5)). What the work does not contain: SGD, Lion and Sophia — other optimisers are expressly declared outside the scope, so the claim is tied to AdamW and carries over to nothing else.

**[The norm-separation delay law](../../concepts/norm-separation-delay-law.md)** — the paper stands to the law of Truong et al. in the position of an empirical calibration and an **inversion**: where they predict the delay through a Lyapunov contraction, here the threshold is predicted through a dual horizon constraint, $\lambda_{c}=-\ln(1-p_{\text{relax}})/(\eta\kappa T_{\max})$, which at $p_{\text{relax}}=0.99$ gives $0.0124$ — inside the empirical interval ([appendix C.5](#p26-4), [§5.2](#p17-2)). Their $\gamma_{\text{eff}}$ corresponds to our $\eta\,\kappa\,\lambda$, and the norm ratio to the ratio $(\Omega_{0}/\Omega_{\infty})^{2}$. The author calls this a calibrated consistency check rather than a derivation from first principles and lists outright what the derivation does not establish. What the work does not contain: an independent check of the delay law itself — the time to grokking is fitted by a power law in $(\lambda-\lambda_{c})$, and the logarithmic form of Truong et al. is not checked on this data.

**[Effective theory / statistical mechanics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — the work uses the vocabulary of statistical mechanics (a control parameter, order parameters, regimes, a critical exponent) and simultaneously fences itself off from it in a separate paragraph: no Hamiltonian, no free energy, no partition function and no declaration of SGD trajectories as a thermal ensemble ([§5.1](#p16-4)). The formal part is reduced to five elementary statements (A1–E1), four of which are machine-checked in Lean 4 against mathlib v4.29.0 with not a single `sorry` — and the author caveats at once that what is proved is the **correctness of the definitions** rather than the empirical claims ([appendix C](#p23-4)). The sharpness of the boundaries in theorem A1 is a property of the setting (the differences of the objectives are affine in $\lambda$) rather than a consequence of the training dynamics. What the work does not contain: a theory proper — the minimal three-regime model is a statement about the existence of thresholds rather than a prediction of their values.

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — a lever that the work holds fixed and about which its own formula says the most. The learning rate $\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$ was not varied in any of the 1120 runs ([§3.1](#p5-1)), whereas the expression derived in appendix C.5, $\lambda_{c}=-\ln(1-p_{\text{relax}})/(\eta\kappa T_{\max})$, contains $\eta$ in the denominator and therefore predicts $\lambda_{c}\propto 1/\eta$ ([appendix C.5](#p26-4)). A checkable prediction of the paper's own theory is left unchecked. From the same formula it follows that $\lambda_{c}$ is a property of the protocol $(\eta,T_{\max})$ rather than of the model — a caveat the author makes for architectures but not for the learning rate and the horizon.

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — random labels are used as a null control for the amplitude: 15 runs give a peak $\sigma_{H}=0.087\pm 0.025$, the same order of magnitude as the low-amplitude regime at $d/H=2$ ([§4.7](#p13-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). What is valuable here is precisely the caution of the conclusion: the difference is significant but small (a permutation test $p=0.009$, Cohen's $d=1.11$), so the control is declared a **lower bound** on the amplitude rather than a statistical equivalence, and the dashed line in figure 4 is labelled a reference scale rather than a claim of equivalence. What the work does not contain: a sweep of the fraction of corrupted labels — the labels are either all genuine or all random.

## Points we could not reconcile

**Two non-matching estimates of one and the same quantity $\lambda_{c}$ for modular addition.** The canonical result is $\lambda_{c}=0.0158$ with an interval $[0.0109,0.0200]$ ([§4.4](#p7-1)); an aggregate refit over four operations on a small model gives $0.0077$ with an interval $[0.0056,0.0106]$, and the intervals **do not overlap** ([§4.7](#p13-2)). The author declares this "a difference of protocol rather than a contradiction" (different horizons, a different density of WD bins) and keeps $0.0158$ as the canon and E9 as a consistency trial. The caveat is honest, but the divergence remains unclosed: a quantity presented in the abstract as a number with an interval depends on the measurement protocol more strongly than the width of that interval.

**Eleven conditions against fifteen cohorts.** The abstract says "across eleven experimental conditions" ([abstract](#p1-2)), while appendix A lists fifteen cohorts E1–E15. What exactly counts as a "condition" is nowhere defined, and the one cannot be reconciled with the other from the text.

**The label E8 denotes two different cohorts.** In [§4.2](#p6-3) and in table 2 of appendix A, E8 is the persistence sweep on a long horizon (4 WD values $\times$ 5 seeds, 20 thousand epochs, $n{=}20$). In [§4.7](#p13-1) the same label is given to the random-label control pilot: "superseding the smaller E8 pilot". The two uses cannot be reconciled.

**The MLP grokking rates are given by mutually exclusive conditions.** [§4.9](#p14-1) says that the MLP groks "at $\lambda{\geq}0.05$ in $3/10$ seeds, at $\lambda{=}0.07$ in $10/10$ and at $\lambda{<}0.05$ in $0/10$". The first and second conditions overlap: $\lambda=0.07$ falls within $\lambda\geq 0.05$. The sum $13/70$ given in the same place adds up only on the reading "at $\lambda=0.05$ — $3/10$", that is, the first condition is to be understood as an equality.

## What is shown, what is calibrated and what is conjectured

Half the work is measurement and the other half the calibration of a single fitted constant, and in the text they stand side by side; a reader taking a number from here does well to know which half it comes from.

**Shown by measurement.** A three-regime map along the $\lambda$ axis with both failure edges ([§4.1](#p6-2)); a monotone fall of the time to grokking $1090\to 83$ epochs; a logistic boundary $\lambda_{c}=0.0158$ over 210 runs; a reproduction of the WD-governed picture over 280 runs across four operations ([§4.7](#p13-2)) and on three architectures without attention ([§4.9](#p14-1), [§4.9](#p14-3)); a paired causal contrast "head re-initialisation against norm clipping" ([§4.6](#p11-4)); and a negative result on the predictiveness of the diagnostics, declared against a pre-set threshold ([§4.8](#p13-4)).

**Calibrated rather than derived.** The bound $\lambda_{c}^{\text{bound}}=0.0124$ rests on two fitted quantities. Of the five values of $p_{\text{relax}}$ in table 4 **only** $0.99$ falls inside the empirical interval (at $0.95$ it already comes out as $0.0081$, a ratio of $0.51$). $\kappa$ itself is bimodal across seeds: fitted over the whole horizon, $\{18.6,19.0\}$ against $\{5.5,6.9,6.4\}$; fitted over the transition window, $\{18.4,19.2,17.9,8.1,8.6\}$ — and seeds 31 and 123 the model, by the author's admission, does not explain ([§5.1](#p16-6), [appendix C.5](#p26-5)). The author lists the limitations himself, in a separate paragraph ([appendix C.5](#p27-1)).

**Conjectured or resting on a single point.** The upper boundary of the developed regime is obtained by symbolic regression (PySR) on the amplitude data: from the form $\sigma_{H}^{\max}\approx c\,(d/H)/(\mathrm{wd}+\sqrt{d/H})$ a half-maximum follows at $\lambda_{c}\approx\sqrt{d/H}=4.0$, and "the two boundaries bracket the developed regime $[0.012,4]$" ([appendix C.5](#p26-6)). Between $\lambda=2.0$ and $\lambda=10$ there are no measurements at all, so the upper boundary is propped up by a single grid point and a fitted formula; nobody measured the dependence of that boundary on $d/H$. The analogy with synchronisation is presented and immediately weakened by the author himself: unconstrained, $16/50$ of the fits are degenerate, and with a constraint on meaningful parameters $34/50$ remain usable with a median $R^{2}=0.63$ ([§3.3](#p5-4)). The analogy with cardiac synchronisation that gave the work its vocabulary is rejected outright: the measured exponent is far from the $\nu=1/2$ of the SNIC model ([§2](#p4-5), [§5.2](#p18-1)). And finally, the learning rate was never varied, although the paper's own formula predicts $\lambda_{c}\propto 1/\eta$ — a checkable prediction of its own theory left unchecked.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

An editorial note on the place where this work conveys someone else's result without the authors' caveat. The "details" links from the "Erroneous retellings" sections of the cited cards lead here.

###### mc-2603-05228

**Yıldırım (2603.05228) — the bypassing of grokking ascribed to the absence of weight decay.** The formulation: `"Yıldırım 2026 presents a counter-framing in which grokking is bypassable via a uniform-attention architectural ablation without weight decay"` ([§2](#p3-1)).

Analysis. In Yıldırım these are two **different** interventions with different settings. The uniform-attention ablation is applied to the baseline configurations, and the baseline configurations are trained at $\lambda=1.0$: appendix A lists outright "Weight Decay: $1.0$ (Baselines & Sphere $\lambda=1.0$) / $0.0$ (Bounded Sphere $\lambda=0.0$)", and the caption to figure 2 says that it is precisely the baseline configuration with LayerNorm that "immediately generalizes when data-dependent routing is removed, bypassing grokking". Zero weight decay stands in the third panel of the same figure and pertains to a different intervention — the bounded sphere, in which $\lambda=0$ removes the conflict between the contraction of the norm and the geometric constraint. By joining them in one sentence, the paper ascribes the bypassing of grokking to the absence of weight decay, whereas in Yıldırım the same bypassing is obtained **at** $\lambda=1.0$ and is explained by the structure of the routing rather than by regularisation.

It matters that what suffers is the attribution rather than the conclusion: the author's own caveat ("we do not claim that weight decay is necessary") does not depend on the inaccuracy and remains correct. The paper's other citations of the corpus have been checked selectively and yielded no discrepancies: its accounts of Power et al. 2022, Nanda et al. 2023, Varma et al. 2023, Kumar et al. 2024, Prakash & Martin 2026a, Bi et al. 2026, Truong Xuan Khanh et al. 2026a and 2026b, Tang et al. 2026, Manir & Rupa 2026, Musat 2025 and Tian 2025 match what those works say, hedges included.
