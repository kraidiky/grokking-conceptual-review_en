# Walker et al. 2026 — The Geometric Structure of Models Learning Sparse Data

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Thomas Walker**, **T. Mitchell Roddenberry** (Rice University), **Ahmed Imtiaz Humayun** (Rice University, now Google Research), **Randall Balestriero** (Brown University), **Richard Baraniuk** (Rice University).

arXiv [2605.08464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08464), May 2026 (v2), 27 pages, 7 figures (11 files), 6 tables. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The spline line of Baraniuk's group rather than mechanistic interpretability: where the manifold hypothesis is inapplicable (such regimes are named *sparse* — from grokking to tabular data), models win at the expense of **normal alignment** — the Jacobian at a training point is of rank one and directed along the point itself. It is optimal under a norm constraint (theorem 1), maximally stable (theorem 2), for piecewise-affine networks it is expressed as the alignment of the centroids of the power diagram (the centroid = the row-wise sum of the Jacobian), and its dynamics is tied to the feature-learning regime (theorem 3). Hence **GrokAlign** — a penalty on the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian, which speeds grokking up by 1.5–19.8 times on five settings against Grokfast and OrthoGrad — and **RFAM**, a covariance initialisation for Recursive Feature Machines on tabular data.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — introduced as a particular case of the sparse setting and as the main proving ground of the technique: GrokAlign reaches the grokked state in 100 % of the runs in all five settings (XOR, sparse parity, MNIST under two losses, modular addition) and speeds it up by 1.51–19.76 times with $p<10^{-4}$, whereas Grokfast is insignificant in four settings out of five and OrthoGrad does not grok at all on two ([`p7-10`](#p7-10)). The analysis: "grokking is a prototypical example of a sparse task by definition" is claimed without a check (definition 2 requires the existence of a normally aligned subclass — it is assumed); the threshold of the "grokked state" is set by hand and differs across tasks (95/90/80/80/99 %); modular addition here is a fully connected network with a quadratic activation after Mallinar et al. rather than a transformer, and it is precisely there that the speed-up is smallest (1.60); the strengths of the GrokAlign penalty are tuned per task, whereas the Grokfast pair is chosen on a single seed.

**[Lazy and rich regimes](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — theorem 3, the key one for grokking: *if* the network has already memorised the training data, then a non-zero second derivative of the inner product $\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})}\rangle$ entails the non-stationarity of the NTK, that is, the feature-learning regime; the common explanation of grokking is a sticking in the linear regime with a delayed switch-on of feature learning ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)). The analysis: the bridge from the theorem to the technique rests on reversing the implication — what is proved is "the centroid moves $\Rightarrow$ the NTK moves", while what is applied is "let us force the centroid to move $\Rightarrow$ we get feature learning"; the statement of the theorem says "changing rate of change", the proof says "increasing"; three different explanations (Lyu, Rubin, Kumar) are reduced to the one formulation of "sticking in the linear regime", of which only the lazy-to-rich of Kumar et al. bears on it directly.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — a cheap measurable geometric indicator: the centroid of a cell of the power diagram equals the row-wise sum of the Jacobian, $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\varphi(\boldsymbol{x})}=\boldsymbol{J}_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\top}\mathbf{1}$, and is available through a single Jacobian-vector product for any network, transformers included; in a single-layer transformer on modular arithmetic the alignment of the centroids grows together with the test accuracy reaching its plateau ([`p5-15`](#p5-15)). The analysis: this is a correlation, there is no intervention on the transformer; an essential caveat of appendix B — under the cross-entropy the centroids of a normally aligned solution are *zero* (the indicator degenerates precisely under the most commonly used loss, and it is saved by the intermediate layers of the ResNet); the left panel of figure 1 is obtained on networks trained with GrokAlign already switched on — the indicator is measured in the presence of the very technique that induces it.

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — GrokAlign as a new lever: the penalty $\mathcal{R}_{\sigma}(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}(\|f(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon})-f(\boldsymbol{x})\|_{2}^{2}/\sigma^{2})\to\|\boldsymbol{J}_{\boldsymbol{x}}\|_{F}^{2}$ removes the orthogonal components of the Jacobian that are invisible at the output; appendix E reduces both the direct optimisation of the alignment and the nuclear norm to the same Frobenius norm, all three being computed by the Hutchinson estimator, and the chosen implementation (random vectors in the output space, a single projection) uses the gradients already accumulated in the forward pass ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)). The analysis: the connection of the norm constraint of theorem 1 with weight decay is declared an analogy rather than derived; in the "OrthoGrad, XOR" cell the mean number of epochs is printed over the 20 % of the runs that survived — a selection on the outcome; on MNIST-CE Grokfast and OrthoGrad are slower than the baseline, but their $p$-values are not given.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The strong points — a theory with a cheap technique derived from it and an honest head-to-head.** The chain "optimality (theorem 1) → stability (theorem 2) → the geometry of the centroids (proposition 1) → the dynamics of the features (theorem 3) → GrokAlign" is a case rare for the corpus, in which an accelerator of grokking is derived from a theorem rather than tuned; the comparison with Grokfast and OrthoGrad is on a common bench with a paired t-test and ten seeds; appendix D conscientiously works through the objection about zero Jacobians; the reproducibility is above average (two open repositories, complete tables of hyperparameters, 50 GPU-hours per table 1). What makes the RFAM branch meaningful for the corpus is its connection with Mallinar et al. — grokking outside neural networks.

**The load-bearing weak link — the reversal of the implication.** Theorem 3 proves "the centroid moves under memorised data $\Rightarrow$ a non-stationary NTK", while GrokAlign is justified by the reverse move; the sparseness itself (definition 2 — a property of the pair "data + model") is nowhere checked in the experiments but taken for granted; the right-hand side of the RFAM trade is modest — an accuracy "no worse" is not shown ($p=1.0$), and $\alpha<1$ helps only in a third of the tabular tasks.

**Traces of editing.** Lemma 3 is printed with a corrupted condition ($\mathbb{E}(\boldsymbol{u})$ and $=\boldsymbol{0}=\boldsymbol{I}_{C}$ run together); "normal alignment serve as", "we consistently accelerates", "an extract dimension"; definition 4 introduces $c_{i}$ while the formula carries $c$ without an index; a broken reference to figure 7 in the source.

**How the work will be cited** ("GrokAlign speeds grokking up several times over") — more precisely: on five small settings with fully connected networks and ten seeds a penalty on the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian gives a speed-up of between 1.5 and 19.8 times, with the strength of the penalty tuned per task; and the bridge from the theorem to the technique rests on reversing the implication.
