## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work moves the question from the model onto the **data**: generalisation sets in only when a certain symmetry is broken in the training set ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)). The phenomenon is studied not in a neural network but in a feature-learning kernel, and so the results depend on neither the architecture of a network nor the choice of optimisation ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)).

**[Feature learning](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the working object: a recursive feature machine (RFM), in which a Mahalanobis kernel alternates with an update of the feature matrix by the average gradient outer product (AGOP) ([`p2-7`](#p2-7), [`p2-9`](#p2-9)). It is precisely in the AGOP that the group structure comes to light.

**[Group representations and cosets](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** — the core of the work. The symmetry group of modular addition is found exactly: it is isomorphic to the dihedral group $D_{2p}$ with the rotations $r^{k}(a,b)=(a+k,b-k)$ and the reflections $sr^{k}(a,b)=(b-k,a+k)$ ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)). For an arbitrary abelian group a more general statement is proved: the symmetry group is isomorphic to the generalised dihedral group $\mathrm{Dih}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}\rtimes C_{2}$ ([`p12-11`](#p12-11)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — all four operations over $\mathbb{Z}_{p}$ and, beyond that, addition in abelian groups of the form $C_{5}\times C_{11}$ ([`p2-5`](#p2-5), [`p17-3`](#p17-3)). Subtraction and division are handled through an action with a right inverse, for which a separate representation is written out ([`p13-7`](#p13-7)).

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — a direct objection to the customary reckoning "the more data the better": under a symmetric split, training on $\sim 99\%$ of the data gives a **zero** test accuracy, while moving one random point from the training set into the test set raises it to $98\%$ ([`p4-1`](#p4-1), [`p4-3`](#p4-3), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). Less data means better generalisation, if what is removed breaks the symmetry.

**[Data splits and symmetries](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — the mechanism of the failure is described exactly. If all the fixed points of one reflection are withdrawn from the training set, what remains becomes $H$-invariant with $H=\{\text{id},sr^{k}\}$, all the orbits are complete, and the RFM does not generalise ([`p5-4`](#p5-4)). Moving the fixed points back does not help ([`p5-5`](#p5-5)), nor does moving symmetric pairs ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)) — only an asymmetric withdrawal helps.

**[The kernel regime and the NTK](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the work lies in the current of the objection of Mohamadi et al.: permutation-equivariant kernels on modular arithmetic are incapable of generalising ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)). Here it is shown exactly what such an equivariance limits and how it is removed by the structure of the data.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the learned AGOP matrix is read literally: instead of the full circulant structure obtained under a random split, bands remain in it exactly where the permutation representations of the reflection $sr^{k}$ and of the identity stand ([`p6-9`](#p6-9), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)). This is a rare case in the corpus in which a learned feature coincides with a matrix written out in advance.

**[Freezing a subnetwork](../../concepts/freezing-subnetwork.md)** — the reverse experiment: if the initial feature matrix $M_{0}$ encoding a subgroup $H$ is **imposed**, the RFM "does not leave the symmetry" and generalises exactly to the orbit $\text{Orbit}_{H}(X_{tr})$ ([`p7-4`](#p7-4), [`p7-7`](#p7-7)). The agreement of the prediction with the experiment is complete: 100 % precision and 100 % recall ([`p7-9`](#p7-9)).

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the conclusion for practice: splits with one and the same amount of data give extremely different results, and the reason lies in their group structure rather than in the quantity ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)).

**[From lazy to rich](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — offered as a conjecture for further work: the breaking of the symmetry in an RFM (the passage from a permutation-equivariant kernel to a kernel aligned with the task) may correspond to the transition from the lazy to the rich regime in neural networks ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There are no theorems about grokking itself: both principal claims are called "findings", that is, empirical regularities rather than proved statements ([`p4-2`](#p4-2), [`p7-5`](#p7-5)). Nor is the frame complete: it is admitted that $H$-invariance is not the whole picture, since imposing structure on the features restores the generalisation without breaking the symmetry of the data ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)). And there is no carry-over to tasks with no meaningful symmetry group — of MNIST it is said outright that it has no such group ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)).

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon; [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — the construction of the data taken from there; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the mechanistic analysis of the circuit; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — the efficiency of circuits; [Kumar et al. 2024](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — the transition from the lazy to the rich regime, against which the work sets its own symmetry breaking; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the extension of the phenomenon beyond algebraic tasks; [Zhang et al. 2026](../2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence/2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence.card.md) — the same circular structure, but found in a neural network.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is experimental and algebraic: nine pages of main text and seventeen pages of appendices, where the proofs about groups live.

**The main experimental result is striking and checkable.** Under a symmetric split the RFM gives exactly $0\%$ on the held-out points, while moving **one** random point from the training set into the test set raises the accuracy to $98\%$ ([`p4-3`](#p4-3), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). This refutes the reckoning "more data is better" in the most graphic way: a smaller training set generalises incomparably better.

**The mechanism of the failure is described exactly rather than approximately.** Withdrawing all the fixed points of a reflection makes the training set $H$-invariant: all the orbits are complete, the model learns that reflection alone, and the held-out points do not lie in its orbit ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). Hence the zero on the test set — not a "difficulty" but a direct consequence of the structure.

**Three controls close the obvious objections.** Moving the fixed points back into the training set does not help ([`p5-5`](#p5-5)); moving symmetric pairs does not help ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)); only an asymmetric withdrawal helps ([`p5-7`](#p5-7)). It is thereby the symmetry that matters, not the amount of data or the points themselves.

**The group-theoretic part is proved rather than claimed.** That the symmetry group of the addition of an abelian group is isomorphic to $\mathrm{Dih}(\mathcal{A})$ is proved in full (lemma A.1 and theorem A.2), as is the statement that every point is fixed by exactly one reflection ([`p12-11`](#p12-11), [`p14-12`](#p14-12)). This is a neatness rare in the corpus.

**The strongest thing is a prediction, not an explanation.** Finding 4.1 predicts **by name** which test points will be classified correctly if the initial matrix is set by a subgroup $H$: it is exactly $\text{Orbit}_{H}(X_{tr})$ ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)). The agreement with experiment is complete — 100 % precision and 100 % recall ([`p7-9`](#p7-9)) — and is checked for every reflection under addition and subtraction modulo 29.

**Both principal claims are nevertheless "findings" rather than theorems.** Neither finding 3.1 nor finding 4.1 is proved; what is proved are only the group-theoretic propositions on which they lean. The work itself declares its aim to be "to clarify questions and put forward conjectures that further work will be able to prove" ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)).

**The $H$-invariance frame is admitted to be incomplete.** It is said outright that breaking the $H$-invariance is **not obligatory**, since imposing structure on the model's features restores the generalisation, and different kernels may behave differently on the same split ([`p9-2`](#p9-2)). The "is needed" of the paper's title should therefore be read as "is needed under the ordinary initialisation".

**The difference between the kernels is not explained.** The Gaussian kernel needs a single removed point, the quadratic one needs about a hundred and still stays at $\sim 60\%$ ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)). Why this is so the authors do not know, and they leave the question to further work ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)).

**The range of applicability is narrow, and this is stated.** The results rest on the target function being fully described by its symmetry group: any two examples with the same label are related by an invertible transformation. Most datasets have no such property, and of MNIST it is said that it has no meaningful symmetry group at all ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)).

**The number of runs is stated for one figure only.** The curves of figure 1 are averaged over 5 independent runs ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)); for table 1 and the remaining experiments neither a number of seeds nor a spread is given, even though the main result is precisely the leap from 0 % to 98 %.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the wiki's first work in which a failure to generalise is explained by the **structure of the training split** rather than by the architecture of the model or the course of the optimisation, and in which a prediction is made point by point rather than by a curve. Two contributions are valuable: the experimental demonstration that a smaller dataset may generalise better, and the coincidence of the learned AGOP matrix with the permutation representation of the reflection written out in advance. The price: the principal statements remain empirical findings, the range of tasks is confined to those whose target function is given by a symmetry group, and the difference in the behaviour of the kernels is not explained.
