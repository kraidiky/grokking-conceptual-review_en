## Concepts covered

**[Spectral analysis / SVD](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the work's main contribution to the corpus and the only thing it introduces as an instrument. The signal is taken from the spectrum of the **weight** matrices and collapsed into a **normalized effective rank**: the entropy of the normalized distribution of singular values (Roy & Vetterli 2007) divided by the rank ([eq. 1](#p4-5), explanation — [p4-4](#p4-4)); the scheme of the idea is [fig. 1](#fig-1). This is a distinct member of the family: not the power-law exponent $\alpha$ of HTSR and not the crossing of the two leading values, but a collapse of the whole mass of the spectrum. Beyond the spectrum of **values** the work adds the spectrum of **vectors** — two quantities that are almost absent from the rest of the corpus: the alignment of the singular vectors of neighbouring layers $A(t)_{jk}$ ([eq. 3](#p4-8)) with a scalar summary over the ten top diagonal entries ([eq. 4](#p7-2)), and the coincidence with the end of training $S(t)_{jk}$ ([eq. 5](#p10-1)), where the comparison is made against the final point $T$. It matters for the corpus that the whole set is computed on any architecture without knowledge of the task: the same quantities are taken from VGG-16, a diffusion UNet, a bidirectional LSTM and a twelve-layer transformer ([methodology](#p6-6), [fig. 3](#fig-3)). What the work does not have: no empirical spectral density, no Marchenko — Pastur edge, and no random-matrix model of any kind; the spectrum of activations and representations is not considered at all, and one-dimensional parameters (biases and normalization) are [excluded from the analysis outright](#p6-7).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — taken as a **test bed** rather than as a subject: the setting is needed by the authors because in it the transition is sharply visible, and the observation from it is carried over to tasks where there is no transition ([section 3](#sec-3)). The main observation: "the sudden drop in the validation loss coincides precisely with the onset of low-rank behaviour in the singular values", and the final low-rank solution "gradually grows out of the middle ranks of the model" ([p6-1](#p6-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). The authors' formulation is about a **coincidence in time**, and they themselves note that they have no exact understanding of the connection between rank and generalization and that "high-rank solutions can generalize too" ([p6-2](#p6-2)); the only support is an argument from Occam's razor. What the work does not have: a mechanism, a theory and a causal experiment on grokking — low-rankness is nowhere removed with everything else preserved; the novelty claimed relative to [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) is not the fact of low rank (which, as the authors themselves acknowledge, "follows directly from it" — from Nanda's Fourier decomposition, [p6-1](#p6-1)) but the **transferability** of the measure.

**[The reference grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting of [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) is taken almost unchanged; the one difference is named in a single sentence of the appendix — sinusoidal positional encodings instead of learned ones ([A.6](#p22-5)). The work neither disputes the testbed nor extends it, but uses it as a place where the phenomenon sought is known to be present. Four regimes of one and the same architecture are examined: 30 % of the data without weight decay, 30 % with weight decay 1.0 (grokking proper), 70 % without weight decay (the slingshot case) and 90 % without weight decay ([fig. 2](#fig-2)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the work's only algorithmic task and only operation: addition modulo 97 ([p4-4](#p4-4), [A.6](#p22-6)). There is no sweep over operations, no sweep over moduli and no analysis of the learned algorithm here — modular addition serves only as the place where the fall of the effective rank can be seen next to a sharp transition.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — the work reproduces the setting of [Thilak et al. 2022](../2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md) as the third regime of figure 2 and adds to it an objection about presentation: "plotted on a linear x-axis, the generalization looks far less sudden than in the weight-decay setting" ([p6-1](#p6-1)), and "the original grokking plot in Thilak et al. 2022 looks far more impressive because the x-axis there is on a logarithmic scale" ([A.6](#p22-6)). The reproduction is meanwhile inexact and noted as such by the authors: the hyperparameters are taken from appendix B of the original, but the architecture is the one-layer one from Nanda et al. instead of the two-layer one specified by Thilak. The spectral signature of the slingshot itself (norm jumps, loss spikes) the work does not measure; in its own quantities the slingshot case simply "follows a similar tendency, although the validation loss is fitted gradually" ([fig. 2](#fig-2)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work's central claim and its main contribution to this notion: weight decay acts as an **implicit low-rank regularizer** on top of its explicit role as a norm penalty, and this is shown not in an idealized setting but in four practical ones ([section 5](#sec-5)). The evidence is a grid of coefficients $\{0,0.0001,0.001,0.01,0.1,1.0,10.0\}$ ([A.5](#p22-4)), of which a subset is carried into the figures: the higher the coefficient, the more insistently the effective rank falls, and too large a value leads to a full collapse of the norm ([p8-3](#p8-3), [fig. 6](#fig-6)). An indirect check is made honestly and in advance: at high weight decay the training loss is still low, while the gap between the truncated and the full model closes — exactly what should be expected if the parameters really are becoming lower in rank ([fig. 14](#fig-14)). The mechanism is meanwhile presented outright as an argument ("we will reflect on what the intuition is", [p8-2](#p8-2)): since the leading singular values grow faster and the top directions are more useful for the loss, then under the penalty $\lVert W\rVert_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{R}\sigma_{i}^{2}$ the simplest solution for the network is to drop the rank while keeping the leading values. A separate detail unexplained by the authors: strong weight decay strengthens the alignment of the top singular vectors of neighbouring layers **only in the transformer**, and the authors call this "curious" ([p8-4](#p8-4), [fig. 7](#fig-7)). What the work does not have: an intervention on the weight decay itself. No experiment removing the low-rankness with the norm preserved (or the reverse) is set up, and the authors themselves refrain from the simple conclusion: "it is hard to assert anything as simple as 'lower rank means better generalization', since the minimal rank required for a given task is unknown" ([p8-4](#p8-4)).

**[When regularization is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work takes an intermediate and careful position of which the corpus has little: weight decay is not declared necessary but called **replaceable by a larger volume of data**. At 30 % of the data and zero weight decay neither grokking nor a low-rank solution arises within the whole training budget, but at 90 % of the data, with no regularization at all, generalization sets in and still coincides with the minimization of the effective rank ([p2-2](#p2-2), [p6-1](#p6-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). It matters separately that the difference between generalization and memorization on random labels is obtained **with no weight decay at all** ([p9-1](#p9-1)) — that is, the low rank there is produced by the structure of the data itself. The work formulates no rule for when regularization is obligatory and does not test its observation beyond a single task.

**[Weight norm minimization](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — a direct empirical counterexample to the norm-centric picture, presented by the authors as a "dialogue" with Omnigrok ([Liu et al.](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md)). The generalizing solution at 90 % of the data without weight decay has a **larger** norm and a larger maximal singular value than the grokked solution — and yet both generalize and both are low-rank ([p6-2](#p6-2)). The difference is reduced to the fact that under strong weight decay the small singular values vanish, while without it they do not. The work thereby proposes that the corpus replace the norm with the rank as the quantity that tracks the transition, but does not prove the replacement: the connection between rank and generalization remains a correlation, which the authors say in the same paragraph.

**[The delay law through norm separation](../../concepts/norm-separation-delay-law.md)** — the same observation reads as an upper bound on any theory that derives the length of the delay from the weight norm: two generalizing solutions of one task have norms differing several-fold, while the low-rank behaviour is common to both ([p6-2](#p6-2)). The work derives no temporal regularity, does not measure the time to generalization and does not refer to norm separation — this connection is entirely on the corpus's side.

**[Data fraction / critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the training fraction acts as a second lever alongside weight decay: 30 %, 70 % and 90 % give three qualitatively different outcomes on one and the same architecture ([fig. 2](#fig-2)). At 90 % the low rank holds "from the very beginning of training", that is, the low-rankness there is not acquired but inherited. The work neither seeks nor names a threshold: there are three points, the intermediate fractions are not swept, and it does not measure a critical dataset size.

**[Label noise / random labels](../../concepts/label-noise-random-labels.md)** — a reproduction of the classical experiment of Zhang et al. 2021 on a four-layer MLP on CIFAR10, with one deliberate difference: a constant learning rate instead of the original's schedule, "since their learning-rate schedule might confound the results" ([A.7](#p22-7)). The result is the cleanest in the work ([section 6](#sec-6)): both settings attain zero error, but with true labels the inner layers are low-rank and the alignment is preserved, whereas with random labels the rank is much higher and the alignment appears and then disappears ([p9-1](#p9-1), [fig. 8](#fig-8)). All of this **without weight decay**. The authors' explanation remains a guess ("this may be explained by the shared structure of the true classes of the dataset"), and the work does not sweep intermediate label-noise fractions.

**[Compression of the representation manifold](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the work gives the corpus a variant of compression measured **in the weights, not in the representations**: the confirmation is the SVD-truncation experiment, in which keeping the top half of the singular values of all matrices except the last layer barely damages the model, while keeping the bottom half damages it ([p7-1](#p7-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). The authors stress that the outcome was not foreordained: simultaneous truncation in all layers might have severed the flow of signal between them. The work computes neither the dimensionality of representations nor the intrinsic dimensionality of activations, so its compression cannot be related directly to compression in feature space.

**[Sparse subnetwork / lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the contribution to this notion is independent and unrelated to grokking: global magnitude pruning (the top 5 % of the weights, the mask from the end of training, rewinding to epoch 4 of 164; [A.8](#p22-8)) **preserves the top singular vectors of the final model**, that is, it behaves like an approximate low-rank truncation ([p11-3](#p11-3), [fig. 10](#fig-10)). The control experiment — a random mask of the same per-layer sparsity — does not do this, and after it the network stops learning, although convergence is still far off; the interpretation of the failure the authors explicitly mark as an interpretation: "the mask somehow severed the flow of signal between layers" ([p11-5](#p11-5)). Unlike [Minegishi et al.](../2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks/2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks.card.md), the tickets here are analysed on VGG-16/CIFAR-10 outside grokking, and the conclusion is different — not "a ticket accelerates the transition" but "magnitude pruning is a low-rank truncation".

**[Loss landscape / basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the second half of section 7 and the most rigorous part of the work. Linear mode connectivity (LMC) is measured by the barrier of Neyshabur et al. 2020 ([eq. 6](#p23-1)), and the presence of LMC correlates strongly with the coincidence of the **top** singular vectors at the ends of the branches ([p13-1](#p13-1), [fig. 11](#fig-11)). Both a control experiment and an intervention are set up: branches from **different** trunks at the same branching time and the same data order give neither LMC nor coincidence ([fig. 12](#fig-12)) — hence the top directions depend on the initialization and not only on the architecture and the data; and random perturbations of strength $\lVert\epsilon\rVert=\eta\lVert W\rVert$ at $\eta$ up to 2.5 destroy the LMC and the coincidence of the top singular vectors **simultaneously** ([p13-2](#p13-2), [p24-4](#p24-4), [fig. 13](#fig-13)). The work gives no explanation of the convexity of the basin and says so outright; the picture proposed at the end (interpolation erases the orthogonal lower components) is marked as a conjecture.

**[Initialization scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — a negative result aimed at the theory of deep linear networks: **balanced initialization** ($W^{\top}_{i+1}W_{i+1}=W_{i}W_{i}^{\top}$), on which the conclusions of Arora et al. and Saxe et al. rest, does not hold in large networks either at the start or statically over the course of training — only a weak alignment signal develops in the top ranks ([p7-4](#p7-4), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). Hence the authors' conclusion: "the mechanism of rank minimization may differ from what the theory describes" ([p2-4](#p2-4)). A second, indirect contribution comes from the LMC experiments: the top singular directions depend on the initialization and not only on the architecture and the data ([p13-1](#p13-1)). The initialization scale as a lever (in the Omnigrok sense) the work does not sweep at all.

**[Optimizer](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the optimizer enters the analysis as an explanation of a discrepancy rather than as a subject: the threshold at which a weight decay is already "too large" and leads to a complete collapse of the norm differs across architectures, and the authors attribute this to the use of SGD with momentum for VGG against AdamW for the other three ([p8-3](#p8-3), [fig. 6](#fig-6)). The formulation is hedged ("may be connected with"), and there is no experiment in the work separating optimizer from architecture: each of the four tasks comes with its own optimizer from the literature.

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — the corpus's broadest set of substrates for a single spectral measure: CIFAR-10 classification (VGG-16), MNIST generation (a diffusion UNet), speech recognition on LibriSpeech (a bidirectional LSTM) and language modelling on Wikitext-103 (a twelve-layer transformer) ([methodology](#p6-6)). The hyperparameters are taken from the literature deliberately, and the authors write that this ensures: "any observed correlations most likely reflect common practice rather than a bias introduced by us". An essential caveat for those citing this breadth: **none of the four tasks has grokking** — there is neither a sharp transition nor a memorization phase there, and the fall of the effective rank there is a background tendency of training ([p2-5](#p2-5)).

**[Learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — it enters the card not as a lever of the phenomenon but as the source of an observable kink: the abrupt transition in the middle of VGG training in the maps of effective rank and singular-vector coincidence the authors twice attribute to a tenfold drop of the learning rate by schedule ([fig. 3](#fig-3), [fig. 9](#fig-9), the schedule — [A.1](#p21-4)). This is a caveat useful for the corpus: a kink in a spectral quantity may be a trace of the schedule rather than of an event in training.

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — there is exactly one intervention in the work, and it is set up cleanly: random normal weight perturbations of growing strength destroy the LMC and the coincidence of the top singular vectors simultaneously ([p13-2](#p13-2), [fig. 13](#fig-13)); the output layer (and the input layer in the transformer) is deliberately left unperturbed ([A.10](#p24-4)). Adjoining it are the control experiment with a random mask of the same per-layer sparsity ([p11-5](#p11-5)) and the SVD-truncation experiment ([p7-1](#p7-1)). It matters that all three interventions concern **singular vectors and connectivity**, not grokking: on modular addition not a single intervention is set up.

**[Correlational traps](../../concepts/correlation-traps.md)** — the work itself gives the corpus a rare model of how a correlation should be spoken of, and at the same time an example of its limits. The title observations about rank are formulated as coincidences in time ("coincides", "accompanies"), the necessity of empirical verification is stated outright ([p7-1](#p7-1): "It might have been otherwise… so empirical confirmation is necessary"), and Occam's razor is called what it is — an argument, not evidence ([p6-2](#p6-2)). The trap, though, is that in the four large tasks the phenomenon the observation is attached to is absent altogether, and the variance, by the authors' own admission, is narrow everywhere **except the generalization epoch in the grokking experiments** ([B](#p24-5)) — that is, exactly where the title claim is made. There are three seeds per experiment ([A](#p21-1)).

**[Heavy-tailed self-regularization](../../concepts/heavy-tailed-self-regularization-htsr.md)** — Martin & Mahoney 2020 and 2021 are cited twice, as empirical support for the line about the low-rank properties of the weights ([p4-2](#p4-2)) and as predecessors in the analysis of effective rank in large models ([p6-8](#p6-8)). The work computes neither an empirical spectral density, nor a power-law exponent, nor a Marchenko — Pastur edge: the connection with HTSR is exhausted by the reference.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — mentioned as a neighbouring genre whose shortfall the work seeks to make up: analysis there is "quite bespoke and scales poorly" ([p1-5](#p1-5)), whereas a spectral measure is computed everywhere. The work carries out no mechanistic analysis of its own; the hope that with few ranks per matrix such analysis will become possible is voiced as a hope ([p8-4](#p8-4)) with a reference to Praggastis et al. 2022.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work does not use the language of phase transitions at all: neither an order parameter, nor criticality, nor the term itself is ever named. Moreover, it explicitly notes that in the four practical settings "there is no sudden transition from memorization to generalization there" ([p2-5](#p2-5)), and it partly attributes the sharpness of grokking in Thilak et al. to the logarithmic axis ([A.6](#p22-6)). A phase-transition framing cannot be attributed to the authors — their subject is a smooth tendency, for which the sharp case merely serves as a convenient place to observe it.

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — a mention only, in the survey: "Some hold that grokking is explained by a transition from the kernel … to the rich … regime", with references to Jacot et al. 2018, Atanasov et al. 2023, [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) and Mohamadi et al. 2023 ([p3-6](#p3-6)). The work attempts neither to relate its measure to this transition nor to measure it.

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — mentioned twice and both times as an adjacent phenomenon to which the view through spectral dynamics "perhaps" ought to be carried over as well ([p3-6](#p3-6), [p14-3](#p14-3)), with a reference to [Davies et al.](../2303.06173.unifying-grokking-and-double-descent/2303.06173.unifying-grokking-and-double-descent.card.md). There are no measurements of double descent in the work.

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — mentioned as an example of a more abstract object which, in the authors' view, lacks resolution and predictive power compared with a direct analysis of the weights ([p1-5](#p1-5)); the Gram matrix stands alongside it there. This is an evaluative remark in the introduction, not a result: the NTK is not computed in the work.

## Points we could not reconcile

**The novelty claim in the survey contradicts an admission in section 3.** Section 2.1 ends with the words that none of the works on grokking listed "explicitly considered the connection with rank, which we do in section 3" ([p3-6](#p3-6)). Section 3 itself says the opposite: "the informed reader will note that Nanda et al. 2023 previously showed that the particular solution found in modular addition is a low-rank Fourier decomposition, so our observations about low-rank weights follow directly from it" ([p6-1](#p6-1)). Nanda et al. are not in the list of section 2.1, so formally there is no contradiction — but a reader taking a priority claim from the work should know that the authors themselves deny the novelty of the fact and locate it in the **transferability** of the measure.

**"A low-rank solution apparently does not develop" is printed without the caveat about the data fraction.** In one and the same paragraph it is said that without weight decay "the grokking phenomenon is absent, and a low-rank solution apparently does not develop", and immediately that "when using 90 % of the data and no weight decay, generalization still coincides with the minimization of the effective rank" ([p6-1](#p6-1)). The first is true only for 30 % of the data. The hedge "apparently" is preserved by the authors, but when the sentence is cited out of the context of the paragraph it reads as a general claim about weight decay, which it is not.

**The categorical bullets of the summary diverge from the caveats of the body.** The list of contributions in the introduction gives four unconditional statements, including "weight decay implicitly acts as a low-rank regularizer" and "generalizing solutions are lower in rank than memorizing ones" ([p3-4](#p3-4)); the abstract says in the same tone that "weight decay strengthens this bias beyond its role as a norm regularizer" ([p1-2](#p1-2)). The body of the work is far more cautious: "we currently have no exact understanding of how the low-rank behaviour relates directly to generalization, and it may not always hold — high-rank solutions can generalize too" ([p6-2](#p6-2)), and "it is hard to assert anything as simple as 'lower rank means better generalization', since the minimal rank required for a given task is unknown" ([p8-4](#p8-4)). One should cite the body.

**The caption of figure 5 and the body of section 4.3 describe the alignment in opposite directions.** The caption says that a slight alignment in the top ranks is visible "shortly after the beginning of training, but it washes out over time" ([fig. 5](#fig-5)); the body of the same section concludes that "a weak alignment signal in the top ranks develops" ([p7-4](#p7-4)). One describes the decay of an initial alignment, the other its growth. This matters because it is precisely on the weakness of this signal that the work's negative conclusion about balanced initialization rests: the direction of its development changes whether the observation counts as a residue of the initialization or a trace of training. (This inconsistency is not in the claims file.)

**Traces of unproofread text.** Appendix A.4 declares the 160-million-parameter Pythia configuration to have "a model dimension of 768 … and a hidden dimension of 768" ([A.4](#p22-1)), whereas that configuration's MLP hidden dimension is four times larger. The barrier formula ([eq. 6](#p23-1)) is printed with unbalanced parentheses and with a free $\alpha$ in the second term, outside the scope of the $\max$. In A.6 "W perform addition modulo 97" has been left in ([A.6](#p22-6)). All three places look the same in the PDF, so these are the paper's typos rather than the conversion's.

## What is shown, what is conjectured and what is untested

The work is arranged so that its strong and weak halves lie in different sections, and a reader taking a number from it should know which one it comes from.

**Shown by experiment, with control comparisons.** The effective rank decreases in almost all parameters of all four architectures over the course of training ([p6-8](#p6-8), [fig. 3](#fig-3)). SVD truncation: keeping the top half of the singular values barely damages the model, keeping the bottom half damages it, and the authors state outright: "It might have been otherwise" ([p7-1](#p7-1), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). Balanced initialization does not hold in large networks — a negative result against the theory of deep linear networks ([p7-4](#p7-4), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). The grid of weight-decay coefficients gives a monotone picture of the rank falling, backed by an indirect check through the closing of the gap between the truncated and the full model ([p8-3](#p8-3), [fig. 6](#fig-6), [fig. 14](#fig-14)). Random labels against true ones: different rank and different alignment at the same zero error and **without** weight decay ([p9-1](#p9-1), [fig. 8](#fig-8)). Magnitude pruning preserves the top singular vectors, a random mask of the same per-layer sparsity does not ([p11-3](#p11-3), [p11-5](#p11-5), [fig. 10](#fig-10)). LMC correlates with the coincidence of the top singular vectors, and this is backed by the control experiment with different trunks ([p13-1](#p13-1), [fig. 11](#fig-11), [fig. 12](#fig-12)) and by the work's only genuine intervention — perturbations that destroy both the LMC and the coincidence simultaneously ([p13-2](#p13-2), [fig. 13](#fig-13)).

**Declared to be argument by the authors themselves.** The mechanism by which weight decay drops the rank is set out as "we will reflect on what the intuition about the mechanism is" ([p8-2](#p8-2)) — there is no experiment separating the fall of the norm from the fall of the rank. The failure of the random mask is interpreted ("we interpret this as evidence that the mask somehow severed the flow of signal between layers", [p11-5](#p11-5)) and not tested. The picture in which interpolation with shared top components erases only the orthogonal lower ones is introduced with the words "we conjecture" ([p13-3](#p13-3)). The connection between rank and generalization rests on Occam's razor and is called that outright ([p6-2](#p6-2)).

**Untested, and the authors acknowledge it.** Three random seeds per experiment, with the confidence intervals narrow everywhere **except the generalization epoch in the grokking experiments** ([A](#p21-1), [B](#p24-5)) — that is, the variance is greatest exactly where the title claim about grokking is made. One-dimensional parameters (biases, normalization) are excluded from the analysis, although one-dimensional parameters, by the authors' own admission, "may be especially crucial (normalization in particular)" ([p6-7](#p6-7), [B](#p24-5)). Alignment across residual connections was not computed because of the combinatorial growth; the largest scale is a twelve-layer transformer ([B](#p24-5)). For convolutional layers the SVD is taken of the flattened tensor $(c_{\text{out}},c_{\text{in}}\cdot h\cdot w)$, that is, it is **not** the SVD of the full feature-map transformation ([A.1](#p21-4)), and the alignment is computed only at the central spatial position ([A.1](#p21-5)).

**What does not follow from what is shown (editor's note).** The title link "spectral dynamics unifies these phenomena" rests on the commonality of the **measure**, not of the phenomenon: in the four large tasks there is no grokking at all, and the authors write this themselves — "we do not see as obvious a rank minimization as with grokking, and there is no sudden transition from memorization to generalization there" ([p2-5](#p2-5)). The fall of the effective rank on VGG or the LSTM is a background tendency of training, not a marker of delayed generalization, and the work gives no right to carry over onto it the conclusions obtained on modular addition.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the corpus works that cite this one (the `original/` folders of all `papers/` directories were surveyed; six works cite it in the body text, one more only in the bibliography). Each entry says what exactly is distorted and leads to the authorial paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. The citing works have no cards yet, so there are no "details" links here; the candidates for `mc-2408-11804` anchors have been passed to the coordinator.

**"The fall of the effective rank coincides with the grokking transition" (over-extending, later contested).** The authors' formulation is about a coincidence in time and was taken on one setting with three seeds ([p6-1](#p6-1), [fig. 2](#fig-2)); in the citing works it has moved into the category of the established and become a support for quantitative laws. A direct objection came from `2607.06639`: in an analysis with long training the rank compression of the embeddings does not coincide with the accuracy transition but lags behind it by an amount of the order of the grokking time itself; those same authors note separately that they share with the present work only the robust part — that a low-rank structure accompanies generalization — and not a general law about the norm. The authors' own caveat, which removes part of the force from their own observation, the citing works usually omit: the seed variance is narrow everywhere **except the generalization epoch in the grokking experiments** ([B](#p24-5)).

**"Generalization corresponds to smaller values of the effective rank" (over-extending).** In this form the claim is attributed to the work in `2605.28975`, where it is said that the authors track the effective rank and **conclude** that generalization corresponds to smaller values of it. There is no conclusion of this kind in the work: it says outright that "we currently have no exact understanding of how the low-rank behaviour relates directly to generalization, and it may not always hold — high-rank solutions can generalize too" ([p6-2](#p6-2)), and that "it is hard to assert anything as simple as 'lower rank means better generalization', since the minimal rank required for a given task is unknown" ([p8-4](#p8-4)). In the same `2605.28975`, the four large tasks are called pretraining tasks, whereas three of the four (CIFAR-10 classification, MNIST generation, speech recognition) are trained from scratch for the end task ([methodology](#p6-6)).

**The work is split into two and supplied with someone else's author list (erroneous).** In `2606.04405` it appears twice — as `[9]` (a workshop version) and as `[10]` (the present preprint), with both entries assigned the author list "D. Yunis, K. Garg, M. Braverman, and S. Kakade", which has nothing to do with the present work; its authors are Yunis, Patel, Wheeler, Savarese, Vardi, Livescu, Maire, Walter. The substantive retellings there are meanwhile close to the text: both "the timing of generalization transitions in grokking closely tracks the emergence of low-rank solutions" and "weight decay strengthens the implicit low-rank bias across diverse architectures" ([p2-2](#p2-2), [section 5](#sec-5)) — the warning here concerns the bibliographic record, not the reading.

An exemplary formulation for comparison is in `2601.19791` ([Xu, Vardi, Safran 2026](../2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression/2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression.card.md)): `"Yunis et al. (2024) showed empirically that grokking is connected to rank minimization, namely, that the sudden drop in the test loss coincides precisely with the onset of low-rank behavior in the singular values of the weight matrices."` Both "empirically" and "coincides" are preserved, that is, exactly what the work asserts; the one divergence is that in the authors' text it is the **validation**, not the test, loss that drops ([p6-1](#p6-1)).

Softer echoes not warranting separate entries: `2504.03162` (a mention among works giving "a more unifying view") and `2506.05718` (a mention of "rank minimization" in a list of readings of grokking).
