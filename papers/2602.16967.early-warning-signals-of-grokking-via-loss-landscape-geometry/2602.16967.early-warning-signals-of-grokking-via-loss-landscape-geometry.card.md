## Concepts covered

**[The loss landscape and basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — the work carries over to two new tasks a quantity built as a local probe of the landscape: the commutator defect is, to first order in $\eta$, the Lie bracket of the gradient vector fields and therefore vanishes wherever the landscape is locally flat, while a large value of it means that "the order of the gradient updates matters — the loss landscape has developed a curvature structure violating path independence" ([`p4-8`](#p4-8)). The picture as a whole is inherited: grokking is a protracted holding on a low-dimensional manifold, an accumulation of transverse barriers and an escape into a generalising solution ([`p20-2`](#p20-2)). What is new here is not the mechanism but its carry-over: the same three stages are traced on an encoder–decoder and on a causal decoder. A separate observation is the guess about shallow basins: at 50 training sequences "the loss landscape may have shallow basins into which the optimiser at large step sizes is able to enter and from which it is able to leave" ([`p15-4`](#p15-4)) — this is a conjecture; there is no measurement of basin depth in the work.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work's chief contribution to this notion is that the precursor quantity is furnished with **a decision rule and a predictable window**. The rule is inherited from Xu 2026b: the onset of the defect is taken to be the first step at which the median exceeds simultaneously ten times the baseline level and an absolute floor of 20 ([`p7-6`](#p7-6)). From the window an observation protocol is made: compute the defect every 100–500 steps (4 forward–backward passes per measurement), wait for a sustained spike, after which the generalisation "will probably follow within a predictable window" ([`p21-1`](#p21-1)). What the work does not contain: an answer to the question **whether** grokking will occur. The measure answers the question "when", taking the "whether" as given — and in two runs at $\lambda=1$ in which no grokking occurred the behaviour of the defect is not described at all (see below).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work brings to three the number of task families in which the lead time obeys a power law with a superlinear exponent: 1.27 (modular arithmetic), 1.18 (SCAN), 1.13 (Dyck) — "All three exponents … exceed one" ([`p14-2`](#p14-2)). The authors use the language of phase transitions sparingly and in one place: the sharp closing of the gap between the lower update modes is called that which "also marks the phase transition" ([`p19-3`](#p19-3)). The work introduces no order parameter and makes no finite-size analysis; the ordering of events (equation 12) is an empirical chronology from six runs rather than a theory of the transition.

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the scheme of four interventions is taken from Xu 2026b entire: a one-off kick along the commutator's eigenvector (1A-kick), a continuous injection of noise orthogonal to the gradient (1A-noise), a projection of the gradient removing its orthogonal component (1B-project), and a penalty on that component (1B-penalty) ([`p12-6`](#p12-6)). The chief novelty is a **divergence of sufficiency across tasks**: on Dyck both amplifying interventions accelerate; on SCAN the mild one accelerates while the harsh one destroys the training; and on modular arithmetic amplification gives nothing — from which a "spectrum of causal responsiveness" is built ([`p13-4`](#p13-4), [`p13-5`](#p13-5)). What the work does not contain: the number of runs per condition — every result is given as a single number per condition, whereas the baseline band on SCAN is spread by almost a factor of two, and the magnitudes of the interventions are never named in the text, although the dose response is declared monotone.

**[Gradient orthogonality](../../concepts/orthogonal-gradient-perp-grad.md)** — here the work gives the corpus its strongest claim and the only one to hold up on all three tasks: "in all three tasks suppressing the orthogonal gradient flow delays or prevents grokking, establishing the *necessity* of transverse-curvature dynamics as a universal finding" ([`p13-6`](#p13-6)). The suppression acts in two ways — by projection and by a penalty ([`p12-6`](#p12-6)) — and on SCAN the mild way proves the stronger: 1B-penalty prevents grokking altogether, whereas 1B-project only delays it. Note that the necessity is shown for *suppressing* interventions rather than for the observed quantity: the work gives no relation between the observed magnitude of the orthogonal flow and the onset of grokking in natural, unperturbed runs.

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — section 8 gives a second view, independent of the commutator defect, and **revises the chronology of the preceding work**. The hypothesis checked: a near-degeneracy of the top singular values of $W_{Q}$ creates an orientational instability that resolves when one mode begins to dominate ([`p15-6`](#p15-6)). The result turned out to be the reverse of the expectation: "the spectral gap is small at the grokking step and goes on growing long afterwards", and the separation of the dominant mode is "a *consequence* of the grokking … rather than its precondition" ([`p16-9`](#p16-9)), whereas in modular arithmetic $\sigma_{1}\gg\sigma_{2}$ was placed before the grokking ([`p17-6`](#p17-6)). The eigenvalue gap of the Gram matrix is reproduced besides: $g_{23}$ falls by 33$\times$ (Dyck) and 43$\times$ (SCAN) at the transition ([`p19-2`](#p19-2)), and the clearest qualitative signature is named as a topological difference of the phase portraits — the grokking trajectories traverse the whole phase space while the controls stay put ([`p18-2`](#p18-2), [`fig-16`](#fig-16)).

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — the learning rate serves here as the chief control quantity: it is precisely by a sweep of it that both power-law fits are obtained, and from $\alpha>1$ it is inferred that the defect "is most useful exactly when it is needed most — at small learning rates, at which the training lasts longest" ([`p21-2`](#p21-2)). Two observations of the work stand apart from this line. First: on Dyck the grokking time is **non-monotone** in the learning rate — at $\eta=10^{-2}$ it is larger than at $\eta=10^{-3}$, which was not observed in modular arithmetic with its $t_{\text{grok}}\propto\eta^{-1}$ ([`p9-4`](#p9-4)). Second: at $\eta=10^{-2}$ the accuracy "oscillates violently instead of passing cleanly from low to high", giving 80–87 crossings of the threshold 0.98 per run ([`p15-1`](#p15-1)). There is no phase diagram in the learning rate and no analysis of the damping regimes of Xu 2026b here.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the setting rests on a **single** value $\lambda=1.0$: "a strong regularisation is necessary in order to induce grokking within feasible training budgets" ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)). The role of weight decay is refined in section 8 in an unexpected direction: the spectral rank reduction is "driven by weight decay acting on an already generalised model rather than being a precondition of the generalisation" ([`p20-5`](#p20-5)). Runs at $\lambda=0$ serve as the sole control — in them the ratio of the executive to the random stays at about 1.0 throughout the training ([`p12-3`](#p12-3)). What the work does not contain: a sweep of $\lambda$; whether the exponent $\alpha$ depends on the strength of the regularisation the authors expressly call an open question.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work is devoted entire to **leaving** the canonical proving ground and enumerates the axes of departure explicitly: the architecture, the input domain, the output type and the construction of the dataset ([`p2-6`](#p2-6)), and in the summary the architectures, the domains, the output types, dataset sizes from 50 to 4,704 and model sizes from 150 thousand to 1.5 million parameters ([`p14-6`](#p14-6)). An essential caveat on the corpus's side: the defining feature of the proving ground of Power et al. — a plateau at a saturated training accuracy — is never measured here, and the training accuracy is not given in the paper at all. What is formally shown is a late generalisation with a precursor; to call this grokking in the sense of Power et al., the measurement missing from the work is required.

**[Compression of the representation manifold](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — here the work places an **honest negative result**, and it is more valuable than its positive ones. On Dyck the turn of PC1 falls long before the grokking (a peak at step 600 against a grokking at 2,900, a lead of 2,300 steps) ([`p10-3`](#p10-3)), and on SCAN "PC1 goes on *growing* through the grokking transition and after it" ([`p11-2`](#p11-2)). The conclusion is stated outright: the thinning of PC1 "is *not* a universal precursor of grokking" ([`p11-3`](#p11-3), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)). The reading of the divergence — that the encoder compresses first while the decoder's diversity is masked in the joint measurement — is given by the authors as a conjecture and is not checked.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the training is divided into four phases (early, memorisation, pre-grokking, post-grokking), and the integrability decomposition shows that the ratio of the executive to the random jumps to 2–3$\times$ **precisely** in the pre-grokking phase, staying at about 1.0 in the early phase and in the memorisation phase ([`p12-1`](#p12-1), [`fig-8`](#fig-8)). This would give the memorisation phase a measurable feature distinguishing it from the pre-grokking window — but figure 8 does not bear out the textual claim (see "What is shown and what is conjectured"). A caveat: the phase boundaries are not defined operationally in the work, and the memorisation phase itself is named without the measurement of training accuracy on which that name usually rests.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — two measurements. First: the PC1 concentration in the attention projections is 70–85 % against 40–60 % in the MLP weights, uniformly on SCAN and Dyck ([`p11-8`](#p11-8), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)). Second: a head-level analysis on Dyck gives a simultaneous alignment of all four heads, whence the conclusion that "the mechanism is not peculiar to an individual head — it operates at the level of the whole layer" ([`p18-4`](#p18-4)). The work does not look for head specialisation and isolates no circuits.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the contribution to the notion itself is made on the **measurement-methodology** side. Grokking is defined operationally: the first step at which the test accuracy exceeds the threshold 0.98 for three consecutive measurements ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)). The work shows why the sustainment is needed: on Dyck at $\eta=10^{-2}$ seed 42 first crosses 0.98 at step 900 but reaches a sustained grokking only by 11,900, so that "Without a sustained-accuracy criterion this run would look as though it grokked 13$\times$ faster than it actually does" ([`p15-3`](#p15-3), [`fig-13`](#fig-13)). The authors call this regime *unstable grokking*. A caveat for the corpus: a definition through test accuracy alone does not distinguish grokking from late generalisation, and in section 8 the threshold silently changes to 0.95 with no sustainment (see "Inconsistencies").

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the data regime is chosen as a lever rather than as a subject of study: on Dyck 50 training sequences are taken against 5,000 test ones, and the authors write outright that "Such a large ratio of the test set to the training set amplifies the grokking effect" ([`p5-8`](#p5-8)). On SCAN 2,048 pairs are selected out of about 14,000. The work measures neither a dependence on the data fraction nor a critical dataset size — both tasks are taken at one point along that axis.

Cards of corpus papers: [Xu 2026b](../2602.16746.low-dimensional-and-transversely-curved-optimization-dynamics-in-grokking/2602.16746.low-dimensional-and-transversely-curved-optimization-dynamics-in-grokking.card.md) — the direct predecessor, from which everything but the two tasks is taken; [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the setting; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — circuits in the Fourier basis, named in the explanation of modular arithmetic's rigidity; [Murty et al. 2023](../2305.18741.grokking-of-hierarchical-structure-in-vanilla-transformers/2305.18741.grokking-of-hierarchical-structure-in-vanilla-transformers.card.md) — cited as a warrant for taking compositional generalisation; [Notsawo et al. 2023](../2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens/2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens.card.md) — the corpus's nearest work in design, not cited in the paper; [Xu 2026, multi-task geometry](../2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure/2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure.card.md) — a parallel work by the same author referring to this one.

**[Grokking time](../../concepts/grokking-time.md)** — the moment of the transition is defined with a guard against a chance crossing of the threshold: the first step at which the test accuracy stays above the threshold for three consecutive measurements is counted ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)).
## Points we could not reconcile

**How many Dyck runs gave a usable lead — 14 or 15.** Section 5.2 says: of 16 runs 15 grok and 14 "exhibit both grokking and a detectable onset of the defect with a positive lead time" ([`p8-10`](#p8-10)); the fit is likewise made "over all 14 runs". But contribution № 1 in the introduction asserts "15 of 15 grokking runs on Dyck" ([`p2-8`](#p2-8)), the caption of figure 4 speaks of fifteen runs with a positive lead, and [table 4](#tab-4) contains exactly 15 rows, in each of which the lead time is entered and is positive. The fifteenth run is in the table but not in the fit; which of them exactly is discarded and why is not said.

**The exponent 1.132 is given twice in different senses.** In section 5.1 it is the result of a fit over the means of five learning rates on SCAN: "$\alpha=1.132$, $R^{2}=0.995$" ([`p8-9`](#p8-9)). In section 5.2 the same number is the exponent of the full fit over 14 runs on Dyck (equation 10). The coincidence to three decimal places in two independent fits on two different tasks is nowhere stipulated, and citing the number 1.132 without naming the task is ambiguous.

**The comparison baseline for the interventions does not agree with the work's own table.** Section 5.4.1 reports that the SCAN baseline runs (3 seeds, $\eta=10^{-4}$) grok at 9,500–17,500 steps ([`p12-7`](#p12-7)), whereas [table 2](#tab-2) for the same three seeds at the same learning rate gives 6,500, 7,000 and 7,200. Not one of the three tabulated runs falls in the band declared. The intervention runs are retrained "from the same initialisation", so a difference of settings does not explain the gap.

**The arithmetic of the acceleration does not agree with the baseline declared.** It is said that 1A-noise groks at ${\sim}8{,}500$ steps, "which is ${\sim}32\%$ faster than the fastest baseline seed", and that 1B-project groks at ${\sim}28{,}500$ steps, "${\sim}2.3\times$ slower than the fastest baseline" ([`p12-8`](#p12-8)). Against the fastest seed of the declared band (9,500) this comes to 10.5 % and 3.0$\times$. Both figures given are consistent with each other at a baseline of about 12,400–12,500 steps — that is, at the **mean** of the three baseline seeds rather than at the fastest. The comparison is apparently made against the mean but is described as against the fastest.

**Section 8 silently changes the definition of grokking.** Everywhere grokking is defined as an accuracy $\geq 0.98$ sustained for three measurements ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)); the caption of [table 8](#tab-8) defines it as "the first step at which the test accuracy is $\geq 0.95$" — with no sustainment. For Dyck seed 42 this gives a grokking step of 600 against 2,900 in [table 4](#tab-4), almost five times earlier. All the lead times of section 8 are computed on that other scale and are not comparable with the leads of section 5; and the PC1 lead of 2,300 steps there ([`p10-3`](#p10-3)) is likewise computed relative to step 2,900 rather than 600.

**Figure 8 does not bear out the claim of section 5.3.3.** The text says that the ratio of the executive to the random jumps to 2–3$\times$ in the pre-grokking phase "for the weight and $\Delta W$ bases", staying at about 1.0 in the early phase and in the memorisation phase ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)). In [figure 8a](#fig-8) all three panels at $\lambda=1.0$ are captioned "(no grok)", and the four phase points are taken at the fixed steps 2000 / 8000 / 16000 / 20000, whereas [table 4](#tab-4) gives for those same three seeds at $\eta=10^{-3}$ groks at steps 2,900, 4,400 and 7,500: the point called "pre-grokking" lies after the grokking in all three runs. By the values the pattern holds on one seed only too: 2–3$\times$ in both bases at once is given only by seed 137; in seed 42 the $\Delta W$ basis stays at about 1.3$\times$; and in seed 2024 both bases at that point are **below** the random level. In the summary [figure 8b](#fig-8) the means over the three seeds at the "pre-grokking" point are about 1.85 (weight basis) and 1.44 ($\Delta W$ basis) at a spread crossing 1.0, and the only basis falling in the band 2–3$\times$ is the gradient one, which stands at about 2$\times$ already in the early phase — that is, precisely not "at about 1.0".

**The ordering of equation 12 is violated in the work's own table.** Equation 12 places the SGD defect spike **before** the peak of the matrix commutator, and section 8.8 calls this two-stage structure uniform "for both datasets and all 6 grokking runs" ([`p20-1`](#p20-1)). In [table 8](#tab-8) for SCAN seed 137 the SGD spike falls at step 1,700 and the commutator peak at 1,500: the order is reversed. Separately, for Dyck seed 42 the commutator peak (600) coincides with the grokking step (600), so the claim of section 9.1 about a peak "before the grokking (6/6 runs)" holds only in the non-strict form $\leq$, in which it is indeed written in section 8.4 ([`p17-4`](#p17-4)).

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`); each mark has been rechecked against the original. The work is arranged as a check of transferability: the measurement apparatus, the thresholds and the intervention scheme are taken from Xu 2026b unchanged, and what is new is two tasks, two architectures and what diverged on them.

**What is shown reliably is the lead.** On SCAN all 11 runs with a detectable defect onset have a positive lead ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)); on Dyck all 14 (or 15, see above). That is 25–26 runs of the 27 that grok on two tasks, and the result in itself is not in dispute. What is in dispute is what is inferred from it.

**The superlinearity is not self-consistent at the work's own extreme points.** The lead fraction cannot by definition exceed one, and so $\Delta t\leq t_{\text{grok}}$. Yet the SCAN fit at the slowest run gives $0.149\cdot 115{,}000^{1.18}\approx 1.40\cdot 10^{5}$ at $t_{\text{grok}}=115{,}000$, and the Dyck fit $0.239\cdot 68{,}000^{1.132}\approx 7.06\cdot 10^{4}$ at $t_{\text{grok}}=68{,}000$. Both predict the impossible. The exponent $\alpha>1$ is therefore a property of a finite stretch of the sweep rather than a law extensible to the right; for the practical protocol ([`p21-1`](#p21-1)) this matters, because the window is predicted by precisely that formula.

**The authors themselves showed that the exponent depends on the coverage, and this weakens the conclusion.** Section 5.2 honestly reports that at three learning rates $\alpha\approx 0.91$ came out, "looking nearly linear", and only the addition of three more raised the exponent above one ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)). This is recorded as an argument for dense coverage, but it also reads as an estimate of the stability of $\alpha$ itself: the exponent varied from 0.91 to 1.13 within one task.

**False positives were not measured.** At $\lambda=1$ no grokking occurred in two runs — on SCAN in seed 137 at $\eta=10^{-3}$ and on Dyck in seed 137 at $\eta=3\times 10^{-3}$ (both absent from [tables 2](#tab-2) and [4](#tab-4) despite the full sweep declared in [`p6-6`](#p6-6)); and in one further run (SCAN, seed 2024, $\eta=10^{-3}$) there was a grokking but no defect onset was detected. Nothing is said about the defect's behaviour in these runs. The single "no false positives" is computed on the controls at $\lambda=0$ ([`p17-4`](#p17-4)), in which the absence of grokking is built into the setting. The discrimination between a grokking and a non-grokking setting is thereby unchecked by the work — which accords with the direct admission of Xu 2026b that the geometric signals do **not** add up into a predictor of whether grokking will occur.

**The observation protocol is closed on itself.** Step 3 of the protocol predicts the window by the law $\Delta t\propto t_{\text{grok}}^{\alpha}$ ([`p21-1`](#p21-1)), but $t_{\text{grok}}$ is precisely what is to be predicted. Inverting the law into an observable form — expressing $t_{\text{grok}}$ through a measured $t_{\text{onset}}$ — the authors do not attempt, although at $\alpha>1$ such an inversion is unique.

**The priority claim is made with no check against the acceleration line.** Section 9.2 says that the work gives "one of the first task-independent, geometric early-warning signals of delayed generalisation" ([`p20-6`](#p20-6)). The corpus's nearest work in design — [Notsawo et al. 2023](../2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens/2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens.card.md), predicting grokking from the early dynamics of the loss — is not cited at all; nor are the works intervening in the gradient in order to accelerate grokking ([Grokfast](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md), [egalitarian gradient descent](../2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking/2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking.card.md)), with which the accelerating half of the causal contribution stands in direct proximity.

**The integrability decomposition is the work's weakest place, and the weakness is visible in its own figure.** This is contribution № 4 of the six declared, and the text formulates it categorically ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)), whereas [figure 8](#fig-8) gives something else: the pattern "2–3$\times$ in both bases in the pre-grokking phase at 1.0 in the others" is reproduced on one seed of three, the means over the three seeds do not reach the band 2–3$\times$ and are not separated from the random level by the spread, and the phase points are taken at fixed steps, of which "pre-grokking" falls at step 16,000 at groks of 2,900–7,500. The details are in "Inconsistencies". Whence a consequence for the corpus: of the three supports on which the conclusion about the structuredness of the non-commutativity rests (a full $\rho\approx 1$, the ratio to the random, the phase attribution), only the first, inherited one is sound here, and the two new ones are not.

**The sufficiency diverged from the preceding work, and an explanation is given but no check.** In Xu 2026b sufficiency was **rejected**: kicks along the commutator of amplitudes up to 500 gradient-step norms accelerated the grokking in none of 27 runs. Here the identically named intervention 1A-kick accelerates the grokking twofold on Dyck ([`p13-2`](#p13-2)). The divergence is explained by a property of the task — the simplicity of the counting mechanism ([`p13-5`](#p13-5)) — but the work does not show that the intervention scheme stayed the same in strength and mode of application: the magnitudes of the interventions are named identically in neither of the two papers.

**The prediction of section 8.7 was not borne out and is declared consistent.** The gap ratio $R$ gave moderate values ($R\approx 2$–6) "with none of the sharp spikes predicted by the thesis", and this is immediately explained by the thesis's own caveat about dimension and the BBP threshold ([`p19-4`](#p19-4)). The caveat may be correct, but there is nothing in this setting with which to check it: the prediction and its failure are described by one and the same frame. The section is separately weakened by the choice of window: at $W=3$ (Dyck) the quantity $g_{23}=\sigma_{2}^{2}-\sigma_{3}^{2}$ is the gap between the second and the **last** singular value, and $k^{*}$ can take only two values, so a universal $k^{*}=1$ at such a window is all but forced ([`p18-9`](#p18-9)).

**The spectral part rests on 27 figures that are not in the paper.** Section 8.8 reports that they are "available in the supplementary material" ([`p20-1`](#p20-1)); no supplementary material is attached to the preprint, and the numbers given in the text cannot be checked against it.

**The work's own limitations are listed fully and honestly.** One seed at the extreme learning rates, a single weight-decay value, models of 2–3 layers and 150 thousand – 1.5 million parameters, artificial tasks only, a hand-picked $10\times$ threshold, and an unverified status on large language models ([`p21-7`](#p21-7)). The list closes almost every objection to sections 5–6; it does not close the fact that the training accuracy is never given and that false positives were not measured.

**The bibliography requires care when citing.** The label "Xu [2026a]" stands in the reference list **twice** — at `arXiv:2602.10496` and at `arXiv:2603.28964` — while the single reference "Xu 2026a" in the body (the Gram-matrix frame, section 8.7) points by sense to the second of them. The author's own preprint "Xu 2026b" is mentioned 55 times in the body against a list of 13 entries, of which 3 are works by the same author; and the central "Low-dimensional and transversely curved" has no arXiv number in the list at all (it is `2602.16746`). Yun et al. and Lyu et al. stand in the list and never appear in the body.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. The section is built only from formulations encountered; each entry says what exactly is distorted and gives references to the authors' paragraphs where the lost caveat stands. Not all the citing works have cards as yet, so detailed "Erroneous citations" entries do not exist everywhere.

**"Amplifying non-commutativity accelerates grokking across all task families" (widening).** In a parallel work by the same author — [Xu 2026, multi-task geometry](../2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure/2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure.card.md) — this paper is retold by the formula `"amplifying non-commutativity accelerates grokking by 32–60% across task families, while suppressing orthogonal gradients delays or prevents it"`. The second half of the sentence is exact and is the chief surviving result ([`p13-6`](#p13-6)). The first half widens: amplification accelerates on **two** families of three, and on modular arithmetic, included in the paper's own summary [table 6](#tab-6), it is expressly named as having no effect ([`p13-5`](#p13-5)); and the upper bound "60 %" does not occur in the paper under analysis at all — the values given here are ${\sim}32\%$ on SCAN and ${\sim}50\%$ and ${\sim}40\%$ on Dyck ([`p12-8`](#p12-8), [`p13-2`](#p13-2)). Exactly the difference for whose sake the "spectrum of causal responsiveness" was built disappears in the retelling.

**"Tracking the trajectory's principal components gives an early precursor" (erroneous).** The work `2602.18649` (no card) ends its analysis of low-dimensionality with a proposal to track the trajectory's principal components during fine-tuning, `"could provide an early-warning signal for capability changes"`, with a reference to this paper. Yet what is declared the early precursor here is the commutator defect, and the PC1 trajectory is given as a **negative** result: on SCAN the thinning of PC1 follows the grokking, and the authors write outright that it "is *not* a universal precursor of grokking" ([`p11-3`](#p11-3), [`p11-2`](#p11-2), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)). The reference credits the paper with exactly what it refutes.

**"The matrix commutator peaks before grokking with superlinear lead times on Dyck, SCAN and modular arithmetic" (widening).** In the work `2604.07380` (no card) two quantities are merged into one: `"tracks $\left\|[W_{Q},W_{K}]\right\|_{F}$ and shows it peaks before grokking, with superlinear lead times across Dyck, SCAN, and modular arithmetic"`. In the paper under analysis the superlinear lead is measured for the **SGD commutator defect** $\mathcal{D}$ rather than for the matrix commutator ([`p14-2`](#p14-2)); the peak of the matrix commutator is measured only on Dyck and SCAN, in six runs in all, and moreover on a different grokking scale ($\geq 0.95$ with no sustainment, [table 8](#tab-8)), with the lead equal to zero in one of the six runs ([`p17-4`](#p17-4)). Modular arithmetic takes no part in that measurement: the result for it is taken from Xu 2026b.

**Accurate retellings for comparison.** The work `2607.06639` (no card) writes: `"Xu [13] uses a loss-landscape commutator defect as an early-warning signal with a power-law lead time"` — a formulation that does not exceed what is shown. Equally accurate are two other sentences from [Xu 2026, multi-task geometry](../2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure/2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure.card.md): that similar early-warning signals arise on Dyck languages and SCAN, and about the power-law scaling of the lead time. One further mention, `2605.20441` (no card), lists this work among complementary results with no substantive retelling.
