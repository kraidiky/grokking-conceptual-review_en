## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work offers the corpus a causal explanation and does so without hedges: the cause is named as the alignment of the network's Jacobian matrices with the training points themselves — *"the cause of grokking is the alignment of a deep network's Jacobian matrices"* ([p2-3](#p2-3)), and the same is repeated in the "Discussion" as established ([p10-3](#p10-3)). The starting point is declared to be an empty spot: there is "no fundamental explanation of why it occurs, nor a practical and interpretable framework" for acceleration ([p1-4](#p1-4)). It matters for the corpus that two different phenomena are joined here under one word — the delayed generalization of Power et al. and the delayed robustness to input perturbations of Humayun et al. ([p1-3](#p1-3)) — and thereafter the paper reasons about them as one; the aligned centroid is directly identified with the "region migration" of [Humayun et al. 2024](../2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why/2402.15555.deep-networks-always-grok-and-here-is-why.card.md) ([p4-9](#p4-9)). What the work does not have: the necessity of alignment is not proved — Theorem 2 gives only optimality under a constraint on the Jacobian norm and zero bias ([p3-5](#p3-5)), while the step to "must necessarily involve" ([p3-8](#p3-8)) rests on an empirical low-rank assumption borrowed from other people's work. Nor is there Jacobian alignment in the sense of [Definition 1](#p3-2) in any experiment: the largest measured cosine similarity of the Jacobian rows with the point is $0{.}709$ ([tab-2](#tab-2)) against the required unity. The work introduces no grokking measure of its own: the time is taken to be the attainment of $85$ % test accuracy ([tab-1](#tab-1)).

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the frame is adopted ready-made and serves as support rather than as subject: delayed generalization is from the outset attributed to the network's transition "from the linear regime to the feature-learning regime" with references to Lyu et al., Rubin et al. and Kumar et al. ([p2-1](#p2-1)), and the linear and feature-learning regimes are defined through the constancy or the variability of the neural tangent kernel, with an authorial footnote that "Lazy and rich are also widely used as names for these two regimes" ([p4-12](#p4-12)). The work's contribution to this notion is a proposed cheap observable marker of the transition: a changing inner product of a point with its centroid is declared a marker of a dynamic kernel, that is, of the feature-learning regime ([p5-6](#p5-6)). What the work does not have: neither a measurement of the kernel as such outside figure 3, nor a check that the marker found distinguishes the transition from other causes of a changing inner product — the inference "a changing inner product entails a changing kernel" does not follow from the formula of Theorem 9 (a constant kernel with non-zero residuals gives exactly the same thing).

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the work gives the notion a new point of attachment: Theorem 9 relates the time derivative of the inner product $\left\langle\mathbf{x},\mu_{\mathbf{x}}\right\rangle$ to a weighted sum of the kernel of the point with the training points ([p5-5](#p5-5)), and through this the centroid — a quantity computable with a single Jacobian-vector product — is proposed as a stand-in for direct measurement of the kernel. The setting is narrow and stated as such: a two-layer network without biases, a scalar output, cross-entropy, full-batch gradient descent; the vector output is relegated to appendix A, where the connection to the kernel is no longer written out. The empirical check is a single one: two-class MNIST, $4000$ steps, one training-set point ([p5-10](#p5-10), [fig-3](#fig-3)). What the work does not have: neither the kernel at initialization against the kernel at the end of training, nor a numerical threshold by which the "feature-learning region" is delimited — the green band in figure 3 is drawn "by the change in the value of the neural tangent kernel" by eye.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work offers the corpus a new trackable quantity without calling it a progress measure: the centroid of a linear region is the sum of the Jacobian rows (Theorem 4, [p4-6](#p4-6)), is computed with a Jacobian-vector product and is therefore far cheaper than the Jacobian itself ([p4-7](#p4-7), [p2-4](#p2-4)), and the degree of its alignment with the point is proposed "as a quantity for effectively tracking the emergence of generalization and robustness" ([p5-8](#p5-8)). The applied turn here is not only "when it began" but also "when one may stop": from the plateau of the alignment and the approach of the effective rank to unity the authors conclude that prolonging training will give nothing further ([p7-5](#p7-5)). The line is inherited directly from the local complexity of Humayun et al.: "region migration" is restated as centroid alignment ([p4-9](#p4-9)). What the work does not have: the words "progress measure" and a reference to Barak et al.; the quantity is not tested for leading the jump (its course is compared with an already-occurred rise in accuracy rather than predicting it), and on modular addition it changes in the opposite direction ([fig-8](#fig-8), [fig-9](#fig-9)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work gives weight decay a mechanistic explanation and at the same time puts it in second place: it is called a "viable technique for imposing the constraint of theorem 2", but a "less direct" one, with an explicit reference to the corpus's disagreement — "in some cases weight decay proved effective for inducing grokking [4, 15, 16, 32], whereas in others it proved insufficient for grokking [12]" ([p4-3](#p4-3)). The mechanism of the delay is written out step by step on XOR clusters: first the centroid norms grow and hold alignment back, and only when alignment becomes the optimum of the training objective does weight decay begin to reduce the Jacobian norm — hence the lag of robustness ([p7-1](#p7-1)); the generalization is that "ordinary deep-network training, weight decay included, cannot maintain the constraint on the Jacobian norm" ([p7-3](#p7-3), [p5-11](#p5-11)). On CIFAR-10 under weight decay alone the robustness falls by the end of training ([fig-5](#fig-5)). What the work does not have: a sweep over the strength of weight decay — in all comparisons it is taken at a single value and "in none of the methods do we change the weight decay of the training procedure" ([p9-2](#p9-2)); nor is there any analysis of the fact that in figure 2 the effective rank falls under weight decay exactly as it does under GrokAlign ([fig-2](#fig-2)).

**[When regularization is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work takes the side of "necessary", but with an unusual argument: not "without regularization there is no grokking" but "without it there is no constraint on the Jacobian norm, and hence no alignment". In appendix B this is said outright: initialization by itself sets the outcome, but "knowing a priori how to initialize a deep network for favourable alignment dynamics is difficult, and therefore in practice some form of regularization is necessary" ([p16-2](#p16-2)). What the work does not have: an experiment with no regularization at all in the main setting — the comparisons of table 1 run at an unchanged weight decay for every run, the baseline included, so the case of "no regularization whatsoever" is not measured, and the claim remains an argument resting on the sweep over initialization scales ([fig-10](#fig-10)).

**[Regularization variants](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — the work introduces a new technique into the corpus: GrokAlign, the addition to the loss of the mean Frobenius norm of the Jacobian matrices on the training data with a coefficient $\lambda_{\text{Jac}}$ ([p4-1](#p4-1)), computed approximately for the sake of computational efficiency ([p7-4](#p7-4)). The technique is not new in itself, and the authors say so: Jacobian regularization has already been used for the sake of robustness, and keeping a low Lipschitz constant has been proposed for reconciling generalization with robustness ([p4-2](#p4-2)); the novelty is that the same penalty is declared a control knob of grokking, and in both directions, deceleration included, if the Jacobian norm is held high ([p8-2](#p8-2)). Appendix C compares a third variant as well — adversarial training: it does give robustness, but does not induce Jacobian alignment ([p16-3](#p16-3), [tab-2](#tab-2)). What the work does not have: a rule for choosing $\lambda_{\text{Jac}}$ — across the experiments it varies by four orders of magnitude ($1{.}0$, $0{.}001$, $0{.}0001$) with no sensitivity analysis; nor is there a comparison with other penalties on the rank or the norm of the weight matrices.

**[Grokfast / gradient filtering](../../concepts/gradient-low-pass-filtering.md)** — the work gives the notion its first direct measurement in the corpus against a new rival, and the outcome for Grokfast is negative: on the MNIST setup of Liu et al. with ten seeds, Grokfast under cross-entropy turns out slightly slower than the baseline run ($\uparrow 1{.}01\times$ in steps, $\uparrow 1{.}03\times$ in time), while under mean squared error it gives $\downarrow 1{.}08\times$ against $\downarrow 1{.}69\times$ for GrokAlign ([tab-1](#tab-1)); in the text this is called a "relatively smaller improvement" in the MSE case and ineffectiveness in the cross-entropy case ([p9-3](#p9-3)). The technique is described in a single phrase — "by processing gradients during training so as to amplify certain signals" ([p9-2](#p9-2)). What the work does not have: Grokfast's settings (neither $\alpha$, nor $\lambda$, nor the choice between EMA and MA), so from what is published one cannot judge whether the technique lost or its tuning did; nor are there absolute numbers for the baseline run — the "Baseline" row of table 1 is empty.

**[Initialization scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the work translates the technique of Liu et al. into its own language: an increased initial weight norm means Jacobians of large norm, and it is precisely that which holds back their alignment ([p9-1](#p9-1)); the same lever is applied in the opposite direction as well — the weights at initialization are increased, "much as Liu et al. do", in order to prevent alignment and with it generalization ([p8-2](#p8-2)). Appendix B examines the case without regularization and finds a trade-off: the rate of change of the inner product can be increased by scaling the weights or the output, but the same scaling increases the centroid norm ([p16-1](#p16-1)); from the sweep over weight and output scales the authors conclude that controlling the centroid norm is the more effective route ([fig-10](#fig-10)). What the work does not have: the values of the initial scale in the experiments themselves — they are taken from Liu et al. by reference; nor is there any remark that across the whole sweep of figure 10 the largest observed alignment is negative (on a scale from $-0{.}3$ to $-0{.}7$).

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — nearly all of the work's empirics stand on vision data, and this sets it apart from its neighbours in the geometry line: MNIST serves as a two-class check of Theorem 9 ([p5-10](#p5-10)), as a deceleration testbed ([fig-7](#fig-7)), and as an acceleration testbed ([tab-1](#tab-1)), while a convolutional network of six convolutional and two linear layers without biases is trained on a CIFAR-10 subsample of $1024$ examples, where robustness is measured with Autoattack under $\ell_{\infty}$ perturbations of amplitude $\frac{4}{255}$ ([p7-5](#p7-5), [fig-5](#fig-5)). What the work does not have: any at all strong model — on CIFAR-10 the test accuracy stays around $0{.}36$ and the robust accuracy around $0{.}14$ against $0{.}02$ for weight decay, that is, what is shown is a relative improvement of an admittedly weak model, not robustness as such; nor are there networks with biases, since the zero-bias assumption is carried over from the theory into the experiments ([p5-9](#p5-9)).

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the work gives the corpus an honestly reported negative outcome on the canonical task: on modular addition in the setup of Nanda et al., GrokAlign does not accelerate grokking ([p9-6](#p9-6)), and the explanation is given after the fact — the transformer learns an algorithmic solution "that is not entirely compatible with the Jacobian and centroid alignment view". The setting is meanwhile changed so that the outcome becomes positive: with the embedding and unembedding matrices frozen, that is, with the model deprived of the possibility of building the algorithm, the transformer groks earlier with GrokAlign than with weight decay ([p10-2](#p10-2), [fig-9](#fig-9)). What the work does not have: neither the modulus, nor the split, nor the number of seeds for these two runs — all of it is left to the reference to the setup of Nanda et al.; nor an acknowledgement that in figure 8 GrokAlign does not merely "fail to accelerate" but decelerates.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work takes one ready-made instrument from Nanda et al. and uses it as an indicator rather than as an analysis of the machinery: the Gini coefficient of the embedding and unembedding matrices is taken as a marker that the transformer is building an algorithmic solution, and the observation that it stays low under GrokAlign serves as an argument that GrokAlign biases toward a "classification-style" solution ([p10-1](#p10-1), [fig-8](#fig-8)). What the work does not have: any mechanistic analysis proper — no Fourier decomposition of the embeddings, no isolation of heads and sub-circuits, no ablations; the "classification solution" is nowhere defined operationally, and the conclusion about it is drawn from a single scalar indicator.

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the work uses a spectral quantity as a prop for an assumption: the fraction of variance explained by the first principal component of the Jacobian, $\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sigma_{i}^{2}}$, averaged over the training points, is tracked over the course of training and read as the effective rank of the Jacobian ([fig-2](#fig-2)); its growth toward unity is what carries the step from Theorem 3 to the claim that alignment is necessary ([p3-8](#p3-8)). The low-rank tendencies themselves are neither derived nor measured as a property of the weights — they are taken by reference to five other works, and the authors openly admit that the rank-reduction assumption "requires deeper characterization" ([p11-3](#p11-3)). What the work does not have: the spectrum of the weight matrices (what is measured is the spectrum of the Jacobian, not of the weights), a comparison with the corpus's ESD and HTSR quantities, and an analysis of the fact that under cross-entropy over $500$ steps the fraction reaches only $\approx 0{.}8$, and that under weight decay and under GrokAlign the curves coincide ([fig-2](#fig-2)).

## Points we could not reconcile

**The acceleration figure differs between the text and the table.** The paragraph with the main result says that GrokAlign reaches the grokked state "$6{.}31$ times faster than the baseline run" under cross-entropy ([p9-3](#p9-3)), while the row of the same table in the same "Absolute time" column carries $\downarrow 6{.}41\times$ ([tab-1](#tab-1)). In the number of steps there is no discrepancy ($7{.}56$ in both places). When citing, one should take the number from the table.

**The sign of the alignment is the opposite of what the text says.** The text of section 4.1 explains what happens by "the growing centroid inner product experienced in the feature-learning regime" ([p5-11](#p5-11)), and Corollary 11 of appendix A ends with the words that "the centroid of a training point is encouraged to align positively with the point itself" ([p15-8](#p15-8)). In the figure that supports this, the centroid inner product *falls* from zero to $-12$, and the alignment from zero to $-0{.}55$ ([fig-3](#fig-3)). In the sweep of appendix B the picture is the same: "the largest alignment observed during training" takes values on the scale from $-0{.}3$ to $-0{.}7$, that is, it is negative in every run of the sweep, and the scaling declared more effective is the one that drives it further into the negative ([fig-10](#fig-10)). Nowhere does the paper remark on this sign; its reasoning has to be read in terms of the magnitude of the alignment rather than its value.

**"Prevents grokking" — in the figure it is a degradation, not an abolition.** Both the text ([p8-3](#p8-3)) and the figure caption say that holding the Frobenius norm of the Jacobian at a high level "prevents grokking". In the figure itself the constrained run reaches about $0{.}5$ test accuracy against about $0{.}85$ for the minimizing one, from a starting level of about $0{.}1$ ([fig-7](#fig-7)); the same figure shows that the constrained run's training accuracy sags too — from unity to about $0{.}95$. That is, generalization is slowed and degraded but not abolished, and the training objective ceases to be attained as well.

**"Does not accelerate" — in figure 8 it is a deceleration.** The text says that on modular addition "applying GrokAlign does not accelerate the rate of grokking" ([p9-6](#p9-6)). In the figure the test accuracy under weight decay reaches unity at about $6\cdot 10^{3}$ steps and under GrokAlign at about $2\cdot 10^{4}$ ([fig-8](#fig-8)), that is, roughly three times later. The wording "does not accelerate" is weaker than what is shown.

**Centroid alignment falls exactly where the network groks.** Section 3 ends with the conclusion that the degree of centroid alignment serves "as a quantity for effectively tracking the emergence of generalization and robustness" ([p5-8](#p5-8)). In both transformer figures the alignment goes into the negative at the moment of grokking: down to $\approx-0{.}35$ for weight decay in [fig-8](#fig-8) and down to $\approx-0{.}3$ for GrokAlign in [fig-9](#fig-9), and in figure 9 both runs grok. The text confines itself to the remark that the alignment "changes in concert with the test accuracy" and does not name the direction.

**The column heading of table 2 is swapped relative to the values.** The column is titled "Alignment of the Jacobian rows (max/min)", yet in every cell of it the first number is negative and the second positive: $-0{.}254$/$0{.}283$, $-0{.}509$/$0{.}478$, $-0{.}638$/$0{.}709$ ([tab-2](#tab-2)). The order of the values is therefore min/max, not max/min. A trifle, but when the table is cited by its heading the largest alignment turns out to be the smallest.

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

The work claims causality in its strongest form — *"the cause of grokking is the alignment of a deep network's Jacobian matrices"* ([p2-3](#p2-3)), and repeats it in the "Discussion" as achieved ([p10-3](#p10-3)). Below is a breakdown of what stands behind that.

**Proved, under the stated assumptions.** Theorem 2: under the constraint $\left\|J_{\mathbf{x}_{p}}(f)\right\|_{F}^{2}\leq\alpha$ and $B_{\omega_{\mathbf{x}_{p}}}=\mathbf{0}$ the minimizer of cross-entropy or of mean squared error is Jacobian-aligned, and the solution is written out explicitly ([p3-5](#p3-5), [p3-6](#p3-6)). Three features of the proof matter: the Lagrangian is written for a single $p$, while $A_{\omega_{\mathbf{x}_{p}}}$ is treated as a free variable — that a whole such family of matrices is simultaneously attainable by one network with shared weights is not shown; the alignment itself is not derived but introduced by the ansatz $\left[A_{\omega_{\mathbf{x}_{p}}}\right]_{c,\cdot}=a\mathbf{x}_{p}$ for $c=y_{p}$ and $b\mathbf{x}_{p}$ otherwise, after which only the sufficiency of the Karush — Kuhn — Tucker conditions is checked; uniqueness of the optimum is not examined. Theorem 4 and Proposition 6 are derived from the results of Balestriero et al. on power-diagram partitions ([p4-5](#p4-5), [p4-6](#p4-6)); the converse of Proposition 6 is not shown, and the weakness of the centroid condition is acknowledged outright ([p4-11](#p4-11)).

**Proved in part.** Theorem 3 asserts that among rank-one matrices with zero bias the most robust to $\ell_{2}$ perturbations is the one with $A_{\omega_{\mathbf{x}}}=\mathbf{c}\mathbf{x}^{\top}$, "where the largest entry of $\mathbf{c}$ sits at the class index of $\mathbf{x}$" ([p3-7](#p3-7)). The proof examines the single sign condition $\mathbf{v}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\epsilon})<0$ ([p20-11](#p20-11), [p20-12](#p20-12)) — in essence the two-class case; the part of the statement about the largest entry of $\mathbf{c}$ does not appear in the proof at all.

**Shown empirically.** The growth toward unity of the fraction of variance explained by the first principal component of the Jacobians ([fig-2](#fig-2)); the joint growth of centroid alignment and robustness on XOR clusters ([fig-4](#fig-4)) and on CIFAR-10 ([fig-5](#fig-5)); the acceleration on the MNIST setup of Liu et al. over ten seeds ([tab-1](#tab-1)); the shift in the cosine similarity of the Jacobian rows with the points under GrokAlign ([tab-2](#tab-2)). The limits of this: in all experiments the networks are without biases — the assumption $B_{\omega}=\mathbf{0}$ is carried over from the theory into the experiment ([p5-9](#p5-9)); the measured alignment nowhere exceeds $0{.}709$ against the unity required by [Definition 1](#p3-2); the baseline row of table 1 is empty, so there are no absolute times; $\lambda_{\text{Jac}}$ has no selection rule, and across the experiments it varies by four orders of magnitude.

**Conjectured or carried by argument.** The necessity of alignment — "delayed robustness must necessarily involve Jacobian alignment" ([p3-8](#p3-8)) — is obtained from Theorem 3 plus the low-rank assumption, taken from other people's work and acknowledged to require "deeper characterization" ([p11-3](#p11-3)). The inference "a changing inner product entails a changing neural tangent kernel" ([p5-6](#p5-6)) does not follow from the formula of Theorem 9 ([p5-5](#p5-5)): a non-zero weighted sum of a constant kernel with non-zero residuals gives exactly the same non-zero derivative. The transfer of the centroid dynamics to a vector output (appendix A) is made without re-deriving the connection to the kernel. The explanation of the negative outcome on the transformer through the Gini coefficient ([p10-1](#p10-1)) is given after the experiment and rests on nothing but a single scalar indicator.

**Claimed in the title but not measured.** The "geometric characterisation" rests on SplineCam visualizations of two-dimensional slices of the input space ([fig-1](#fig-1), [fig-6](#fig-6), [fig-11](#fig-11), [p16-4](#p16-4)). A numerical measure of the geometric change — unlike the local complexity of the same research line — the work neither introduces nor measures. The authors name two limitations of their own: the theory is built for continuous piecewise-affine networks ([p11-2](#p11-2)) and the assumption of rank reduction in the weight matrices requires deeper analysis ([p11-3](#p11-3)).

## Common misreadings and overstatements

Editorial notes on the places where this work passes on other people's results with distortion or without the original authors' caveats.

###### mc-2311-06597

**Tan, Huang (2311.06597) — the technique is renamed and its rule inverted.** Describing what GrokAlign is compared against, the paper writes: [`"Tan and Huang [39] motivated adversarial training for accelerating grokking by establishing a connection between robustness and generalisation."`](#p9-2); and further: [`"The method of adversarial training involves perturbing the inputs during training with noise proportional to the training accuracy of the deep network."`](#p9-2).

Both halves diverge from the source. The first: [Tan and Huang](../2311.06597.understanding-grokking-through-a-robustness-viewpoint/2311.06597.understanding-grokking-through-a-robustness-viewpoint.card.md) do not propose adversarial training — their technique is called "degrokking" by its authors and consists of adding Gaussian noise $\Delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I})$ to the input, `"We made minimum changes to the initial training by only adding $\Delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I})$ to the input."`; there is no adversary choosing the direction of the perturbation there at all, and the word "adversarial" does not occur in their text even once. The second, more substantial: the perturbation strength there is set as `"we adaptively update $\sigma=\max(\lambda_{1}(1-\text{train acc}),\lambda_{2})$"`, that is, it is proportional to the training *error* and decreases over the course of training; "proportional to the training accuracy" describes the opposite behaviour — noise growing as memorization proceeds. It is in exactly that form that the technique is presented in table 1 as having lost the comparison ([tab-1](#tab-1)), and from what is published one cannot check whether the source's rule was reproduced or its inverted version.

The motivating part is meanwhile conveyed correctly: the connection "the more robust, the earlier it generalizes" is indeed present in Tan and Huang, and in appendix C the paper repeats it without distortion — [`"Although both GrokAlign and adversarial training are motivated as inducing grokking through improving model robustness"`](#p16-3). The warning concerns the name of the technique and the rule for setting the noise, not the work as a whole.

###### mc-2301-05217

**Nanda et al. (2301.05217) — the Fourier basis has dropped out of the quantity.** The argument that GrokAlign biases a transformer toward a "classification" solution is built on an indicator: [`"We support the idea that GrokAlign biases toward the classification style solution by tracking the Gini coefficient of the embedding and unembedding matrices [15]."`](#p10-1); the figure caption adds [`"as proposed in Nanda et al. [15]"`](#fig-8).

[Nanda et al.](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) propose a different quantity: `"the Gini coefficient (Hurley & Rickard, 2009) of the norms of the Fourier components of $W_{E}$ and $W_{L}$, which measures the sparsity of $W_{E}$ and $W_{L}$ in the Fourier basis"`. Sparsity in the Fourier basis specifically is what ties the indicator to the algorithmic solution; the Gini coefficient of the matrices themselves is a different quantity, and from the paper's text and captions it is impossible to establish which of the two was computed. The meaning is shifted too: in Nanda et al. this indicator grows in the cleanup phase, after the generalizing circuit has already formed, whereas here a high value of it is directly identified with "carrying out an algorithmic solution" ([p10-1](#p10-1)).

The other references to Nanda et al. are conveyed accurately: both the observation that a one-layer transformer groks by learning an algorithm ([p9-5](#p9-5)) and the fact that the training pipeline is taken from them unchanged apart from the addition of GrokAlign ([p9-6](#p9-6)).

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns observed in the corpus works that cite this one. Each entry says what exactly is distorted and leads to the relevant authorial paragraphs of this paper.

**"The lazy→rich transition yields delayed generalization without requiring explicit regularization, as Walker et al. showed" (erroneous).** [Saheb Pasand and Dohmatob](../2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking/2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking.card.md) place this work alongside Kumar et al. as formalizing the conditions under which a transition from the lazy regime to the rich one yields delayed generalization: `"Kumar et al. (2024); Walker et al. (2025) formalize conditions under which such a transition yields delayed generalization without requiring explicit regularization."` What is attributed here is exactly the opposite of what is said. The "conditions" of Theorem 2 are precisely an explicit constraint on the Jacobian norm ([p3-5](#p3-5)), and the whole applied conclusion of the paper is that without a special penalty the alignment does not come about: [`"conventional training practices do not keep the Jacobian norm constrained, which highlights the need for a method such as GrokAlign"`](#p5-11), and GrokAlign is introduced precisely as a penalty added to the loss with a coefficient $\lambda_{\text{Jac}}$ ([p4-1](#p4-1)). More than that, weight decay remains switched on in every run compared, the baseline included: [`"In none of the methods do we change the weight decay of the training procedure"`](#p9-2). There is no run without regularization in the work at all, and appendix B ends with the directly opposite conclusion — [`"in practice some form of regularization is necessary"`](#p16-2).

**"Walker et al. found that low-rank structure facilitates representation compression" (over-extending).** [Hwang and Park](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md) place the work among prior findings about low rank: `"prior findings that low-rank structure facilitates representation compression (Walker et al., 2025; DeMoss et al., 2025; Junior et al., 2025)"`. The direction of the inference is reversed here. Low-rankness in Walker et al. is not a finding but an adopted assumption: it is attributed to other works ([22]–[26]) and propped up by a single figure with the fraction of explained variance ([p3-8](#p3-8), [fig-2](#fig-2)), while the "Limitations" say outright that this assumption [`"requires deeper characterization"`](#p11-3). Representation compression the work does not measure in any form; its own causal arrow points the other way — a penalty on the Jacobian norm produces the alignment that is tracked through the centroids ([p7-5](#p7-5)). The citing context is meanwhile correct: what is at issue is that geometry-encouraging biases shorten the time to generalization, and such a result is indeed present in Walker et al. ([tab-1](#tab-1)).

The direct continuation of this work is 2605.08464 (Walker, Roddenberry, Humayun, Balestriero, Baraniuk, 2026), where the same alignment is renamed "normal alignment", GrokAlign is carried over wholesale and extended to Recursive Feature Machines; there is no card for it in the corpus yet, and this work cannot be counted as a citation — it is the authors' own development.
