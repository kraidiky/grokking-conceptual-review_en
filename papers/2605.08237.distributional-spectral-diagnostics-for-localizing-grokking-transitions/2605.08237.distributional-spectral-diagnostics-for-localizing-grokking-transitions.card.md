## Concepts covered

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work does not explain grokking and openly refuses to do so ("This is a different question from explaining *why* grokking arises", [p. 1, para. 3](#p1-3)); it turns it into an **object of detection**. Grokking here is defined operationally and narrowly: the onset is the first step at which the test accuracy crosses 99 %, and "grokking" is separated from "early generalization" by a step threshold placed in the bimodal trough of the onset distribution ([p. 5, para. 2](#p5-2), [app. M](#p25-5)). The mean onset at wd$=1$ is 3398 steps, at wd$=2$ about 1528; the whole analysis is cut off at 6000 steps. It matters for the corpus that the separating threshold is admitted to have been chosen from the data post hoc, and that the non-grokking class is assembled from runs that did not reach onset within the budget — the same paper says that without weight decay "some seeds do not generalize up to more than $5\times 10^{5}$ steps" ([p. 16, para. 4](#p16-4)). What the work does not have: no mechanism, no measure of internal progress, and no claim that grokking is predictable in advance in general; the claim is confined to "the transformer settings studied on modular arithmetic" ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4)).

**[The reference grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work takes the testbed of Power et al. wholesale and says so outright: the task of modular addition mod 97, the training codebase `openai/grok`, a decoder-only transformer with $d_{\mathrm{model}}=128$, 2 layers, 4 heads, AdamW, up to 6000 steps; **only** the training fraction is changed, raised to 0.4 ([app. D.2](#p16-1), [p. 4, para. 8](#p4-8)). The contribution to the notion is not a new configuration but a new **use** of the testbed: it serves not as a showcase for the phenomenon but as a bench on which a detector is measured, with a threshold, a false-alarm rate and a lead time. A caveat that matters when citing: grokking here is induced by weight decay and fits into 6000 steps, whereas in Power et al. the time scale is orders of magnitude larger — the lead times from this work are not directly comparable with the original setting.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the only task on which detection numbers are presented: modular addition at $p=97$. Transfer within the family is declared a **check rather than a result**: the same rule fires on modular multiplication at wd$=1$, but with a shorter lead time ([app. S](#p30-2)), while a sweep over the training fraction and the size of the prime modulus gives "partial transfer with clear cases of failure" ([app. S](#p30-1)). Not a single number — neither lead time nor FPR — is given in appendix S. What the work does not have: an analysis of what makes modular arithmetic special for the distribution of log-probabilities; the task is taken as a convenient carrier of grokking, not as an object.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — serves as the **control knob of the whole experiment** rather than as a subject of study. Three settings $\mathrm{wd}\in\{0,1,2\}$ define three regimes: without weight decay the runs stay overfitted and some seeds do not generalize within the budget, at wd$=1$ grokking sets in with a delay, at wd$=2$ there is early generalization with no pronounced overfitting stage ([p. 16, para. 4](#p16-4), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). In the extended sweep over five values $\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2\}$ the qualitative ordering is preserved: among the grokking runs a larger weight decay is associated with an earlier onset ([app. E](#p17-2), [fig. 9](#fig-9)), while the residual and the effective rank track the accuracy curves ([fig. 8](#fig-8)). A separately valuable observation for the corpus: the peak residual **decreases** as weight decay grows (0.780 at wd$=0$, 0.625 at wd$=1$, 0.584 at wd$=2$ for the baseline architecture, [app. F](#p17-9)), that is, the greatest spectral disorder sits with the runs that precisely do **not** grok. What the work does not have: a mechanism for the effect of weight decay and any theory at all — the ordering is declared a descriptive summary, "not a calibrated trend extrapolated to other regularization regimes".

**[When regularization is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work gives one more piece of evidence that in this setting weight decay is practically necessary for generalization **within a reasonable budget**, and it formulates this carefully: without weight decay "some seeds do not generalize up to more than $5\times 10^{5}$ steps", whereas "all runs with weight decay generalize within 6000 steps" ([p. 16, para. 4](#p16-4)). This is a claim about **time**, not about possibility: the authors do not say outright that generalization is impossible without weight decay. A caveat easy to lose: it is precisely this fact that makes the detector's negative class inhomogeneous — some of the "non-grokking" runs are grokking runs cut off by the budget.

**[The memorization phase / plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the plateau is taken as a **source of difficulty rather than as an object**: "The flatness is the difficulty — the loss and the accuracy by themselves do not directly indicate when the transition will come, even in runs that eventually grok" ([p. 1, para. 3](#p1-3)). Hence the whole design of the work: an averaged loss collapses the distribution of an observable into a single scalar, while the distribution continues to change ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)). The contribution to the notion is the presentation of a quantity that is **not** flat on the plateau. What the work does not have: an analysis of what happens inside the model during the plateau, and any substantive description of the memorization phase at all — the training accuracy appears in the figures only "as a reference to the memorization stage" ([fig. 2](#fig-2)).

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the work gives the corpus **a new place from which the spectrum is taken**. All the corpus's other spectral lines read the eigenvalues or singular values of the network's matrices: weights, gradient, activation covariance. Here the spectrum is taken from a **linear operator fitted to the trajectory of the observable itself**: the distribution of log-probabilities of the correct answer on a fixed probe set is reduced to 19 quantile coordinates, and on a window a Hankel DMD is computed from them, yielding a spectrum $\{\lambda_{j}\}$, an effective rank $r_{0.99}$ and a reconstruction residual ([fig. 1](#fig-1)). The pipeline is deliberately one-dimensional: for multidimensional distributions "there is in general no global isometric isomorphism mapping Wasserstein space into a linear one" ([app. A](#p13-1)). The second move, still more unusual for the corpus: the main diagnostic is made **not the spectrum but its failure**. The residual is declared a "validity gate": at a small residual the spectrum and the effective rank read as a description of the local regime, at a large one "the residual itself should be interpreted as a signal of transition or fragility, and the spectral points and the effective rank should not be reinterpreted as stable regime descriptors" ([p. 4, para. 3](#p4-3)). What the work does not have: a comparison with any of the corpus's spectral measures — neither HTSR, nor the weight spectrum, nor the spectral entropy of representations; the only numerically compared rival is the weight norm, and it wins at the run level. Extending the diagnostic to genuinely multidimensional observables the authors place outside the scope of the work outright ([p. 9, para. 5](#p9-5)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work deliberately **dissociates** itself from this rubric, using the term itself: the residual "is intended as a window-level monitoring signal, not as a progress measure tied to a particular mechanism" ([app. U](#p31-1)). The distinction is substantive rather than verbal: a progress measure ought to grow monotonically along with hidden advancement toward the solution, whereas the residual is a **spike** localizing the window of reorganization, and after the transition it falls — on the AGOP pool "saturated post-grokking checkpoints stand at large AGOP and yet small residual" ([fig. 12](#fig-12)). What the corpus may take from this besides the quantity is the **form of the report**: an early indicator is presented as a detector with a threshold, TPR, FPR, lead time and confidence intervals, and it is declared in advance what counts as a miss. What the work does not have: a comparison with the progress measures of Nanda et al. or with the circuit efficiency of Varma et al. — they are named as an explanatory line and never once compared numerically with the residual.

**[Weight norm minimization](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work's most honest and most strained neighbourhood. At the run level the norm beats the residual by an enormous margin, and the authors print it: the full parameter norm gives an AUROC of 0.9829, $\lvert\Delta\mathrm{norm}\rvert$ 0.9915, and the residual (maximum over the trajectory) 0.4103 ([app. Q](#p28-5)). The claim is shifted accordingly: the value of the residual is declared **window-level rather than run-level**. The one argument in its favour is a window-level control on the same data: if the same 21 perturbed runs are relabelled by the percentile of the full norm instead of the percentile of the residual, the "high/low" fragility ratio flips from $\approx 3.82\times$ to $\approx 0.41\times$ ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1), [app. Q](#p29-1)). The authors' caveat that must not be lost: the norm and the residual are temporarily anticorrelated in the wd$=1$ dynamics, so the two labellings select windows adjacent in time, and the control "does not establish a causal mechanism" ([p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2)). What the work does not have: a claim that the residual anywhere surpasses the norm — "We do not claim that RR universally surpasses norm-based baseline signals".

**[Feature emergence](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — AGOP is introduced as a **parallel path rather than a rival**, and the reason is named outright: "the sparse checkpoint coverage does not permit a fair quantitative comparison" — on pool N1 full coverage exists for exactly one non-grokking run, because of which the TPR for AGOP is simply undefined ([p. 4, para. 5](#p4-5), [app. H](#p20-1), [fig. 13](#fig-13)). What the work does nevertheless add to the notion is an observation about the **non-monotonicity** of the relation: the residual is elevated in the middle range of AGOP, which contains the transition windows, while saturated post-grokking points give a large AGOP at a small residual ([fig. 12](#fig-12)). What the work does not have: any measurement of features, of their number, quality or moment of appearance; AGOP is taken as someone else's quantity for a qualitative check, and the authors themselves call this "qualitative corroboration, not independent confirmation".

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — mentioned only as a line from which the work separates itself, and the separation is drawn by question rather than by quality: "Mechanistic explanations remain complementary: they explain *why* grokking arises, whereas the residual addresses *where in training* the transition can be located" ([p. 9, para. 6](#p9-6)). The practical sense the work attributes to its quantity is auxiliary to mech-interp: the residual "helps to determine where mechanistic or gradient analyses should be directed" ([p. 9, para. 3](#p9-3)). What the work does not have: a single mechanistic measurement — no circuits, no heads, no probes; the work does not even look inside the network, and this is its deliberate choice, since the diagnostic is computed on the output distribution and "does not depend on hidden-unit indexing" ([p. 2, para. 1](#p2-1)).

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the work makes two interventions and both times refrains from causal conclusions, which is a rarity. The first: identical multiplicative perturbations of scales $\{0.005,0.01\}$ applied in high- and low-residual windows give a short-term deviation of $0.090$ against $0.029$ and $0.107$ against $0.034$ ([p. 7, para. 1](#p7-1), [fig. 3](#fig-3)); the conclusion is formulated as a measurement of sensitivity and "does not establish a causal mechanism". The second is an intervention on alarm, and it is **negative and presented as such**: halving the learning rate on the residual signal does not accelerate grokking reliably, but introduces 10 % failures that fixed and random schedules do not have, whence the conclusion "Monitoring does not directly entail useful control" ([app. R](#p29-4), [fig. 19](#fig-19)). What the work does not have: ablations of parts of the network, interventions on representations and any attempt at all to establish causality — the authors declare this the boundary of the claim.

**[Learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — it appears only as a **lever of intervention**, not as a factor under study: three strategies apply a single halving of the learning rate — at a fixed step 4000, at a uniformly random step inside the window, and on a residual alarm ([app. R](#p29-4)). The result for the notion is negative and therefore useful: none of the three strategies accelerates grokking reliably, and tying the reduction to the signal brings a failure rate of 0.10 against zero for the other two. What the work does not have: a sweep over learning rates, schedules and their connection with the delay; the base rate of $10^{-3}$ is taken from the Power et al. repository unchanged ([table 6](#sec-d-2)).

**[Optimizer](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the transformer part runs entirely on AdamW, and a comparison of optimizers is relegated to secondary settings. On FCNs trained on MNIST the work reports that "AdamW tends to give a smaller effective rank than SGD at matched settings", while the clearest differences in the dynamics still come from the width rather than the optimizer ([app. T](#p30-5)). On CIFAR-10 the two optimizers give a similar peak residual (0.619 against 0.595 at 32 channels). What the work does not have: a connection between optimizer and grokking — both settings in which optimizers are compared contain no grokking and are declared regime checks rather than diagnostic claims.

**[Data fraction / critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the training fraction is **the only thing the work changed** in the setting of Power et al.: it is raised to 0.4 ([app. D.2](#p16-1)), which is what makes grokking observable within 6000 steps. Separately, the training fraction is swept in appendix S together with the size of the prime modulus, and there it gives "partial transfer with clear cases of failure under the same working rule" ([app. S](#p30-1)); one such failure is named in the summary table — a fraction of $0.5$. What the work does not have: neither a curve of the dependence on the fraction, nor numbers for the failures, nor a notion of a critical size — the sweep itself exists only as a summary row, and appendix S gives not a single number for it.

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — merely mentioned: Varma et al. are named among the mechanistic explanations — "circuit efficiency trade-offs" giving progress measures that track the formation of circuits ([app. U](#p30-7)). There is no comparison with the residual, numerical or qualitative, and the work measures no circuits.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — merely mentioned in the same sentence: "Fourier features for modular addition" as an example of emerging computational circuits ([app. U](#p30-7)). It is telling that the work conducts its entire analysis on mod 97 and yet never once looks at the Fourier structure of the representations: the observable is taken from the output, not from within.

**[Softmax collapse](../../concepts/softmax-collapse.md)** — merely mentioned as one of the "stability-based explanations" connecting grokking "with logit scaling and softmax collapse" ([p. 1, para. 4](#p1-4), [app. U](#p30-7)). The logits do nevertheless appear in the work — as an alternative observable in an ablation, where they raise alarms on 3/4 of the runs and none before onset ([app. P](#p28-2)) — but no connection with softmax collapse or numerical stability is drawn.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — merely mentioned: Thilak et al. stand in the same bracket of "stability-based explanations" ([p. 1, para. 4](#p1-4)). The work analyses neither slingshot spikes nor the behaviour of the norm over time, although its own quantity is precisely a measure of short-term instability within a window.

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — mentioned in the survey appendix as one of the lines describing learning: "the different lazy/rich regimes", where "the precise scaling determines whether feature learning takes place" ([app. U](#p31-2)); in the same enumeration stand the [neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md) and works on the mean-field limit. The work's own position is opposite in spirit: mean-field results "require particular parameterizations and asymptotic regimes not directly applicable to our finite-width, finite-step setting", and the Wasserstein geometry is taken merely as a descriptive coordinate system, with no asymptotic claims. The architectural ablation is meanwhile explicitly stated to be a check of model scale, "not of standard versus mean-field parameterization" ([p. 8, para. 4](#p8-4)).

**[Edge of stability](../../concepts/edge-of-stability.md)** — merely mentioned in a list of spectral and geometric diagnostics of training: "the edge-of-stability phenomenon, in which the top Hessian eigenvalue saturates near $2/\eta$" ([app. U](#p31-2)). Curvature is not measured in the work at all; the contrast is drawn by the location of the signal — "on the empirical distribution of a chosen observable rather than on the raw parameters or the curvature".

**[Heavy-tailed self-regularization](../../concepts/heavy-tailed-self-regularization-htsr.md)** — merely mentioned: "The spectra of weight matrices have been studied through random matrix theory and heavy-tailed self-regularization" ([app. U](#p31-2)). There is no comparison with the exponent $\alpha$ or with the ESD; for the corpus this is a notable omission, since HTSR is the line closest in intent — "cheap spectral diagnostics without access to the test set" — and both lines measure the same event from different sides.

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — two secondary settings, both with the claim explicitly withdrawn. CIFAR-10 with a Tiny CNN (8 runs, 5 epochs, 2 channel widths, 2 optimizers) "is included only as a check of transferability, not as a reference test of grokking diagnostics": there is no grokking transition in this setting, and "no detection measure (TPR/FPR/AUROC) is reported" ([app. J](#p23-1)). An FCN on MNIST serves as a use of the residual and the effective rank as regime descriptors at low residual — "We do not interpret these FCN results as a diagnostic claim about grokking" ([app. T](#p30-4), [app. T](#p30-5)). What the work does not have: grokking on real data at all — neither in the Omnigrok setting nor in any other; both chapters give only evidence that the pipeline runs to completion.

## Points we could not reconcile

Below are the places where the paper diverges from itself. This is an editorial section: it does not contest the conclusions but records what exactly is written differently in different places, so that a citer checks against the right one.

**Two incomparable numbers stand under the name "run-level AUROC": 0.93 and 0.4103.** In [§3.2](#p6-2) the residual gives an AUROC of $\approx 0.93$ on a held-out fold, and this number is carried into the [abstract](#p1-2) with the words "at the run level". In [appendix Q](#p28-5), when evaluated by the maximum over the trajectory, the same residual at the run level gives an AUROC of $0.4103$ — against 0.9829 and 0.9915 for the norm-based signals. The authors warn about the incomparability ("This run-level RR AUROC should not be compared directly with the selected held-out-threshold result of §3.2; the protocols differ", [p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)), but only the larger number is carried into the abstract, and a reader who knows only the abstract gets the opposite impression of how the residual compares with the norm.

**The number of seeds in the base pool does not agree across four places.** [§3.1](#p4-8): "$41$ distinct runs ($8$ seeds $\times$ $5$ weight-decay settings $+\,1$ pilot run)". [Table 6](#sec-d-2): "$\mathrm{wd}\in\{0,1,2\}$ with 10 seeds". [Appendix D.2](#p16-2): "we train one model per setting". [Appendix M](#p25-4): "a standard deviation of $361$ across $9$ wd$=1$ runs" — with eight seeds per setting. Adjoining this is [appendix E](#p17-2), where the sweep over five weight-decay values is described as an **extension** of the original three and is done on four seeds, whereas the base pool of §3.1 already consists of five settings with eight seeds each. How many runs stand behind which number cannot be recovered from the text.

**The peak residual of the baseline setting at wd$=1$ is given as two numbers.** The text of [appendix F](#p17-9) says "baseline $\sim 0.68$", while its own [table 7](#sec-f) gives 0.625 in that same row. The value 0.676 stands in [table 8](#sec-g) — but for a different subset of runs and a different segmentation, so the match with "$\sim0.68$" points to a mixed-up table rather than resolving the discrepancy.

**The behaviour on seeds 42–45 is described twice and differently.** [§3.2](#p6-3): the rule "raises almost no alarms". [Appendix N](#p26-2): "raises no alarms at all". Both places give TPR $=0$ and FPR $=0$, which means exactly zero alarms; the softening in the main text matches neither its own appendix nor its own numbers.

**The residual peak is declared to coincide with the onset, while the table shows a gap of thousands of steps.** [Appendix E](#p17-3): "the residual peak roughly coincides with the generalization onset for grokking runs ($\mathrm{wd}\geq 1$)". [Table 8](#sec-g), under the same condition, gives for wd$=1$ a peak step of 4062–4374 against an onset of 3387, and for wd$=2$ a peak step of 5124–5312 against an onset of 1539, that is, the peak lags the onset by three and a half thousand steps. The caveat that makes the work coherent is not stated in the text: the lead is given by the **leading edge** of the residual, not by its peak — and that is exactly how [figure 2](#fig-2) is captioned ("the leading edge of the residual precedes the onset").

**The number of seeds of the architectural ablation differs from its own table.** [§3.3](#p8-4) and [appendix F](#sec-f) say "$2$ seeds per scale" and "Each variant is trained at $\mathrm{wd}\in\{0,1,2\}$ and 2 seeds", whereas in [table 7](#sec-f) the "Seeds" column carries 2 only for the wd$=0$ rows, while for wd$=1$ and wd$=2$ in the small and large variants it carries 5.

**The number of alarms for the logits differs between the text and the figure caption.** [Appendix P](#p28-2) and [§3.1](#p5-4): "The logits ($19$ dimensions) raise alarms on $3/4$ of the runs". The caption of [figure 18](#fig-18): "the logits fire once and land after the onset (FN)". In the same place there is a small arithmetic slip: the median of the two lead times 142 and 1062 is 602, not $\approx 601$.

**The arithmetic of the perturbation pool does not close.** [§3.3](#p7-3) and [appendix Q](#p29-1) speak of "$21$ perturbed runs ($17$ reused… plus $4$ new)", whereas [appendix K](#p24-1) describes the pool as "$4$ high-RR runs and $5$ low-RR runs at each scale, $21$ runs in total counting the additional low-norm seeds": two scales of nine runs give 18, not 17. The pools themselves are meanwhile small — four and five runs per scale, with two unrecoverable failures, one of which is assigned to the borderline cases on a substantive rather than a pre-declared criterion.

**The words "test fold" denote two different pools.** In [§3.2](#p6-1) this is 17 runs ($n_{\mathrm{grok}}{=}5$, $n_{\mathrm{non}}{=}12$), in [appendix Q](#p28-6) under the same name stand 9 ($n_{\mathrm{test\_grok}}{=}4$, $n_{\mathrm{test\_non\_grok}}{=}5$). The difference of protocols is noted, the difference of pools is not, and the numbers TPR $0.75$ and a lead time of $273.5$ steps from appendix Q refer to a different fold from the one to which the TPR $0.80$ and the lead time of $1068$ steps of the main text refer.

**Figures 7 and 10 are the same file.** This is so in the source PDF as well: the image on pages 19 and 20 has one and the same internal identifier. The caption of [figure 7](#fig-7) promises "Test-accuracy trajectories at five weight-decay values", while what is shown is an overlay of training and test accuracy, that is, the content of [figure 10](#fig-10). There is therefore no single figure in the paper with test trajectories alone across the five weight-decay values.

**A trace of an unfinished edit in appendix S.** The appendix on transfer to the task family gives not a single number and ends with a sentence saying that transfer claims will be supplied with FPRs "as soon as the corresponding rows are added to the index" ([app. S](#p30-3)). Meanwhile [table 4](#p8-5) already refers to this appendix with a row about "a shorter lead time than mod-add". Similarly, some of the checks in [appendix D.1](#p15-2) are declared without numbers: "we report only the direction and omit the numerical tables".

## What is shown, what is checked and what is admitted as failure

The work is unusual in that it sets these boundaries itself, and the section below merely draws them together. There is nothing **proved** in the paper: appendices A and B retell known properties of one-dimensional Wasserstein space following Villani, and formulate and prove no new statements.

**Measured and presented with a cost of error.** Detection on a held-out fold of 17 runs: AUROC $\approx 0.93$, at the chosen operating point TPR $=0.80$ at FPR $=0.50$, a median lead time of 1068 steps with a bootstrap CI of $[142,\,2426]$ ([p. 6, para. 2](#p6-2)); the width of the intervals is acknowledged as a consequence of the small split, and the authors note separately that the intervals "are not evidence that a fixed operating point is statistically calibrated or confirmed" ([app. O](#p27-3)). The threshold trade-off is written out in full rather than at one convenient point: the instantaneous rule $\tau{=}5\times$ catches all grokking runs at the cost of an FPR of 0.917, $\tau{=}20\times$ lowers the FPR to 0.250 at a recall of 40 % ([table 3](#sec-3-2)). The threshold is declared heuristic and, by the authors' explicit statement, was not selected by optimizing AUROC on any fold ([p. 5, para. 3](#p5-3)).

**Measured on small pools.** Fragility under perturbations: a ratio of short-term deviations of $\approx 3.1\times$ and $\approx 3.2\times$ at two noise scales with four and five runs per scale ([p. 7, para. 1](#p7-1)). The window-level norm control on the same data: the ratio flips from $3.82\times$ to $0.41\times$ ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)). This is the work's strongest argument — and it rests on 21 runs.

**Checked, but not claimed as a result.** The extended weight-decay sweep, the sensitivity to segment size, the architectural ablation, the check against AGOP, the transfer to the task family, CIFAR-10 and the FCN are declared "checks of the boundaries of applicability, not claims of universal robustness" ([p. 2, para. 3](#p2-3)). For two of them the claim is withdrawn entirely: on CIFAR-10 there is not a single detection measure ([app. J](#p23-1)), and for the FCN there is no diagnostic claim about grokking ([app. T](#p30-5)). The check against AGOP did not come off because of coverage: one run, TPR undefined ([app. H](#p20-1)).

**Admitted as failure and not hidden.** Three places, each of which the authors could have omitted. First: on the split with reused seeds 42–45 the same fixed rule raises no alarms at all — TPR $=0$, FPR $=0$ ([app. N](#p26-2)); the threshold is uncalibrated, and this is said outright. Second: at the run level the weight norm separates the regimes far better than the residual ([app. Q](#p28-5)). Third: intervention on alarm does not accelerate grokking and introduces 10 % failures ([app. R](#p29-4)).

**Not shown, contrary to the title.** "Localizing" in the title promises more than the operating point delivers: an FPR of $0.50$ at a base rate of 0.29 means that half the non-grokking runs give a false alarm; the authors themselves describe the purpose of the rule as selecting "candidate transition windows for further analysis" rather than as a decision rule. The median lead time of 1068 steps is comparable with the duration of the phenomenon itself (a mean onset of 3398 steps), and the lower bound of the CI, 142 steps, means an alarm practically at the moment of the transition. That a false alarm is not a speculative danger is shown in the paper itself: [figure 17](#fig-17) presents a non-grokking run whose residual grows while no generalization follows.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

This section is built only from observed formulations in the corpus works that cite this one. The work is new and has been cited only twice so far, so there is one pattern here; it is nevertheless telling, because it distorts exactly the feature of the method that constitutes its novelty.

**"The diagnostic is read off the spectra of weights and activations" (erroneous).** [Verma 2026](../2605.20441.weight-decay-regimes-in-grokking-transformers-cheap-online-diagnostics/2605.20441.weight-decay-regimes-in-grokking-transformers-cheap-online-diagnostics.card.md) retells the design correctly but places the signal where it is not taken from: `"Wang et al. 2026 likewise localize grokking transitions via distributional spectral coordinates (Wasserstein/quantile, Hankel DMD residual, effective rank) on modular-addition Transformer trajectories; this is another transition-localization diagnostic at a different signal location (weight/activation spectra) rather than an attention-head activation order parameter."` In Wang et al. the signal is taken **from the output**: the observable is the distribution of log-probabilities of the correct answer on a fixed probe set of 100 training examples ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)), and the authors stress separately that the construction "uses a chosen output distribution rather than hidden-unit coordinates" and therefore "does not depend on hidden-unit indexing" ([p. 2, para. 1](#p2-1)) — this is claimed as a direct advantage over weight coordinates. Moreover, hidden-state observables were tested and **never once worked**: top-$k$ and hidden raised $0/4$ alarms ([app. P](#p28-2)). The formulation "weight/activation spectra" thereby attributes to the work exactly the signal location it defines itself against; in other respects Verma's retelling is accurate, and the work is correctly assigned to the transition-localization line.

For comparison, an accurate formulation of the same contribution stands in the work `2607.06639` (Golwala 2026, "At-Grok Is Not Converged", no card in the corpus yet): `"Wang et al. [11] map trajectory observables to Wasserstein/quantile coordinates and a Hankel-DMD residual (alongside spectrum and effective rank) and report run-level grok-vs-no-grok discrimination with a threshold/false-alarm/lead-time trade-off"`: the signal location is not named at all, and the false-alarm trade-off is retained. Neither of the two citing works reproduces the number 0.93 without its protocol or attributes universal prediction to the work — the pattern the corpus should expect in future has not been observed here yet.

###### p1-2

In grokking, a model first fits the training data while test accuracy remains low, and only later begins to generalize. We ask whether this transition can be localized from observed training trajectories before the test accuracy rises, and formulate grokking transition localization as a diagnostic problem with an explicit threshold/FPR/lead-time trade-off. Task-dependent observables are summarized as empirical distributions, mapped to Wasserstein/quantile coordinates, and analyzed by Hankel dynamic mode decomposition (DMD); the resulting reconstruction residual, together with spectrum and effective rank, forms the diagnostic output. On held-out modular-addition Transformer runs, the residual achieves AUROC $\approx$ 0.93 for grokking-vs-non-grokking discrimination at the run level; under a fixed sustained-threshold operating rule, true-positive alarms can precede onset, with lead time reported jointly with false-alarm rate and uncertainty intervals. Perturbation experiments show that, in the tested $wd=1$ pool, high-residual windows exhibit about $3\times$ larger short-horizon perturbation deviation than low-residual windows. In a same-data norm-window control, perturbation sensitivity aligns with the residual ordering rather than total-parameter-norm ordering, suggesting that the residual is not merely a total-norm proxy at the window level in the studied $wd=1$ dynamics. Norm signals remain strong run-level regime indicators, and log-probability performs best among the observables tested under the current protocol. We position the residual as a window-level monitoring and localization signal in the studied modular-arithmetic Transformer settings, not a universal early-warning predictor or an intervention rule.

###### p1-3

Grokking is a striking failure mode of standard training summaries: on certain algorithmic tasks, a model fits the training set early yet generalizes only after a long delay, with test-accuracy curves remaining nearly flat throughout the intervening period [31]. The flatness is the difficulty—loss and accuracy alone do not directly indicate when the transition will occur, even in runs that eventually grok. We address a narrow question motivated by this gap: can transition windows be localized from observed training trajectories, using generic distributional observables and an explicit threshold/FPR/lead-time diagnostic protocol? This is a different question from explaining *why* grokking occurs.

###### p1-4

A growing body of work explains grokking through mechanism. Mechanistic interpretability identifies emergent computational circuits [28, 43]; implicit-bias analyses attribute the transition to late-phase norm minimization on the zero-loss manifold [20, 22, 27]; stability-based accounts link grokking to logit scaling and softmax collapse [42, 32]. Norm-growth, logit-scaling, AGOP-based feature emergence [33], and circuit-formation views are the natural reference points for our work. Our goal is complementary: a thresholded localization signal computed from task-**[p. 2]** dependent observables and reported with explicit false-alarm/lead-time trade-offs, intended to flag transition windows rather than explain them.

###### p2-1

We summarize a chosen task-dependent observable $o_{t}$ at each training step as an empirical distribution $\mu_{t}$. Because the diagnostic is computed from the chosen output distribution rather than from hidden-unit coordinates, it does not depend on hidden-unit indexing. Wasserstein/quantile coordinates convert $\mu_{t}$ into a vector observation $z_{t}$, and windowed Hankel dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) provides a local dynamical approximation [38, 15, 2]. The reconstruction residual $\mathrm{Res}^{(r)}$ is the primary diagnostic; the spectrum and effective rank $r_{0.99}$ are auxiliary descriptors interpretable mainly in low-residual windows. Our primary setting takes the observable from the empirical distribution of correct-answer log-probabilities on a fixed probe set in Transformers trained on modular addition; secondary observables and FCN comparisons appear later as scope checks.

Empirically, the residual rises around grokking transitions in the modular-addition Transformer setting. On a held-out test split with fresh seeds, the residual gives nontrivial run-level detection behavior under an explicit threshold/FPR/lead-time trade-off (precise numbers in §3.2 and Table 3). A perturbation experiment shows that high-residual windows exhibit larger short-horizon deviation than low-residual windows under matched noise. An observable ablation finds log-probability to be the best-performing observable among those tested under the current protocol.

###### p3-1

For Transformers on modular addition (primary setting), we fix a probe set $\mathcal{P}=\{(x_{i},y_{i}^{\star})\}_{i=1}^{M}$ ($M=100$ examples), where $y_{i}^{\star}$ is the correct-answer token for input $x_{i}$. At training step $t$ the per-sample observable is the scalar correct-answer log-probability $o_{t,i}=\log p_{\theta_{t}}(y_{i}^{\star}\mid x_{i})$, and the distributional state is the empirical distribution of these $M$ scalars:

$$
\mu_{t}\;:=\;\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\delta_{o_{t,i}}. \tag{1}
$$

###### p3-2

The diagnostic therefore tracks the empirical distribution of correct-answer log-probabilities over a fixed probe set; an averaged loss or accuracy collapses this distribution to a single scalar. Because the construction uses the chosen output distribution rather than hidden-unit coordinates, it does not depend on hidden-unit indexing. Wasserstein/quantile coordinates provide the representation: they convert distribution-valued states into vector-valued observations. Hankel-DMD provides the local dynamical approximation: it analyzes how these vectors evolve over short training windows. FCN observables, used as a secondary low-residual descriptor, are defined in Appendix T.

###### p4-3

As summarized in Figure 1, the residual serves as a validity gate for the auxiliary descriptors. In low-residual windows, the spectrum of $A_{H}^{\star}$ and $r_{0.99}$ can be read as empirical summaries of the local linear-evolution regime. In high-residual windows, the low-rank linear approximation is poor; the residual itself should then be interpreted as a transition or fragility signal, and spectral points and effective rank should not be over-interpreted as stable regime descriptors.

$$
\underbrace{o_{t}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{selected}\\ \text{observable}\end{subarray}}\;\longrightarrow\;\underbrace{\mu_{t}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{distributional}\\ \text{state}\end{subarray}}\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{fixed quantile map}\;}\;\underbrace{z_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{Wasserstein}\\ \text{coordinate}\end{subarray}}\;\xrightarrow{\;\text{windowed Hankel-DMD}\;}\;\underbrace{\bigl(\{\lambda_{j}\},\,r_{0.99},\,\mathrm{Res}^{(r)}\bigr)}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{spectrum / effective rank}\\ \text{/ residual}\end{subarray}}
$$

> *How to read the diagnostics.* Low $\mathrm{Res}^{(r)}$: interpret the spectrum and $r_{0.99}$ as local regime descriptors; high $\mathrm{Res}^{(r)}$: interpret the residual itself as a transition / fragility signal.

###### p9-3

The main value of the reconstruction residual is not to replace mechanistic, norm-based, or gradient-based analyses, but to provide a lightweight window-level readout of when the observed output distribution is being reconfigured. Unlike run-level norm scores, which are strong regime indicators, the residual is evaluated locally on fixed step windows and highlights candidate transition periods for further inspection. The perturbation and norm-window controls suggest that these windows have functional meaning: matched noise produces larger deviations in high-RR windows, and the same runs do not show the same ordering when re-labeled by total parameter norm. Thus, the residual provides a complementary output-distribution view of training dynamics: it can be computed post hoc from a fixed probe set, does not rely on hidden-unit indexing, and helps identify where mechanistic or gradient-based analyses should focus.

###### p9-5

The current implementation assumes a scalar empirical observable represented by one-dimensional quantile coordinates; the failure of top-$k$ and hidden-state observables to trigger any alarm under the current DMD configuration is best interpreted as a limitation of this scalar-distribution implementation and fixed DMD setup rather than as a verdict on those observables’ information content. Extending the diagnostic to genuinely multidimensional observables would require separate geometric embeddings, such as sliced Wasserstein representations, kernel mean embeddings, or layer-wise scalar summaries, and is outside the scope of the present study. The signal is observed across the three tested Transformer scales but with shrinking residual amplitude at small scale (Appendix F); coarse conclusions are stable across segment sizes $\{250,500,1000\}$ while fine ordering may vary (Appendix G). Cross-task transfer is partial, with shorter lead on modular multiplication and explicit failure cases on the fraction/prime sweep (Appendix S).

###### p9-6

Layer-wise observables and multi-dimensional Wasserstein embeddings such as kernel mean embeddings [40] that respect distributional geometry beyond the one-dimensional case; per-signal threshold recalibration for fully fair head-to-head detection comparisons against norm-based baselines; non-autonomous extensions accounting for schedule-induced drift [23]; and large-scale evaluation across architectures and task families. Mechanistic accounts remain complementary: they explain *why* grokking occurs, while the residual addresses *where in training* the transition can be located from chosen task-dependent observables.

###### p13-1

Section 2 introduces how to project a one-dimensional distribution onto a tangent space and obtain a finite-dimensional coordinate representation via fixed quantile levels. However, for multi-dimensional distributions, there is generally no global isometric isomorphism that maps the Wasserstein space into a linear space [44], which creates a practical obstacle to constructing the finite-dimensional snapshot coordinates required in Section 2.2. One may instead use kernel mean embeddings (KME) [40] or multidimensional scaling (MDS) to obtain a finite-dimensional representation, but this typically introduces systematic distortion and does not preserve Wasserstein geometry, limiting the fidelity of the resulting dynamical characterization in the chosen observation space.

###### p30-7

Since the original observation of delayed generalization on algorithmic tasks [31], several lines of work have sought to explain grokking. Mechanistic interpretability identifies the emergence of specific computational circuits—Fourier features for modular addition [28] and circuit-efficiency tradeoffs [43]—and yields progress measures that track circuit formation. Implicit-bias accounts attribute grokking to late-phase norm minimization on the zero-loss manifold [20, 22, 27]; stability-based accounts link it to logit scaling and softmax collapse [42, 32]. These works primarily address why grokking occurs and yield signals tied to specific circuits or mechanisms. Our reconstruction residual addresses a different question—**[p. 31]** window-level transition localization from chosen task-dependent distributional observables—and is intended to complement rather than substitute for these mechanistic accounts.

###### p31-1

A separate line of work tracks training through gradient-derived quantities. The Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) [33] has been used to study feature emergence and grokking-related transitions, providing another route to identifying transition windows. We view our residual as complementary: it is derived from windowed distributional dynamics rather than from gradient outer products, and is intended as a window-level monitoring signal rather than a mechanism-specific progress measure. In our setup, sparse checkpoint coverage prevents a fair head-to-head comparison; AGOP appears in Appendix H as corroborative rather than competitive evidence.

###### p31-2

A large body of work characterizes training through curvature spectra [37, 29, 13], gradient confinement to top Hessian subspaces [14], and the edge-of-stability phenomenon in which the top Hessian eigenvalue saturates near $2/\eta$ [7, 9]. Weight-matrix spectra have been studied via random-matrix theory and heavy-tailed self-regularization [30, 25]. Trajectory-based analyses report low-dimensional structure of parameter evolution [18, 1, 24, 19], correlated dynamics [3], and distinct lazy/rich regimes [16, 6, 17, 45], with the precise scaling determining whether feature learning occurs [46]. Our diagnostic operates on the empirical distribution of a chosen observable rather than on raw parameters or curvature, and is computed over windows rather than as instantaneous summaries; for output-distribution observables the construction does not depend on hidden-unit indexing.
