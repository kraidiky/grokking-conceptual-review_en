## Concepts covered

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work does not compare grokking with a phase transition by analogy but builds a **mapping**: an order parameter, an action, saddles, a mixed phase. The transition is precisely of the **first order** — the minima of the action exist separately before the transition and merely cross in value at the transition point ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). Hence something an analogy cannot give: a finite interval of the control parameter over which two saddles remain degenerate — the mixed phase itself.

**[The order parameter](../../concepts/order-parameter.md)** — a concrete quantity is exhibited: $\Phi=\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}^{*}$, the overlap of a neuron's weights with the teacher's. The network's high-dimensional posterior distribution is reduced to a **scalar** probability of that one quantity ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)). This is a rare case in the corpus in which the order parameter is not postulated but derived from a decoupling of the modes.

**[Effective theory / statistical mechanics](../../concepts/effective-theory-statistical-mechanics.md)** — grokking is studied as an **equilibrium (Bayesian) phenomenon** governed by the sample size, the noise or the width rather than as a phenomenon in time ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). A Langevin dynamics with weight decay gives a posterior distribution coinciding with Bayesian inference under a Gaussian prior; from there the ordinary machinery of mean field and saddle points takes over.

**[Feature learning](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — a classification is introduced rather than a single notion: **GFL** (the weights fluctuate Gaussianly around a unique minimum at zero), **GMFL-I** (a mixture of the zero and non-zero saddles — some neurons "know" about the teacher and some do not) and **GMFL-II** (the zero saddle is suppressed) ([`p5-5`](#p5-5)). Grokking begins at the entry into GMFL-I. The neurons fluctuating around a teacher-aware saddle the authors call **droplets** — by a direct analogy with droplets of water in the water-vapour transition ([`p4-4`](#p4-4)).

**[The NTK](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** and **[from lazy to rich](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the GFL phase is "smoothly connected to the GP limit", that is, to the lazy regime, and grokking is the departure from it. The point of divergence from Kumar et al.: there the lazy-to-rich transition is described as **dynamical**, here as an equilibrium phase transition in a control parameter.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the second model is taken directly from Gromov: a two-hot input, a quadratic activation, a $\delta$ function as the target ([`p3-3`](#p3-3)). It matters that the theory **reproduces** his solution: the weights near the saddle with $\Phi,\Psi\neq 0$ correspond to his cosine expressions, and the phase constraint $\varphi^{(1)}+\varphi^{(2)}=\varphi^{(3)}$ becomes unnecessary here because the formalism marginalises over the readout layer ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). Two independent derivations of one and the same solution.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — after the transition the network produces internal representations of the teacher that are **sharply different** from the previous ones ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). "Sharply" here is not rhetoric: before the transition there is one representation, after it another, and there are no intermediate ones by the construction of a first-order transition.

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — a mechanism of reducing the **sample complexity** is claimed ([`p2-3`](#p2-3)). Countably: the NNGP kernel of a fully connected network imposes a uniform prior over cubic polynomials, of which there are $O(d^{3})$ — learning them would require $n\approx 5\cdot 10^{5}$ points; a finite network manages at $n=3000$, two orders of magnitude earlier ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)). The explanation: the features are learned from the easily available linear components of the target and applied to the non-linear ones.

**[Critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — there are three control parameters here and they are interchangeable: the sample size, the noise and the width. Reducing $\sigma^{2}$ is equivalent to raising $n$, since only the combination $n/\sigma^{2}$ enters the theory; and the transition in appendix D is induced by **reducing the width** from 2800 to 700. This places the critical dataset size on a par with the other control parameters rather than above them.

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — feature learning is defined as a deviation of the hidden kernels from those of a random network; the kernel develops **peaks in the direction of the target** ([`p1-4`](#p1-4)). In the conclusion a practical proposal is drawn from this: pruning and regularisation by removing the low-lying eigenvalues of the hidden kernel ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — one is proposed but not checked: to track **outliers** in the distributions of the weights or the pre-activations along the dominant eigenvectors of the kernel, so as to tell whether the model is close to grokking ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)). This is a direct consequence of the droplet picture — an outlier is the first droplet.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — enters the setting twice. As the $\gamma$ of the Langevin dynamics it sets the covariance of the prior ([`p3-8`](#p3-8)); in the modular-algebra model a **non-linear** term $\Gamma\propto(w_{k}w_{-k})^{3}$ is additionally introduced, chosen so that the modes decouple. The authors caveat that for a more natural $\sum_{n}w_{n}^{6}$ the results are qualitatively the same but were derived approximately.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the notion shifts: in the main part grokking means a **jump of the discrepancy** $a$ (the test RMSE) at the transition rather than a delay in time ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)). Over the GMFL-I interval the discrepancy falls by $30\%$ ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)). A delayed generalisation in the customary sense is shown separately, in appendix D, and already outside the equivalent-kernel limit.

Cards of corpus papers: [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — the source of the second model and of the solution derived anew here from the posterior; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the work in which the connection of grokking with a phase transition was conjectured but not derived; [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — the same departure from the lazy regime, but as a dynamics rather than as an equilibrium; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — the competition of circuits, mentioned in the survey as an alternative explanation; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — the preceding "effective theory" with one trainable layer, whose limitation this work removes.

**[The role of gradient noise](../../concepts/gradient-noise.md)** — the "temperature" here enters the setting rather than the explanation of trajectories: the training is a Langevin dynamics run to equilibrium ([`p3-8`](#p3-8)), and the noise is part of the Bayesian frame. Carrying the conclusions over to the corpus's deterministic and full-batch settings is an open question; the chain of noiseless setups is gathered in the concept card.

**[The phase diagram](../../concepts/phase-diagram.md)** — a rare case of an analytical diagram: it is obtained by numerically solving the resulting equation rather than by a grid of runs ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)), and therefore predicts a boundary where no experiments were run.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). This is the corpus's most theoretical work: nearly everything in it is derived rather than measured, and it is therefore more important than usual to distinguish what exactly is derived and in what limit.

**The mapping is built rather than claimed.** Grokking is not compared with a phase transition by analogy: an order parameter, an action, the saddles and the degeneracy condition are written out, and for the modular model the transition point is determined analytically ([`p6-6`](#p6-6)). After half a dozen works in which "phase transition" is a figure of speech, this is a qualitatively different level.

**The predictions were confirmed where they could be checked.** A finite probability of finding a neuron with $\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}^{*}\approx1$ — that is, a droplet — is predicted before the experiment and is visible in histograms over 200 trained networks ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)). The agreement improves exactly as the theory requires — as the scale parameters grow.

**The main caveat is made by the authors themselves.** The equivalent-kernel approximation **washes out** the phenomena of generalisation: within it there is no train-test gap by construction. That is, the principal analytical result describes feature learning rather than delayed generalisation. The connection with observed grokking is restored numerically and only in appendix D — on 50 networks, three values of the width and one set of hyperparameters.

**"Grokking" here does not mean what it does in the rest of the corpus.** In the main part it names a jump of the discrepancy $a$ under a change of the control parameter, and over the mixed-phase interval the discrepancy falls by $30\%$ ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)) — not a rise to a near-perfect accuracy. Reading the work as an explanation of grokking in general, one may easily fail to notice this substitution.

**The training is Langevin, not SGD.** All the results refer to an equilibrium ensemble of fully trained networks equivalent to Bayesian inference ([`p3-8`](#p3-8)). A carry-over to ordinary optimisers is not justified in the paper; and this matters, because the whole of the rest of the corpus studies precisely the trajectories of SGD and Adam.

**The gain in sample complexity is shown indirectly.** The numbers are striking — $n=3000$ against $\approx 5\cdot 10^{5}$ ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)) — but the comparison is with an $O(d^{3})$ estimate from another work rather than with a run of an infinitely wide network. The authors write outright that a proof requires further work.

**The decoupling of the modes in the modular model is bought by the choice of regulariser.** The non-linear term $\Gamma\propto(w_{k}w_{-k})^{3}$ is chosen so that the modes separate; for the natural $\sum_{n}w_{n}^{6}$ it is only said that the results are "qualitatively similar", with no numbers. And there are no training experiments with this model at all — only a numerical solution of the equation for $a$.

**The most valuable thing for the corpus is not the explanation but the junction with Gromov.** The formalism reproduces his cosine solution as a saddle of the posterior and removes one of his restrictions along the way: the phase condition $\varphi^{(1)}+\varphi^{(2)}=\varphi^{(3)}$ turns out to be an artefact of a fixed readout layer rather than a property of the solution ([`p9-1`](#p9-1)). Two independent derivations of the same answer are a rarity.

**What has remained a supposition.** The generalisation to deep networks — "we believe". A measure of proximity to grokking through outliers along the eigenvectors of the kernel — a proposal with no check. Pruning by the low-lying eigenvalues — likewise ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)). Each of the three looks checkable and none is checked.

**Its place in the corpus.** With Kumar et al. the relation is not worked out, although the same distinction is described — the lazy regime against the rich one — only there as a dynamics and here as an equilibrium. With Nanda et al. all the more so: their argument that the "suddenness" is an artefact of the metric is directly opposite to the claim of a first-order transition, but it is not discussed in the paper.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns of erroneous or over-extended citation of this work observed in the citing papers of the corpus. Each entry describes what exactly is distorted in the paraphrase and leads to the authors' own paragraphs, where the lost caveat stands. The analysis of particular formulations is in the citing works' cards, in their "Erroneous citations" sections, to which the "details" links lead. The regularity with this work: the shorter the citation, the more accurate it is — the list-style "phase transition / mean-field" references are error-free, and it is the extended paraphrases that confabulate.

**A projection of training dynamics onto an equilibrium theory (erroneous).** The work is cited as a model of "phase transitions during training" and included in lists of dynamical lazy-to-rich theories. In the authors' account, however, grokking is studied [as an equilibrium Bayesian phenomenon governed by the sample size, the noise and the width](#p2-1) — there is no training time in the theory, and ["training" is a Langevin dynamics run to equilibrium, an equivalent of Bayesian inference](#p3-8), not an SGD trajectory. A delayed generalisation in time appears only numerically, in appendix D. A related inversion: "a mean field in which the hidden weights barely depart from their initialisation" — whereas [in the authors' account the kernel adapts and the droplet neurons travel towards the teacher's solution](#p7-1). The details: [2511.12768](../2511.12768.evidence-of-phase-transitions-in-small-transformer-based-language-models/2511.12768.evidence-of-phase-transitions-in-small-transformer-based-language-models.card.md#mc-2310-03789), [2402.16726](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md#mc-2310-03789), [2509.21519](../2509.21519.provable-scaling-laws-of-feature-emergence-from-learning-dynamics-of-grokking/2509.21519.provable-scaling-laws-of-feature-emergence-from-learning-dynamics-of-grokking.card.md#mc-2310-03789); a milder echo: [2603.01192](../2603.01192.a-basin-selection-perspective-on-grokking-via-singular-learning-theory/2603.01192.a-basin-selection-perspective-on-grokking-via-singular-learning-theory.card.md).

**The ascription of first-order attributes the work does not have (erroneous).** Those who cite it supplement the picture with hysteresis, an "entropy barrier" and the kinetics of an escape from a "memorisation basin". In the authors' account [the saddles exist separately and remain degenerate in the mixed phase](#p6-6) — there is neither a barrier, nor hysteresis, nor metastability in the paper (the words hysteresis, barrier and metastable do not occur in the original at all); and [the zero saddle is "unaware of the teacher" rather than memorising](#p5-5). The details: [2511.12768](../2511.12768.evidence-of-phase-transitions-in-small-transformer-based-language-models/2511.12768.evidence-of-phase-transitions-in-small-transformer-based-language-models.card.md#mc-2310-03789), [2606.17120](../2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking/2606.17120.noise-driven-escape-from-metastable-phases-explains-grokking.card.md#mc-2310-03789).

**"Observed grokking on modular arithmetic" (erroneous, mild).** In lists of empirical observations the work stands beside experimental ones — but its modular model is solved analytically and numerically, and there are no training experiments with it; the observations of the delay are on the teacher–student model of appendix D. A milder echo: [2310.19470](../2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks/2310.19470.bridging-lottery-ticket-and-grokking-understanding-grokking-from-inner-structure-of-networks.card.md).

Examples of careful citation: [2603.24746](../2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition/2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition.card.md) — separates the motivation from the proof and checks the prediction ("motivate first-order expectations… the present finite-width data do not yet show coexistence"); [2410.04489](../2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability/2410.04489.grokking-at-the-edge-of-linear-separability.card.md) — a hedged extension "(though not necessarily stated in these terms)"; [2511.04760](../2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold/2511.04760.when-data-falls-short-grokking-below-the-critical-threshold.card.md) — "a first-order phase transition in feature learning", retaining the domain of applicability.
