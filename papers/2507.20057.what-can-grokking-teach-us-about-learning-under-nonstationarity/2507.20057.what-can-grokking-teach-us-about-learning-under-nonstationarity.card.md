## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work changes the notion's role: grokking here is not a phenomenon to be explained but a measuring instrument. The definition is taken from Power et al. 2022 word for word ([p1-5](#p1-5)), and the section on modular arithmetic says outright: "we focus on *using* grokking as an indicator of feature learning in the network" ([p5-4](#p5-4)). The point of that role is that a change of features has no scale in itself, whereas grokking gives a sharp and unambiguously readable external jump of the test accuracy. The chief observation for the corpus: grokking is induced on demand — after several hundred thousand steps of training at a deliberately low rate, switching on a learning-rate schedule produces grokking ([p7-2](#p7-2), [fig-3](#fig-3)), that is, the delayed generalisation turns out to be a controllable event rather than a property of the initial conditions. What the work does not contain: neither a theory of grokking nor an analysis of the representation of its own — the corpus's mechanistic results are taken as ready knowledge ([p3-1](#p3-1)); the training-accuracy curves are not given at all ([p5-5](#p5-5)), so the memorisation phase that defines the phenomenon is shown in no figure; and priority is misassigned — the first notable observation is named as Liu et al. 2022a rather than Power et al. 2022 ([p3-1](#p3-1)).

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work merely inherits the notion and weakens it in one place. Grokking is defined through memorisation ([p1-5](#p1-5)), and the diagram in [fig-1](#fig-1) allots the memorisation phase a band of its own and names as its cause the absence of feature learning. But the phase is measured only indirectly: the authors admit outright that they do not plot the training accuracy "for want of space" and merely assert that nearly all the networks reach a perfect or near-perfect training accuracy usually within a thousand steps ([p5-5](#p5-5)). A separate subtlety of the measurement, which the authors name themselves: the pairs $(x,y)$ and $(y,x)$ are not distinguished in the split, so a network that has learnt the symmetry alone scores about 20 % on the test, and in Figure 2 the networks at times fall *below* that floor ([p15-2](#p15-2)) — that is, the plateau here is neither flat nor zero.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the work uses the language of phase transitions, but as someone else's vocabulary rather than as an instrument of its own. Grokking is called "a phase transition from „lazy“ to rich training dynamics" in a survey paragraph ([p3-1](#p3-1)), and in the same place the observation of Thilak et al. 2022 is adduced that phase transitions in a model's ability to generalise are accompanied by periods of instability and a rapid growth of the parameter norm ([p3-2](#p3-2)). There is no order parameter, no critical value and no analysis of the transition's sharpness in the work; the authors describe their own transitions with the words "onset" and "induce" rather than as a critical phenomenon.

**[The slingshot effect](../../concepts/slingshot.md)** — the notion enters by a single reference: Thilak et al. 2022 are named as the source of the observation that transitions in generalisation are accompanied by periods of instability in the optimisation dynamics and a rapid growth of the parameter norm ([p3-2](#p3-2)). The work measures neither slingshots nor loss spikes and does not discuss any relation of its own ELR re-warming to the slingshot — even though both are a transient increase of the step.

**[Catastrophic forgetting](../../concepts/catastrophic-forgetting.md)** — the work comes to the corpus from the side of continual learning and brings with it an adjacent phenomenon the corpus did not have before: *primacy bias* — a spoiling of generalisation on later tasks by early data ([p1-3](#p1-3), [p1-5](#p1-5)). Primacy bias is not forgetting: the network loses not what it has learnt but the ability to learn something new well, and the authors expressly subsume under that name the "critical periods" of Achille et al. 2017 too ([p1-3](#p1-3)). The work's contribution on that side is a conjecture about a shared mechanism: the same course of feature learning by which a network replaces memorising features with generalising ones during grokking allows previously *learnt* features to be overwritten as well ([p2-1](#p2-1)); in the warm-starting setting it is put still more plainly — "the network must learn to overwrite previously learnt features — a task that may prove substantially harder than the grokking regime analysed earlier" ([p7-3](#p7-3)). What is absent: the commonality is declared a conjecture rather than shown — all that coincides is that one intervention helps in both settings; and no measure of feature learning is applied to both settings at once.

**[The emergence of features](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the work gives the corpus two ready-to-use measures of feature learning: the shift of the normalised feature covariance $\Delta_{C}^{\ell}$ ([p4-3](#p4-3), formula 3, after the definition of Yang et al. 2022) and the shift of the binary pattern of ReLU activations $\Delta_{A}^{\ell}$ ([p4-4](#p4-4), formula 4), introduced precisely because the covariance does not capture how far the network uses its nonlinearities. Both measures are increments over an interval $T$, that is, a rate rather than a state. In [fig-4](#fig-4) they behave in step with the learning rate: they rise and then fall with each cycle. What the work does not contain: no absolute scale for these quantities (which is why grokking was needed as an external marker), no analysis of *which* features arise, and no application of either measure to the grokking setting — in section 4 the features are measured by the fraction of activation patterns changed ([fig-3](#fig-3)) and by the rank of the attention outputs ([p16-2](#p16-2)).

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — the notion enters as one of two disputing sides that the work undertakes to adjudicate: Varma et al. 2023 "argue the reverse: grokking occurs when the optimiser converges on an „efficient“ circuit that generalises well, and from that moment weight decay can lower the parameter norm without harming the accuracy" ([p3-2](#p3-2)). The work's experiment is arranged not as a check of circuit efficiency but as a separation of the parameter norm from the ratio of the step and parameter norms ([p5-6](#p5-6)); the conclusion is that "there is a grain of truth in both points of view". The work measures neither circuits nor their cost; the architecture, meanwhile, is borrowed precisely from Varma et al. 2023 ([p5-5](#p5-5)).

**[The lazy→rich transition](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — this is the frame in which the whole work is written, but it is taken ready-made. Feature learning is defined outright in opposition to the lazy regime, "in which the dynamics mimics that of a linear model with respect to some fixed kernel" ([p1-5](#p1-5)), and the reduction of grokking to a lazy→rich transition is presented as established by predecessors ([p3-1](#p3-1), with references to Xu et al. 2024, Kumar et al. 2024, Lyu et al. 2024). Its own contribution is a lever: the rich dynamics, it turns out, can be switched on at an arbitrary moment of the training by raising the ratio of the step norm to the parameter norm ([p5-3](#p5-3), [p7-2](#p7-2)). What is absent: the work computes neither a kernel nor its change; "lazy" and "rich" remain words, and what is measured is $\Delta_{C}$ and $\Delta_{A}$.

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the notion enters as a vocabulary for describing what determines the regime at initialisation: the NTK regime "holds the kernel fixed throughout the training, ensuring an $O(1)$ change of the network's outputs by balancing vanishing parameter updates against a growth of the network's Jacobian" ([p4-6](#p4-6)). From there too is taken the list of three quantities that set the regime — the initial parameter norm, the learning rate and the layer's output multiplier. The work computes no kernel and checks no claims about width.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — the one place where the work looks inside the network, and it is an incidental result of appendix B.1: grokking stably coincides with a fall in the rank of the attention heads' outputs, and in networks with layer normalisation lowering that rank requires lowering the norm either of the attention heads' inputs or of the key and query matrices ([p16-2](#p16-2), [fig-8](#fig-8)). The same mechanism explains the negative result of the main text: layer normalisation increases the attention inputs, whereupon the attention matrices have "a far lower entropy and are harder to optimise", and a network with projection cannot correct this until weight decay is applied to the normalisation's scale parameters — a device the authors call "scale decay" ([p6-1](#p6-1), [fig-2](#fig-2)). What is absent: an analysis of what the heads compute; the rank here is a counter rather than a reading.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the task is taken as a working bench, and its setting departs from the corpus's usual one in two places at once. The modulus is 117, a composite number ($9\cdot 13$), whereas the corpus almost everywhere takes a prime; the consequences of a composite modulus (non-trivial zero divisors, a decomposition into subgroups) are nowhere discussed. The input is written as $x\cdot y=\square$, while the correct answer is declared to be $x+y\mod 117$ ([p5-5](#p5-5)). There is one task and no sweep of operations.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the work inherits the frame of Power et al. 2022 through Varma et al. 2023, whose architecture is directly repeated, but shifts three of its supports: a training fraction $\rho=0.2$ instead of the canonical half, a modulus of 117 instead of a prime, and a budget of 1 million steps ([p5-5](#p5-5)); the architecture is a transformer with one attention block, a two-layer MLP and absolute positional encoding, with no bias terms and RMSNorm by default ([p15-1](#p15-1)). A departure from the proving ground of substance for the reader: the training accuracy is not plotted, and grokking is identified only by the late step in the test curve ([p5-5](#p5-5)); and the test curve itself has a floor of 20 % on account of $(x,y)$ and $(y,x)$ not being distinguished ([p15-2](#p15-2)).

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the training fraction is fixed at $\rho=0.2$ and is never swept ([p5-5](#p5-5)); the work looks for no critical size. Alongside, in the section on warm-starting, the data fraction acts as a control parameter of another kind: a network is pretrained on 10 %, 50 % or 100 % of CIFAR-10 and then fine-tuned on the full set ([p7-4](#p7-4)), and the chief result is a convergence to one and the same final performance regardless of the initial fraction ([p7-5](#p7-5), [fig-4](#fig-4)).

**[Real data and vision](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — CIFAR-10 enters the work not as a proving ground for grokking but as a second, independent check of the same lever: convolutional networks and a ResNet-18 with no bias terms, 70 epochs on a fraction of the data, then the addition of the remainder and 70 more epochs after the protocol of Ash & Adams 2020 ([p7-4](#p7-4), [p15-1](#p15-1)). The work neither shows nor claims grokking on vision data; what it shows is that ELR re-warming closes the generalisation gap produced by warm-starting ([p7-5](#p7-5)). A separate negative result for the corpus: the scale of the last linear layer, on whose importance for grokking in vision tasks Liu et al. 2022b insisted, had no marked effect here ([p8-3](#p8-3), [fig-5](#fig-5)).

**[Weight decay / L2 regularisation](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work inverts weight decay's customary role: it acts not as a cause of generalisation but as one of two ways of raising the ratio of the step norm to the parameter norm, and is therefore interchangeable with the learning rate — "a larger weight decay can compensate for a smaller learning rate and vice versa" ([fig-2](#fig-2)). The causal question is posed outright: "did a smaller parameter norm contribute to a better-generalising solution, or did the network's finding a generalising solution allow it to lower the norm without harming the accuracy?" ([p5-6](#p5-6)). The answer is experimental: with a periodic projection of the weights the generalisation is fast and stable across seeds, whereas networks with a free norm need a large weight decay for the same, and the influence of the learning rate proves stronger than that of the weight decay ([p5-6](#p5-6)). A second, separate use of weight decay is "scale decay" on the scale parameters of the normalisation layers ([p6-1](#p6-1)).

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work gives the corpus a clear negative argument: weight decay is not necessary for grokking, because holding the norm replaces it. Networks with a periodic projection of the parameters to their initial norm grok fast and stably, and there weight decay is not needed, its effect being precisely "blunted" by the projection ([fig-2](#fig-2), [p5-6](#p5-6)). A caveat that must not be lost: a high ELR alone is not enough either — with layer normalisation a network with projection does not generalise at all until "scale decay" is added ([p6-1](#p6-1)), so what proves necessary is not some one regulariser but a combination of a sufficient change of representation and a workable landscape.

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the work gives different expressions for the effective learning rate under different optimisers: $\tilde{\eta}(\theta)=\frac{\eta}{\Vert\theta\Vert^{2}}$ for gradient descent and $\tilde{\eta}(\theta)=\frac{\eta}{\Vert\theta\Vert}$ for adaptive optimisers such as RMSProp and Adam, which approximate the update $\frac{\nabla f(\theta)}{\Vert\nabla f(\theta)\Vert}$ ([p3-4](#p3-4)). Adam is used in every experiment ([p15-2](#p15-2), [p15-6](#p15-6)); there is no sweep of optimisers, and the question of whether the re-warming depends on adaptivity is raised only in appendix C, in which it is shown that with normalised parameters and a step-decaying optimiser a re-warming on a stationary task is harmless, while on a changed task it accelerates convergence ([p22-2](#p22-2)).

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work enters the corpus's dispute about the norm and proposes to settle it by changing the quantity: what matters is not the parameter norm in itself but its ratio to the step norm. The dispute is named outright — the "Goldilocks zone" of Liu et al. 2022b against the "efficient circuit" of Varma et al. 2023 — and the work "builds a bridge towards a causal understanding of that relation… and finds a grain of truth in both points of view" ([p3-2](#p3-2)). The experimental argument: grokking sets in at a large effective learning rate "even if the parameter norm never falls below its initial value" ([p5-6](#p5-6)). What is absent: neither a claim that the generalising solution is of minimal norm nor a measurement of the norm after the grokking — the work disputes the norm's role rather than its value.

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — the initial parameter norm is named as one of the three quantities that set the regime of the dynamics at initialisation ([p4-6](#p4-6)), and appendix C carries this to an estimate: on scaling the first layer by $\alpha$ the expected rotation of the features goes as $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{\eta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\sigma_{g}^{2}}}$ and the probability of a ReLU neuron's switching as $\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\eta\sigma_{g}}$ ([p22-1](#p22-1)). Whence the work's chief conclusion in its most distinct form: "at large parameter norms the learning rate must be raised correspondingly in order to produce the same effective change in the representation's activation patterns". What is absent: a sweep of initialisation scales in the experiments and any proof — appendix C the authors themselves call a justification, and the gradients in it are modelled by a Gaussian perturbation.

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — this is the work's chief lever, and it introduces for it a quantity the corpus has hardly used: the *effective learning rate* (ELR), a scale-invariant reading of the learning rate that accounts for the parameter norm ([p3-3](#p3-3), [p3-4](#p3-4)). The method proposed is "ELR re-warming": a modification of Normalize-and-Project such that the ELR does not merely avoid sagging but is periodically raised — by a schedule or by a change-point detection (CUSUM) on the loss ([p5-3](#p5-3), Algorithm 1). Three experimental consequences matter for the corpus: a re-warming to 0.01 induces grokking while one to 0.001 does not ([fig-3](#fig-3)); the re-warming works even hundreds of thousands of steps after the start ([p7-2](#p7-2)); and sometimes closing the gap fully requires a re-warming *above* the initial value of the rate, that is, "putting it back as it was" is not enough ([p18-2](#p18-2), [fig-13](#fig-13)). The reverse side is named by the authors: raising the ELR raises the instability too, and so it must be transient ([p2-6](#p2-6), [p10-2](#p10-2)).

**[The Goldilocks zone](../../concepts/goldilocks-zone.md)** — the notion enters as a named position of others: Liu et al. 2022b "describe grokking as a convergence of the parameter norm to a „Goldilocks zone“ (Fort & Scherlis 2019) permitting generalisation" ([p3-2](#p3-2)). The work neither seeks nor measures that zone; its own argument shifts the sought "just right" quantity from the parameter norm to the ratio of the step and parameter norms, but does not call it a "zone".

**[The edge of stability](../../concepts/edge-of-stability.md)** — the notion enters a survey paragraph about the loss of plasticity: the curvature and the effective step size are related "through the *catapult mechanism* (Lewkowycz et al. 2020), implicit regularisation (Barrett & Dherin 2020) and edge-of-stability dynamics (Cohen et al. 2021; Agarwala et al. 2022; Roulet et al. 2023)" ([p1-3](#p1-3) — the framing, [p3-1](#p3-1) — an adjacent survey). One application is nonetheless substantive: escaping a local minimum is proposed by raising the learning rate "to a value exceeding the sharpness of the local basin (Lewkowycz et al. 2020)" ([p7-5](#p7-5)). The work measures neither sharpness nor Hessian eigenvalues.

**[The spherical norm constraint](../../concepts/spherical-weight-norm-constraint.md)** — the work gives the corpus a ready working form of this intervention: Normalize and Project inserts normalisation layers before every nonlinearity and periodically projects each linear layer's weights onto the unit sphere in the Frobenius norm, formula 2 ([p3-4](#p3-4)); the projection may be applied every $k$ steps — either for cheapness or as a device intermediate between projection and norm regularisation ([p4-1](#p4-1)). The projection's role here is twofold and both sides matter: it makes the ELR directly readable off the learning-rate schedule ([p4-1](#p4-1)) and it is itself a necessary component of the method, without which the re-warming loses its force ([p8-1](#p8-1), [fig-5](#fig-5)). An incidental result worth knowing: with layer normalisation the projection deprives the network of its only route to lowering the norm of the attention inputs, and the generalisation disappears entirely ([p6-1](#p6-1)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work only mentions the notion, but in a rare role: progress measures are named as a means able "to identify the emergence of these generalising features even before the test performance rises, at times tracking along the way the formation of partially generalising solutions" ([p3-1](#p3-1), with references to Nanda et al. 2023 and Varma et al. 2023). The work's own quantities — $\Delta_{C}$ and $\Delta_{A}$ — are not called progress measures and are not advanced as precursors: they measure the rate of change of the features rather than proximity to a generalising solution.

**[Selective weight decay](../../concepts/selective-weight-decay.md)** — a rare converse case: the decay is imposed where it is usually removed. Under layer normalisation a large effective learning rate is not enough, and the norm of the attention heads' inputs is lowered by applying weight decay to the layernorm scale parameters ([`fig-2`](#fig-2)).
## Points we could not reconcile

**The abstract inverts the ratio by which the ELR is defined.** In the abstract the effective learning rate is called "the ratio between the parameter and update norms" ([p1-2](#p1-2)), whereas in section 2.3 it is defined exactly the other way round, as $\tilde{\eta}(\theta)=\frac{\eta}{\Vert\theta\Vert^{2}}$ (and $\frac{\eta}{\Vert\theta\Vert}$ for Adam), that is, as the ratio of the step to the parameter norm ([p3-4](#p3-4)). Read literally, the abstract would make "raising the ELR" mean growing the parameter norm — the very thing the work counteracts. The abstract's formulation is already being carried over by citing works (see the section on erroneous retellings below).

**Algorithm 1 is assigned to the wrong section.** The introduction says that the method is given "by Algorithm 1 in Section 3.1" ([p2-4](#p2-4)), but Section 3.1 is the feature-learning measures ([p4-3](#p4-3)), and the algorithm itself stands in Section 3.2 ([p5-3](#p5-3)).

**The number of layers.** In the main text the architecture is described as "a transformer with one attention block" ([p5-5](#p5-5)), in the appendix as "one attention block, then a two-layer MLP", and in Table 1 stands `number of layers = 2` ([p15-1](#p15-1)). What exactly counts as a layer is not said.

**The statement of the task does not agree with the answer.** The input is written as $x\cdot y=\square$, while the correct answer is declared to be $x+y\mod 117$ ([p5-5](#p5-5)); the operation sign in the input and the operation in the answer are different.

**The cycle period in RL is named in two different units.** In the main text it is "4 million optimiser steps", and ten cycles are equated with 40 million frames ([p10-2](#p10-2)); in appendix A.3, "4 million environment steps" ([p15-6](#p15-6)). At the replay ratio of 1 declared in the same place these two quantities do not coincide.

**The low-UTD result undermines the argument for re-warming.** The work's design requires the benefit to come from a periodic return into the region of high ELR. But in the low-UTD regime the authors themselves conclude that the schedules converge to roughly the same performance "regardless of the number of cycles applied" and that the benefit "can be ascribed chiefly to sufficient time spent at the key learning rates rather than to the network's being exposed to higher ELRs later in the training" ([p9-3](#p9-3), [fig-6](#fig-6)) — that is, it is precisely the repetition that gives nothing here.

**The training budget in the text and in an appendix figure differs by a factor of two.** In the experimental setting it is said that the network is trained for 1 million steps ([p5-5](#p5-5)), and Figure 3 speaks of a performance persisting "even after a million optimiser steps" ([fig-3](#fig-3)); the step axis of Figure 8 meanwhile runs to $2\cdot 10^{6}$, and some of the runs grok after the 1-million mark ([fig-8](#fig-8)). Which budget pertains to which set of runs is nowhere said.

**Appendix B.5 refers to the wrong figure.** The section is titled and opened by an analysis of Figure 13, but the conclusion is supported by a reference "to the plot in the right-hand part of Figure 5", in which the change-point detection is shown, while the growth of the rank at issue is depicted in Figure 8 ([p18-2](#p18-2), [fig-5](#fig-5), [fig-13](#fig-13)).

**Traces of an unfinished edit.** Checked against the PDF and not artefacts of the conversion: in [p7-1](#p7-1) a sentence breaks off mid-way — "the learning rate should be reduced to improve stability and In Figure 3 we apply…" (`should be reduced to improve stability and In Figure 3 we apply`), and in the same place stands `we train a transform` instead of `transformer`; in [p6-1](#p6-1) the introduction of the method's name is doubled (`a technique which we refer to as which we refer to by the term 'scale decay'`); and in [p15-6](#p15-6) `reply ratio` is written instead of `replay ratio`. The typos are translated by sense, and the broken sentence is left broken.

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

**Conjectured.** The shared mechanism of grokking and primacy bias is a conjecture, and the authors call it so: the work "conjectures that the same feature-learning dynamics that promotes generalisation under grokking also underlies the ability to overwrite previously *learnt* features" ([p1-2](#p1-2), [p2-1](#p2-1)). Figure 1 is a diagram of that correspondence rather than a measurement ([fig-1](#fig-1)). Whence too the abstract's hedged claim that methods accelerating grokking are "promising candidates" against primacy bias: exactly one such method is tested, their own, and not one accelerator of grokking known to the corpus is tried in the non-stationary setting. The work's second conjecture is hedged too — about cycling's suitability for RL in particular: "We hypothesise that *cycling* the learning rate may therefore suit reinforcement learning especially well" ([p9-1](#p9-1)).

**Shown by experiment.** The separation of the parameter norm from the effective learning rate by a double sweep — at a free norm and at a periodic projection ([p5-6](#p5-6), [fig-2](#fig-2)); the necessity of "scale decay" under layer normalisation ([p6-1](#p6-1)); the inducement of grokking on demand hundreds of thousands of steps in, and a threshold in the magnitude of the re-warming ([p7-2](#p7-2), [fig-3](#fig-3)); the closing of the warm-starting gap and an ablation of both components of the method ([p7-5](#p7-5), [p8-1](#p8-1)); the gain in RL from early cycling with a subsequent annealing ([p10-2](#p10-2)) — together with an acknowledged price: in the high-UTD regime the spread in score is large "chiefly because of the instability of large learning rates" ([p10-1](#p10-1)); and the coincidence of grokking with a fall in the rank of the attention outputs ([p16-2](#p16-2)).

**Justified by argument but not proved.** The whole of appendix C: the gradients are modelled by a Gaussian perturbation, and the derivation of the ReLU switching probability rests on a Stack Overflow answer given in a footnote ([p21-1](#p21-1), [p22-1](#p22-1)). There is no theory of grokking and no theory of primacy bias in the work: the relation between them rests on the diagram of Figure 1 and on one intervention's helping in both settings; and the conclusion sums up in the same cautious form — the method is "powerful" but must be applied with care ([p10-3](#p10-3)).

**What to keep in mind when citing the numbers.** The training curves are not given ([p5-5](#p5-5)); the test accuracy has a floor of 20 % on account of $(x,y)$ and $(y,x)$ not being distinguished ([p15-2](#p15-2)); and there are no compute-matched comparisons with other remedies against primacy bias (Shrink and Perturb, spectral regularisation) in the warm-starting section, although both are named in the introduction ([p1-3](#p1-3), [p7-5](#p7-5)).

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns noticed in the citing works of the corpus; for each it is said what exactly is distorted, with references to the authors' paragraphs. Four works of the corpus citing this paper have been surveyed, and their retellings are on the whole accurate — the more important, then, are the two places in which a formulation diverges from the text.

**"The ELR is the ratio of the parameter norm to the update norm" (erroneous — and generated by the paper itself).** Truong et al. 2026 (`2606.13753`, no card) retells the work thus: `"Lyle et al. 2025 argue that what drives the transition is the *effective learning rate* — the ratio of parameter norm to update norm — rather than the parameter norm on its own"`. The second half of the sentence is accurate and repeats [p5-6](#p5-6) almost word for word; the first inverts the definition: in [p3-4](#p3-4) the ELR is $\frac{\eta}{\Vert\theta\Vert^{2}}$, that is, the ratio of the step to the parameter norm. The pedigree of the error is traceable: it is exactly thus, inverted, that the ratio is named in the paper's own abstract ([p1-2](#p1-2)), and the citing author reproduced the authors' formulation. The warning concerns the formulation rather than the work: the substantive conclusion it conveys correctly.

**"The intervention removes the grokking delay" (widening).** [Saheb Pasand and Dohmatob 2026 (2510.04930)](../2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking/2510.04930.egalitarian-gradient-descent-a-simple-approach-to-accelerated-grokking.card.md) counts the work among those whose `"Practical interventions have been shown to be effective to shorten or remove the grokking delay"`. In the authors it is put more cautiously and in both directions: an ELR schedule shortens the delay, but it also lengthens it — in the experiment of [fig-3](#fig-3) the network is deliberately held in a non-grokking regime for hundreds of thousands of steps, and this is presented as an achievement in itself ([p7-2](#p7-2)). The same citing work's second, fuller passage — `"propose effective learning rate scaling and re-warming as a method to trigger and accelerate feature-learning dynamics during training which can both accelerate grokking and address the issue of primacy bias in continual learning"` — is accurate in substance; the one caveat lost is that in the authors the commonality of the two settings is declared a conjecture ([p2-1](#p2-1)) rather than shown.

Accurate retellings usable as models: [He et al. 2026 (2601.09049)](../2601.09049.is-grokking-worthwhile-functional-analysis-and-transferability-of-generalization-circuits-in-transformers/2601.09049.is-grokking-worthwhile-functional-analysis-and-transferability-of-generalization-circuits-in-transformers.card.md) counts the work among those that `"leveraged the phenomenon to improve model performance"` — that is, it names exactly the role the work assigns to grokking itself ([p5-4](#p5-4)).
