# Notsawo, Dumas, Rabusseau 2025 — Grokking Beyond the Euclidean Norm of Model Parameters

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Pascal Jr Tikeng Notsawo**, **Guillaume Dumas**, **Guillaume Rabusseau** (Université de Montréal and Mila).

arXiv [2506.05718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05718), June 2025; version v2 is analysed. Presented at ICML 2025. The corpus table has no citation count for it. The work is a large one: 67 pages, 35 figures, eight appendices, of which appendix C is forty pages of proofs. It shifts the focus from the weight norm to the **agreement of the regulariser with the structure of the solution** and gives the grokking delay a law $1/(\alpha\beta)$ that covers weight decay as a particular case.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work gives it a condition not tied to a norm. If there exists a model with a property $P$ (sparse or low-rank weights) that generalises on the task, then gradient descent with a small but non-zero regularisation towards $P$ yields grokking ([`p1-2`](#p1-2), [`p1-4`](#p1-4)). The course of it falls into two: first the iterates stay near the initialisation and minimise the loss (memorisation), then the regularisation begins to prevail and leads them to the solution of least $h$ (generalisation) ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)).

**[The delay law](../../concepts/norm-separation-delay-law.md)** — the central quantitative result: the generalisation delay grows as $1/(\alpha\beta)$, where $\alpha$ is the learning rate and $\beta$ the strength of an **arbitrary** suitable regulariser (theorem 2.1, [`p1-5`](#p1-5), [`p3-6`](#p3-6), [`p4-1`](#p4-1)). This directly generalises the earlier laws derived for weight decay: in Lyu et al. the grokking time grows as $1/\beta$, and $\alpha$ fails to appear only because they analyse a continuous-time gradient flow ([`p4-1`](#p4-1)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — it is shown not to be necessary. On modular addition the $\ell_{1}$ and nuclear norms act on the grokking in exactly the way $\ell_{2}$ does: the smaller $\alpha\beta$, the longer the delay ([`p3-1`](#p3-1), [`fig-1`](#fig-1), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**[Varieties of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — $\ell_{1}$, the nuclear norm $\ell_{*}$ and a **domain-specific** regularisation are checked: a Sobolev penalty on the agreement of the derivatives, from the training of physics-informed networks ([`p9-4`](#p9-4), [`fig-11`](#fig-11)). All of them govern the delay identically.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — refuted outright as a universal measure. When the model is regularised towards a different property, the $\ell_{2}$ norm **grows**, and the model generalises all the same ([`p1-2`](#p1-2), [`p3-1`](#p3-1), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). Moreover, in the overparameterised case the minimum-$\ell_{2}$-norm solution yields memorisation only: theorem 3.6 shows that at $N<n$ it cannot generalise if the true signal has a component orthogonal to the column space ([`p7-6`](#p7-6), [`p8-2`](#p8-2)).

**[Grokking without understanding](../../concepts/correlation-traps.md)** — a separate finding and a warning. At a large initialisation and with a small weight decay alone, a sharp transition is visible in the generalisation error, but this is **not generalisation**: it appears even where the task has no solution at all — at a number of examples far below the theoretical limit ([`p7-4`](#p7-4), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)). The transition is sharp simply because at a large initialisation the model's outputs are large, and as the $\ell_{2}$ norm falls they fall too ([`p48-2`](#p48-2)). Without a logarithmic axis this looks like grokking ([`fig-16`](#fig-16)).

**[The initialisation scale](../../concepts/initialization-scale.md)** — here it is contested as a necessary condition. The work points out exactly which assumption of Lyu et al. is violated: *assumption 3.2* requires the model's output to be zero at the initialisation, and at a large scale this is almost surely not so ([`p48-3`](#p48-3), [`p49-2`](#p49-2)).

**[The sparse subnetwork](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — sparsity is taken as the sought property $P$: the recovery of a sparse vector by the lasso exhibits a grokking impossible under $\ell_{2}$ alone ([`p4-5`](#p4-5), [`p4-6`](#p4-6), [`fig-3`](#fig-3)). The delay comes straight out of the mechanism: the components of the iterate are shrunk by $\alpha\beta$ per step until all of them lie within $[-\alpha\beta,\alpha\beta]$ ([`p37-2`](#p37-2)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the low-rank case. The generalisation sets in through a **multiscale decay of the singular values**: after the memorisation the smallest singular value converges to zero, then the next, and so on until $\|\mathbf{A}^{(t)}\|_{*}\approx\|\mathbf{A}^{*}\|_{*}$; this takes $\Theta(1/\alpha\beta)$ steps ([`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)).

**[Ungrokking](../../concepts/ungrokking.md)** — depth as a knob. At $L\geq 2$ an explicit $\ell_{1}$ regularisation becomes unnecessary: gradient descent preconditions itself by a matrix $\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{c}^{(t)})$ and inclines towards sparsity ([`p8-5`](#p8-5), [`p49-3`](#p49-3)). At $L>2$ the delay disappears altogether — the authors call this ungrokking outright ([`p50-2`](#p50-2)) — and the loss falls in a staircase with level stretches.

**[The lazy and rich regimes](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — the first era is described as a motion towards the kernel solution in the vicinity of the initialisation ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)); the difference from Kumar et al. is that here the transition is induced by the regularisation rather than by a change of regime in itself ([`p17-2`](#p17-2)).

**[The critical data size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — grokking is strengthened by **data selection alone**, with every other setting fixed, and this, the authors claim, is one of the first works in which such a thing is considered ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)). The knob is the coherence $\tau$: for matrix completion, choosing the entries of highest leverage score accelerates the generalisation exponentially ([`p8-3`](#p8-3), [`fig-8`](#fig-8)), whereas for compressed sensing it is the other way round — a large coherence is harmful ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)).

**[The neural tangent kernel](../../concepts/neural-tangent-kernel-ntk.md)** — the Chatterjee–Łojasiewicz inequality on which theorem 2.1 rests is required only **at the point of initialisation**, whereas the ordinary Polyak–Łojasiewicz inequality is required over the whole domain ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)); for wide overparameterised networks it holds in a neighbourhood of the initialisation.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — a large initialisation and the weight norm as a progress measure, both contested here; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — representation learning as the cause of generalising solutions; [Kumar et al. 2024](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — grokking without weight decay as a lazy-to-rich transition; [Notsawo et al. 2023](../2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens/2306.13253.predicting-grokking-long-before-it-happens.card.md) — a work by the same first author on predicting grokking from the oscillations of the loss.

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the "trigger, not condition" version: [grokking can be induced by a regularisation — explicit or implicit](#p1-2) towards an arbitrary suitable property of the solution; depth works as an implicit regularisation, and an excess of it yields "grokking without understanding" ([`p7-4`](#p7-4)).

## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is large and predominantly theoretical: 67 pages, 35 figures, eight appendices, of which appendix C is forty pages of proofs.

**The generalisation is placed where it ought to be.** The earlier delay laws are derived for weight decay and speak of the $\ell_{2}$ norm. Here it is shown that the matter lies not in the norm but in the **agreement of the regulariser with the property the generalising solution possesses**, and that the delay grows as $1/(\alpha\beta)$ for any suitable $h$ ([`p1-5`](#p1-5), [`p3-6`](#p3-6)). This is exactly the generality the corpus lacked.

**The refutation of the $\ell_{2}$ norm as a progress measure is given both by experiment and by a theorem.** By experiment: on modular addition under an $\ell_{1}$ regularisation the $\ell_{2}$ norm grows and the model generalises ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)). By theorem: the minimum-$\ell_{2}$-norm solution at $N<n$ cannot generalise ([`p7-6`](#p7-6)). The second is the stronger and closes the question for this setting.

**"Grokking without understanding" is the part most useful to the corpus.** It is shown that a sharp jump of the generalisation error at a large initialisation may not be grokking at all and appears even where no solution exists ([`p7-4`](#p7-4), [`p48-2`](#p48-2)). Its source is given too: at a large initialisation the model's outputs are large, and as the norm falls they fall with it. This is a direct warning to anyone who identifies grokking by the look of a curve.

**The violated assumption is named by name.** Not "our results diverge from Lyu et al." but "assumption 3.2, requiring a zero output at the initialisation, is violated", with an analysis of the two remaining assumptions too ([`p48-3`](#p48-3), [`p49-2`](#p49-2)). The dispute is set up so that it can be checked.

**Depth is analysed down to the mechanism.** The preconditioner $\mathbf{P}^{(t)}=(1-\alpha\beta)\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{c}^{(t)})$ is written out explicitly, with an explanation of why it favours sparsity: at a small $\mathbf{c}^{(t)}_{i}$ the update becomes a geometric contraction towards zero, at a large one it amplifies the gradient ([`p49-3`](#p49-3)).

**Data selection is a knob new to the corpus.** That grokking can be governed by **the choice of examples alone**, with every other setting fixed, had not been shown before. It is valuable too that the direction of the effect is opposite in the two tasks: for matrix completion coherence helps, for compressed sensing it harms ([`p8-3`](#p8-3), [`p8-4`](#p8-4)).

**The chief theorems, however, do not describe neural networks.** Theorems 3.1 and 3.3–3.6 are proved for the recovery of sparse vectors and for matrix factorisation — that is, for **convex** problems with a linear model, in which "memory" is the least-squares solution and "generalisation" the minimum-$\ell_{1}$- or minimum-$\ell_{*}$-norm solution. Theorem 2.1 is general, but its condition (the Chatterjee–Łojasiewicz inequality at the point of initialisation) is for networks merely **plausible** and is not checked here on a single trained network.

**The experiments on networks are pictures of curves rather than a measurement of the delay.** On modular addition, on a nonlinear teacher–student setting and on MNIST it is shown that a smaller $\alpha\beta$ gives a longer delay ([`fig-1`](#fig-1), [`fig-10`](#fig-10), [`fig-35`](#fig-35)), but nowhere is it checked that the delay grows precisely as $1/(\alpha\beta)$: there is no fit of the law to networks, only a qualitative direction. The check of the law itself is carried out on convex problems ([`fig-4`](#fig-4), [`fig-6`](#fig-6)).

**Seeds, spreads and the number of runs are nowhere named.** Neither the main text nor the eight appendices contains a single error bar or a number of repetitions. For a work whose claims are quantitative this is a gap of substance.

**The conjecture that sparsity is the cause of grokking is left unchecked.** The authors suppose that it is sparsity that gives both sparse parity and algorithmic data their grokking nature, and leave this outright to future work ([`p17-5`](#p17-5)).

**The text has not been proofread.** "ovaparametrization" instead of "overparametrization" ([`p49-5`](#p49-5)); "Matrix matrix sensing" in the captions of two figures ([`fig-14`](#fig-14), [`fig-15`](#fig-15)); appendix G.2.3 says that as the rank grows the number of examples required **falls** ([`p60-5`](#p60-5)), which contradicts both the theory of that same section and the figures; "$\nu_{j}$ for row $j$" instead of column ([`p59-2`](#p59-2)); in definition C.46 "the singular values of $\mathbf{U}$" instead of $\mathbf{A}$ ([`p43-6`](#p43-6)).

**Its place in the corpus.** The work shifts the focus from the weight norm to the **agreement of the regulariser with the structure of the solution** and gives this a quantitative law $1/(\alpha\beta)$ that covers weight decay too as a particular case. Four things are valuable: the law itself and its generality; the rigorous refutation of the $\ell_{2}$ norm as a progress measure; the recognition of "grokking without understanding" — a sharp jump that is not grokking; and data selection as a new knob for governing the delay. The price: all the rigorous theorems are proved for convex problems with linear models, the carry-over to networks rests on a condition not checked here, and the experiments on networks are qualitative and without spreads.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2206-04817
**Thilak et al. (2206.04817) — a decay of the norm ascribed to them.** `"the l2 norm of the model weights can be used during optimization as a progression measure for generalization since it generally decreases during the transition from memorization to generalization (Liu et al., 2023a; Thilak et al., 2022; Varma et al., 2023)"`: in Thilak [the norm of the classification layer grows rapidly in cycles and plateaus](../2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking/2206.04817.the-slingshot-mechanism-an-empirical-study-of-adaptive-optimizers-and-grokking.card.md#p5-1) — an observation of the opposite sign. In its related-work survey the same paper cites the target correctly ("last layer norm", "the instability induced by the Adam optimizer"). A separate inaccuracy: the argument "our algorithms are deterministic" against "stochasticity or an anomaly (Power; Thilak)" does not touch Thilak — the slingshot is reproduced with full-batch, deterministic Adam.

###### mc-2501-04697
**Prieto et al. (2501.04697) — an inversion of the causality of Softmax Collapse.** `"Prieto et al. (2025) explain grokking as a result of Softmax Collapse—numerical instability from floating-point errors that halts learning after memorization"`: the sentence is internally contradictory — in Prieto [SC zeroes out the loss and the gradient](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p4-4) and therefore explains the *absence* of grokking rather than its mechanism; [the delay is explained by NLM](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md#p7-1), and grokking sets in once SC is removed. Elsewhere the same paper conveys the direction correctly: "accelerate grokking using a stable softmax variant … to prevent collapse".

###### mc-2405-15071
**Wang et al. (2405.15071) — "grokking yields reasoning" with no bounds.** `"Wang et al. (2024) and Abramov et al. (2025) show that grokking enables Transformers to develop reasoning abilities, emerging only after extended training"`: the cited work's key distinction is dropped — [in distribution the generalisation always sets in; out of distribution, for composition, never, and for comparison, yes](../2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners/2405.15071.grokked-transformers-are-implicit-reasoners.card.md#p2-2); "develop reasoning abilities" without those bounds retells the title rather than the result.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns of erroneous or widened citation of this work observed in the citing papers of the corpus. Each entry describes what exactly is distorted in the retelling and leads to the authors' own paragraphs, where the lost caveat stands. The analysis of the particular formulations is in the cards of the citing works, in their "Erroneous citations" sections, to which the "details" links lead. The work is young (five substantive citing papers); the errors so far are of classification rather than of fact.

**The wrong genre and the wrong thesis (erroneous).** The work is assigned to mechanistic interpretability and called "descriptive rather than predictive" — even though it is a theoretical work with a [quantitative delay law scaling as 1/(αβ)](#p3-6), predictive by construction; there is no interpretability in it. Another classification error is an ascribed "compression of representations": [low rank here is a regularisable property of the solution of a convex problem](#p3-1); the work says nothing about the network's representations. Details: [2603.29262](../2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence/2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence.card.md#mc-2506-05718), [2603.01968](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md#mc-2506-05718).

**A narrowing of the general law to a particular property (widening, mirrored).** The general result — a delay under regularisation towards an arbitrary property P (sparsity, low rank, a Sobolev penalty) — is cut down to "low-rank biases": not an exaggeration but a loss of generality. Details: [2603.01968](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md#mc-2506-05718).

**The name trap (for information).** Some citing works parse "Pascal Jr Tikeng Notsawo" as "Junior et al., 2025" — such citations are not found by searching for "Notsawo"; when surveying the corpus one must search for "Junior", for "Rabusseau" and for "euclidean norm" as well. The result most valuable to the corpus — ["grokking without understanding": a jump of the generalisation error from weight decay with no correspondence to genuine generalisation](#p7-4) — is so far cited by no one.

An example of accurate citation: [2601.19791](../2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression/2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression.card.md) — a precise retelling of the theorem with both phases and the time scale, with the limit of applicability and an honest positioning of its own contribution, all in a single paragraph.
