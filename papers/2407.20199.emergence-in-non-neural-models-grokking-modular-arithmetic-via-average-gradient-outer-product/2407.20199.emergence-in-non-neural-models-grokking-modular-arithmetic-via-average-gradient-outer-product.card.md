## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work carries the phenomenon over to a substrate that has neither a neural architecture nor gradient optimization of parameters: grokking of modular arithmetic is reproduced in a Recursive Feature Machine (RFM) — a kernel machine given feature learning from outside, through the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP) ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)). It matters for the corpus that here the delay cannot be a struggle between a memorizing and a generalizing solution: ridgeless kernel regression is solved exactly, and the training loss is identically zero at **every** iteration, while test accuracy and test loss sit at chance level for the first eight iterations ([p. 7, para. 2](#p7-2), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). What the work does not have: a transfer of the point "not predicted by the training loss" to neural networks — the authors themselves note in footnote 2 that for SGD-trained networks emergence cannot be detached from the training loss, since a non-zero loss is needed for training to proceed at all ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4)). Nor is there any variance: the word "seed" does not occur in the work even once, all the curves come from a single run, and the modulus in all the main experiments is one and the same ($p=61$).

**[Emergence](../../concepts/emergence.md)** — the work's claim is direct: the abrupt emergence in modular arithmetic follows entirely from feature learning, independently of the other aspects of how the model is built and trained ([p. 2, para. 2](#p2-2)). Against the "mirage" of Schaeffer et al. the work objects along the compute axis: the transitions are not predicted by standard *a priori* performance measures, neither continuous nor discontinuous ([p. 14, para. 7](#p14-7)). But along the data-volume axis the authors agree with the "mirage": accuracy rises abruptly around a training fraction of $25$%, whereas the test loss and the AGOP alignment change smoothly, and emergence in data volume "may be a 'mirage' in the sense of [38]" ([p. 15, para. 6](#p15-6), [app. fig. 6](#fig-app-6)). Along the model-size axis the work says nothing and says so outright: they regard a kernel machine as an infinite-width network. A separate observation for the "skills" frame, set out in a remark of the main text ([p. 10, para. 1](#p10-1)) and supported by the experiment of appendix E: two tasks trained together grok at different times ([p. 21, para. 3](#p21-3), [app. fig. 3](#fig-app-3)).

**[Feature emergence](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — here is the centre of gravity of the work. AGOP is defined for any differentiable predictor, including one that learns no features itself ([Def. 2.1](#p5-5)), while RFM alternates the exact solution of kernel regression with the update $M_{t+1}=[G(f^{(t)})]^{s}$ at $s=\frac{1}{2}$ ([p. 6, para. 1](#p6-1)). The work gives the corpus a rare thing — a separation of feature learning from predictor training **by construction** rather than by coincidence: the features develop while both losses are silent. The network side: the neural feature matrix $W_{1}^{\top}W_{1}$ and the square root of the AGOP are related with a Pearson correlation above $0.92$ (0.955 / 0.942 / 0.924 / 0.929 across the four tasks) — the Neural Feature Ansatz holds in this setting ([p. 11, para. 6](#p11-6), [fig. 6](#fig-6)). What the work does not have: an explanation of **why** the AGOP arrives at a circulant specifically — it is shown that it does, and that a random circulant works no worse.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — four operations ($+,-,\times,\div$) over $\mathbb{Z}_ {p}$ at a single modulus $p=61$; the input is a concatenation of two one-hot vectors $\boldsymbol{e}_ {a}\oplus\boldsymbol{e}_ {b}\in\mathbb{R}^{2p}$, the output is $\boldsymbol{e}_ {f^{ * }(a,b)}$, and training runs on a random fraction $r$ of all discrete pairs ([p. 4, para. 3](#p4-3)). The contribution to understanding the task is a reordering technique: multiplication and division become addition and subtraction after the coordinates are reordered by a discrete logarithm, and only then does the circulant structure of the features become visible ([fig. 3](#fig-3), [fig. 8](#fig-8)); lemma G.1 shows that the choice of generator does not affect this. The title [figure 1](#fig-1) is built at $p=59$, while the modulus $p=97$ occurs only in two appendix figures ([app. fig. 2](#fig-app-2), [app. fig. 6](#fig-app-6)).

**[Learning structured representations](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the form of the learned representation is written out explicitly. Observation 1: the final feature matrix has the form $M^{ * }=\begin{pmatrix}A&C^{\top}\cr C&A\end{pmatrix}$, where $C$ is a non-symmetric circulant and $A=c_{1}I+c_{2}\boldsymbol{1}\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}$ ([p. 7, para. 5](#p7-5)). This is claimed precisely as an *observation* rather than a theorem, and it refers to addition/subtraction (for multiplication/division — after the reordering). A caveat that matters for the corpus: the learned features are **full-rank**, even though a low-rank solution (an index-$4$ model) exists for these tasks and is written out in appendix H — a low-rank explanation of grokking is not adopted by the work but refuted by experiment ([p. 15, para. 4](#p15-4), [p. 23, para. 8](#p23-8)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work offers two "hidden progress measures" (itself referring to Barak et al.) — the circulant deviation and the AGOP alignment ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2)). Both grow smoothly, almost linearly at first, exactly during the time when the test loss stands still — both in the RFM and in networks ([p. 8, para. 8](#p8-8), [fig. 2](#fig-2), [fig. 5](#fig-5)). What the work does not have: a practical predictor. Both measures are declared *a posteriori* outright: the circulant deviation requires knowing the form of the answer in advance, the AGOP alignment requires access to a fully trained model, and alongside them the authors place the Fourier gap of Barak et al. and the inverse participation ratio of Doshi et al. ([p. 9, para. 3](#p9-3), [p. 14, para. 7](#p14-7)).

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the Fourier Multiplication Algorithm (FMA) is carried over from networks to kernel machines: [theorem 5.1](#p14-1) asserts that kernel predictors with block-circulant $M_{\ell}$, trained on **all** the discrete data, exactly reproduce the FMA for modular subtraction ([section 5](#sec-5)). The corollary for the corpus: Fourier multiplication turns out to be a property of the task and of the form of the features, not a property of the transformer. What the work does not have: a proof that the RFM **learns** the FMA. The matrices $M_{\ell}=\begin{pmatrix}0&C^{\ell}\cr (C^{\ell})^{\top}&0\end{pmatrix}$ are set by hand, one per output coordinate — and the authors themselves note that "our construction differs from RFM" ([p. 14, para. 2](#p14-2)); the connection between the construction and the RFM's success on a fraction of the data is declared a guess ("we conjecture", [p. 14, para. 4](#p14-4)).

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the strongest place in the work for the corpus: a check by intervention rather than by correlation. A **random** circulant matrix applied to transform the inputs allows an ordinary Gaussian kernel machine — now without RFM and without any feature learning — to solve modular arithmetic, and it outpaces kernel-RFM at that ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4), [fig. 4](#fig-4)). The same intervention for networks: at a training fraction of $17.5$% a network on transformed inputs reaches $100$% within a few hundred epochs, while on ordinary one-hot inputs it does not reach it even in $3000$ ([p. 12, para. 1](#p12-1), [fig. 7](#fig-7)). A third, weaker intervention: imposing an exact circulant structure on the submatrices at every RFM iteration accelerates grokking and improves the test loss ([p. 21, para. 1](#p21-1), [app. fig. 2](#fig-app-2)). Hence the authors' conclusion: no structure beyond a typical circulant is required. A side corollary the authors do not stress: feature learning through AGOP turns out not to be the best route to the same feature.

**[The memorization phase](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives the corpus a plateau on which there is nothing to memorize and nothing to be displaced: in the RFM the training loss is numerically zero from the very first iteration, that is, "overfitting" is attained instantly and does not change, and yet a plateau in test performance is there all the same ([p. 7, para. 2](#p7-2)). The language of "memorizing" and "generalizing" solutions the work does not use at all; the plateau is described as the time of acquiring the feature.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the transition is observed and called abrupt, but the work neither introduces nor uses the apparatus of phase transitions (order parameter, critical exponents, thermodynamic limit). Its contribution to the discontinuity dispute is the separation of two axes: in compute the transition is abrupt and not predictable by a priori measures, in the volume of training data it may be a "mirage" ([p. 14, para. 7](#p14-7), [p. 15, para. 6](#p15-6)).

**[From lazy to rich](../../concepts/lazy-to-rich-kernel-to-feature-learning.md)** — a direct objection: "our experiments clearly show that grokking in RFM does not undergo such a transition", and therefore "the two-stage account cannot serve as a general explanation of grokking in compute" ([p. 16, para. 1](#p16-1)). The objection is addressed to [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md), [Lyu et al. 2024](../2311.18817.dichotomy-of-early-and-late-phase-implicit-biases-can-provably-induce-grokking/2311.18817.dichotomy-of-early-and-late-phase-implicit-biases-can-provably-induce-grokking.card.md) and [Mohamadi et al. 2024](../2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition/2407.12332.why-do-you-grok-a-theoretical-analysis-of-grokking-modular-addition.card.md). It should be read with a correction worth keeping in mind: the RFM has no lazy stage **by construction** — the kernel step is exact at every iteration — so what is shown is the non-universality of the explanation, not its incorrectness for networks; about their own networks the authors say something weaker — "we did not observe substantial evidence". The language of the NTK the work does not use: its only remark about kernels and networks is that a kernel machine may be regarded as an infinite-width network ([p. 15, para. 6](#p15-6)).

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — the same objection, addressed to [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) and [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md): since grokking occurs in a kernel model with a linear feature-learning mechanism, explanations depending on the magnitude of the weights, on the efficiency of neural circuits or on properties of the optimization "are unable to explain the phenomena described in our work" ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)). The work carries out no analysis of circuits, of displacement, or of their efficiency — it merely exhibits a substrate on which these notions are undefined.

**[Weight norm minimization](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the same paragraph ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)) also strips explanations through the magnitude of the weights of any claim to generality: a kernel machine has no network weights, and grokking is there all the same. The work offers no norm theory of its own; note that it does not show the converse either — that the norm is unimportant for networks.

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work gives the corpus a mechanistic guess about **how** weight decay helps: under the NFA the penalty $\Vert W_{1}\Vert_{F}^{2}=\mathrm{tr}(W_{1}^{\top}W_{1})$ should behave similarly to a penalty on the AGOP trace, $\mathrm{tr}(G(f))$ — that is, weight decay acts as a stand-in for an AGOP penalty ([p. 12, para. 3](#p12-3)). The experimental support: on vanilla SGD without regularization there is no grokking, whereas with weight decay and with AGOP regularization there is ([app. fig. 5](#fig-app-5)). The cost of that support is small but should be named: the experiment is a single one (addition, $p=61$, fraction 40 %, width 512), and the weight decay in it is taken to be $10^{-5}$, whereas in the main network experiments it equals $1.0$ ([p. 20, para. 7](#p20-7)); the comparability of the arms in regularization strength is not shown.

**[When regularization is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the work adds a third answer to the dispute: what is necessary is not the $\ell_{2}$ norm of the weights specifically but **some** penalty acting on the AGOP; in its experiment a penalty on the AGOP trace replaces weight decay without loss of grokking ([p. 12, para. 3](#p12-3), [app. fig. 5](#fig-app-5)). Note that the RFM setting itself takes no part in this dispute: the kernel step has no regularizer at all (the regression is ridgeless), and grokking arises there without any regularization.

**[Data fraction / critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the training fraction serves both as a lever of the experiments (50 % in the main network ones, 17.5 % in the random-circulant intervention, 40 % in the regularization experiment, 80 % in the multi-task one) and as a separate axis of emergence. Along that axis one curve is measured: for $x+y\mkern3mu\mathrm{mod}\mkern3mu97$ the test accuracy rises abruptly around a training fraction of $25$%, whereas the test loss and the AGOP alignment rise smoothly ([p. 15, para. 6](#p15-6), [app. fig. 6](#fig-app-6)). The work gives neither a name nor a formula for a critical threshold.

**[The reference grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — from the testbed of [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) only the data frame is taken: a binary-operation table, one-hot symbols, the training fraction as a lever. Everything else is replaced: no transformer, no cross-entropy, no $p=97/113$ — the modulus is $61$, the loss is squared, and the network counterpart is a one-layer network with a quadratic activation from [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md). There are no transformers in the work at all, whereas the frame of the argument is about the emergence of skills in general and about large language models.

**[Optimizer](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the claim "not tied to gradient-based optimization methods" refers to the optimization of **parameters**: the RFM has no parameters updated by a gradient step, and the kernel problem is solved exactly ([p. 7, para. 2](#p7-2)). The very quantity on which everything rests is an average of outer products of the predictor's **gradients** with respect to the input ([Def. 2.1](#p5-5)); in the text the authors are careful and write "gradient descent-based optimization", in the title this caveat is absent. In the network experiments — AdamW, step $10^{-3}$, weight decay $1.0$, batch 32, width 1024, 50 epochs; vanilla SGD appears only in the AGOP-regularization experiment ([p. 11, para. 1](#p11-1), [p. 20, para. 7](#p20-7)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the analysis here is not by circuits but by matrices: the learned representation is read directly off the feature matrix (stripes = circulant blocks), and its computational meaning is established by the theorem on FMA equivalence ([p. 7, para. 5](#p7-5), [theorem 5.1](#p14-1)). The authors' caveat about the limits of the technique is valuable for the corpus: the structure is visible only after reordering by the discrete logarithm, and before it there is no visible pattern ([fig. 8](#fig-8)) — "in other settings hidden feature structures may be hard to recognize for want of comparable mathematical insight" ([p. 15, para. 1](#p15-1)). There are no ablations, no frequency excision and no analysis of individual neurons in the work.

**[Margin maximization](../../concepts/margin-maximization-implicit-bias.md)** — a mention only, but with a clear assessment: several works have proved that the learning of modular arithmetic may be explained by margin maximization with respect to certain norms, and "although the direction is promising, the general underlying principles have not yet been clarified" ([p. 15, para. 2](#p15-2)). The work carries out no margin analysis of its own.

**[Neural collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — a mention only, in the conclusion: deep neural collapse is named as one of the phenomena explicable by AGOP-based feature-learning mechanisms, alongside multi-index models and low-rank matrix completion ([p. 16, para. 4](#p16-4)). There are no measurements of collapse in the work.

Corpus paper cards this work adjoins: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the original phenomenon and the data frame; [Gromov 2023](../2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic/2301.02679.grokking-modular-arithmetic.card.md) — the network counterpart (a one-layer quadratic network); [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — the FMA, adopted here as the target algorithm without revision; [Barak et al. 2022](../2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit/2207.08799.hidden-progress-in-deep-learning-sgd-learns-parities-near-the-computational-limit.card.md) — the "hidden progress measures" the work refers to when introducing its own; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) and [Kumar et al. 2023](../2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics/2310.06110.grokking-as-the-transition-from-lazy-to-rich-training-dynamics.card.md) — explanations declared here unable to explain what is shown; ## Points we could not reconcile

**The matrix power in the ansatz is named in three ways.** In [Ansatz 1](#p20-2) the power is declared to be $\alpha\in(0,1]$, in formula (15) itself it is $s$ ($W_{1}^{\top}W_{1}\propto{G}(f^{\mathrm{NN}})^{s}$), and the next paragraph says "we choose $\alpha=\frac{1}{2}$". It should be read as one and the same quantity under two letters — the same as $s=\frac{1}{2}$ in the RFM algorithm.

**An index error in lemma G.1.** Formula (18) writes $C_{q_{1}^{i},q_{1}^{j}}=C_{q_{1}^{i+s},q_{1}^{i+s}}$, whereas the circulance assumption yields $C_{q_{1}^{i+s},q_{1}^{j+s}}$ — the second index on the right should carry $j$, and it is in exactly that form that the lemma is used further on in the proof ([p. 22, para. 2](#p22-2)).

**The sign of the power and the transpose in the proof of theorem 5.1.** In the derivation ([p. 23, para. 3](#p23-3)) the equality $f_{\ell}(X)=\left(\ldots\right)^{\top}=C^{-\ell}$ turns, via "$\iff$", into $\ldots=C^{\ell}$: the right-hand side changes the sign of the power, and the transpose disappears along the way without explanation. The result of the proof ($f_{\ell}(x)=x_{[1]}^{\top}C^{-\ell}x_{[2]}$) does not conflict with this, but the intermediate step is written incorrectly.

**A reference to the wrong formula in appendix H.** The phrase "We extend $\Phi$ to $\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}$ as in eq. 19 above" ([p. 24, para. 9](#p24-9)) points to equality (19), which concerns the decoding map $\Psi$; the extension of $\Phi$ to pairs is given by the neighbouring unnumbered equality. Nearby is a scattering of typos unusual for an ICML oral: "a liner map", "The construction is for multiplication is very similar" ([p. 24, para. 8](#p24-8)), "this regularizer been used prior work".

**Algorithm 1 returns something other than the last feature matrix.** In [the algorithm listing](#p5-3) the loop computes $M_{t+1}$ for $t=0,\ldots,T-1$, that is, it reaches $M_{T}$, while $M_{T-1}$ is returned; in the list of input arguments ($X,y,k,T,L$) the matrix power $s$ does not appear, although the line's explanation names it. Typesetting slips, but they stand in the only formal statement of the algorithm.

**The test loss: "does not predict" in the abstract against "begins to improve" in the text.** The [abstract](#p1-2) says that the transition cannot be predicted "either from the training loss, which is identically zero, or from the test loss, which remains unchanged in the initial iterations". The description of figure 1 says otherwise ([p. 3, para. 1](#p3-1)): "around iteration $10$ the test loss begins to improve, and several iterations later the test accuracy rapidly transitions to $100$%", while in fig. 2A the loss and the accuracy stand still for the first eight iterations and then diverge in time. That is, the test loss in the RFM is a leading indicator with a margin of several iterations, and it is unchanged only over an initial stretch. The caveat is lost when the work is cited: the abstract's wording passes into the citing works as "the transition is predicted neither by the training nor by the test loss" (see [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md)).

## What is proved, what is shown and what is conjectured

Notes by the card's editor following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is an experimental one: one theorem, two lemmas, and sixteen pages of experiments and appendices.

**The strongest thing is the setting, not the result.** A kernel machine has no feature-learning mechanism of its own, and ridgeless regression is solved exactly, so the training loss is identically zero at every iteration ([p. 7, para. 2](#p7-2)). Feature learning is detached from the decrease of the training loss **by construction** rather than by coincidence — and this is what cannot be arranged in an SGD-trained network, as the authors openly admit ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4)).

**Shown by intervention, not by correlation.** A random circulant, substituted both into an ordinary kernel and into a network, removes the delay ([p. 9, para. 4](#p9-4), [p. 12, para. 1](#p12-1)). This is the corpus's strongest check that the delay is the time of acquiring a feature: the feature is set at random, bypassing any learning — and there is no delay. Hence too the authors' conclusion that "no additional structure" beyond a typical circulant is required.

**A check against the obvious objection is carried out.** The plateau is not a consequence of averaging the loss over the $61$ outputs: the loss on the coordinate of the correct class repeats the total one — both in the RFM and in the network ([fig. 2](#fig-2), [fig. 5](#fig-5)).

**Less is proved than it sounds.** [Theorem 5.1](#p14-1) describes not the RFM run under analysis but a construction: (1) a separate matrix $M_{\ell}$ per output coordinate instead of one shared matrix — a divergence acknowledged by the authors ([p. 14, para. 2](#p14-2)); (2) training on **all** $p^{2}$ points rather than on a fraction; (3) $M_{\ell}$ set by hand as $C^{\ell}$ rather than obtained as an AGOP; (4) $A=0$, whereas in [Observation 1](#p7-5) $A=c_{1}I+c_{2}\boldsymbol{1}\boldsymbol{1}^{\top}$. The claim that it is precisely the implicit Fourier representation that lets the RFM learn from a fraction of the data is declared a guess: "we conjecture" ([p. 14, para. 4](#p14-4)).

**Observation 1 is an observation.** The form of the final feature matrix is claimed as an observation, not a theorem ([p. 7, para. 5](#p7-5)); the support is figures and the fact that the circulant deviation is close to zero by the end of training ([p. 8, para. 8](#p8-8)).

**The progress measures are a posteriori, and this is said outright.** The circulant deviation requires knowing the form of the answer in advance, the AGOP alignment requires a trained model ([p. 9, para. 3](#p9-3), [p. 14, para. 7](#p14-7)). The work neither gives nor claims a practical predictor.

**The objections to the neighbours are not of equal force.** The argument against the two-stage lazy → rich transition rests on a mechanism that has no lazy stage by construction ([p. 16, para. 1](#p16-1)); the argument against explanations through the magnitude of the weights and the efficiency of circuits rests on a mechanism where neither network weights nor circuits exist ([p. 16, para. 2](#p16-2)). In both cases what is shown is the non-universality of the explanation, not its incorrectness for networks; about their own network experiments the authors indeed say something weaker — "we did not observe substantial evidence".

**The experimental support is narrower than the frame of the argument.** A single modulus $p=61$ in the main experiments, one-layer networks with a quadratic activation, quadratic and Gaussian kernels chosen to fit the bilinear nature of the task; no seeds, no repeats, no error bars — the word "seed" does not occur in the work even once. The key experiment with AGOP regularization is a single one, and the weight decay in it is five orders of magnitude weaker than in the main network experiments ([p. 20, para. 7](#p20-7)). There are no transformers at all, whereas the discussion is about the emergence of skills in general.

**The title's claim is broader than the text's.** "Grokking without gradient descent" refers to the optimization of parameters: the AGOP is an average of outer products of the predictor's **gradients** ([Def. 2.1](#p5-5)). In the text the authors write carefully — "gradient descent-based optimization"; in the title the caveat is absent, and it is precisely that which is lost when the work is cited.

**Priority is not discussed.** The claim of "grokking outside neural networks" was made earlier — [Miller, O'Neill, Bui 2024](../2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity/2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity.card.md) on Gaussian processes, linear regression and Bayesian networks. Here that work is mentioned only by a number in an enumerating bracket in the introduction and is nowhere analysed; the difference (there — models with a complexity penalty, here — a kernel with feature learning) is nowhere spelled out.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

Notes by the card's editor on the places where this work passes on other works' results with strengthening or without the original authors' caveats.

###### mc-2303-06173

**Davies, Langosco, Krueger (2303.06173) — a necessity for weight decay attributed to a work that speaks of acceleration.** The paper's claim: `"It has been argued in prior work that weight decay ($\ell_{2}$ regularization on network weights) is necessary for grokking to occur when training neural networks for modular arithmetic tasks [42, 10, 31]."` ([p. 12, para. 3](#p12-3)). Of the three references, two withstand checking. Nanda et al. do indeed write of necessity outright: `"we provide additional evidence that weight decay is necessary for grokking"`, adding, however, the caveat `"without weight decay or some other form of regularization"` — that is, the point is about some regularization rather than about $\ell_{2}$ specifically ([the Nanda et al. 2023 card](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md)). Varma et al. require the presence of weight decay inside their theory — `"just on weight decay being present at all"` ([Varma, p. 3, para. 9](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md#p3-9)). Davies et al., however, say something else: `"In the grokking setting, however, weight decay plays a significant role in speeding up time to generalization (Power et al. 2022)."` ([Davies, p. 5, para. 3](../2303.06173.unifying-grokking-and-double-descent/2303.06173.unifying-grokking-and-double-descent.card.md#p5-3)); they assert no necessity, and they call their own explanation a conjecture ("we speculate"). Class: **over-extending** — "accelerates" is strengthened into "is necessary", and the contrast with AGOP regularization is built on that strengthened form. Note that on recounting with the two remaining references the work's claim does not collapse: its own experiment shows only that a penalty on the AGOP trace replaces weight decay, and that observation needs no contrast.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns noticed in the corpus works that cite this one: what exactly is distorted and which of the authors' caveats are lost in the process. The links lead to the authors' own paragraphs.

**"It is proved that the RFM learns the Fourier Multiplication Algorithm" (erroneous).** The citing works carry [theorem 5.1](#p14-1) over from a construction to learning: in [Truong et al. 2026](../2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation/2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation.card.md) — `"They prove (their Theorem 5.1, with the argument building on two diagonalisation lemmas for circulant matrices) that RFM with Gaussian and quadratic kernels learns block-circulant feature transformations that implement the Fourier Multiplication Algorithm on cyclic groups $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$"`, that is, the theorem is read as a claim that RFM with Gaussian and quadratic kernels *learns* such features. The authors assert something different. The feature matrices $M_{\ell}$ in the theorem are set by hand as $C^{\ell}$, one per output coordinate, the training runs on **all** the discrete data, and the result is proved for modular **subtraction**; the authors themselves write that "our construction differs from RFM" ([p. 14, para. 2](#p14-2)). The block-circulance of the learned features is an [observation](#p7-5) resting on figures and on the closeness of the circulant deviation to zero, while the connection between the construction and the RFM's success on a fraction of the data is called a guess outright ([p. 14, para. 4](#p14-4)). The same transfer occurs in [Zhang et al. 2026](../2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence/2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence.card.md): `"Motivated by the empirical consensus that neural networks implementing modular arithmetic converge to the FMA (Mallinar et al., 2025)"` — here a single theorem about kernel machines is turned additionally into an "empirical consensus" about neural networks.

**"Grokking is shown on neural networks" (erroneous).** The work lands in lists of evidence about networks, whereas its subject is a non-neural substrate: in [Gu et al. 2025](../2504.03162.beyond-progress-measures-theoretical-insights-into-the-mechanism-of-grokking/2504.03162.beyond-progress-measures-theoretical-insights-into-the-mechanism-of-grokking.card.md) — `"This view was quickly overturned by the grokking phenomenon that appeared on DNNs (Gromov, 2023; Lyu et al., 2024; Mallinar et al., 2024)."`. Network experiments do exist in the paper (section 4), but they serve as a counterpart to the main result rather than being it; the work's claim is that the phenomenon is reproduced **outside** networks and outside gradient optimization of parameters ([p. 1, para. 2](#p1-2)).

**"Establishes rather than suggests" (over-extending).** In [Truong et al. 2026](../2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation/2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation.card.md): `"This establishes, rather than merely suggests, that grokking in modular arithmetic does not require neural architecture or SGD."` The direction is right, the weight of the evidence is not: the experiments run at a single modulus $p=61$, one run per curve, with no seeds and no error bars, and the strengthening rests on the reading of theorem 5.1 from the previous pattern.

**"The RFM's features are Fourier-aligned by design" (erroneous).** From the same source: `"In our language, RFM training targets high $A_{\mathrm{Fourier}}$ by design; SGD-trained Transformers reach high $A_{\mathrm{Fourier}}$ *through the entropy-collapse trajectory*."` Nothing Fourier-like is set in the RFM algorithm: the AGOP step knows nothing of frequencies or circulants, and the striped structure precisely **emerges** over the course of the iterations and serves as the subject of the two proposed progress measures ([p. 8, para. 8](#p8-8), [fig. 2](#fig-2)). Fourier-aligned "by design" is only the separate kernel machine of theorem 5.1.

**"Expanded the range of tasks" (mild shift).** In [Zhang et al. 2026](../2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence/2603.29262.grokking-from-abstraction-to-intelligence.card.md): `"While these works have offered various insights and expanded the scope of tasks exhibiting grokking (Miller et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2023a; Mallinar et al., 2025)"`. The range of tasks here is precisely the usual one — four modular operations at a single modulus ([p. 4, para. 3](#p4-3)); what was expanded is the range of **models**.

Retellings exemplary in their accuracy, fit to serve as a model: [Tomàs, Mallinar, Belkin 2026](../2604.00316.breaking-data-symmetry-is-needed-for-generalization-in-feature-learning-kernels/2604.00316.breaking-data-symmetry-is-needed-for-generalization-in-feature-learning-kernels.card.md) — `"They argue that grokking is a manifestation of a gradual feature learning process, which can be understood via the Average Gradient Outer Product (AGOP), and that these feature transformations on the input data are sufficient to enable generalization."`; [Xu et al. 2026](../2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression/2601.19791.to-grok-grokking-provable-grokking-in-ridge-regression.card.md) — `"Mallinar et al. (2025) revealed that grokking is not specific to neural networks nor to GD-based optimization methods by showing that grokking occurs when learning modular arithmetic with Recursive Feature Machines (RFM)."` Weaker echoes without analysis: [Prieto et al. 2025](../2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability/2501.04697.grokking-at-the-edge-of-numerical-stability.card.md) (repeats the abstract's wording about the test loss — see the section on inconsistencies), [Xu et al. 2025](../2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model/2504.13292.let-me-grok-for-you-accelerating-grokking-via-embedding-transfer-from-a-weaker-model.card.md), [Notsawo et al. 2025](../2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters/2506.05718.grokking-beyond-the-euclidean-norm-of-model-parameters.card.md). ---
