## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the work adds nothing to the notion but takes it ready-made from Power et al. 2022 and replaces it with an operational definition of its own: the grokking step is named as [the first measurement at which the accuracy on the held-out set exceeds $95\%$](#p5-3). The opening paragraph retells the phenomenon as a two-phase dynamics in which the network "attains a nearly perfect training accuracy" and much later "passes abruptly to a strong generalisation" ([p. 1, para. 3](#p1-3)). What the work does not contain: the **training** accuracy is never shown or measured — neither in a plot nor in a table — so the classic feature of grokking (a fit to the training set long before generalisation) is not observed here; what is observed is only the moment at which the held-out accuracy crosses the threshold $95\%$. Runs in which no grokking occurred are likewise absent from the assessment of the feature — except for those in which the transition is suppressed by the work's own intervention ([p. 12, para. 2](#p12-2)).

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the work's contribution is addressed precisely to this notion: a scalar quantity is proposed, computed from the network's internal state and, by design, crossing a threshold before the external jump. The argument for which it is introduced is formulated against the earlier features: [all of them are indirect](#p2-7), because they look at the weights or the gradients rather than at the structure of the learnt representations. It matters that the author does **not** use the term "progress measure": the words `progress measure` do not appear in the text, Nanda et al. 2023 stands in the bibliography and is cited once for another purpose ([p. 10, para. 2](#p10-2)), and a framing about progress measures cannot be credited to the author. Nor are the checks customary for the notion present here: ILDR is compared with neither the restricted nor the excluded loss, does not predict how many steps remain to the transition ([p. 11, para. 1](#p11-1): "does not predict how long the full generalisation will take"), and is not checked for false positives.

**[Grokfast / gradient filtering](../../concepts/gradient-low-pass-filtering.md)** — the work's most valuable measurement for the corpus is a negative one and belongs here: the slow EMA of the gradient, taken as a passive feature, is unstable across seeds. Over eight seeds it leads on three and lags on four or five, a mean of $-1862 \pm 3532$ steps — [the standard deviation exceeds the mean in absolute value](#p7-5), which makes the feature statistically uninformative. The caveat without which this measurement cannot be cited: the amplification for whose sake Grokfast was created is [deliberately removed](#p2-5) here, so what is tested is not the method but only its EMA in the role of an observer (more in the section "[Erroneous citations](#mc-2405-20233)"). The construction of the threshold is worth remembering separately: [the flag is raised when the EMA falls below $50\%$ of its value at step 3000](#p2-6), and in the three "leading" runs it is raised exactly at step 3100, that is, at the very first measurement after the baseline. The depth sweep adds that [Grokfast's lag shrinks with depth — 13600, 4900 and 1000 steps at 1, 2 and 4 layers](#p9-4), that is, it depends on the model rather than on the task.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — the work gives the corpus an eight-seed measurement of the weight norm's lagging as a feature: [$-450 \pm 200$ steps across all eight seeds](#p7-4), with a mechanical explanation that $\lVert W \rVert$ falls when the weight decay cuts away parameters already unneeded, that is, after the geometry has rearranged. A caveat is obligatory: what is measured is not the norm as such but the moment at which it falls [below $75\%$ of the step-3000 baseline](#p5-6) — a threshold tuned on one seed. And on the only long-horizon task ($S_5$) that same weight norm leads the transition by 12 600 steps, so the thesis of a "consistent lag" is refuted by the work's own table 3 (see "Inconsistencies").

**[Compression of the representation manifold](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the work gives the notion a cheap scalar instrument: the ratio of the mean squared distance between class centroids to the mean within-class spread, computed [on the penultimate layer's representations](#p2-8) and [on held-out inputs only](#p4-2). The mechanism is described as a contraction of the examples of one class towards common centroids with a simultaneous separation of the classes. What the work does not contain: not a single curve of the measured ILDR — the only illustration is synthetic ([p. 4, para. 1](#p4-1)); the separate decomposition of the between-class and within-class parts promised in [§3.3](#p6-2) is not given in the results; and the dimension of the manifold, the effective rank and other measures of compression are not measured at all.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the work takes the side of the reading by which the representations rearrange earlier than is visible in behaviour: "the model may develop a clean latent structure a little earlier than its output head begins to use it fully" ([p. 4, para. 2](#p4-2)). In the introduction the same thought is given generally — the representations "[rearrange over time and eventually settle into a more compact and structured form supporting generalisation](#p2-1)". The strongest evidence is division modulo $p$, in which the lead reaches 2900 steps against ~400 for addition and multiplication ([p. 8, para. 1](#p8-1)), with a reading through the learning of modular inverses. The author himself marks that reading as unconfirmed: "although this still requires confirmation" ([p. 8, para. 3](#p8-3)). The work inspects no structure of the representations — no circle of embeddings, no Fourier components and no probes: the separability of the classes is measured by one number.

**[Neural collapse](../../concepts/neural-collapse.md)** — the work stands nearer to the notion than it realises. The definition of ILDR ([p. 3, para. 2](#p3-2) and [para. 3](#p3-3)) at equal class sizes is, up to a factor, the ratio of the traces of the scatter matrices $\operatorname{Tr}(S_B)/\operatorname{Tr}(S_W)$, that is, the reciprocal of the standard measure $\mathrm{NC1}$; and the description of the post-grokking state — a contraction to centroids and a stabilisation of the centroids at separated positions — is a verbal description of the first property of neural collapse. Yet the relation to the collapse is relegated [to future work](#p13-3): "may be related to the dynamics of neural collapse (Papyan et al., 2020)". The simplex equiangular configuration, the angles between centroids and the other properties $\mathrm{NC2}$–$\mathrm{NC4}$ are absent here; and there is no check against the corpus's works that analysed the same quantity — [Sakamoto & Sato 2025](../2509.20829.explaining-grokking-and-information-bottleneck-through-neural-collapse-emergence/2509.20829.explaining-grokking-and-information-bottleneck-through-neural-collapse-emergence.card.md) and [Han et al. 2025](../2509.17738.flatness-is-necessary-neural-collapse-is-not-rethinking-generalization-via-grokking/2509.17738.flatness-is-necessary-neural-collapse-is-not-rethinking-generalization-via-grokking.card.md).

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the language of a phase transition is present in the work but rests on a single measurement: [a fall of the ILDR variance by 1,696× between the regimes](#p10-2) is called "evidence of a phase transition in representation space". The work introduces no order parameter, no finite-size analysis and no critical exponents and does not refer to the theory of phase transitions; the transition is identified from one extended run. The three numbers of that experiment do not besides agree with each other or with its description (see "Inconsistencies"), so 1696× cannot be cited as a measured sharpness of the transition.

**[The memorisation phase / the plateau](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the work gives the phase a geometric characterisation: during the memorisation "the representations of one class are scattered in the latent space", the within-class spread is large, the between-class separation small, and ILDR stays low ([p. 4, para. 2](#p4-2)). The length of the plateau and its ending, however, are determined only from the held-out accuracy; there is no training accuracy and no training loss in the work, so the plateau as "the training is fitted, the test stands still" is never shown.

**[The canonical grokking proving ground](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting of Power et al. is reproduced almost unchanged and with no reference to that fact: modular arithmetic at [$p = 97$ and a training fraction $f = 0.3$](#p5-1), [AdamW at a rate of $10^{-3}$ and a weight decay of $1.0$, a batch of 512, with no schedule and no dropout](#p5-3). Two departures from the proving ground matter when comparing numbers: the input is fed as [three tokens $[a,\, b,\, {=}]$](#p4-3) rather than five, and the model is an encoder transformer rather than decoder-only. The training fraction is swept ([$\{0.2, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5\}$](#p6-7)) but as a condition changing the length of the compaction phase rather than as a control parameter of the transition itself.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — three binary operations over $\mathbb{Z}_p$: [addition, multiplication and division via Fermat's little theorem](#p5-1), plus a sweep over the modulus $p \in \{43, 67, 97, 113\}$. The work gives the corpus an observation about the order of difficulty within this family: [the lead of the geometry over the behaviour grows from addition and multiplication (~400 steps) to division (2900 steps)](#p8-1), while the grokking time for division is twice as long. The mechanism of division the work does not analyse — the modular inverses are called "a substantially harder algebraic structure" ([p. 8, para. 2](#p8-2)) and it is left there.

**[Group composition / non-commutativity](../../concepts/group-composition-non-commutative-s5.md)** — $S_5$ is taken as a task with [120 output classes and $120^2 = 14{,}400$ input pairs](#p5-2), trained by [a two-layer transformer with $d = 256$ at a budget of $40{,}000$ steps for the sake of a substantially longer grokking time](#p6-3). This is the work's only non-arithmetic task and the only one in which the lead is large (14 800 steps, 73%). The non-commutativity of $S_5$ is nowhere used and is not even mentioned as a property: the group is taken for the number of classes and the length of the run. A caveat for citing works: the $S_5$ row is precisely the one case in which both baseline measures lead alongside ILDR, so it will not serve as evidence of ILDR's advantage.

**[The data fraction / the critical dataset size](../../concepts/data-fraction-critical-dataset-size.md)** — the work gives an observation useful to the whole corpus: the lead of the geometry over the behaviour disappears at an abundance of data. [At $f=0.2$ the grokking occurs at step 15800 and the lead equals 3400 steps (22%); at $f=0.5$ the time collapses to about 3100 and the lead falls to zero](#p10-1). The author's reading is that the "compaction phase" has a finite length that shrinks monotonically as $f$ grows. The work does not measure a critical dataset size and introduces no such notion; each of the four values of $f$ is obtained on one seed, and at $f = 0.4$ and $f = 0.5$ the transition occurs at steps 3000 and 3100, that is, practically at the baseline of step 3000 — so the zero lead here may be a property of the measurement rather than of the task.

**[Weight decay / L2 regularisation](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — the work's strongest intervention is made precisely on weight decay: [weakening it tenfold at the moment the flag fires postpones the grokking to a mean of $16267 \pm 1694$ steps against 5567 for the baseline](#p12-2), that is, by a factor of 2.9, while strengthening it threefold accelerates the transition. The author's reading: "at that moment the weight decay leads the model from memorisation to a simpler solution" ([p. 12, para. 3](#p12-3)). The caveats without which the experiment is no evidence for the role of ILDR: there is no control with the same change of weight decay at a fixed step independent of the flag, and the caption of table 11 says that the intervention is rolled back after 500 steps — how a 500-step weakening gives a delay of 10–14 thousand steps the text does not explain.

**[The learning rate](../../concepts/learning-rate.md)** — the second lever of intervention: [multiplying it by $5\times$ accelerates the transition, dividing it by $10\times$ leaves two seeds of three without grokking within 20 000 steps](#p12-2). There is no sweep of the learning rate as such in the work — only two values switched by the flag; and no learning-rate schedule is used at all ([p. 5, para. 3](#p5-3)).

**[The optimiser](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — AdamW is taken by default and is compared with nothing; the work touches the notion only in that [the interventions change the optimiser's hyperparameters on the fly](#p7-2) and roll them back. There is no SGD, no other optimisers and no analysis of what in AdamW is responsible for the effect observed.

**[Causal ablation](../../concepts/causal-ablation-intervention.md)** — the work claims causality but checks it not by ablating part of the network but by changing the optimisation hyperparameters when the feature fires: [six settings, three seeds, a rollback after 500 steps](#p7-2). The conclusion is cautiously formulated — "consistent with ILDR tracking a condition underlying the transition rather than merely correlating with it" ([p. 12, para. 3](#p12-3)) — and the abstract calls the evidence "mechanistically suggestive". What is absent: a control run with the same intervention at a fixed step. The flag is raised at step 4300–5100, that is, at about 85 % of the baseline run's duration, and without such a control the experiment reproduces the long-known dependence of grokking on weight decay rather than showing any role of the measure itself.

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the spectral entropy of the singular values is mentioned twice: in the introduction it is [rejected as indirect and of little lead](#p2-3), and in the section on the measures it is [declared a diagnostic not used for raising the flag](#p5-8). Not a single value of it is given in the work — it appears only as a line in the runtime table. The work cannot be credited with measuring spectral quantities: it does not measure them.

**[Seed variance and reproducibility](../../concepts/seed-variance-reproducibility.md)** — the spread is turned into a criterion of a feature's usability: a lead is useless if its standard deviation exceeds the mean lead itself ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)).

**[Grokking time](../../concepts/grokking-time.md)** — the prediction of the time is assessed not by the fact of a lead but by its stability across seeds ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). A feature that leads the transition on average but unstably is judged unusable.
## Points we could not reconcile

**"The weight norm consistently lags" — except on the single task with the largest lead.** The thesis is declared in the abstract and confirmed on eight seeds ([p. 7, para. 4](#p7-4)). But table 3 ($S_5$) gives the weight norm a lead of 12 600 steps (62%) and Grokfast one of 12 500 (62%) against ILDR's 14 800 (73%). The row that gives the work its best result is the only one in which both baseline measures lead too, and the general thesis is refuted by it. The text does not notice this, because it reads its own table wrongly: the [paragraph](#p8-6) asserts that the other features "peak only much later, at around step 12500", whereas 12 500 is Grokfast's lead and its flag is raised at step 7700. Whence too the "gap of about 7000 steps": in fact the gap between ILDR (5400) and the weight norm (7600) equals 2200 steps.

**"As $p$ grows, both the grokking step and the lead grow" — by the work's own table they do not.** The [paragraph](#p9-1) asserts an increasing trend and itself lists the leads "$1800$, $2600$, $500$ and $800$ steps", that is, a non-monotone series; the grokking times in table 4 are 6500, 8500, 3900 and 4500, and the smallest of the four falls on the largest of the first three moduli. The deviation is written off to "the spread of a single seed", although what is violated is the declared relation itself rather than its precision.

**The abstract promises a lead of "9–73 % of the training budget"; table 6 gives a lag and a zero.** [In the abstract](#p1-2) the range is given with no caveats. In experiment 6 at $f = 0.4$ ILDR **lags** by 500 steps, and at $f = 0.5$ the lead equals zero ([p. 10, para. 1](#p10-1)). The author's explanation ("the compaction phase disappears at an abundance of data") is besides confounded with the construction of the measurement: the baseline of every measure is taken [at step 3000](#p5-4), and the grokking at $f = 0.4$ and $f = 0.5$ occurs at steps 3000 and 3100 — the flag cannot physically lead the transition here.

**"About 17–24 % of the grokking step" against the work's own table, in which 9 % stands.** The [paragraph on experiment 1](#p7-3) names the range $17\text{--}24\%$, whereas the very first row of table 1 (seed 0) gives "(+500, 9%)". The eight fractions by the table are 9, 19, 22, 17, 20, 21, 24 and 17 per cent; the lower bound of the range in the text is twice the actual one.

**The "Lead" column of table 11 is labelled as something other than what stands in it.** The caption calls it "steps saved relative to the baseline", while the numbers equal the difference between the grokking step and the intervention step: $5200-5100=100$, $15900-5100=10800$ and so on. Because of this the conclusion that ["increasing the LR or the WD accelerates the grokking by 200–600 steps"](#p12-2) is taken from the wrong column: relative to the baselines of 6400, 4800 and 5500 the actual acceleration is 300–1200 steps by seed (567–667 by the means of the three accelerating settings), and the "delay of 11 600" in fact equals $16267-5567=10700$.

**The complexity is declared $O(|\mathcal{C}|^2 + N)$, while in experiment 10 the same quantity is estimated as $O(N\cdot d)$.** The claim of a reduction of the cost "from $O(d^2)$ to $O(|\mathcal{C}|^2 + N)$" stands [in the second of three differences from Fisher's criterion](#p3-10) and is repeated in the abstract as $O(|C|^2 + N)$; the [paragraph on the measured time](#p12-1) explains the shape of the curve by "most of the cost falling on the computation of the centroids over N points, which grows as O(N·d)". The factor $d$ (128–256) has dropped out of the estimate declared, although both the centroids and the pairwise distances are computed in $d$ dimensions.

**The 41 % overhead does not follow from the 82 ms given.** The [paragraph](#p12-1) says: "ILDR takes 82 ms per computation against 3.31 ms per training step, which gives a 41 % overhead when logging every 100 steps". The ratio $82/(100\cdot 3.31)$ equals 24.8 %, while the 41.26 % of the lower right table comes from 136.39 ms — a value from the upper table, for which the sample size $N$ is nowhere named. The caption of table 10 meanwhile assigns the lower right part to the case $n{=}1000$, for which the measured time is 81.8 ms. The plateau of "79–83 ms" between $N = 750$ and $N = 2000$ declared in the same paragraph does not agree with the table either: at $N = 1500$ it stands at 86.4 ms.

**One and the same setting gives different grokking times in different experiments.** Seed 42 on modular multiplication: 5000 (experiment 1), 5900 (experiment 9), 6400 (experiment 11); seed 0: 5300, 4800, 4800; seed 1: 5400 and 5500; seed 123: 5200 and 3700; seed 2: 5900 and 5100. The cause is nowhere stipulated. To the same heading: [experiment 8 is declared to measure "the same parameters as experiment 2"](#p10-3), but the addition row in table 8 is different (4800 and 4100 against 4400 and 4000), whereas multiplication and division coincide word for word.

**"Grokfast did not grok within the budget" contradicts section 3.2.** The caption of table 4 explains the dashes by Grokfast's not having reached grokking, and the [paragraph](#p9-2) develops this as a property of the method — "does not give a consistent convergence to a generalising solution within the optimisation horizon allotted". But [section 3.2](#p2-5) says that the amplification step is removed, "so that the training remains unchanged in all the conditions": Grokfast optimises nothing here, and a dash means that no flag was raised rather than that the run did not grok. The training in those cells is the same as in the neighbouring ones.

**The three numbers of experiment 7 agree neither with each other nor with its description.** [Given](#p10-2) are a coefficient of variation of 121.8 % before the transition, 61.6 % after, and a variance ratio of 1,696×. From these it follows that the mean ILDR after the transition is about 21 times **lower** than before it ($\sqrt{1696}\cdot 0.616/1.218 \approx 20.8$) — directly contrary both to the description "ILDR settles into a high steady state with a small variance" and to the whole frame of the work, in which ILDR rises at the transition. Separately: the normalised spread fell only twofold and remained at 61.6 %, which is hard to call "a small variance", while a fall of the **un**normalised variance in a quantity whose mean has changed says nothing in itself about the sharpness of the transition.

**The intervention is rolled back after 500 steps, and the delay it induces is 10–14 thousand steps.** The caption of table 11 says outright: "The interventions are applied at the ILDR flag and rolled back 500 steps later". The weakening of the weight decay is applied at steps 4300–5100 and postpones the grokking to 14 400–18 500. The text makes no attempt to reconcile the one with the other.

**Promised measurements are absent, and the table captions diverge from their contents.** [§3.3 promises](#p6-2) that "the between-class and within-class distances are decomposed separately in order to confirm the geometric rearrangement" — no such decomposition appears in the results. The spectral entropy is [declared a diagnostic](#p5-8) and is thereafter given nowhere, although the introduction rejects it precisely for its small lead. Table 3 ($S_5$) is called "an experiment with a sequence length… and savings relative to the baseline", and table 8 "an extended run"; in table 10 the number of held-out examples is called "the sequence length" $n$, whereas in §3.3.5 the same quantity is the sample size $N$; and [§3.3.6 declares six settings](#p7-2) while listing five (the sixth apparently being the baseline).

## What is measured, what is shown and what is conjectured

**Measured with repetitions.** The only experiment with repetitions over seeds is the eight runs of modular multiplication at $p=97$. In it ILDR leads the transition on all eight seeds ($950 \pm 250$ steps), the weight norm lags on all eight ($-450 \pm 200$), and Grokfast's slow gradient as a feature is unstable ($-1862 \pm 3532$: leading on three seeds, lagging on five). The arithmetic of table 1 agrees with the results given exactly. This is the work's checkable core.

**Shown on one seed.** All three sweeps — over the modulus, over depth and over the training-data fraction — and the oscillation experiment run [at one seed per condition](#p6-4); this is stipulated outright. Experiment 11 runs on three seeds, experiment 9 on five. It follows that the claims "the lead grows with the difficulty of the operation" and "the lead grows with $p$" rest on single runs, while the seed spread for the same setting, measured in experiment 1, is $\pm 250$ steps — of the same order as the leads themselves in most rows of those sweeps (300, 400, 500, 800 steps).

**Shown, but not by what it is called.** The thresholds of all three measures ($2.5\times$, $75\%$, $50\%$ of the step-3000 baseline) are [tuned by a preliminary sweep on one held-out seed](#p5-4) and fixed; the sensitivity of the conclusions to the threshold is not checked. False positives were not measured at all: there are no non-grokking runs in the assessment of the feature, and the claim "the feature fires before the transition" is checked only where the transition occurred. ILDR requires **labelled** held-out data — exactly what the validation accuracy is measured on — so the gain is not in the cheapness of the observation (136.39 ms against 0.34 ms for the weight norm and 3.31 ms for a training step, that is, 400 times dearer than the norm and 41 times dearer than a training step) but only in the geometry's stirring earlier.

**The stability of the estimate itself is not discussed.** ILDR is computed [on a subsample of up to $N = 1{,}500$ held-out examples](#p5-5): that is about 15 examples per class at $p = 97$ and about 12 per class in $S_5$. The within-class spread in 128–256 dimensions is estimated from a dozen points; there are no confidence intervals and no dependence of the measure on $N$ in the work.

**Conjectured.** The causal role of ILDR is the conclusion of experiment 11, and it is hedged by the author himself: "consistent with ILDR tracking a condition underlying the transition" ([p. 12, para. 3](#p12-3)), and the abstract calls the evidence "mechanistically suggestive". The reading of the lead on division is marked as unconfirmed: "although this still requires confirmation" ([p. 8, para. 3](#p8-3)). The relation to neural collapse is relegated [to future work](#p13-3), although the quantity measured is already, up to a factor, the reciprocal of $\mathrm{NC1}$. The reference to circuit formation (Nanda et al., 2023) in [experiment 7](#p10-2) is the only one and rests on a resemblance of words: there are no Fourier measures and no other check of circuit formation here.

**Acknowledged by the author.** [The scope is narrowed](#p13-2) to algorithmic tasks with a clear class structure; the behaviour at blurred boundaries and at a very large number of classes is unknown; the 41 % overhead is called unacceptable for short runs; and the spread of the validation accuracy at the early-stopping point is admitted to be high. Of early stopping it is said outright that the figure of 18.6 % "reflects steps saved rather than a successful early stopping in every case" ([p. 11, para. 1](#p11-1)) — in four seeds of five the training is cut off before the generalisation (validation accuracies of 7.1 %, 31.6 %, 35.2 % and 36.5 % against 99.3 % in the fifth).

**There is not a single plot of measured quantities in the work.** Eleven tables and one synthetic illustration are the whole of its graphical content. The ILDR curves the text refers to ("ILDR reaches its principal transition at step 5400", [p. 8, para. 5](#p8-5)) are not shown. The illustration itself besides does not match its description: [it says](#p4-1) "$K$ class centroids equidistantly on a circle of radius $\delta$", while three centroids are drawn at $(-2.4, 1.2)$, $(2.4, 1.2)$ and $(0, -2.4)$ — with neither a common circle nor equal pairwise distances (4.8 against 4.33).

## Common misreadings and overstatements

Editorial notes on the places where the present work conveys someone else's result erroneously or without the authors' caveats. The "details" links from the "Erroneous retellings and citations of this paper" sections in the cards of the cited works lead here.

###### mc-2405-20233

**Lee et al. (2405.20233) — GrokFast credited to other authors and recast as a passive measure, after which it is declared unusable.**

The formulations: `"GrokFast (Liu et al., 2024) is originally an optimizer modification that computes an exponential moving average (EMA) of the gradient"`; in the bibliography, `"[2] Liu, Y., et al. *Grokfast: Accelerating Grokking by Amplifying Slow Gradients*. arXiv preprint arXiv:2405.20233, 2024."`; and [the conclusion of the depth sweep](#p9-4): `"This is a fundamental failure since this method is designed to accelerate grokking by amplifying low-frequency gradients but cannot track the transition it targets."`

Analysis. First, the authorship: GrokFast is the work of [Lee et al. 2024](../2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients/2405.20233.grokfast-accelerated-grokking-by-amplifying-slow-gradients.card.md) (Jaerin Lee, Bong Gyun Kang, Kihoon Kim, Kyoung Mu Lee), and the error is carried through consistently — in the text of section 2.2, in the caption of the measure in section 3.2 ("Liu, 2024") and in the bibliography itself, in which only the arXiv identifier and the title are correct. Second, the subject: [the present work itself reports](#p2-5) that "the amplification step is removed, so that the training remains unchanged in all the conditions" — that is, what is tested is not GrokFast but its EMA in the role of an observer. The conclusion about a "fundamental failure of the method, since it is designed to accelerate grokking" substitutes the subject: the amplification for whose sake the method was created is switched off here, and there is not a single run with GrokFast switched on in the work. Of the same kind is the caption of table 4 ("Grokfast did not grok within the training budget") — it directly contradicts the work's own section 3.2 and is analysed above, under "Inconsistencies".

What is conveyed correctly meanwhile: the EMA formula, the value $\alpha = 0.99$ and the effective window $1/(1-\alpha)=100$ steps ([p. 2, para. 4](#p2-4)) correspond to the original, and the measured instability of the EMA itself as a feature is a conscientious result if read as a claim about the EMA rather than about the method. The warning concerns the formulation and the attribution rather than the measurement.
