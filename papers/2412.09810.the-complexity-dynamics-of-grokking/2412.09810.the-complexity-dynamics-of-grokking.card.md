## Concepts covered

**[Kolmogorov complexity](../../concepts/kolmogorov-complexity.md)** — the work makes it a **measurable quantity** rather than a framing notion. $K$ itself is uncomputable, but compression gives an upper bound: a compressed file together with a decompressor forms a program generating the original weights ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)). Everything that follows is a struggle to make that bound tight enough: a naive bzip2 over the weights gives a curve indistinguishable from a straight line ([`fig-9`](#fig-9)), and the dynamics becomes visible only after a coarsening.

**[Compression and the MDL](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the minimum description length principle is taken as the foundation: the best model minimises the sum of the entropy of the data given the model and the complexity of the model itself ([`p2-1`](#p2-1)). Hence a direct consequence about memorisation: a lookup table drives the entropy of the data to zero, but its own complexity equals the original entropy, so that the total description length does not change and no compression occurs. This is the shortest formulation of why memorisation is counted "not a solution" in the corpus.

**[Generalisation bounds](../../concepts/generalization-bounds.md)** — the connection of complexity with generalisation is drawn not by analogy but through a ready bound: $R(h)\leq\hat{R}(h)+\sqrt{(K(h)+2\log K(h)+\log(1/\delta))/2n}$ ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)). The work gives its values for its own models and honestly reports the result: **not one model yields a non-vacuous bound** ([`fig-6`](#fig-6)) — the models are too over-parametrised and the dataset is small. The authors' regulariser merely gives the best (still vacuous) bound of those compared.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — grokking is proposed to be considered a **phase transition in complexity**: the complexity grows during the memorisation and falls sharply at the generalisation ([`p1-5`](#p1-5), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)), whereas without regularisation the model remains in the high-complexity phase indefinitely ([`fig-3`](#fig-3)). The analogy with physics here is not decorative: the very scheme is borrowed from Aaronson et al., who studied the rise and fall of complexity in a cellular automaton mixing two liquids ([`p5-7`](#p5-7)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — a **spectral entropy** $H_{\text{svd}}=-\sum\bar{\sigma}_ {i}\log\bar{\sigma}_ {i}$ over the normalised singular values is introduced, and through the result of Roy & Vetterli it is identified with the effective rank: $\text{rank}_ {\text{eff}}=\exp(H_{\text{svd}})$ ([`p10-1`](#p10-2)). The quantity serves two purposes at once — measuring the intrinsic dimension over the course of the training, and acting as a penalty in the loss function.

**[Variants of regularisation](../../concepts/regularization-variants.md)** — a new regulariser is proposed: a penalty on the spectral entropy plus a Gaussian noise on the weights at every forward pass. The noise sets an effective lower bound on the precision of the parameters, preventing the network from storing structure below the noise floor ([`p8-7`](#p8-7)). The claimed result is that this technique **induces grokking** and yields networks of lower complexity than weight decay alone ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — receives an analysis of its limitations. Weight decay controls the information capacity through the parameter range $\lambda$, but the network can still store details in high-frequency information, and the authors' experiments show that it does exactly that ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)). In other words: small norms do not mean low complexity.

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — a direct argument with the norm-based explanations. A small weight norm and a linear structure are declared **different ways of being simple** rather than explanations in their own right, since complexity can arise by different routes ([`p12-3`](#p12-3)). The effective rank is given as one more such route.

**[Circuit efficiency](../../concepts/circuit-efficiency.md)** — the theory of Varma et al. is mentioned as an explanation through the ratio of the logit and parameter norms ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)) and assigned to those very "more particular indicators" against which complexity is set. The work gives no analysis of the theory itself.

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — named as a related phenomenon that a unified theory of complexity and generalisation ought to predict ([`p12-4`](#p12-4)). There are no experiments with it here — only a programmatic statement.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the setting: four operations modulo 113, decoder-only transformers (one layer for addition and multiplication, two for subtraction and division), 20 % of the data for training for addition and multiplication and 30 % for subtraction and division ([`p11-11`](#p11-11), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the notion receives an assessment unusual for the corpus: grokking is called a **sign of improperly regularised training**, and its value lies in the separation of the phases making it a convenient model system for studying phase transitions in complexity ([`p12-4`](#p12-4)). Not a phenomenon requiring explanation but an instrument.

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. The complexity measure here is not a property of the function the network computes but a property of its description: it depends on the coarsening procedure ($\tau$, $\Delta$), on the distortion bound $\epsilon$ and on which archiver does the compressing. The authors do not conceal this — the robustness of the rise-and-fall picture across different $\epsilon$ is given a figure of its own ([`fig-5`](#fig-5)) — but "complexity" in their sense remains an upper bound depending on the chosen set of instruments rather than a measured quantity.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the tasks reproduced here; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — the four regimes and the linear structure, against which complexity is set here; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the explanation through the parameter norm, called a particular indicator; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — circuit efficiency, assigned to the same place; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — progress measures, whose task-boundness the work takes as its point of departure ("we lack a general notion of criticality").

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the "necessary" pole: [an unregularised network never generalises, and its complexity stays high](#fig-1); the work's own regulariser (a spectral-entropy penalty with noise) does induce grokking. In the card of the dispute the work stands beside Nanda on the side of necessity.

**[Scaling laws](../../concepts/scaling-laws.md)** — the work sets its own explanation against them: the present view that "bigger models are better" rests on empirical scaling laws ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)), whereas grokking speaks of a reduction of complexity at an unchanged size.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work divides in two: a measurement part (how to estimate a network's complexity accurately enough) and an explanatory one (what that estimate says about grokking). The first is done carefully, the second is claimed more broadly than it is shown.

**The main measurement result is honest and checkable.** A naive bzip2 compression of the weights shows nothing: the spread of the estimates over the whole training is so small that on a common scale it would look like a straight line ([`fig-9`](#fig-9)). After coarsening — a low-rank approximation plus quantisation, with a Bayesian choice of $\tau$ and $\Delta$ under a constraint on the distortion of the loss — the estimate improves by 30–40 times, and the rise and fall become visible. Coarsening by quantisation alone gives estimates about twice as poor and already fails to distinguish the dynamics in the models with weight decay on two of the four tasks ([`fig-7`](#fig-7)). That is, the picture exists exactly to the extent that the estimate is accurate, and the authors show this outright.

**The robustness to arbitrariness is checked in part.** The rise-and-fall picture persists across different distortion bounds $\epsilon$ ([`fig-5`](#fig-5)) — a substantial check, since $\epsilon$ is chosen by hand. Something else is not checked: the dependence on the choice of archiver and on the coarsening procedure itself. Complexity here is a property of the description, not of the function.

**The connection with generalisation is established formally but does not work numerically.** The bound from [15] is substituted in and computed; the result is that all the bounds are **vacuous** ([`fig-6`](#fig-6)), and the authors name the reason (over-parametrised models, a small dataset). The statement "complexity explains generalisation" is therefore supported here by the course of the curves rather than by numbers: models of lower complexity do generalise, but the quality cannot be predicted from the bound.

**The regulariser works, but the comparison is narrow.** It is claimed that a spectral-entropy penalty with noise induces grokking and yields the lowest complexity of the methods compared ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)). The comparison is against weight decay and against no regularisation on four modular tasks; there are no MSE settings, no non-algorithmic data and no comparison with other techniques that induce grokking.

**Causality is not shown — a correspondence is.** The statement that "a phase transition in complexity is the principal mechanism of grokking" ([`p12-3`](#p12-3)) rests on a coincidence in time: the peak of the complexity falls at the memorisation and the fall at the generalisation. The work contains no intervention that would separate complexity from the other quantities changing along with it (the norm, the effective rank, the accuracy); the regulariser changes them all at once.

**The argument with the norm-based explanations is stated more strongly than it is checked.** The thesis that "a small norm and a linear structure are merely particular ways of being simple" ([`p12-3`](#p12-3)) is convincing as an argument, and it has an empirical support: weight decay reduces the norms but does not prevent details from being stored in high-frequency information ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)). But the work makes no direct comparison with Omnigrok or Varma et al. — in their settings, by their metrics.

**The claim of generality remains a programme.** Double descent, algorithmic thermodynamics, "the apparent complexity of nature" ([`p12-4`](#p12-4)) — all of this is named as a direction rather than a result. Of the corpus's phenomena one is measured here: grokking on four modular operations.

**Its place in the corpus.** This is the corpus's only work in which the complexity of a model is measured **directly** — by compression rather than through a proxy such as the weight norm or the efficiency of a circuit. Hence its main value for the wiki: it checks a premise on which several other explanations silently lean ("the generalising solution is simpler than the memorising one") and shows that the premise holds, but only when "simplicity" is measured with a sufficiently fine instrument.

## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Patterns of erroneous or over-extended citation of this work observed in the citing papers of the corpus. Each entry describes what exactly is distorted in the paraphrase and leads to the authors' own paragraphs, where the lost caveat stands. The analysis of particular formulations is in the citing works' cards, in their "Erroneous citations" sections, to which the "details" links lead. The work is cited surprisingly carefully: most paraphrases convey exactly "a rise and fall of complexity".

**The measure carried over from parameter space into representation space (an over-extension; refuted).** The one substantive shift: the entropy measure is paraphrased as "the compression of representations" — whereas [the measure lives in parameter space: the spectral entropy of the singular values of the weight matrices and the compression of the coarsened weights](#p10-2). The extension is refuted within the corpus: [2604.13123](../2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation/2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation.card.md) records outright that the parameter and the representation spaces "are not interchangeable" and that in DeMoss it is the former. The details: [2603.01968](../2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks/2603.01968.intrinsic-task-symmetry-drives-generalization-in-algorithmic-tasks.card.md#mc-2412-09810).

The authors' caveats — that causality is not shown, that [the generalisation bounds are numerically vacuous](#fig-6), that the complexity depends on the coarsening — go unmentioned by those who cite the work, but they build no claims stronger than the original on them either. Examples of careful citation: [2604.13123](../2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation/2604.13123.spectral-entropy-collapse-as-a-phase-transition-in-delayed-generalisation.card.md) — reproduces the formula, the numbers and the limit of applicability, explicitly separating the objects of measurement; [2603.24746](../2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition/2603.24746.grokking-as-a-falsifiable-finite-size-transition.card.md) — an exact paraphrase plus honest criticism; [2504.17243](../2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation/2504.17243.neuralgrok-accelerate-grokking-by-neural-gradient-transformation.card.md) — an exact rendering of the argument with the norm-based explanations.
