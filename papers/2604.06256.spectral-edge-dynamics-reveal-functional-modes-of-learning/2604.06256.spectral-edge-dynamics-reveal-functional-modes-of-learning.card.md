## Concepts covered

**[Spectral analysis / FVE](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the paper opens a **new branch** for this notion: the diagnostic matrix is assembled neither from the weights, nor from the gradient, nor from the covariance of the activations, but from the **trajectory of the updates** — the Gram matrix $G_{ij}=\langle\delta\theta_{i},\delta\theta_{j}\rangle$ over a sliding window of $W=20$ consecutive $\delta\theta_{t}$ restricted to the attention parameters ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)). The observable is the spectral gap $g_{23}=\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3}$, and the "position of the edge" $k^{*}$ is defined as the index with the largest ratio $\sigma_{k}/\sigma_{k+1}$, weighted by the accumulated mass of the signal ([`p3-6`](#p3-6), [`p3-7`](#p3-7)). The main quantitative result: across 36 single-task conditions a decline of $g_{23}$ is found in 12/12 grokking runs (by a factor of $15$–$110\times$) and in 1 of 24 controls ([`p6-7`](#p6-7), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). The paper also claims an agreement of three independent decompositions — the Gram matrix, the PCA of the displacement and the SVD of the attention matrices themselves ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)). What is missing: none of the three spectral curves is shown in a figure or a table — the agreement is reported in words; $k^{*}$ is never given as a number, and the division "above the edge / below the edge" is made by a fixed triple $v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}$ against $v_{4},v_{5}$; the spectrum is taken from a $20\times 20$ matrix, that is, from a sample of 20 updates in a $D=131{,}072$-dimensional space, with no random baseline (Marchenko–Pastur or shuffled updates) and no check of stability with respect to the window width $W$; and the whole MLP part is excluded from the spectral analysis.

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the phenomenon is taken as a given of the corpus and defined by a purely working threshold: an operation counts as grokking if it reaches $>95\,\%$ validation accuracy within 5000 steps ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)). There is not a single training curve in the paper, the onset of grokking is nowhere measured as an event, and the setting of Power et al. 2022 is taken unchanged. The paper's contribution to the notion lies not in a description of the phenomenon but in a sign: the spectral gap $g_{23}$ separates grokking runs from non-grokking ones across 36 conditions ([`p6-7`](#p6-7)). What is missing: an analysis of what exactly happens at the transition (beyond the verbal "the edge sharpens at the transition", [`p7-3`](#p7-3)), and a reconciliation of the paper's own threshold with its own numbers — the tri-task model groks at steps 17,900, 24,800 and 30,500 ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)), while a special control event is placed at step 41,700 ([`p6-7`](#p6-7)), that is, five and ten times later than the declared 5000.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — six binary operations modulo $p=97$: $(a+b)$, $(a-b)$, $(a\cdot b)$, $(a^{2}+b^{2})$, $(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})$, $(a^{3}+ab)$; the first four are declared grokking, the last two are taken as non-grokking controls ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)). Beyond the single-task runs, models with a shared trunk and a head per task are trained — a dual-task one (add + mul) and a tri-task one (add + mul + $x^{2}+y^{2}$) ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). What matters for the corpus is that the operations here are analysed **by the algebraic structure of the group** rather than by difficulty: addition and subtraction live in the additive group $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$, multiplication in the multiplicative $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}$ with a discrete-logarithm basis at the primitive root $g=5$ ([`p9-5`](#p9-5)), while $x^{2}+y^{2}$ is also tried through the Gaussian integers at $i_{p}=22$ ([`p11-3`](#p11-3)). What is missing: the same operations in Furuta et al. (2402.16726), where at the same $p=97$, the same weight decay $\lambda=1.0$ and AdamW the set of grokking and non-grokking operations coincides with the one here, and $a^{2}+b^{2}$ is already described as a superposition of the representations of the elementary arithmetic; that paper is not cited.

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the main substantive contribution: an update direction $v_{k}$ is turned into a field over the inputs $f_{k}(a,b)=\|\Delta h_{k}(a,b)\|^{2}$, and it is that field which is decomposed over the characters of the group ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). For addition all three leading directions settle on $\omega\approx 25$–$26$, with a maximum concentration $F=0.40$ against a uniform baseline of $0.021$ ([`p8-10`](#p8-10), [`tab-2`](#tab-2)); for multiplication there is no signal in the additive basis ($F\leq 0.11$), while in the discrete-logarithm basis all three settle on $\omega=29$ with $F$ for $v_{1}$ growing by $5.9\times$ ([`p9-7`](#p9-7)). The strongest place in the paper is a two-sided control: the concentration of **addition** in the discrete-logarithm basis, conversely, falls to $0.2$–$0.3\times$ of its additive-basis value ([`p9-8`](#p9-8)), which makes the claim about a "symmetry-aligned basis" refutable. Equally substantial are two negative results: for subtraction each of the three directions settles on a frequency of its own ($v_{1}\to 16$, $v_{2}\to 6$, $v_{3}\to 32$, the union being five modes $\{6,10,16,32,41\}$, [`p10-3`](#p10-3)), but the concentration of the triple ($F\approx 0.16$) is not separated from the bulk ($F\approx 0.16$), and the multi-mode description is stated outright to be a plausible interpretation not separated from the null model ([`p10-5`](#p10-5)); and for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ none of the five bases tried yields a dominant mode ($F\approx 0.10$–$0.15$) ([`p11-4`](#p11-4), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). What is missing: an analysis of the final weights of its own — the comparison with the Fourier structure of a trained model is made in a single phrase about Nanda et al. 2023 ([`p14-7`](#p14-7)), and it is exactly that phrase that contains an error (see "Common misreadings and overstatements"); and the authors deliberately avoid the word "circuits" for their own directions ([`p17-5`](#p17-5)).

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the paper gives the corpus three negative results in a row, set out with no attempt to soften them. A head purity of $\approx 0.14$ for all three top directions across all four grokking operations — the conclusion "the edge is globally distributed across the heads" ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)); an effective rank of the activation perturbation of $\approx 40$ out of $d_{\mathrm{model}}=128$ — "the perturbation is smeared across the activation space" ([`p7-6`](#p7-6)); and a Jaccard overlap over the features of a sparse autoencoder lying at or below the null model of a random direction ([`p8-4`](#p8-4)). The authors' diagnosis is a mismatch of levels rather than an absence of structure: the instruments work in the space of representations while the subject lives in the space of functions ([`p8-5`](#p8-5), [`p13-6`](#p13-6)). What is missing: an analysis of the fact that a deviation of the Jaccard overlap **downwards** from the random baseline (the observed $0.25$–$0.60$ against a baseline of $0.30$–$0.83$) is itself a signal rather than its absence ([`p8-3`](#p8-3)); and the baseline of $1/32$ corresponding to the declared maximum over $2$ layers $\times$ $4$ heads $\times$ $4$ matrix types (see "Points we could not reconcile").

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the paper sets the notion a negative limit: the structure that distinguishes grokking is **not** found at the level of representations. The induced perturbation $\Delta h_{k}(x)$ has a high rank in the activation space, whereas the scalar field $f_{k}(x)$ is low-dimensional over the inputs — "the structure of learning is not in how the perturbation is distributed across neurons, but in which inputs are most affected" ([`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`p5-5`](#p5-5), [`p5-6`](#p5-6)). The change of level of description proposed is from neurons, heads, circuits and features to "functional modes over the input domain" ([`p13-6`](#p13-6)). What is missing: measurements of the representations themselves (the embeddings, the logits, the neuron-to-logit map) — the whole analysis is conducted on the response to a perturbation, and the effective rank of the activations is given only as a negative result.

**[Attention routing](../../concepts/attention-routing-heads.md)** — every spectral observable of the paper is computed **only** over the attention parameters: 4 matrices $\times$ 2 layers, an unfolded dimension $D=131{,}072$ ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)). Hence a direct result for the notion: the leading update directions are not concentrated in a single head — a head purity of $\approx 0.14$ barely exceeds the uniform baseline of $1/8$ named by the authors ([`p7-5`](#p7-5)). What is missing: any justification for excluding the MLP part — the very part in which Nanda et al. 2023 found the Fourier structure; and any analysis of which of the four matrices ($W_{Q},W_{K},W_{V},W_{O}$) carries the edge, beyond a mention of $W_{Q}$ and $W_{K}$ in the third decomposition ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — weight decay enters the paper in two ways. As an experimental lever: the 36 single-task conditions are 6 operations $\times$ 2 settings ($\mathrm{wd}=1.0$ and $\mathrm{wd}=0$) $\times$ 3 seeds, and at $\mathrm{wd}=0$ a decline of $g_{23}$ is found in none of the 12 runs ([`tab-1`](#tab-1), [`p6-7`](#p6-7)). As a supposed cause of the selection: "Fourier representations are parameter-efficient for group-theoretic tasks…, so regularization biases the optimizer toward these solutions", and the authors hedge outright — "weight decay likely plays a role in this selection" ([`p5-11`](#p5-11)). What is missing: any experiment in which the selection of a Fourier mode is measured as a function of the strength of the weight decay — there are only two settings, and both are extremes; and any analysis of the fact that at $\mathrm{wd}=0$ the model simply does not grok, so that 12 of the 24 controls lack both the grokking and the weight decay at once.

**[When regularisation is necessary](../../concepts/regularization-necessity.md)** — the paper states the necessity in a strong form: "the spectral edge forms only under weight decay ($\mathrm{wd}=1.0$) and does not form without it" ([`p5-11`](#p5-11)). It matters that the design of the experiment partly separates this claim from grokking: the row of table 1 for "non-grokking operations at $\mathrm{wd}=1.0$" gives 1/6 — that is, with weight decay present but without grokking the edge almost never forms ([`tab-1`](#tab-1)). What is missing: a test of the necessity as such — not a single attempt to obtain grokking without weight decay (by another norm constraint, another optimizer, another data fraction), so "only under weight decay" refers to the two settings tried rather than to weight decay as such.

**[Group representations and cosets](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** — the notion enters the very core of conjecture 1: for tasks that factor through a group homomorphism $\phi:\mathcal{X}\to G$, the natural eigenmodes are declared to be the irreducible representations (characters) of the group $G$, and it is claimed that the edge selects directions whose fields are concentrated on those same characters ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)). In practice this is played out by changing the group: additive characters for $a\pm b$, characters of the multiplicative group through the discrete logarithm for $a\cdot b$ ([`p9-5`](#p9-5)), Gaussian integers and an angular coordinate for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ ([`p11-3`](#p11-3)); the authors' conclusion is that "the simplicity of the spectral edge is not intrinsic to parameter space; it depends on expressing the perturbation response in the right functional coordinates" ([`p10-1`](#p10-1)). The authors state outright that Fourier is not declared universal and that for non-abelian groups the irreducible representations rather than the characters would be what matters ([`p5-11`](#p5-11)). What is missing: not a single non-abelian task — $S_{5}$ and group composition were not tried, and Chughtai et al. 2023 and Stander et al. 2024 are mentioned in a single phrase ([`p14-7`](#p14-7)); and the conjecture predicts neither which representations are selected nor how many.

**[The canonical grokking testbed](../../concepts/canonical-grokking-testbed.md)** — the setting is taken wholly canonical and without explanation: a two-layer transformer, $d_{\mathrm{model}}=128$, 4 heads, $d_{\mathrm{ff}}=256$, pre-norm, GELU, about 290 thousand parameters; AdamW ($\mathrm{lr}=10^{-3}$, $\mathrm{wd}=1.0$, $\beta_{2}=0.98$), $p=97$, a 50/50 split, three seeds $\{42,137,2024\}$ ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)). The paper adds two extensions to the testbed: a pair of non-grokking operations as a control, and models with a shared trunk on two and three tasks ([`p3-4`](#p3-4)). What is missing: any sweep over $p$, over the data fraction or over depth; and — essential for reading all the Fourier quantities — tables 2–5 and figure 1 are computed on **one** seed (42) and on checkpoints after the grokking ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)), while the three seeds support only table 1.

**[Feature emergence](../../concepts/feature-emergence-feature-learning.md)** — the paper offers the notion a new subject: a *functional mode*, that is, an edge direction whose induced field $f_{k}$ varies in an ordered way over the inputs ([`p4-2`](#p4-2)). The claim of reusability is tested by the shared trunk: for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ in the tri-task model the additive concentration at $\omega=26$ grows from $F=0.09$ to $F=0.20$, the mutual support from $+0.024$ to $+0.043$, and the peak additive frequency shifts from a scattered $\omega=17$ exactly onto the mode of addition ([`p12-4`](#p12-4), [`tab-4`](#tab-4)). Hence the authors' conclusion that functional modes are reusable primitives of the learned computation ([`p12-6`](#p12-6)). What is missing: conjecture 2 is written as a strict inclusion of subspaces ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)) but is tested by two scalars, neither of which is an inclusion, and the subspaces themselves are taken from two **different** trained models; the ablations "addition only / multiplication only / both" the authors call an obvious next step and leave to further work ([`p14-3`](#p14-3)).

**[Linear probing](../../concepts/linear-sparse-probing.md)** — probing enters twice and with opposite outcomes. A TopK sparse autoencoder ($d_{\mathrm{SAE}}=512$, $k=32$) on the residual stream after grokking gives a negative result ([`p8-2`](#p8-2)). Ridge regression on additive, multiplicative and cross Fourier features ([`p11-6`](#p11-6)), by contrast, gives the paper's only positive evidence of compositionality: for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ separately $R^{2}\leq 0.02$, together $0.03$–$0.04$, with cross terms $0.15$–$0.16$ ([`p11-7`](#p11-7), [`tab-3`](#tab-3)). The authors themselves insist that what supports the claim is not the magnitude of $R^{2}$ but the qualitative selectivity — the cross terms help precisely $x^{2}+y^{2}$ ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)). What is missing: the probe as a causal instrument — not one intervention confirming that the modes found carry the computation; and any analysis of why adding cross terms also raises $R^{2}$ for addition ($0.18$–$0.31\to 0.29$–$0.40$), where there should be no compositionality.

**[The optimizer](../../concepts/optimizer-adam-adamw-sgd.md)** — the paper trains with AdamW throughout ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)) while formulating conjecture 1 about SGD: "SGD concentrates its updates along directions aligned with the task-relevant eigenmodes" ([`p5-10`](#p5-10)). The discrepancy is nowhere noted, and the question of what in the observed spectrum of the updates belongs to the optimizer (the moment state, the element-wise normalisation) and what to the landscape is not raised. What is missing: not a single run with another optimizer, and no analysis of the influence of $\beta_{2}=0.98$ on the Gram matrix of consecutive $\delta\theta_{t}$.

**[Phase transition](../../concepts/phase-transition.md)** — the notion is only mentioned: grokking is called a phase transition in a single phrase of the introduction ([`p1-7`](#p1-7)), and the BBP (Baik–Ben Arous–Péché) frame is given as the source of the reading of a declining $g_{23}$, with a reference to Xu 2026c ([`p3-6`](#p3-6)). There is no order parameter in the paper, no analysis of the sharpness and no finite-size analysis; the BBP threshold is not given as a number either, and no quantity is compared with it.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the notion enters only through a reference to Nanda et al. 2023 ([`p14-7`](#p14-7)); the authors do not call their own quantity a "progress measure". Yet by construction $g_{23}$ is a quantity that distinguishes grokking from its absence across 36 conditions ([`p6-7`](#p6-7)), and the authors set it up precisely as a contrast of "the trajectory against the destination" ([`p15-1`](#p15-1)). What is missing: a measurement of the lead — not one number about how many steps before the grokking the decline of $g_{23}$ becomes distinguishable, whereas neighbouring works by the same author do give such numbers.

**[Clock and Pizza algorithms](../../concepts/clock-vs-pizza.md)** — a mention only: "Zhong et al. 2024 described the clock and pizza representations in this setting" ([`p14-7`](#p14-7)), a single phrase in a list of related works and dated 2024 instead of 2023 (the preprint appeared in June 2023). There is no separation of the clock from the pizza in the paper and no attention strength as a lever, and the question of which of the two algorithms corresponds to the mode $\omega\approx 25$–$26$ found here is not raised.

**[Loss landscape / basins](../../concepts/loss-landscape-basins.md)** — a mention with a claim attached: conjecture 1 is declared "a claim about the geometry of the loss landscape, not about representation theory" ([`p5-10`](#p5-10)). The paper measures no quantity of the landscape whatever — no curvature, no Hessian, no barriers; the link to curvature is drawn only by a reference to an earlier work by the same author on commutator defects ([`p7-3`](#p7-3), [`p15-2`](#p15-2)).

## Points we could not reconcile

**The decline of $g_{23}$ is read in two opposite ways.** In the [definition of the edge](#p3-6) a declining $g_{23}=\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3}$ means that "the top block of two directions is tightening", whereas [in section 4.1](#p6-7) the same quantity is introduced with the words "the two top eigenvalues separate from the rest". A decline of the difference $\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{3}$ literally means that $\sigma_{2}$ approaches $\sigma_{3}$, that is, a **weakening** of the separation of the second eigenvalue from the third. The reconciliation is given in the paper in words — "the rest of the spectrum contracts" ([`p3-6`](#p3-6)) — but there is nowhere a normalisation of $g_{23}$ by the trace or by $\sigma_{1}$, nor a ratio $\sigma_{2}/\sigma_{3}$, so the reader has nothing on which to check that the decline of the difference is caused by an overall contraction.

**A mean over the bulk is replaced by a single value.** [In section 6.2](#p8-10) it is said: "the mean over the top triple $F=0.29$ exceeds the mean over the bulk $F=0.18$ by $1.6\times$". By [table 2](#tab-2) the below-edge values are $0.18$ ($v_{4}$) and $0.12$ ($v_{5}$), whose mean is $0.15$ rather than $0.18$; with the correct mean the excess comes out as $1.9\times$ rather than $1.6\times$. What was taken, apparently, is the single value of $v_{4}$ instead of the mean over the two. The error is in the direction of a weaker conclusion, but the number $1.6\times$ and the number $0.18$ do not agree with the paper's own table.

**The peakedness of $32.8\times$ is ascribed to the wrong object and the wrong frequency.** [In section 5](#p8-1) it is said that the Fourier peakedness "reaches $32.8\times$ uniform for $v_{1}$ at the grokking transition (addition, $\omega=25$)". By [table 2](#tab-2) the peak for $v_{1}$ stands at $\omega=26$ with $F=0.33$, that is, $15.7\times$ over the baseline of $0.021$. The value $32.8\times$ corresponds to $F\approx 0.69$ — and there is exactly one such quantity in the paper: $F=0.67$ at $\omega=26$ for the **first principal component** of the displacement $\Delta h_{1}$ [in section 6.2](#p9-1), where the subject is not $v_{1}$ but a decomposition into individual principal components. The moments of measurement differ too: section 5 says "at the transition", whereas the caption of [figure 1](#fig-1) declares all the spectra to be computed at checkpoints after the grokking.

**The baseline for head purity does not match the maximum being measured.** [In hypothesis 1](#p7-5) the head purity is defined by the formula $\max_{\ell,h,m}\|v_{k}[\text{block}_{\ell,h,m}]\|^{2}/\|v_{k}\|^{2}$ with the maximum taken over $2$ layers $\times$ $4$ heads $\times$ $4$ matrix types, that is, over 32 blocks, and the measured $0.14$ is immediately compared with a uniform baseline of $1/8=0.125$ "for 8 layer$\times$head combinations (averaging over the matrix type)". A maximum over 32 blocks and a baseline for 8 are quantities of different sets: for 32 blocks the uniform baseline is $1/32\approx 0.031$, and the same $0.14$ would then be an excess of roughly $4.5\times$. The conclusion "the edge is globally distributed across the heads" rests on which of the two baselines is right, and the paper does not say which of the two quantities was in fact measured.

**The working definition of grokking does not agree with the paper's own numbers.** [In section 2.1](#p3-2) the operations called grokking are those that reach $>95\,\%$ validation accuracy **within 5000 steps**. Meanwhile the [tri-task model](#p3-4) groks at steps 17,900, 24,800 and 30,500, and a special control event is placed [at step 41,700](#p6-7). The threshold of 5000 steps is nowhere revised and nowhere restricted to the single-task runs; read literally, the tri-task model does not fall under the paper's own definition of grokking.

**A reading trap, not a contradiction: table 5 and the text give different summaries.** [Table 5](#tab-5) gives for subtraction a "peak $F$ = 0.19", whereas [the text of section 6.4](#p10-5) names $F\approx 0.16$. The discrepancy is removed by the fact that the column of table 5 is the **maximum** over the three top directions (for addition it holds $0.40$ — the value of $v_{3}$; for multiplication $0.32$ — the value of $v_{1}$), while the text gives the **mean** over the triple. This reconciliation is nowhere stated, and citing the number $0.19$ alongside the textual $0.16$ creates the appearance of a contradiction where there is none.

## What is shown, what is conjectured and what the paper does not claim

The paper itself divides its content into three kinds — definitions, an experimental observation and conjectures ([section 3](#p4-2), [`p5-4`](#p5-4), [`p5-9`](#p5-9)) — and in a separate section lists what it does **not** claim: an exact decomposition, universality beyond modular arithmetic, a complete theory, and a description of the functional subspace of subtraction ([`p14-2`](#p14-2)). The marking of boundaries here is unusually honest, and a citing author would do well to hold to it rather than to a retelling.

**Shown and supported by a control.** The separation of grokking by $g_{23}$ across 36 conditions with three seeds ([`p6-7`](#p6-7), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)) — the paper's only quantity computed on more than one seed. The two-sided basis control on multiplication: a gain of $5.9\times$ on moving to the discrete logarithm **and** an opposing loss for addition in the same basis ([`p9-7`](#p9-7), [`p9-8`](#p9-8)) — it is the second half that makes the claim about a "symmetry-aligned basis" refutable. The qualitative selectivity of the cross terms: they raise $R^{2}$ for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ fourfold and do not help the simple operations ([`p11-7`](#p11-7), [`tab-3`](#tab-3)) — and the authors themselves insist that what supports the claim is the selectivity rather than the magnitude of $R^{2}$ ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)).

**Shown, but on one seed and after the transition.** All the Fourier quantities — tables 2–5 and figure 1 — are computed on seed 42 and on checkpoints after the grokking ([`fig-1`](#fig-1)). There are no spreads across seeds in the paper, no confidence intervals and no time course of the concentration; and yet section 5 speaks of the peakedness "at the transition" ([`p8-1`](#p8-1)), and section 4.2 of the edge "forming during the memorisation period and sharpening at the transition" ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)), without a single curve.

**Negative results, set out plainly.** Three failures of representation-level instruments ([`p7-5`](#p7-5), [`p7-6`](#p7-6), [`p8-4`](#p8-4)); the absence of any separation of the edge from the bulk for subtraction, which the authors call an honest negative result ([`p10-5`](#p10-5)); the absence of a dominant mode for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ in any of the five bases ([`p11-4`](#p11-4)). The caveat about the strength of the signal — "$F=0.40$ means that 60% of the power is spread over the other frequencies" ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)) — stands in the text, and it is the first thing lost in retelling.

**Conjectured and stated outright to be unproved.** Both conjectures: about the selection of the task's eigenmodes by the dynamics ([`p5-9`](#p5-9)) and about compositional inheritance under a shared trunk ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)); the authors say "we do not prove either conjecture" ([`p6-4`](#p6-4)). The role of weight decay in the selection of Fourier modes is hedged — "likely plays a role" ([`p5-11`](#p5-11)). Conjecture 2 is written as a strict inclusion of subspaces $\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\mathrm{shared}}\cap(\mathcal{F}_{T_{2}}+\mathcal{F}_{T_{3}})\supsetneq\mathcal{F}_{T}^{\mathrm{single}}\cap(\dots)$, but the subspaces are taken from two different trained models, between which no inclusion is defined, and the conjecture is tested by two scalars — the concentration at $\omega=26$ and the increment of the mutual support ([`p12-4`](#p12-4)); neither of them is an inclusion.

**Not tested and not qualified.** The position of the edge $k^{*}$ is never given as a number, and its definition — "weighted by the accumulated mass of the signal" ([`p3-7`](#p3-7)) — is not a formula: neither the weights nor the threshold is written out, and the division "above the edge / below the edge" is nowhere derived from a measured $k^{*}$. The spectrum is taken from a $20\times 20$ Gram matrix in a space of dimension $D=131{,}072$ ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)) — with no random baseline and no check of stability with respect to $W$. The value $\varepsilon=0.005\cdot\|\theta_{\mathrm{attn}}\|$ ([`p4-1`](#p4-1)) is chosen with no sensitivity check, and a finite difference is substituted for the derivative of definition 1 at a single step. The grouping by discrete logarithm is undefined at $a=0$ or $b=0$ (about 2 % of the input pairs at $p=97$), and how those pairs were excluded or handled is not said ([`p9-5`](#p9-5)). Section 7 and appendix A contain neither a table nor a figure: only the numbers $S\approx 0.8$–$0.9$ above the edge and $0.3$–$0.5$ below it are reported ([`p13-5`](#p13-5), [`p17-4`](#p17-4)) — and at $T=3$ the lower bound of the scale is $1/T\approx 0.33$, so the "below-edge" values lie on the very floor.

**The refutation by sparse autoencoders is built so that the negative result is left uninterpreted.** The observed Jaccard overlap of $0.25$–$0.60$ turns out to be **below** the null model of a random direction, $0.30$–$0.83$ ([`p8-3`](#p8-3), [`p8-4`](#p8-4)). A deviation downwards from chance is also a signal (the edge directions overlap on SAE features **less** than random ones do), but it is read as an absence of structure and is not discussed.

**The nearest neighbour is not named.** Furuta et al. 2024 ([2402.16726](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.card.md)) are not cited at all, although at the same $p=97$, the same weight decay $\lambda=1.0$ and AdamW they have the same set of operations: $a^{2}+b^{2}$ groks, while $a^{2}+ab+b^{2}$ and $a^{3}+ab$ do not, on account of their non-decomposability ([Furuta, fig. 6](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/original/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.md#fig-6), [fig. 7](../2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials/original/2402.16726.towards-empirical-interpretation-of-internal-circuits-and-properties-in-grokked-transformers-on-modular-polynomials.md#fig-7)). There too $a^{2}+b^{2}$ is described as a superposition of the representations of the elementary arithmetic — in substance the same observation as the "cross terms" here — and a multi-task mixture with co-grokking has already been analysed. The settings differ (Furuta use a one-layer transformer and a data fraction $r=0.3$), but the choice of the six operations and the division of the sample into grokking and non-grokking ones coincide down to their composition.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks about places where this paper restates the results of other works with an error or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2301-05217

**Nanda et al. (2301.05217) — both the modulus and the number of key frequencies are substituted.** [In section 9](#p14-7) it is said: `"Nanda et al. 2023 identified Fourier-basis representations (“grokking circuits”) in 1-layer models trained on modular addition, showing that the trained network uses specific Fourier frequencies (e.g., $\omega=25$ for mod-97 addition) in its internal computation."` In the main experiment of Nanda et al. the modulus is **113**, not 97 ([Nanda, §3](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md#sec-3)), and **five** key frequencies were found, $k\in\{14,35,41,42,52\}$, not one ([Nanda, §4.1](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md#sec-4-1)); the frequency $\omega=25$ is not among them. The price of the error is high: the coincidence of "their own" $\omega\approx 25$–$26$ with "their" $\omega=25$ is the main argument of [section 8.1](#p13-8) and [section 9](#p15-1) — "the same frequencies arise in the dynamics and in the final weights, therefore Fourier modes are selected in the course of the optimisation". In reality there is nothing to coincide: at different moduli the frequencies need not agree, and Nanda et al. have five of them. Note at the same time that the second citation of the same work — [in conjecture 1](#p5-10), where it is said that the model's use of group characters "is to be expected from prior work (Nanda et al. 2023)" — is accurate.

###### mc-2602-18523

**Xu (2602.18523) — the order of the grokking in the tri-task model is transposed.** [In section 2.2](#p3-4) it is said: `"The tri-task model groks on all three operations (at steps 17,900, 24,800, and 30,500 respectively), with the staggered ordering providing a natural testbed for compositional reuse (Xu 2026b)."` The word "respectively" refers to the order in which the tasks are listed in the same sentence — add + mul + $x^{2}+y^{2}$ — that is, it ascribes step 17,900 to addition, 24,800 to multiplication and 30,500 to the quadratic sum. In the cited work, from which these runs are taken, the order is the reverse — multiplication groks first, then the quadratic sum ($x^{2}+y^{2}$), then addition: multiplication 17,900, $x^{2}+y^{2}$ 24,800, addition 30,500 ([Xu 2602.18523, p. 4, para. 5](../2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure/original/2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure.md#p4-5), [table 2](../2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure/original/2602.18523.the-geometry-of-multi-task-grokking-transverse-instability-superposition-and-weight-decay-phase-structure.md#tab-2)). ## Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper

Below are the patterns observed in the citing works of the corpus. For each one it is said what exactly gets distorted, with links to the authors' paragraphs where the qualification that goes missing still stands.

**"It is shown that the edge collapses onto Fourier frequencies for modular arithmetic" (an overstatement).** The only substantively citing work of the corpus — 2604.07380 (Xu, "The Lifecycle of the Spectral Edge…", no card in the corpus) — retells the paper thus: `"A companion paper (Xu 2026h) shows that edge directions are *functional modes*—structured perturbation patterns that collapse to Fourier frequencies for modular arithmetic."` The generalisation to "modular arithmetic" discards exactly the half of the paper for the sake of which it was written: the collapse onto a single mode is obtained for addition and multiplication, whereas for subtraction the concentration of the triple is **not** separated from the bulk and the multi-mode description is declared a plausible interpretation with no support from a null model ([`p10-5`](#p10-5)), and for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ there is no dominant mode in any of the five bases tried ([`p11-4`](#p11-4)). The caveat about the strength of the signal is lost as well: $F=0.40$ means that 60 % of the power lies on the other frequencies ([`p9-3`](#p9-3)), and the authors expressly decline to claim an exact decomposition ([`p14-2`](#p14-2)). The heading of section 6.5 of the paper says the direct opposite of the retelling: a low-dimensional structure is not the same as a harmonic one ([`p11-1`](#p11-1)).

**"The edge carries $\omega=25$ for addition" (an overstatement).** The same work goes on: `"Edge directions carry distinct Fourier frequencies ($\omega=25$ for addition, $\omega=29$ for multiplication in the discrete-log basis (Xu 2026h))."` For multiplication the number is exact ([`p9-7`](#p9-7)); for addition the paper everywhere gives **not** one frequency but a band $\omega\approx 25$–$26$, and by [table 2](#tab-2) $v_{1}$ and $v_{2}$ peak at $26$, and only $v_{3}$ at $25$ ([`p8-10`](#p8-10)). The one place where a bare "$\omega=25$" stands is precisely [section 5](#p8-1), which contains the internal inconsistency analysed above. The numbers $25$ and $26$ are indistinguishable in a retelling, but it is exactly on the coincidence with "their own" frequency that the paper's main argument about the agreement of the dynamics with the final weights is built ([`p15-1`](#p15-1)), and there it rests on a mis-cited Nanda et al. (see [`mc-2301-05217`](#mc-2301-05217)).

**An accurate restatement, for comparison.** In 2605.20441 (Wang, "Weight Decay Regimes in Grokking Transformers", no card in the corpus) the work is named in a list of the same author's diagnostics: `"Xu’s diagnostics operate in weight and update space (PCA trajectory variance, the commutator norm $\|[W_{Q},W_{K}]\|_{F}$, spectral-edge gradient/decay decomposition, functional-mode spectra) and require full checkpoint access"` — the "functional-mode spectra" are placed in the space of weights and updates, and it is stated that the quantities require full access to the checkpoints. This is correct both in subject and in the cost of the measurement; no claim of a universal collapse is introduced into the retelling.

**The risk this paper invites.** It will be cited as "it is shown that grokking selects a single Fourier mode". It would be more accurate to say: on one seed, at checkpoints after the grokking, over the attention parameters of a two-layer transformer at $p=97$, it is shown that the three top update directions induce fields with a concentration $F\leq 0.40$ against a uniform baseline of $0.021$ — and only for addition and multiplication; for subtraction there is no separation from the bulk at all, and for $x^{2}+y^{2}$ there is no harmonic description in any of the five bases.
