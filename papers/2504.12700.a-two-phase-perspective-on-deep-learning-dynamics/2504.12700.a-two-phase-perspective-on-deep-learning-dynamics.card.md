# de Mello Koch & Ghosh 2025 — A Two-Phase Perspective on Deep Learning Dynamics

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Robert de Mello Koch** (School of Science, Huzhou University; the Mandelstam Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of the Witwatersrand), **Animik Ghosh** (School of Science, Huzhou University).

arXiv [2504.12700](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12700), April 2025, hep-th, 17 pages, 6 figures (11 panels), 7 display formulas, no appendices; neither code nor data. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 4, in the corpus — 1. The claim: the training of a deep network proceeds in two periods — a fast curve fitting and a slow compression ("forgetting") — and grokking, double descent and the information bottleneck are three views of one two-period progression. The argument is a coincidence of three transition times read off the same runs (division modulo 96 and the multiplication table of $S_{5}$); the mutual information $I(T;X)$ is proposed as a progress measure, and the compression is read on the pattern of the renormalisation group.

## Concepts covered

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — called the "fruit fly of generalisation": a convenient testbed rather than a subject in its own right ([`p2-4`](#p2-4)). The definition is taken from Power et al. unchanged; the main thesis is that grokking is a manifestation of a deeper structure: training proceeds in two stages, a curve fitting and a coarse-graining "forgetting" ([`p11-6`](#p11-6)). An essential mismatch with part of the corpus: the grokking is obtained at a **zero** weight decay, and the paper does not note this.

**[Double descent](../../concepts/double-descent.md)** — the third unifying work of the corpus (after 2303.06173 and 2402.15175, which are not cited): the ascending branch of the classical U-curve is identified with the fitting period and the second descent with the compression period ([`p7-2`](#p7-2)); double descent, however, is defined by the complexity of the model and measured by the number of training steps, and the move from one axis to the other is nowhere justified.

**[Representation compression](../../concepts/manifold-representation-compression.md)** — the information bottleneck is adopted as the frame of the second period: once the training error is small, $I(T;X)$ falls at a roughly constant $I(T;Y)$ — the network "forgets" the non-predictive details of the input ([`p7-6`](#p7-6)); the compression is read on the pattern of the renormalisation group (the unimportant parameters die away — the network forgets the fine structure), and three geometric moves of coarse-graining the partition of the input space are proposed: an alignment, a disappearance and a merging of the zero lines ([`p13-5`](#p13-5), [`fig-6`](#fig-6)) — none of which is counted in any trained network.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — the claimed contribution: $I(T;X)$ between the input and the output of a hidden layer as a natural progress measure "complementary" to the circuit measures — local complexity (LC) and the linear mapping number (LMN) ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)); yet neither LC nor LMN is measured once in the paper, and the abstract and §4.2 differ on the status of the measure (a progress measure against a process measure, [`p12-3`](#p12-3)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — a silent departure from the corpus's canon: AdamW with a weight decay of 0.0, and the grokking is observed all the same on both tasks ([`p8-5`](#p8-5)); the role of the weight decay is not analysed at all — even though in part of the corpus it counts as a condition of the transition.

**[The information bottleneck](../../concepts/information-bottleneck.md)** — the frame enters here not in its own right but as one of three: it is supposed that grokking, double descent and the information bottleneck reflect a common underlying structure ([`p3-2`](#p3-2)). The value of the unification is a shared vocabulary rather than a new measurement.
## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**The argument is a coincidence of times read by eye.** The three transition points (a zero training error; the start of the second descent; the rightmost point of the trace on the information plane) are not defined numerically — there is no threshold, no fitting rule and no confidence interval; all are read off plots on a logarithmic axis. One run per task, with no seeds and no spreads. Of the three "aligned" points two are read off the validation quality of one run (two records of one curve), and only the information one is measured independently; and all three are defined as "after the training error hits zero", so the closeness is partly built into the setting.

**The method of estimating the mutual information is not described at all** — no binning, no estimator, no number of samples and no handling of the degeneracy of $I(T;X)$ for a deterministic network; and yet it is precisely on the method of estimation that the whole known dispute around the information bottleneck turns (the known objection — that a fall of $I(T;X)$ can be a consequence of a saturating non-linearity — is not mentioned). The extent of the compression is small: on division $I(T;X)$ goes from 4.26 to 4.18 (about 2 %); the units are not named. The conclusion of insensitivity to depth is drawn from two measurement points.

**The modulus 96 is composite** — division is defined only for divisors coprime to the modulus; there is neither a count of the admissible pairs nor a caveat (Power et al. took the prime 97).

**The explanatory part is an argument.** The section on the renormalisation group is posed as a question, and not a single coarse-graining transformation is constructed; the three moves of coarse-graining are drawn by hand and not counted; and the claim about accelerating the second period is supported by neither a device nor an experiment — with a line of grokking accelerators already in existence by April 2025. Of the five references in the renormalisation-group passage three are self-citations of the first author.

**The predecessors of the unification are not cited.** Neither Davies et al. (2303.06173) nor Huang et al. (2402.15175) — the claim is made as if there were no predecessors; of the three unifying works of the corpus this is the poorest in measurements and the only one to add a third phenomenon (the IB) to the pair. Information-theoretic progress measures for grokking (Clauw et al., Song et al.) were likewise proposed earlier and are not cited.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is shown that grokking, double descent and the IB are one phenomenon") — more precisely: on two algorithmic tasks, one run each, a coincidence of three times is noted by eye; the method of estimating the mutual information is not described, the circuit measures are not measured, and the explanatory part is an argument. What is valuable is the setting itself (three frames on the same runs) and the question posed outright: could the second period be optimised deliberately.
