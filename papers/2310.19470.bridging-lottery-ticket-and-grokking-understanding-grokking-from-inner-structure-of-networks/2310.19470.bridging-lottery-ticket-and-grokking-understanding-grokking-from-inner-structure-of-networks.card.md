## Concepts covered

**[Sparse subnetworks and the lottery ticket](../../concepts/sparse-subnetwork-lottery-ticket.md)** — the work is the first to relate the lottery-ticket hypothesis to grokking directly, and the relation is an operative rather than a metaphorical one. The mask is taken from a network that has **already generalised** (whence the name "grokked ticket"), applied to the original initialisation $\boldsymbol{\theta}_ {0}$ — and training from scratch under that mask arrives at generalisation almost simultaneously with the memorisation ([`p5-2`](#p5-2), [`p5-3`](#p5-3), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)). What is shortened is not only the difference $t_{\text{gen}}-t_{\text{mem}}$ but also the **ratio** $\tau_{\text{grok}}=t_{\text{gen}}/t_{\text{mem}}$, that is, the matter is not a general acceleration of the training ([`p5-1`](#p5-1), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). An essential caveat of the authors' own: tickets taken **before** 25 thousand epochs do not fully generalise, and their generalising ability equals the accuracy of the base model at that time ([`p6-1`](#p6-1), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)).

**[Grokking](../../concepts/grokking.md)** — the explanation is built as an elimination: the differences between the grokked ticket and a randomly initialised dense network are decomposed into three components — a small weight norm, sparsity and a good structure ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`fig-1`](#fig-1)). The first two are checked by controlled experiments and rejected; the third remains ([`p7-11`](#p7-11)). This is a setting rare in the corpus: not "here is a quantity that correlates" but "here are three possible causes, two of them removed by construction".

**[Weight norm minimisation](../../concepts/weight-norm-minimization.md)** — a direct objection to the corpus's dominant line. Hypothesis 1 states that grokked tickets simply have a smaller norm ([`p7-1`](#p7-1)); it is checked by manufacturing **controlled dense models** — the original weights multiplied by a scalar so that the $L_{1}$ or $L_{2}$ norm coincides with the ticket's ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)). At an equal initial norm the ticket generalises far faster than either ([`p7-9`](#p7-9), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)). A curious detail: by the $L_{2}$ norm all the models converge into one "generalisation zone", while by the $L_{1}$ norm they converge to different values ([`fig-6`](#fig-6)).

**[Weight decay](../../concepts/weight-decay.md)** — reinterpreted as an **explorer of structure** rather than as a means of reducing the weights ([`p13-7`](#p13-7)). The argument of appendix H: if the structure has already been found, the network generalises fully **without** weight decay — at a critical pruning fraction $k=0.81$, found by a sweep in steps of 0.01 ([`p24-3`](#p24-3), [`fig-17`](#fig-17)). Whence the authors' conclusion: weight decay is needed in order to find a good subnetwork and becomes superfluous once it has been found.

**[Progress measures](../../concepts/progress-measures.md)** — a measure of structural shift is proposed: the **Jaccard distance** between the masks taken at epochs $t$ and $t+\delta t$ ($\delta t$ = 2 thousand epochs) ([`p8-6`](#p8-6)). The greatest change of the mask falls exactly on the era of the greatest growth of the test accuracy (16–20 thousand epochs), and layer by layer at that ([`p9-1`](#p9-1), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)). The measure is computed on the training data and requires no test set, but it is **synchronous** with the transition rather than anticipating it.

**[Structured representation learning](../../concepts/structured-representation-learning.md)** — the work's conclusion is formulated as a correction to the line "it is a matter of the quality of the representations": a good structure (the topology of the subnetwork) **contributes to** the acquisition of good representations, that is, the network's internal arrangement stands earlier in the explanation than the representations do ([`p13-6`](#p13-6)).

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — periodicity is taken from Nanda et al. as a known feature of a generalising solution ([`p9-3`](#p9-3), [`p10-3`](#p10-3)) and turned into a measurable quantity: the **Fourier entropy** (FE) of the weights of each neuron ([`p10-5`](#p10-5)). A small FE means that the frequencies of the weights are little dispersed and converge to particular ones ([`p11-1`](#p11-1)). The chief observation: the grokked ticket arrives at a periodic structure already at 2 thousand epochs, whereas the base model does so at 30 thousand ([`p10-4`](#p10-4), [`p11-2`](#p11-2), [`fig-8`](#fig-8)). The ticket's masks themselves repeat the periodic pattern of the weight matrices ([`p26-3`](#p26-3), [`fig-18`](#fig-18)), whereas the masks of pruning-at-initialisation methods do not ([`fig-19`](#fig-19)).

**[Spectral analysis](../../concepts/spectral-analysis-svd-esd-fve.md)** — the second view of "good structure" is graph-theoretic: the weighted spectral gap, the Ramanujan gap, the average path length and the clustering coefficient ([`p28-1`](#p28-1)). The weighted spectral gap grows sharply in the memorisation phase (the weights are dominated by a single principal component) and narrows at the passage to generalisation ([`p11-4`](#p11-4), [`fig-9`](#fig-9)); the average path length grows and the clustering coefficient falls, and both quantities settle in a **moderate** regime rather than at an extreme ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)).

**[Freezing the subnetwork](../../concepts/freezing-subnetwork.md)** — the work's strongest experiment. From the memorised solution $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{mem}}$ an edge-popup is launched: the weights **are not changed at all**, and only the edge scores that determine what is kept are trained ([`p12-3`](#p12-3), [`p8-4`](#p8-4)). By pruning alone the test accuracy reaches 0.92 ([`p13-1`](#p13-1), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)). Combining pruning with weight decay gives the fastest growth of accuracy of the three methods tried.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the work does not analyse the mechanism anew but **inherits** the analysis of Nanda et al.: the network encodes the inputs in a Fourier basis and combines them by the addition formulas, whereupon a constructive interference arises at the correct answer ([`p10-1`](#p10-1), [`p10-3`](#p10-3)). Its own contribution here is not the mechanism but a demonstration that this mechanism is tied to the structure of the subnetwork rather than only to the values of the weights.

**[Memorisation](../../concepts/memorization-phase.md)** — the definitions are operational and positional: $t_{\text{mem}}$ is the step at which the training accuracy exceeds $P$ %, $t_{\text{gen}}$ the step at which the test accuracy exceeds it, with $P=95$ for the modular-arithmetic tasks ([`p5-1`](#p5-1)). Everything else in the work is measured by the ratio of these two steps.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — the principal setting: $(a+b)\bmod p$ at $p=67$, 2211 pairs with $a\geq b$, a split of 40 % for training and 60 % for test ([`p4-5`](#p4-5)); an MLP of four trainable matrices ($W_{\text{emb}}$, $W_{\text{in}}$, $W_{\text{out}}$, $W_{\text{unemb}}$) with ReLU, an embedding dimension of 500 and a hidden layer of 48 neurons ([`p4-6`](#p4-6), [`p4-7`](#p4-7)), AdamW at a rate of $10^{-3}$ and a weight-decay coefficient $\alpha=1.0$ ([`p4-8`](#p4-8)). The default pruning fraction is $k=0.6$ ([`p5-2`](#p5-2)).

**[Sparse parity](../../concepts/sparse-parity.md)** — the set of tasks is deliberately wider than modular arithmetic: polynomial regression and sparse parity are checked besides it, and everywhere the grokked ticket shortens the delay ([`p5-3`](#p5-3), [`fig-2`](#fig-2)).

**[Real data](../../concepts/vision-real-world-data-mnist-cifar.md)** — MNIST is declared in the abstract ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)) but lives in appendix B: a four-layer MLP with a **mean-squared** loss following Liu et al., and it is supported only by the accuracy curves of figure 10 ([`p19-1`](#p19-1)).

It is worth saying separately what the work does **not** contain. There is no way of obtaining a grokked ticket without training a network to generalisation: the mask is taken from a solution that has already generalised, and earlier tickets do not work ([`p6-1`](#p6-1)) — that is, the method explains grokking but does not in fact accelerate training. Nor is there an explanation of **why** this particular structure is good: the periodicity and the graph properties are described but not derived from the task.

Cards of corpus papers: [Power et al. 2022](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md) — the task and the phenomenon, from which the setting is taken; [Omnigrok](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.card.md) — the weight norm as an explanation, objected to here; [Nanda et al. 2023](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md) — periodic representations and progress measures, taken as a basis; [Merrill et al. 2023](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md) — the nearest work on the sparse subnetwork, against which the authors compare themselves; [Liu et al. 2022](../2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning/2205.10343.towards-understanding-grokking-an-effective-theory-of-representation-learning.card.md) — the quality of the representations as the key; [Varma et al. 2023](../2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency/2309.02390.explaining-grokking-through-circuit-efficiency.card.md) — the competing explanation through circuit efficiency.

**[Sparsity](../../concepts/activation-sparsity.md)** — the work's hypothesis 2 is set out checkably: grokked tickets are sparser than the base model, and it is *this* sparsity that yields the shortening of the delay ([`p7-3`](#p7-3)). The check is honest — models of the same sparsity are built in order to separate the concomitance from the cause. Sparsity and the weight norm go here as a pair, as two competing explanations ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)).

**[Seed variance and reproducibility](../../concepts/seed-variance-reproducibility.md)** — three seeds are named outright as a measure of reproducibility rather than as a way of averaging the curves ([`p20-1`](#p20-1)).
## What is shown and what is conjectured

The card editor's remarks following the analysis of the claims (`…claims.txt`). The work is entirely experimental: one method, three hypotheses of which two are rejected, and four sets of structural measurements.

**The work's design is the right one, and this is its chief merit.** The three hypotheses are named **before** the experiments, and the experiments are set up so as to reject rather than confirm them ([`p6-2`](#p6-2), [`p7-1`](#p7-1), [`p7-3`](#p7-3), [`p7-5`](#p7-5)). The controlled dense model equalises the norm ([`p7-7`](#p7-7)), the pruning-at-initialisation methods equalise the sparsity ([`p7-10`](#p7-10)) — and both hypotheses fall. The remaining hypothesis, however, is obtained by **subtraction** rather than checked in its own right: "a good structure" is what is left after the two explicable differences have been removed ([`p7-11`](#p7-11)).

**The edge-popup experiment is worth all the rest of the work.** A test accuracy of 0.92 **with not a single weight changed** ([`p13-1`](#p13-1), [`tab-1`](#tab-1)) is an argument hard to explain otherwise than through structure. Two things must be stipulated. First: 0.92 is not the 1.00 that weight decay gives in the same table, so "turning a memorising network into a generalising one" is true with a caveat about degree. Second: edge-popup **is trained** — the edge scores are updated by backpropagation ([`p12-3`](#p12-3)) — so "with no update of the weights" does not mean "with no optimisation"; the search for structure here is the same gradient search, only over other variables.

**The synchrony of the structural shift is shown; the causality is not.** The Jaccard distance spikes exactly when the test accuracy rises ([`p9-1`](#p9-1), [`fig-7`](#fig-7)), and this is a sound observation. But the spike of the mask and the rise of the accuracy are two projections of one event; nowhere does the work intervene in the structure in order to check the direction of the relation. The edge-popup experiment comes closest to an intervention, but it is set up from a different point and on a different optimisation.

**The graph-property values are given with a spread exceeding the change.** In table 1 the clustering coefficient changes from $0.733\pm 0.41$ to $0.719\pm 0.28$, and the average path length from $1.964\pm 0.006$ to $1.959\pm 0.001$ ([`tab-1`](#tab-1)). In both cases the shift is smaller than the declared spread, and a spread of $\pm 0.41$ on a quantity lying in $[0,1]$ and equal to 0.733 is hard to interpret at all. The conclusion that "both quantities converged to similar values" ([`p13-2`](#p13-2)) is not supported by these numbers.

**The average path length is described in the paper in three different ways.** The abstract promises an **increased** average path length ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)); the main text says that it grows and, once the solution is found, **falls** and stabilises ([`p12-1`](#p12-1)); appendix K says that it grows and then simply stabilises. Table 1 meanwhile shows a slight fall. A reader citing "an increased path length" should know that this place in the paper is itself inconsistent.

**The definition of the Fourier entropy is imprecisely formulated.** In the formula $FE=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\log p_{i}$ the quantity $p_{i}$ is called the normalised value of $\mathcal{F}(\omega)$, that is, the index ought to be the frequency, while $n$ is declared to be the **number of neurons** ([`p11-1`](#p11-1), [`p10-5`](#p10-5)). In the caption to figure 8 the same quantity is called "frequency entropy" and in the text "Fourier entropy". The observation does not collapse on that account (the picture of the weights in figure 8 is visible without the quantity), but FE cannot be reproduced from the paper.

**The magnitudes obtained by the pruning-at-initialisation methods are not analysed.** All three (GraSP, SNIP, SynFlow) at the same sparsity work worse than the base model, and sometimes worse than random pruning ([`p7-10`](#p7-10), [`fig-5`](#fig-5)). For rejecting hypothesis 2 this suffices, but the conclusion "sparsity has nothing to do with it" is weaker than "these three ways of choosing a mask are bad": their pruning fraction is the same, but **what** exactly is pruned is quite different.

**By construction the method cannot run ahead of the training.** The grokked ticket is taken from a solution that has already reached generalisation; tickets taken earlier generalise exactly as far as the base model had generalised by that time ([`p6-1`](#p6-1), [`fig-4`](#fig-4)). The authors do not conceal this and present the method as an instrument of analysis rather than as a way of accelerating training. To cite the work as an acceleration method is to credit it with what it does not contain.

**The claim that "weight decay is an explorer of structure" rests on a single appendix figure.** The assertion is carried into the discussion section ([`p13-7`](#p13-7)), and the evidence beneath it is a single pruning fraction $k=0.81$, found by a sweep in steps of 0.01 on one task ([`p24-3`](#p24-3), [`fig-17`](#fig-17)). The observation itself is curious: at a precisely chosen fraction the ticket generalises with no weight decay at all. But the "critical fraction" here is fitted to the desired outcome rather than predicted. The appendix, moreover, sets out one and the same experiment twice, almost word for word — two paragraphs before figure 17 and one after ([`p24-3`](#p24-3), [`p24-4`](#p24-4)).

**The reproducibility is checked, if modestly.** Three seeds for figures 2 and 5 and for the experiments of section 4.1, and for the transformer as well ([`p20-1`](#p20-1), [`fig-12`](#fig-12)). This is more than the corpus average, but the curves of the principal figures are given without error bars.

**MNIST carries more weight in the abstract than in the paper.** The task is named among the four on which a shortening of the delay is shown ([`p1-2`](#p1-2)), and is supported by appendix B: a four-layer MLP with a mean-squared loss and the accuracy curves of figure 10 ([`p19-1`](#p19-1)). There is no structural analysis, no graph properties and no Fourier entropy for MNIST — all of that is done for modular addition alone.

**Its place in the corpus.** The work moves the question from *quantities* (the norm, the sparsity) to the *arrangement* of the network, and does so in an experimentally checkable way: a mask taken from a generalised solution accelerates the transition; masks of the same sparsity but of a different origin do not. The most valuable thing for the corpus is not the grokked ticket itself but the edge-popup experiment: it shows that the passage from memorisation to generalisation can occur with the weights **entirely immobile**. The price: a narrow setting (modular addition, an MLP of 48 neurons), measurements that are synchronous but not causal, and claims about graph properties unsupported by their spread.

## Common misreadings and overstatements

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text: places where this paper restates someone else's results with an error, or without the qualifications their authors attached. The "details" links in the "Common misreadings and overstatements of this paper" sections of the cited works' cards lead here.

###### mc-2201-02177
**Power et al. (2201.02177) — weight decay declared necessary.** The paper twice credits the original work with a thesis of the necessity of weight decay: `"The original paper (Power et al., 2022) showed that adding weight decay during training is necessary for triggering grokking"` and `"the original paper (Power et al., 2022) posited that weight decay plays a pivotal role in grokking, i.e., test accuracy will not increase without weight decay"`. Both claims contradict the very paper cited: its canonical plot is obtained [on Adam with no weight decay at all](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#sec-a1-2), and validation accuracy on it does reach the training level — merely after $\sim 10^{6}$ steps. Power et al. asserted only an [effectiveness](../2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets/2201.02177.grokking-generalization-beyond-overfitting-on-small-algorithmic-datasets.card.md#p2-3): weight decay is "particularly effective at improving generalisation" — which concerns the economy of data rather than a condition for the effect's existence. The likely source of the error: an observation resembling necessity does exist in the corpus, but in other authors — [Nanda et al., appendix D.1](../2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability/2301.05217.progress-measures-for-grokking-via-mechanistic-interpretability.card.md): in their setting (cross-entropy, AdamW) grokking without regularisation is far slower and far harder to elicit. An observation made with a caveat about their own setting has been carried over to "the original paper" with the caveat dropped.

###### mc-2210-01117
**Omnigrok (2210.01117) — "with cross-entropy, with no problems".** `"In (Liu et al., 2023a), it was confirmed in the Appendix that grokking occurred without any problems, even when trying with cross-entropy"`. The reference leads to that very [appendix E](../2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data/original/2210.01117.omnigrok-grokking-beyond-algorithmic-data.md#sec-e) in which the authors describe precisely the problems: cross-entropy required an initialisation scale of α=100 — two orders of magnitude above the usual — the test accuracy first plateaus above the level of chance, and "the dynamics is not as clean as with the MSE loss"; the authors call it "grokking" with a caveat that they consider such a classification "still fair". "Without any problems" turns a cautious "it reproduces, but worse" into an unconditional confirmation.

###### mc-2310-17247
**Miller et al. (2310.17247) — a double substitution of Merrill → Miller.** (1) `"previous works proposed possible explanations, including … sparsity of generalizing network (Miller et al., 2023)"` and (2) `"good subnetwork (grokked ticket) weights become stronger, as indicated in Miller et al. (2023)"` — both theses belong to [Merrill et al.](../2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks/2303.11873.a-tale-of-two-circuits-grokking-as-competition-of-sparse-and-dense-subnetworks.card.md), whom the same paper cites correctly elsewhere (n−k parity, the emergence of a sparse subnetwork). Miller contains nothing about a strengthening of subnetwork weights: [the work widens the range of models — Gaussian processes, linear regression, a Bayesian neural network](../2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity/2310.17247.grokking-beyond-neural-networks-an-empirical-exploration-with-model-complexity.card.md#p1-1) — and merely retells the sparsity thesis with a reference to its primary source. Both substantive references to Miller in the paper have turned out to be erroneous — a classic collision of similar surnames.
