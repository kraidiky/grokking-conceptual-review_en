# Nguyen 2026 — The Discrete-Log Clock: How a Transformer Learns Modular Multiplication

See also: [the global concept index](../../concepts/index.md).

**Huu Danh Nguyen** (Stanford University).

arXiv [2606.17399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17399), June 2026 (v1), 5 pages, 5 figures, 2 tables. A workshop paper (the Mechanistic Interpretability Workshop, ICML 2026), a single author. Citations by Semantic Scholar — 0, in the corpus — 0. The subject: the "dense" Fourier spectrum of the embeddings of a grokked transformer on modular multiplication is declared an artefact of the basis — the right instrument is the Fourier transform on the multiplicative group $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}$, that is, an ordinary DFT after reindexing the rows of the embedding by the discrete logarithm. In that basis the embedding spectrum of a one-layer transformer on $a\cdot b\bmod 113$ is sparse (a Gini of 0.579 against 0.071, 4 key frequencies $\{2,8,47,56\}$), 96.9% of the MLP neurons are single-frequency, and the activation heat maps in log order give diagonal bands — the signature of addition in log space. The mechanism is named the "Discrete-Log Clock": multiplication is reduced to addition through the discrete logarithm, and from there the "clock" mechanism of Nanda et al. takes over.

## Concepts covered

**[Fourier features and circuits](../../concepts/fourier-features-circuits.md)** — the central claim: the "dense" spectrum of multiplication in earlier works is not a property of the algorithm but a consequence of analysing it in the additive basis; after reindexing by the discrete logarithm the embedding spectrum gathers into 4 peaks at the frequencies $\{2,8,47,56\}$ (a Gini of 0.579 against 0.071, a growth of 8.1 fold), and 496 of the 512 MLP neurons hold $>$85% of their energy at a single multiplicative frequency against exactly zero in the additive basis ([`p3-5`](#p3-5)). Analysis: a warning to every work in which "the density of the Fourier spectrum" serves as an index of progress — a measure of sparsity depends on the choice of basis; but the "additive basis" here is built modulo $q=112$ rather than $p=113$ as in the works cited, and part of the claimed "density" may be an artefact of the shifted modulus; and the three "independent" pieces of evidence (the spectrum, the neurons, the SVD) are taken from one object — the embedding matrix and its image in the MLP.

**[Modular arithmetic](../../concepts/modular-arithmetic.md)** — a setting on the multiplicative group: $a\cdot b\bmod 113$ over all pairs from $\{1,\ldots,112\}$ with zero deliberately excluded, a primitive root $g=3$, and the isomorphism $\log_{g}:(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}/112\mathbb{Z}$ turning the multiplication table into an addition table modulo 112; the setup is an exact repetition of Nanda et al. (30% of the pairs, AdamW, weight decay 1.0, 40,000 full-batch epochs), with grokking between epochs 9,000 and 14,000 ([`p2-2`](#p2-2)). Analysis: excluding zero makes the task a group operation but moves the setting away from the one in which the earlier works obtained "all frequencies" — this possibility is not considered; and the analysis is done on the final model without checkpoints, so the tie of the sparse structure to the moment of grokking is not measured.

**[Group representations and cosets](../../concepts/group-representations-cosets.md)** — the basis of multiplicative characters as a Fourier transform on the group $(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{*}$: the embedding decomposes over the irreducible representations of the cyclic group of order 112, the principal components of $W_{E}$ in log order are sinusoidal, and an effective rank of 10.3 out of 112 agrees with the four frequencies ([`p3-8`](#p3-8)). Analysis: the works of Chughtai and Stander on irreducible representations are set aside as being "about other groups", although the multiplicative characters are exactly the same apparatus for an abelian group, and the connection is not spelled out; and the "4 incommensurable frequencies" in the interference argument are wrong — 2, 8 and 56 share a common divisor, and at a shift of $\Delta=56$ the discrimination rests entirely on the single frequency 47, which the paper does not notice.

**[Mechanistic interpretability](../../concepts/mechanistic-interpretability.md)** — the methodological contribution: the basis of the analysis must match the algebraic structure of the task, or else the standard instruments see noise where there is structure; the algorithm is laid out in four steps (a sinusoidal embedding of $\log_{g}(a)$, a transfer by attention, a trigonometric addition in the MLP, and scoring by constructive interference) ([`p3-9`](#p3-9)). Analysis: there are no causal interventions at all — no ablation of the key frequencies, no activation patching, no check that the logits are assembled by the formula; appendix A is an exposition rather than a derivation (the amplitudes and phases are nowhere measured, and the section on attention is two sentences without measurements); and the addenda of section 5 (10 primes, ~80% of seeds, exponentiation) are given with no numbers, no figures and no protocol.

## What is shown and what is conjectured

These are the card editor's remarks, not the authors' text, drawn from a numbered pass over the paper's claims.

**What is valuable — a short and clean demonstration that the measure depends on the basis.** The contrast is sharp and measured four ways (the Gini, the number of key frequencies, the fraction of single-frequency neurons, the mean maximum share — Table 1); the heat maps in log order and the sinusoidal principal components give a vivid signature of addition in log space; the effective rank of 10.3 agrees numerically with the four frequencies (the paper does not note this, but it works in its favour); and the idea joins the corpus directly — in Zhang et al. 2026 the reindexing by the discrete logarithm for multiplication is already mentioned, and here it acquires empirical content.

**The mechanism is postulated rather than proved, and the novelty was already partly published.** The whole mechanistic part rests on correlations of the final model: no causal interventions, no checkpoints and no measured amplitudes; §1 declares the multiplication algorithm "poorly characterised", whereas §2 itself reports that Doshi et al. analytically found the discrete logarithm in the MLP weights — what remains is an empirical confirmation in a trained ReLU transformer, not a discovery; the dispute with Furuta is conducted against a coarser formulation than the one measured there (Furuta's index is the cosine shift of the FCR, not the density of the frequencies); one run at $p=113$, with no spreads and no statistics; and the "Inverse Participation Ratio" behaves as a participation ratio — the name contradicts the direction of the scale.

**How the work will be cited** ("it is proved that a transformer solves modular multiplication by the Discrete-Log Clock algorithm") — more precisely: on one model at $p=113$ it is shown that changing the basis to the multiplicative one makes the embedding spectrum sparse and the neurons single-frequency; the algorithm itself is postulated and not verified causally, and the tie of the structure to the moment of grokking is not measured.
